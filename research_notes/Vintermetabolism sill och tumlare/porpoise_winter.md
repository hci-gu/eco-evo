# Harbour porpoise (Phocoena phocoena) winter energetics, diet and movements - Skagerrak / Kattegat / Belt Sea / North Sea

Model reference values being checked (from the assignment): ~126 MJ per tonne per 6-h tick when feeding
(= ~0.50 MJ/kg/day), reserve lasting ~5.5 days from full, max intake ~14 % of body mass per day, diet = herring
(4.5-8.7 kJ/g) + gadoids only.

Unit conversions used below (my arithmetic, not from sources): 1 MJ/kg/day = 1000 MJ/t/day = 250 MJ/t per 6-h tick.

---

## 1. Seasonal food intake in captivity (Kastelein / SEAMARCO, Fjord&Baelt)

### Takeaway
Captive porpoises held at natural North Sea / Danish water temperatures eat roughly 4-7 % of body mass per day
(~0.3-0.5 MJ/kg/day), with intake rising from mid-summer to a peak in autumn (Nov), body mass and blubber peaking
in winter and the minimum in summer. Intake, mass and blubber are all correlated with (inversely to) water temperature.
High confidence on pattern, medium on exact numbers (very small n: 1-3 animals per study).

### Cited Findings
- Kastelein et al. 2018 (Aquatic Mammals 44:76-91), one male at SEAMARCO (NL, North Sea water): food consumption
  ~1,200-4,400 g/day, generally ~2,400 g/day ("nearly 7 % of body mass"); energy intake 9,000-26,000 kJ/day, average
  ~18,000 kJ/day; food intake fluctuated seasonally with increases in food intake preceding weight gains; large weight
  increase in winter months with colder water, correlated with increases in girth and blubber thickness; blubber
  thickness 15-32 mm. Fish energy: sprat 7.9 kJ/g, herring 6.5 kJ/g, mackerel 7.7 kJ/g, capelin 6.1 kJ/g —
  [Aquatic Mammals article page](https://www.aquaticmammalsjournal.org/article/vol-44-iss-1-kastelein-seasonal/);
  [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322507348_Seasonal_Changes_in_Food_Consumption_Respiration_Rate_and_Body_Condition_of_a_Male_Harbor_Porpoise_Phocoena_phocoena)
  (values taken from search-engine abstract summaries; the PDF itself returned 403, so medium confidence on exact figures).
- Kastelein (SEAMARCO) WOZEP 2018 summary: "The body mass, blubber thickness, & food consumption of harbor porpoises
  fluctuate seasonally & are correlated with water temperature." Body mass peaks of 44-45 kg (male) and 51-56 kg
  (female) in winter, lows of 41-44 kg and 47-48 kg in summer —
  [Kastelein WOZEP slides](https://tethys.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/publications/10_marine_mammals_seamarco_r_kastelein.pdf);
  mass ranges from [search summary of Kastelein papers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330398861_Reduction_in_Body_Mass_and_Blubber_Thickness_of_Harbor_Porpoises_Phocoena_phocoena_Due_to_Near-Fasting_for_24_Hours_in_Four_Seasons) (medium confidence).
- Kastelein et al. 2018 fasting report (SEAMARCO final report 2018-01; published Aquatic Mammals 2019), two animals in
  Eastern Scheldt seawater, diet 65 % sprat / 25 % herring / 10 % mackerel (lab-measured 7.9 / 6.5 / 7.7 kJ/g wet).
  Season means (Table 1) —
  [SEAMARCO fasting report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf):

- Same report: animals were fed 4-5 meals/day; the ration was adjusted weekly by body mass and "the expected change in
  water and air temperatures in the following week"; initial food passage time ~2.5 h (Kastelein et al. 1997d) —
  [SEAMARCO fasting report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf).
- Rojano-Donate et al. 2018 (captive animals at Fjord&Baelt, Kerteminde, Denmark, in a net pen with natural fjord water
  < 0 to > 20 deg C): energy intake 95 % of data between 15.5 and 31.3 MJ/day; intake "increased steeply in mid-July,
  reaching a peak in mid-November", > 50 % above the minimum; assimilation efficiency assumed 90 %; fat 24-37 % of
  body mass; mid-dorsal blubber 17-38 mm seasonally, "approximately doubling in blubber thickness from summer to winter";
  lean body mass stable year-round —
  [Rojano-Donate et al. 2018, J Exp Biol 221: jeb185827](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/221/23/jeb185827/20508/High-field-metabolic-rates-of-wild-harbour).
- Kastelein et al. 1997: porpoises eat 2-6 kg fish/day = 4-9.5 % of body mass depending on size and reproductive
  state, = 8,000-25,000 kJ/day (8-25 MJ/d) —
  [Kastelein et al. 1997 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/40204137_Food_consumption_and_body_weight_of_harbour_porpoises_Phocoena_phocoena)
  (via search summary; also cited as 8-25 MJ/d in [Andreasen et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf)).
- Lockyer et al. 2003 (NAMMCO Sci Publ 5:107-120; captive Eigil and Freja at Fjord&Baelt, Kerteminde, natural Danish
  water): daily food consumption ~7-9.5 % of body weight; intake "increased steeply in mid-July, reaching a peak in
  mid-November", then decreased to a minimum in April; animals fattened seasonally in response to declining sea
  temperature (and perhaps daylight) in winter —
  [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275956052_Monitoring_growth_and_energy_utilisation_of_the_harbour_porpoise_Phocoena_phocoena_in_human_care)
  (search-summary level; monthly tables not retrieved). Note the Fjord&Baelt animals ate a higher %BM (7-9.5 %) than
  the SEAMARCO animals (4-6 %).
- Lockyer (2007) estimated intake 21-42 MJ/d from porpoises in an outdoor enclosure (Fjord&Baelt) — as cited in
  [Andreasen et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf).
- Maximum intake per feeding (Kastelein et al. 2019, "High levels of food intake ... insight into recovery from
  disturbance"): up to ~3 kg (~2,700 ml) in one feed for ~155 cm animals, i.e. up to ~98 % of the normal daily food
  mass in a single bout after 2-24 h fasting; normal meal ~20 % of daily ration; ad libitum feeding was discontinued
  because of severe constipation —
  [Porpoise Research Library abstract](https://porpoise.org/library/high-levels-of-food-intake-in-harbor-porpoises-phocoena-phocoena-insight-into-recovery-from-disturbance/).

### Inferences
- From the SEAMARCO Table 1 (diet ~7.5 kJ/g by mass-weighted mix, my calculation): F05 ate ~12.8 MJ/d in summer
  (0.31 MJ/kg/d, 4.1 % BM/d) and ~19.6 MJ/d in autumn (0.44 MJ/kg/d, 5.8 % BM/d); winter ~15 MJ/d (0.35 MJ/kg/d,
  4.7 % BM/d). Subadult M06: 0.41-0.48 MJ/kg/d, 5.5-6.4 % BM/d. Kastelein 2018 male: 18 MJ/d / ~44 kg ~ 0.41 MJ/kg/d.
- So captive *gross intake* is ~0.3-0.5 MJ/kg/day; the model's 0.50 MJ/kg/day sits at the upper end (autumn / growing
  animal) rather than the mean. Assimilated (x0.9) it is ~0.28-0.45.
- The model's 14 % BM/day max intake has no direct support as a sustained daily rate: observed daily intakes are
  4-9.5 % BM; single meals of ~3 kg (~7 % BM for a 43 kg animal) are possible. 14 %/day is plausible only as a
  short-term catch-up ceiling, and ad lib feeding caused constipation in captivity.
- The intake peak is autumn to early winter (mid-November in Fjord&Baelt animals; Lockyer et al. 2003 and
  Rojano-Donate 2018), falling to a minimum in April. In the SEAMARCO data the adult female ate most in autumn, while
  the growing subadult male ate slightly more in winter (2.1 vs 1.9 kg/d) - so late winter is intermediate, not minimal.

### Gaps
- Month-by-month kJ/kg/day table and the exact food-intake vs water temperature regression from Kastelein et al. 2018
  could not be read (PDF blocked, 403). Same for the Kastelein female/Lockyer et al. 2003 monthly tables.

## 2. Field metabolic rate (FMR) and resting metabolism

### Takeaway
Wild adult porpoises in inner Danish waters have FMR ~21.7 MJ/d (range 7.8-31 MJ/d across all sizes), 3-4x Kleiber BMR;
captive animals ~17-18 MJ/d, remarkably stable across seasons. Per kg this is ~0.33-0.43 MJ/kg/d. High confidence.

### Cited Findings
- 13 wild porpoises tagged after release from pound nets in inner Danish waters 2012-2016: FMR 7.8-31.0 MJ/day
  (8.8-20.1 ml O2 kg-1 min-1); adults (n=5) mean 21.7 MJ/day (12.1 ml O2 kg-1 min-1); 3-4x Kleiber-predicted BMR;
  28 % above Nagy's terrestrial prediction (41 % for adults); FMR scales with body mass^0.9; wild body mass range
  26-76 kg — [Rojano-Donate et al. 2018](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/221/23/jeb185827/20508/High-field-metabolic-rates-of-wild-harbour).
- Captive female Freja (56 kg) ~18.2 MJ/d, with measurements in December, August and February differing only 1-14 %
  ("surprisingly stable" despite large annual water temperature fluctuations); Sif and Eigil 17.2 and 17.7 MJ/d;
  ~5.2 kJ per breath; respiration rate varies < 15 % seasonally (~10 % lower in warm months) —
  [Rojano-Donate et al. 2018](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/221/23/jeb185827/20508/High-field-metabolic-rates-of-wild-harbour).
- Hunting small prey is cheap (< 20 % increase in FMR) but requires > 60 % of time foraging —
  [Rojano-Donate et al. 2018, as summarised by search results](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/221/23/jeb185827/20508/High-field-metabolic-rates-of-wild-harbour).
- Other literature estimates: 14.6 MJ/d for a 40 kg porpoise (Kenney et al. 1997); 10.3 MJ/d for wild non-lactating
  porpoises (Yasui & Gaskin 1986, NW Atlantic - flag: other region); Andreasen et al. used 10 / 20 / 30 MJ/d as
  low/medium/high scenarios, with 20 MJ/d as the central value —
  [Andreasen et al. 2017, Mar Mamm Sci 33:1053-1079](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf).
- Wild foraging rates (Wisniewska et al. 2016, Current Biology; 5 tagged porpoises, Danish waters): up to 550 small
  fish per hour, > 90 % capture success; 0-200 prey/h by day, 50-550 prey/h at night; prey mostly 3-10 cm —
  [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303557672_Ultra-High_Foraging_Rates_of_Harbor_Porpoises_Make_Them_Vulnerable_to_Anthropogenic_Disturbance);
  [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/556064); prey size per
  [Andreasen et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf).
- Hoekendijk et al. 2018 challenged the "must feed continuously" interpretation (Mar Mamm Sci 34:258-264) —
  [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mms.12446) (content not read; title only).

### Inferences
- Adult wild FMR 21.7 MJ/d at ~50-60 kg = 0.36-0.43 MJ/kg/d; captive 18 MJ/d / 56 kg = 0.33 MJ/kg/d. The model's
  0.50 MJ/kg/d (126 MJ/t/tick) is ~15-50 % above measured FMR. If the model value is "intake while feeding" with a
  ~90 % assimilation factor, it corresponds to ~0.45 MJ/kg/d assimilated - still at the high end. For smaller/younger
  animals (mass^0.9 scaling is close to linear) per-kg values are only slightly higher.
- Converting 21.7 MJ/d at 90 % assimilation into prey mass: ~3.7 kg/d of 6.5 kJ/g herring (~6-7 % BM), ~5.2 kg/d
  of 4.6 kJ/g gobies or whiting (~9-10 % BM). This is the range in which a 14 %/day ceiling would rarely bind.

### Gaps
- No measured lower critical temperature for porpoises found. Kastelein lab resting metabolic rate values not retrieved.
- Lockyer 2007 (J Mar Biol Assoc UK) review not read directly.

## 3. Thermoregulation, cold water and blubber seasonality

### Takeaway
Porpoises handle winter cold mainly by insulation: blubber roughly doubles from summer to winter, and both captive
measured FMR and respiration rate stay nearly constant across 0-20 deg C. The energetic "cost of winter" is paid in
autumn (fattening), which is when intake peaks and when fasting causes the largest mass loss. Medium-high confidence.

### Cited Findings
- FMR stable across seasons despite < 0 to > 20 deg C water; blubber approximately doubles summer to winter to offset
  thermoregulatory costs; blubber 17-38 mm; fat 24-37 % of body mass —
  [Rojano-Donate et al. 2018](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/221/23/jeb185827/20508/High-field-metabolic-rates-of-wild-harbour).
- SEAMARCO: blubber 16-18 mm in summer (19.9 deg C) vs 24-25 mm in winter (4.7 deg C) for both animals; body mass is
  highest in winter, lowest in summer; "In autumn, porpoises should be gaining weight so that they can be sufficiently
  insulated for the cold water in winter." —
  [SEAMARCO fasting report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf).
- As blubber thins the porpoise loses more heat and must catabolise more fat to keep its 36 deg C core; emaciated sick
  porpoises in rehabilitation could sometimes only be saved by hourly maximal feeding and keeping them in 20-30 deg C
  water — [SEAMARCO fasting report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf).
- Starvation removes blubber mainly from the thorax: starved porpoises 9-11 mm thoracic blubber vs 18-20 mm in normal
  animals (50-60 %), tailstock blubber barely depleted; lipid withdrawn only from the inner thoracic blubber layer —
  [Koopman et al. 2002, Physiol Biochem Zool](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/342799) (abstract-level).
- Blubber thickness vs time of year was most pronounced in porpoises (especially juveniles) among three marine
  mammals in the southern Baltic —
  [Variation of blubber thickness ... southern Baltic Sea (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9726720/) (abstract-level).

### Inferences
- In the model, winter should not by itself raise maintenance cost much if the animal enters winter fat; it is the
  autumn build-up (extra ~50 % intake over the summer minimum, Rojano-Donate) and the thermal penalty of a *thin*
  animal that matter. A temperature-dependent maintenance term would be better expressed as a function of reserve
  (insulation) than of water temperature alone.
- Body condition lowest in summer (captivity) - consistent with summer stranding peaks (section 4).

### Gaps
- No quantitative lower critical temperature or cost-per-degree for porpoises found. Koopman 1998 and Lockyer et al.
  2003 seasonal blubber series not retrieved in detail.

## 4. Starvation timescale and starvation in strandings

### Takeaway
Measured: a 24-h near-fast costs ~3-5 % of body mass (~3.3 % excluding gut contents), most in autumn, least in
summer. Estimated: usable reserve lasts somewhere between ~4.5 days (hard lower bound from measured mass loss) and
~10-20 days (energy route, unverified secondary figure), since only about half of the blubber lipid is mobilisable
(Koopman 2002) and heat-loss feedback shortens survival. Starvation/emaciation is a common stranding finding (~12-17 % in
Baltic-area animals), but strandings peak in summer, not winter. Medium confidence; the time-to-death figure is poorly
constrained.

### Cited Findings
- 24-h near-fasting (< 10 % of ration), 30 trials, both animals lost mass every time: 0.7-3.0 kg per 24 h; mean
  -4.1 % (F05) and -4.0 % (M06) of pre-fast mass, of which ~0.7 % was gut contents; mass loss approx. linear over 24 h;
  F05 -3.1 % summer, -5.4 % autumn, -3.3 % winter, -4.2 % spring; M06 -4.1 / -4.7 / -4.5 / -3.4 %; blubber change 0-3 mm;
  season effect significant (P = 0.003), greatest in autumn, least in summer; double rations for two days restored
  pre-fast mass and blubber —
  [SEAMARCO fasting report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf).
- "If fasting continues for longer than 24 h ... effects on porpoises are likely to be severe"; mass loss may become
  non-linear as blubber thins — [same report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf).
- Secondary summary, primary source NOT verified: "given the calorific value of blubber and a fasting metabolic rate of
  ~20 MJ/day, a fasting porpoise will lose ~1 kg of blubber every 2 days. The 10-20 kg of blubber that adult porpoises
  have, depending on season, will deplete in less than 20 to 40 days", excluding rising thermoregulatory costs and the
  finding (Koopman et al. 2002) that about half the lipid mass is structural blubber not mobilised in starvation. This
  text surfaced in a search summary next to Koopman 2002 but is discussion-style language from a later energetics
  paper; it is NOT in Rojano-Donate et al. 2018 (checked). Only the "structural lipid not mobilised" part is
  attributable to [Koopman et al. 2002](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/342799). Low-medium confidence
  on the 20-40 day numbers.
- Strandings: in the North Sea most porpoises strand in June-August; in the Baltic July-September; Danish coast
  2003-2008 averaged 1.23 strandings/week —
  [NAMMCO harbour porpoise page](https://nammco.no/harbour-porpoise/) (monthly peaks confirmed on page);
  Danish figure from [PLoS ONE 2013, Danish mass stranding 2005](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3584061/) (search summary).
- NAMMCO: "Because of their small size and limited energy reserves, they must feed frequently in order to maintain
  good body condition. If their food source is unavailable, they may die." Also: each spring many emaciated juveniles
  strand on the NE US coast, apparently starved (flag: other region) — [NAMMCO](https://nammco.no/harbour-porpoise/).
- Emaciation in Baltic-area strandings/bycatch: 13 % (Schleswig-Holstein + Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania), 12 %
  (Denmark), 17 % (Poland) — [Health assessment, Environment International 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412020318596)
  (search-summary level, medium confidence). Emaciation a common cause of death in Dutch strandings —
  [same search summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412020318596).
- Leopold 2015 (PhD, 829 Dutch strandings 2005-2014): proposed the "junk food" hypothesis - porpoises may starve by
  eating too much lean, low-energy prey (gobies, gadoids) after leaving formerly sandeel-rich areas; body condition of
  stranded animals ranged from very good to very poor —
  [Leopold 2015 thesis (VLIZ)](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/309933.pdf);
  [phys.org "Harbour porpoises need large, oily fish"](https://phys.org/news/2015-11-harbour-porpoises-large-oily-fish.html)
  (search-summary level).

### Inferences
- Two ways to bound the reserve (my arithmetic):
  (a) Mass route, hard lower bound: fat ~30 % of BM (Rojano-Donate 24-37 %), about half mobilisable (Koopman 2002)
      -> usable ~15 % BM; divided by the measured day-1 tissue loss of ~3.3 % BM/day (4 % minus 0.7 % gut contents)
      -> ~4.5 days. This is a lower bound, because day-1 mass loss also includes water and lean tissue, not only lipid.
  (b) Energy route: ~15 % of a 50 kg animal = 7.5 kg mobilisable blubber; at ~1 kg blubber per 2 days at 20 MJ/d
      (the unverified secondary figure above) -> ~15 days; the 20-40 day figure halved for structural lipid gives
      ~10-20 days. Thermal feedback as blubber thins shortens this.
  The model's 5.5 days sits near bound (a) - defensible for a lean (summer) or juvenile animal, short for a fat
  winter adult (blubber ~25 mm, BM at annual maximum).
- Reserve capacity in the model could reasonably vary seasonally (larger in winter) rather than being constant.
- Starvation in the wild seems to hit juveniles/post-weaning and summer-lean animals; there is no evidence here that
  winter is the main starvation season in the Skagerrak/Kattegat region.

### Gaps
- No direct measurement of time-to-death without food found. The frequently repeated claim that porpoises "starve to
  death in less than a week" could not be traced to a primary source (not on the NAMMCO page) - treat as unverified.
- Swedish stranding pathology (Neimanis et al. 2022, Animals 12:369, causes of death in Swedish waters) - fetch blocked
  (403); month-wise emaciation not obtained. Siebert / Kesselring German seasonal emaciation data not retrieved.

## 5. Winter diet in Skagerrak / Kattegat / Belt Sea / North Sea

### Takeaway
In inner Danish waters, winter (Q1) diet of adults is dominated by cod and herring (> 80 % of mass in Q1 and Q4), and
herring is especially a winter/spring prey (> 50 % of annual herring intake in Q1 in the western Baltic). Juveniles eat
many gobies (~28-38 % of mass outside Q3). In Kattegat/Skagerrak herring is the top prey by weight, with cod and
poor cod; in the eastern North Sea whiting dominates. The model's herring + gadoid diet covers the bulk of adult winter
diet but omits gobies (juveniles) and sprat. High confidence on composition, medium on exact percentages.

### Cited Findings
- Western Baltic (339 stomachs, 1980-2011): adults cod 36 %, herring 34 % (together ~70 % of diet mass); juveniles cod
  26 %, gobies 25 %, herring 18 %, whiting 7 %, sprat 6 %; whiting 21 % of juvenile diet in Q3; juvenile gobies
  28-38 % except Q3 (10 %); herring mainly eaten in Q1-Q2; adults: in Q1 and Q4 cod + herring > 80 % of diet; eelpout
  ~25 % in Q3; sandeels mostly Q2; > 50 % of annual herring intake in Q1, mostly intermediate size class; herring timing
  "coinciding with the migration of spring-spawning herring from the feeding grounds in the Skagerrak and North Sea to
  the Rugen area"; cod age 1-2 most taken —
  [Andreasen et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf).
- Belt Sea population review (Sveegaard thesis Paper V, Lick 1986-1994 + Andreasen 2009 data): overall cod 34 % and
  herring 28 % of ingested mass, whiting 9 %, gobies 7 %; cod 38-56 % of mass in every quarter (largest in Q1, Q3,
  Q4); herring largest share in Q1 (18 %); gobies largest in Q2 (22 %); whiting 8 % in Q1, 19 % in Q3; gobies occur in
  53-58 % of stomachs in Q1, Q2, Q4; herring occurred in 22-30 % of stomachs in all quarters. Winter (Q1) prey summary:
  "Herring"; herring at high densities in the Sound, Great Belt and Kattegat in winter. Gobies suggested to be
  "back up" prey —
  [Sveegaard 2011 PhD thesis, Aarhus Univ./NERI](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- The Sound (53 stomachs): low porpoise density Nov-Mar, high Apr-Oct; in the low (winter) season mean prey weight per
  stomach, frequency of occurrence and prey diversity were lower; cod main prey by weight in the high season, herring in
  the low (winter) season; overwintering herring abundant in the Sound Aug-Feb, but porpoises are scarce there in winter
  (suggested cause: heavy boat traffic making herring unavailable) —
  [Sveegaard 2011 thesis, Paper VII](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- Kattegat and Skagerrak (Aarefjord et al. 1995): most dominant prey by weight herring, then cod and poor cod
  (Trisopterus minutus); eastern North Sea: whiting, then cod and long rough dab —
  [Sveegaard 2011 thesis](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- Borjesson et al. 2003 (Mar Mamm Sci; 112 bycaught porpoises, Kattegat/Skagerrak 1989-1996): diet mainly herring,
  sprat and juvenile gadids (cod, whiting); 35-71 stomachs needed to detect all common prey —
  [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230241141_Diet_of_harbor_porpoises_in_the_Kattegat_and_Skagerrak_seas_Accounting_for_individual_variation_and_sample_size);
  [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/DIET-OF-HARBOR-PORPOISES-IN-THE-KATTEGAT-AND-SEAS:-B%C3%B6rjesson-Berggren/f6866b4d20409ae243b7587068b51cc2d4ad4533)
  (abstract-level only).
- Southern North Sea (Dutch strandings): diet mainly gobies, whiting, sandeels, herring and sprat —
  [Leopold 2015 search summary](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/309933.pdf).
- Prey size: wild tagged porpoises targeted mostly 3-10 cm prey (Wisniewska et al. 2016), as cited in
  [Andreasen et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf).

### Inferences
- For the Skagerrak/Kattegat model area, a winter porpoise diet of herring (+ sprat) and gadoids (cod, whiting, poor
  cod) is realistic for adults. Adding a small benthic/goby-like fallback prey would matter mostly for juveniles.
- Winter consumption by mass is highest in Q1 because prey (herring, sprat) are leanest then (see section 7).

### Gaps
- Borjesson 2003 per-prey %W and prey lengths, and the seasonal breakdown for Kattegat/Skagerrak specifically, were not
  retrieved (403). No Skagerrak-specific winter-only stomach data found.

## 6. Seasonal distribution and movements

### Takeaway
Two populations meet in the area: the Skagerrak/North Sea population shifts west in winter, the Belt Sea population
shifts south; this is a gradual net shift, not a migration. Porpoise density in Kattegat/Skagerrak correlates strongly
with herring density, especially at fronts (Norwegian Trench slope, Kattegat-Skagerrak transition). Medium-high confidence.

### Cited Findings
- Satellite tracking of 64 porpoises over 10 years (North Sea to western Baltic) identified nine high-density areas in
  Kattegat and the Belt Seas, plus the "Tip of Jutland" (northern Kattegat / SE Skagerrak) and the southern slope of the
  Norwegian Trench for the Skagerrak population —
  [Sveegaard et al. 2011, Mar Mamm Sci 27:230-246 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227797014_High-density_areas_for_harbor_porpoises_Phocoena_phocoena_identified_by_satellite_tracking);
  [Sveegaard 2011 thesis](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- "The Skagerrak/North Sea population moves west in the winter and the Belt Sea population moves south ... not a
  coordinated migration but a gradual net movement ... resulting in very low winter abundance in some of the summer
  high-density areas, e.g. the Sound." Births mainly June-July, conception July-August, nursing 8-10 months —
  [Sveegaard 2011 thesis](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- In Kattegat and Skagerrak, porpoise densities (satellite tracks) were strongly positively correlated with herring
  densities (ICES acoustic herring survey), mainly in frontal zones along the Norwegian Trench and the
  Kattegat-Skagerrak transition — [Sveegaard 2011 thesis, Paper VI](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- Baltic (SAMBAH): Belt Sea and Baltic Proper populations separate May-Oct (esp. May-Aug); in winter (esp. Jan-Mar)
  animals are more dispersed and overlap with the Belt Sea population —
  [HELCOM harbour porpoise distribution indicator](https://indicators.helcom.fi/indicator/harbour-porpoise-distribution/);
  [SAMBAH project](https://www.deutsches-meeresmuseum.de/en/science-research/projects/completed-projects/sambah/)
  (search-summary level).

### Inferences
- In a 60x60 km offshore grid in Skagerrak/Kattegat, winter porpoise presence should track herring aggregations
  (fronts, deeper slopes), and some net outflow (westward) in winter is realistic - the model's movement policy
  following herring is consistent with this.

### Gaps
- No quantitative seasonal density ratio (winter/summer) for Swedish Skagerrak/Kattegat waters found; SCANS surveys
  are summer-only.

## 7. Prey energy density values used in porpoise studies

### Takeaway
Herring 4.6-8.4 kJ/g (size/season dependent), sprat 7.9, whiting 4.1-5.0, cod (20 cm) 4.2, gobies 4.62 kJ/g wet.
Gadoids and gobies are roughly half the energy density of fat clupeids. High confidence (well-cited primary values).

### Cited Findings
- Herring 4.6-8.4 kJ/g for 8-30 cm fish; whiting 4.1-5.0 kJ/g for 10-30 cm (Pedersen & Hislop 2001); cod 20 cm
  4.2 kJ/g (Lawson et al. 1998); gobies 4.62 kJ/g for 3-13 cm (Temming & Herrmann 2003); "to optimize energy intake,
  the harbour porpoise would benefit from prioritising herring ... which may also seem to be the case especially in the
  first quarter" — [Sveegaard 2011 thesis, Paper V](https://www2.dmu.dk/pub/phd_sign.pdf).
- Lab-measured fed fish (SEAMARCO): sprat 7.9, herring 6.5, mackerel 7.7, capelin 6.1 kJ/g wet —
  [SEAMARCO fasting report](https://www.noordzeeloket.nl/publish/pages/185078/study_of_fasting_and_body_condition_of_harbor_porpoise.pdf);
  [Kastelein 2018 abstract summary](https://www.aquaticmammalsjournal.org/article/vol-44-iss-1-kastelein-seasonal/).
- Andreasen et al. used quarter-, species- and length-specific energy densities (Pedersen & Hislop 2001; Temming &
  Herrmann 2003); daily prey mass need at 20 MJ/d varied 3.4-3.8 kg (adults) and 3.7-3.8 kg (juveniles) by quarter;
  highest in Q1 "reflecting lower prey energy densities, particularly for herring and sprat"; seasonal variation in
  required mass up to 10 % — [Andreasen et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52475/noaa_52475_DS1.pdf).

### Inferences
- The model's herring 4.5-8.7 kJ/g range matches the Pedersen & Hislop range; Andreasen indicates herring/sprat are at
  the lean end in Q1, so a winter herring value near the bottom of the range is appropriate.
- At 20 MJ/d, a pure lean-winter herring diet (~4.6 kJ/g, 90 % assimilation) needs ~4.8 kg/d (~9-10 % BM for 50 kg)
  - below the 14 % cap but well above captive daily intakes (4-7 %), consistent with wild porpoises needing to forage
  > 60 % of the time.

### Gaps
- No sandeel or sprat seasonal energy density values retrieved directly from primary sources (Pedersen & Hislop 2001
  not read).
