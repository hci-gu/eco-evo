## Q1. Meta-analyses and syntheses of predator dependence

### Takeaway
Every synthesis that tested it finds that feeding rates usually fall with predator density. Purely prey-dependent responses (w = 0) are the exception: 1 of 35 unbiased estimates in DeLong & Vasseur, and 1 of 19 datasets in Skalski & Gilliam. Interference is "mostly intermediate". The Hassell-Varley exponent m has median 0.7 (DeLong & Vasseur sign convention: -0.7), sits mostly between 0 and 1, and 9 of 35 estimates cannot be told apart from ratio dependence (m = 1). Arditi & Akçakaya 1990 found m mostly between 0.5 and 1 after correcting for saturation. Fish are nearly absent from these compilations. DeLong & Vasseur name no fish species in the main text, and the public Stouffer & Novak data list contains two fish predators, of which only one (goldfish on *Daphnia*) appears to vary predator density. None of the syntheses reports Beddington-DeAngelis (BD) parameters with units that could be compared across studies.

### Cited Findings
- DeLong & Vasseur compiled "51 estimates from 37 studies"; 35 came from the unbiased methods (Arditi-Akçakaya regression of log a on log C after a type II fit, or a full simultaneous fit). For those, m has "median -0.7, mean -0.8, mode -0.7", range -2 to 0 (excluding three values of -2: -1.3 to 0). The Discussion gives a central tendency "around -0.6 to -0.7". 9 unbiased estimates were statistically indistinguishable from ratio dependence and only 1 from prey dependence. Title claim: "Mutual interference is common and mostly intermediate in magnitude". Taxa named in the text: parasitoids, knot on mussels, wolf on moose, *Tetrahymena*; no fish named. BD is not fitted. The authors leave open whether interference is "characteristic" (one m at all densities) or "shifting" (rising with density), and see some evidence for shifting in Arditi & Akçakaya's datasets 9, 10 and 15. — [DeLong & Vasseur 2011, BMC Ecology 11:1](https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6785-11-1) (Fetched full text; supplementary table not checked for fish)
- Skalski & Gilliam 2001, Ecology 82(11):3083-3092: 19 datasets from 15 sources, each with feeding rates at two or more prey and two or more predator densities under experimental control. Predator-dependent models (BD, Crowley-Martin, Hassell-Varley) described 18 of 19 better than Holling II, but "no single functional response can best describe all of the data sets". The dataset list, and so the number of fish systems, could not be seen. — [Skalski & Gilliam 2001](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658(2001)082[3083:FRWPIV]2.0.CO;2) (Fetched abstract via secondary snippets; full text 403)
- Stouffer & Novak 2021 reanalysed 77 single-resource consumer-dependence datasets (61 predator, 17 parasitoid). ### Inferences
- In the attack-rate form, BD gives a'(P) = a/(1+wP), so the local Hassell-Varley exponent is m_local = wP/(1+wP). A BD curve therefore reproduces any m < 1 at one reference density, through wP_ref = m/(1-m): m = 0.3 gives 0.43, m = 0.5 gives 1.0, m = 0.7 (the DeLong-Vasseur median) gives 2.33, and m = 0.8 gives 4.0. BD can never give m >= 1, and its m falls toward 0 at low predator density, which matches the "shifting" interference hypothesis.
- "Intermediate interference" in the meta-analytic sense therefore means w*P of order 0.5-4 **at the densities of the experiments**. Whether that holds at the model's cell densities is the unit problem in Q4.
- The evidence base is dominated by arthropods, protists, parasitoids and a few vertebrates. Calling it evidence about gadoids is extrapolation across taxa.

## Q2. Fish-specific evidence, especially gadoids

### Takeaway
No tank or field experiment that estimates conspecific interference (m, BD parameters or ratio dependence) for cod, whiting, haddock or saithe was found. The fish-specific evidence is indirect and mixed. Essington & Hansson (2004) fitted predator-dependent responses for Baltic cod on herring and sprat from field data and argued that predator dependence weakens top-down control. The best cell-scale field test for a gadoid found no predator-density effect: about 2000 cod stomachs, 12 years, 2 km resolution, and cod abundance "had no effect on capelin consumption" (Fall et al. 2021). Population-scale density dependence in cod does exist: Baltic cod condition fell with cod abundance and with density per suitable area (Casini et al. 2016), and Barents Sea cod spread over a larger area as stock size rose (Fall et al. 2018). These studies do not separate interference from exploitative competition or prey depletion. Multispecies models that use predator dependence for gadoids (Ecosim) carry it as a fitted "vulnerability", and fitted values span bottom-up to almost mass-action.

### Cited Findings
- Essington & Hansson 2004, CJFAS 61(11):2215-2226. Baltic cod on herring and sprat. "Predator-dependent functional responses decouple predation mortality from fluctuations in predator abundance." Cod predation was the main source of mortality for young sprat and much less important for herring. Sprat mortality was far more sensitive to cod biomass, and sprat "increased nearly threefold following a sharp decline in the cod population" while herring did not. The fitted interference values could not be read. — [Essington & Hansson 2004](https://doi.org/10.1139/f04-146) (Fetched abstract summary; full text 403)
- Secondary only: a 2025 Baltic optimal-control model (Braack & Taylor, Nat. Resour. Model. 38(1)) reportedly takes "attack rate 0.2 per year" and "interference 0.6" for gadids on clupeids from Essington & Hansson. Its functional response is **Crowley-Martin**, and the units and form of the 0.6 could not be verified, so the value should not be transferred to w. — [Braack & Taylor 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/nrm.70000) (Snippet)
- Fall, Johannesen, Englund, Johansen & Fiksen 2021, Ecography 44(5):802-815. Stomach contents of about 2000 Barents Sea cod over 12 years, analysed with GAMs at 2 km resolution. "no indication of food competition as cod abundance had no effect on capelin consumption" (wording from the Bergen repository abstract). Capelin abundance had small effects. Capelin coming close to the seafloor (shallow banks, bank edges) mattered most. — [Fall et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.05473) (Fetched abstract)
- Fall, Ciannelli, Skaret & Johannesen 2018, PLoS One 13(10):e0205921: "Both species expanded their distribution areas as their stock sizes increased". The increased autumn overlap "could mainly be attributed to the shift in cod distribution with increased cod stock biomass". Stock-biomass effects were larger than temperature effects in all models. — [Fall et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0205921) (Fetched full text)
- Casini et al. 2016, R. Soc. Open Sci. 3:160416. Eastern Baltic cod condition, GAMs. 1976-1993: cod abundance had a negative effect and was the only retained predictor (36.4 % deviance explained; 38.9 % with cod density = abundance/suitable area). 1976-2014: cod abundance (negative), sprat (positive), seals (negative) and hypoxic area (negative), 85.9 %. Suitable habitat shrank from about 140 to 90 x 10^3 km2. Condition fell 11-22 % by size class between 1992-96 and 2010-14. The paper speaks of "intra-specific competition" and crowding, not interference versus exploitation. — [Casini et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160416) (Fetched full text)
- Moustahfid et al. 2010, Oecologia 163:1059-1067: 7 piscivores on 4 prey from 30 years of NE US shelf stomach data. "Holling's types II and III ... are the most common" responses. Predator dependence does not appear in the abstract. — [Moustahfid et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-010-1596-2) (Snippet)
- Ecosim, the most widely used multispecies model with predator-dependent consumption, uses Q_ij = v_ij a_ij B_i B_j T_i T_j S_ij M_ij / D_j divided by (v_ij + v_ij T_i M_ij + a_ij M_ij B_j S_ij T_j / D_j), with v = vulnerability. — [Ribeiro et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.00613](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00613) (Fetched full text). The FAO review: "EwE's foraging arena (per-capita consumption by a predator decreases with the overall abundance of that predator)". The IWC called its biological underpinnings "controversial and uncertain" because "there appears to be little observational evidence to distinguish the two models". — [Plagányi 2007, FAO FTP 477](https://www.fao.org/4/a1149e/a1149e03.pdf) (Fetched full text of chapter)
- Fitted gadoid vulnerabilities, Iceland: cod 4+ v = 1.186 (the only fitted group below 2, read as "affected by the abundance and/or availability of their prey"); saithe 4+ and haddock 3+ v > 100. The authors warn that the per-predator search gives one value for all prey of a predator. — [Ribeiro et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00613) (Fetched full text)
- Fitted vulnerabilities, North Sea (1973 and 1991 models, fitted to MSVPA output). The 1991 matrix contains values 1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6, with default 2. The text-extracted table does not align columns reliably, so no value is attributed to a specific gadoid predator here. Rationale: "species whose biomass was far from carry capacity would be able to exert higher mortality on their prey (i.e. a higher v applied by predator)". — [Mackinson & Daskalov 2007, Cefas Tech. Res.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165783600002885); [IMR report 2025-5](https://www.hi.no/en/hi/nettrapporter/rapport-fra-havforskningen-en-2025-5) (Snippet)

### Inferences
- The one direct test at roughly the model's spatial scale (2 km vs 1 km cells) is a **null** for cod on capelin. It cannot rule out weak interference, but it argues against interference strong enough to matter at the cell densities that occur in the field.
- The population-scale signals (Casini, Fall 2018) fit any density-dependent mechanism: exploitative depletion, interference, or ideal-free redistribution. The model already has exploitative depletion, so these signals do not specifically justify a larger w.
- Gadoid vulnerabilities in fitted Ecosim models range from strongly predator-dependent (Iceland cod v = 1.19) to effectively mass-action (haddock, saithe v > 100). The estimate depends on how the fit was set up, which data it was fitted to, and the predator. It is not a robust biological constant.
- The model's w acts on **pooled** gadoid biomass from five species. Every m in Q1 is conspecific. Interference between species (cod-whiting, cod-saithe) is less studied and probably weaker, because diets and depths differ. A w estimated for one species would overstate pooled interference.

## Q3. Mechanism and theory: interference vs exploitation, ideal free distribution, regulation

### Takeaway
"Interference" in this literature is generic. Arditi et al. use it for "any mechanism by which predator density depresses individual predatory performance", including aggregation, not only fights over food. BD can be derived from time lost in predator-predator encounters (Beddington 1975), and it can also be derived from **prey** refuge-switching (Geritz & Gyllenberg). Ecosim's foraging arena produces the same predator dependence from prey behaviour. Under ideal-free redistribution, interference makes predators spread out from the best patches (van der Meer & Ens 1997), and different interference formulations give qualitatively different spatial predictions. On dynamics, weak to moderate BD interference stabilises the predator-prey equilibrium. Above a threshold (w > h/e in Arditi's time units) the system is always stable, but very strong interference drives predator equilibrium density toward zero and lets prey escape control.

### Cited Findings
- Arditi et al. 2004 use "interference" in a generic sense: "any mechanism by which predator density depresses individual predatory performance", noting that Hassell and Varley first attributed it to direct behavioural interference but "other mechanisms (e.g., predator aggregation) could lead to a similar effect". — [Arditi et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crvi.2004.06.007) (Fetched full text)
- Beddington's mechanism, as written by Arditi et al.: predators "also wasted a time w on encounters with other predators", giving g = aN/(1 + ahN + aw(P - P0)). Here w is a **time** and the denominator term is a*w*P. DeAngelis et al. 1975 (Ecology 56(4):881-892) proposed "the same model with purely phenomenological arguments". For w = (a*P0)^-1 the function becomes ratio-dependent, and BD "tends to the ratio-dependent model if the interference parameter w is large". — same source (Fetched full text)
- Dynamics (abstract): "when interference is low, increasing it has a positive effect on asymptotic stability ... When it is high, it is the contrary (with logistic prey growth, increasing the interference parameter ensures stability but leads to very small predator densities)." With logistic prey: "For w > h/e, we always have stability" and "there is always a value of the interference parameter above which the Paradox of Enrichment disappears". The boundary w = (e - hq)/(er) "is the equivalent of m = 1 ... beyond it, the prey is no longer predator-controlled but resource-controlled", and "when w -> +infinity, P* -> 0". — same source (Fetched full text)
- As cited by Arditi et al.: Hwang (two papers, refs [11,12] in Arditi et al.; one is 2003, J. Math. Anal. Appl.) showed for the BD model that "locally stable equilibria are also globally stable, and that periodic orbits, if existing, are unique". Cantrell & Cosner (2001) studied persistence and extinction in reaction-diffusion versions. A consensus holds "that interference has a stabilizing influence on population dynamics", with Hassell & May's caveat of an upper limit. — same source (Fetched full text); originals not retrieved
- Huisman & De Boer 1997 (J. Theor. Biol.) and Geritz & Gyllenberg derived BD from individual-level models. Geritz & Gyllenberg did so "avoiding the usual interference between predators", using prey in active and refuge classes. Under diffusion, BD "is known to produce patterns". — [Conforto, Desvillettes & Soresina, arXiv:1712.10159](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.10159) (Fetched full text; originals not retrieved)
- van der Meer & Ens 1997, J. Anim. Ecol. 66:846-858 (summary): "intake rate is supposed to decrease with increasing predator density as a result of interference among predators, instead of immediate consumption of the prey". The "different models of interference result in qualitatively different predictions for 'ideal' and 'free' predators on: (i) the form of the aggregative response; (ii) the trajectory of the aggregative response as prey is depleted; and (iii) the change in the aggregative response following an influx of predators". This "sheds doubt on the general relevance of any one of these models, particularly if the mathematical formulation of interference is phenomenological". In the body, predators that aggregate strongly in the best patches deplete them and "subsequently spread out to patches of lower quality". — [van der Meer & Ens 1997](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/262864.pdf) (Fetched full text, OCR)
- Sutherland 1983, J. Anim. Ecol. 52, "Aggregation and the 'ideal free' distribution": citation confirmed. Per a secondary source, it adds an interference parameter to the ideal-free gain function for continuously renewed (non-depleting) resources. The equations were not read. — [secondary listing](https://www.u-picardie.fr/~LaboECCHAT/EMEAB4/Commun/Abstracts/Sokolowski2.html) (Snippet)
- Shore crabs, the best-documented marine BD case: 1, 2 or 4 crabs per 0.25 m2 (4-16 per m2). Feeding rate fell with crab density only at one mussel density, through both longer search and agonistic time. A stochastic BD for finite predator numbers was then fitted by maximum likelihood. — Smallegange et al. 2006, Oikos 113:157-167 (Snippet; collected in [Smallegange 2007 PhD thesis](https://www.waddenacademie.nl/fileadmin/inhoud/pdf/06-wadweten/Proefschriften/IMSmallegange_PhDthesis.pdf), DOI not verified); [van der Meer & Smallegange 2009, J. Anim. Ecol. 78:134-142](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2656.2008.01480.x) (Crossref metadata).

### Inferences
- **Fixed-prey regulation.** With prey held fixed, BD gives the predator a per-capita brake. Intake falls to the maintenance level I_m at w*P* = aN/I_m - 1 - a*h*N, so the predator density at which growth stops scales as 1/w. Doubling w halves P* for the same prey field. This is the only sense in which w "regulates" gadoids directly. It reduces intake; it does not remove biomass, unlike the cannibalism the model dropped.
- **Saturation dilutes interference.** Interference acts relative to 1 + a*h*N. Resume 86.3 gives the gadoid saturation term sum_j a*h*B_j = 6.3 in the median occupied cell, and gadoid p90 = 1.95 t/km2. At w = 1 the peak-cell intake reduction is (1+6.3)/(1+6.3+1.95) = 0.79, a 21 % cut. At w = 2 it is 0.65. At the median gadoid density (0.017 t/km2) the cut is 0.23 % at w = 1 and about 1.1 % at w = 5.
- **Redistribution escapes a per-cell w.** Because w is per cell and the policies choose movement, learned redistribution can escape it, as the van der Meer & Ens mechanism predicts. The brake only bites once the **mean** density over the occupied area, B_tot / area, is large enough that w*B_mean is comparable to 1 + a*h*N. Raising w mainly flattens the map until that point and then regulates. The 2-cell bands disappeared at w = 1 for exactly this reason.
- **Translating Arditi's always-stable threshold.** Arditi's w > h/e becomes w_model = a*w_A > a*h/e in model units. For gadoids on herring a*h = 8.7 km2/t. With a biomass conversion efficiency e of order 0.1-0.3 (an assumption; the model's e is energy-based and the system is multi-prey with type III on herring), the threshold is of order 30-90 km2/t, far above w = 1. This is a sufficient condition for a two-species logistic system, not a target. Near it, Arditi warns, P* -> 0 and gadoids stop controlling their prey.

## Q4. Unit conversion: from published interference estimates to w in km2/t

### Takeaway
No published estimate converts cleanly. Hassell-Varley m is dimensionless and scale-free; Arditi et al. note the raw form a = alpha*P^-m is "dimensionally unbalanced" and must be read against P0, the density of one predator in the habitat. BD parameters, where reported, are per individual per arena. Three bridges are possible. (1) HV to BD at a chosen reference density: w = [m/(1-m)]/P_ref. (2) Beddington's mechanism: w = (conspecific encounter rate) x (time lost per encounter) / (individual mass). (3) Ecosim's vulnerability multiplier K: w*P0 = 1/(K-1). All three put the answer in the choice of reference density, which is exactly what the literature does not fix for gadoids at 1 km2 scale.

### Cited Findings
- HV with saturation: g = alpha*N*P^-m / (1 + alpha*h*N*P^-m). The proper dimensionless form is alpha*(P/P0)^-m, with P0 "the density of a 'population' consisting of a single predator in the whole habitat". m = 1 "corresponds to a large interference, while its BDA equivalent is a very large value of w". — [Arditi et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crvi.2004.06.007) (Fetched full text)
- No source in this search reported a BD interference coefficient with units for a fish predator. The crab experiments report densities (1-4 per 0.25 m2) but the fitted values were not visible. — see Q3 (Snippet)
- Ecosim's predator dependence saturates: total consumption "saturates at a constant level for high numbers of predators" (FAO Fig. 4b), and the multiplier caps the predation-mortality increase at K-fold (Q2 snippet). — [Plagányi 2007](https://www.fao.org/4/a1149e/a1149e03.pdf) (Fetched full text)

### Worked conversions (inferences, derivations shown)
1. **Hassell-Varley m to w at a reference density.** From a' = a/(1+wP), m_local = wP/(1+wP), so w = [m/(1-m)] / P_ref.
   - *Scale-free reading* (HV taken literally, same m at every density): at a model gadoid density of 1 t/km2, m = 0.7 gives w = 2.3 km2/t, m = 0.5 gives w = 1.0, m = 0.3 gives w = 0.43. Under this reading the current w = 1.0 equals m = 0.5 at 1 t/km2 and m = 0.66 at the gadoid p90 of 1.95 t/km2 (since w*P = 1.95). At the median 0.017 t/km2 it is m = 0.017, effectively prey-dependent.
   - *Tank-anchored reading* (m measured at the experiment's density and BD used to scale): suppose m = 0.7 was measured at 4 predators per m2, the low end of the crab design. Then w_ind = 2.33/4 = 0.58 m2 per individual. For a 2 kg gadoid (0.002 t), w_model = 0.58 x 10^-6 km2 / 0.002 t = 2.9 x 10^-4 km2/t, so 1 t/km2 of gadoids (500 fish/km2 = 0.0005 per m2) gives w*P = 0.0003, negligible. Matching w = 1 would require w_ind = 2000 m2 per fish, an interference neighbourhood of about 45 x 45 m.
   - **Where it breaks down.** Field mean densities are about 10^4 times lower than tank densities, and the two readings differ by more than three orders of magnitude. Which one holds is the open "characteristic vs shifting" question (DeLong & Vasseur) and the formulation dependence of van der Meer & Ens. Real gadoids aggregate on prey patches and banks, so the density that matters for interference is the local patch density, not the 1 km2 cell mean. A tank m cannot be carried to the field without a model of within-cell aggregation.
2. **Beddington mechanism.** If gadoids meet each other at the same per-area rate at which they search for prey (a = 0.04 km2 per t per tick), then w = a*tau, with tau the ticks lost per encounter-equivalent. w = 1 then requires tau = 1/0.04 = 25 ticks (about 6 days) per encounter-equivalent. Even with a conspecific detection rate 10 times the prey search rate, tau = 2.5 ticks (15 h). Direct behavioural interference at 1 km2 mean densities is implausibly weak to give w = 1. A w of that size must stand for something else, such as within-cell aggregation, shared-patch depletion or prey refuging (the Geritz-Gyllenberg / foraging-arena reading).
3. **Ecosim vulnerability.** With h = 0, total consumption by the predator group is aNP/(1+wP), which tends to aN/w as P grows. The ratio of maximum to baseline predation mortality is therefore K = (1 + wP0)/(wP0), which gives **w*P0 = 1/(K-1)**. The same result follows from Ecosim's own equation with T = S = M = D = 1: Q = (a/2) N P / (1 + aP/(2v)). Values:
   - default K = 2 gives w*P0 = 1.0;
   - North Sea matrix values 1.1-6 give w*P0 = 10 to 0.2;
   - Iceland cod 4+ (K = 1.186) gives 5.4; haddock and saithe (K > 100) give below 0.01.
   - Model equivalents: w = 1 at the gadoid p90 (1.95 t/km2) corresponds to K = 1 + 1/1.95 = 1.51. At the median 0.017 t/km2 it corresponds to K of about 60.
   - Converting to w needs P0, the **base-year mean gadoid biomass density over the whole modelled area** (t/km2): w = 1/((K-1)*P0). For example, P0 = 0.5 t/km2 and K = 2 give w = 2 km2/t.
   - Caveats: Ecosim's K is fitted to aggregate time series and absorbs unmodelled processes. Its predator dependence comes from a prey-side refuge exchange, not predator behaviour. It applies to area-averaged biomass, not cells. Handling time (D_j) changes the algebra. The "at most K-fold" definition is snippet-level.
   - Also, at h = 0 the BD cap on predation mortality in a cell is a/w per tick: 0.04 per tick (about 0.16 per day) at w = 1, and half that at w = 2.

## Implications for gadoid interference in the model

- **Zero vs positive.** The literature clearly supports w > 0. Prey dependence is the exception in every synthesis (1/35 unbiased m estimates, 1/19 datasets), and the classic dynamic result is that moderate BD interference stabilises. A positive gadoid w is defensible in principle, even though the current value was chosen for numerical reasons.
- **Weak vs strong.** Across taxa interference is intermediate, not ratio-dependent: median m of about 0.7, mostly 0.5-1 after the saturation correction. Read scale-free at 1 t/km2 that would mean w of about 1-4 km2/t, so the current w = 1.0 is not out of line. Read as BD anchored to experimental densities, w for gadoids at 1 km2 mean densities would be tiny (around 10^-4). The cell-scale field test for cod (Fall et al. 2021) found no predator-density effect, which points toward the weak end at the scales the model resolves.
- **What the literature cannot pin down.** (i) No gadoid-specific interference estimate exists in the retrieved literature, and fish are about 1 dataset in the interference syntheses. (ii) The conversion to km2/t hinges on the reference density, which differs by about 10^4 between tanks and fields and depends on within-cell aggregation. (iii) Fitted Ecosim vulnerabilities for gadoids range from strongly predator-dependent to mass-action, and they encode prey-side processes. (iv) The model applies w to five pooled species, whereas all estimates are conspecific. Any specific w between about 0.5 and 4 km2/t can be argued for, and none can be derived.
- **Is raising w the right brake for 35-40 %/yr growth? (inference)** BD interference regulates only by cutting intake, P* scales as 1/w for a fixed prey field, and it is diluted by saturation: at w = 1 the cut is 21 % in p90 cells and almost nothing in median cells. Policies can escape a per-cell w by spreading out, so a larger w first flattens the distribution and brakes only once mean density catches up. Very large w (Arditi's threshold, of order 30-90 km2/t here) drives gadoid equilibrium density toward zero and releases the prey from control. In real gadoid stocks, density dependence is documented mostly as exploitative (food limitation, habitat compression, condition) and as cannibalism. Neither is interference. A moderate increase of w (for example to about 2, which is m of about 0.67 at 1 t/km2 or Ecosim K = 1.5 at 1 t/km2) is consistent with the literature. Using w as the main population brake goes beyond what the evidence supports. Check first whether the missing brake should come from prey depletion, cannibalism or recruitment.
