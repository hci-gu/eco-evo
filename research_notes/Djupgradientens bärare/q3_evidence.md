# Q3. Dark detection of krill by gadoids (2026-11-22, time-boxed ~15 min)

Tags: [F] full text, [A] abstract only, [S] secondary / analogue, [D] derived, [J] judgement.

## Verdict

**No measurement was found of a gadoid's (or any fish's) detection or reaction distance on euphausiids or mysids in
darkness or very low light.**

- The nearest gadoid number is on Artemia. Juvenile walleye pollock (about 40-115 mm TL) in total darkness
  detected adult Artemia "only when they were very close (<=1 cm)". The authors judge detection was by the lateral
  line, "at distances less than 10 mm" (Ryer & Olla 1999 [F]).
- The prey is a slow brine shrimp in a tank, not krill. The fish are small juveniles, not adult saithe.
- The visual threshold is "approximately 2 orders of magnitude below" sablefish's 5 x 10^-5 uE/m2/s. That gives
  about 5 x 10^-7 [D]; the abstract exponent itself is OCR-garbled.
- No study shows fish using krill bioluminescence or krill sounds to find krill. Both cues exist (Widder 1992;
  Kühn 2022).
- Fish-prey Ke and R_nv are in "Torskfiskars syn i mörker" and are not repeated here.

## Entries

### Q3-1. Ryer & Olla 1999, "Light-induced changes in the prey consumption and behavior of two juvenile planktivorous fish"

- **Reference:** Mar Ecol Prog Ser 181:41-51. doi:10.3354/meps181041.
- **Tag:** [F] for walleye pollock; [S] as an analogue for saithe on krill.
- **Quotes file:** `data/q3_ryer1999/ryer1999_quotes.txt`. The PDF was deleted (© Inter-Research).
- **Material:** age-0 walleye pollock (experiments about 42-114 mm TL) and sablefish. Infrared-observed laboratory
  tanks, live adult Artemia, about 2 prey/l.
- **Quotes:**
  - Abstract: "both species were capable of detecting and capturing prey in darkness."
  - Results: "In the dark, both species adopted a more exaggerated mode of particulate feeding ... Since prey were
    detected only when they were very close (<=1 cm), this often involved convoluted body gyrations and sinusoidal
    backing behavior. Attempts to engulf prey were often repeated 2 or 3 times in slightly different directions,
    suggesting that fish were unable to precisely determine prey location."
  - Discussion: "we consider it probable that juvenile walleye pollock and sablefish detected prey in the dark
    using their lateral line systems. In both species, detection appeared to occur at distances less than 10 mm,
    as has been reported for other species, e.g. common bream ... and alewife (Janssen et al. 1995)".
  - Discussion: the walleye pollock visual threshold, "approximately 5 x 10^? uE s-1 m-2" (exponent garbled), "is
    ... approximately 2 orders of magnitude below the threshold for juvenile sablefish (5 x 10^-5 uE s-1 m-2)".
  - Discussion: "high prey densities are probably necessary for fish such as juvenile walleye pollock and
    sablefish to forage effectively in darkness."
- **Caveats:**
  - Juveniles, in a tank, on Artemia, which "possess rather feeble predator avoidance".
  - The "<=1 cm" is an observation from video, not a measured distribution.
  - No krill and no adults.

### Q3-2. Montgomery & Macdonald 1987, "Sensory tuning of lateral line receptors in Antarctic fish to the movements of planktonic prey"

- **Reference:** Science 235:195-196. doi:10.1126/science.235.4785.195.
- **Tag:** [A] for Pagothenia borchgrevinki; [S] for gadoids.
- **Quote:** "Simultaneous recordings from a swimming zooplankter held close to the fish and from primary afferent
  neurons provided direct confirmation that swimming movements of crustaceans are a potent natural stimulus of the
  lateral line system."
- **Caveat:** this is frequency tuning with the prey "held close". No behavioural detection distance.

### Q3-3. Kühn, Utne-Palm & de Jong 2022, "Two of the most common crustacean zooplankton Meganyctiphanes norvegica and Calanus spp. produce sounds within the hearing range of their fish predators"

- **Reference:** Bioacoustics 32:73-89. doi:10.1080/09524622.2022.2070542.
- **Tag:** [A].
- **Quotes:**
  - "In krill, the sound co-occurred with a tail flip".
  - "zooplankton sounds could be used as a cue for their predators".
- **Caveat:** whether fish use the cue is not tested. No detection distance.

### Q3-4. Widder, Greene & Youngbluth 1992

- **Reference:** J Plankton Res 14:1607-1624. doi:10.1093/plankt/14.11.1607.
- **Tag:** [A].
- **Quote:** "The two brightest sources of bioluminescence were identified as the euphausiid, Meganyctiphanes
  norvegica and the cydippid ctenophore". The emission was stimulated, not spontaneous: "spontaneous
  bioluminescence was not detected".
- **Caveat:** no fish response is measured.

### Q3-5. Forbes & Stockwell 2026, "Comparing 3-D visual and 2-D tactile encounter rates and their consequences for predation in benthic and pelagic habitats"

- **Reference:** J Great Lakes Res 52(5):102926. doi:10.1016/j.jglr.2026.102926.
- **Tag:** [A]. CC-BY per OpenAlex, but the full text returned 403.
- **Companion:** Forbes, Ballard, O'Malley & Stockwell 2026, JGLR 52:102927, doi:10.1016/j.jglr.2026.102927 [A].
- **Content:** a lateral-line ("tactile") encounter model for slimy sculpin on Mysis at night.
  - The companion measures in situ strike distances and reports that they "did not vary across individual
    sculpins".
  - The abstracts give no numbers.
- **Caveat:** a freshwater benthic sculpin, not a gadoid. These are the nearest candidates for a measured
  non-visual strike distance on a mysid, and they were not read.

### Q3-6. Boscarino 2009, PhD thesis, Cornell

  Mysis relicta, and the alewife, Alosa pseudoharengus, in Lake Ontario". OpenAlex W84467617.
