B_mechanisms_notes.txt - Why wild demersal fish feed below Cmax when prey is plentiful
==========================================================================================

Research agent B, 2026-10-26. Research only: no code, library, manifest, AGENTS.md or resume
was edited. Evidence rows with full citations: tables/B_mechanisms_evidence.csv. Arithmetic:
scripts/B_jensen.py, B_evacuation.py, B_feeding_level.py (stdout in tables/B_*_stdout.txt).
Tags: [F] full text read, [A] abstract, [S] secondary (named), [D] arithmetic here.
J = judgement. Nothing here was chosen to suit a model outcome.

Engine reminder (from the task): per tick, intake = B_pred x eat x hunger x a N/(1 + a h N + w B_pred),
1/h = Cmax per tick. On benthic pools x = a h N ~ 100-500, so intake = Cmax x eat x hunger
until the pool is nearly empty.

0. Headline
-----------
1. Three mechanisms (b, c, f) and the stomach-scale half of (e) leave the saturated plateau
   at Cmax when prey is plentiful. This is computed below, not assumed. They change the
   response near the knee (b, f) or the timing of meals (c, stomach satiation; an ad-lib
   digestion-limited fish still averages Cmax, Jeschke eq. 15). Reserve-scale regulation
   (e, weeks) can lower the plateau; its strength in wild gadoids is unmeasured.
2. Only a constraint on WHEN or HOW LONG fish can feed while prey is exposed (a, with d
   setting the exposure windows) lowers the plateau, and only if the stomach cannot be
   filled within the window. The field signature fits this: Rindorf & Gislason 2005 found
   saturation for every fish prey of whiting, but at 6-17 % of Wmax per prey group, with
   fish eaten "almost exclusively during dawn and dusk". Johnson et al. 2015 found plaice
   and dab stomach fullness flat across a trawling gradient that cut prey biomass. Wild
   fish are thus on a plateau, and that plateau sits below Cmax.
3. Correction to the premise "wild cod/haddock feed at ~0.3 of Cmax" [D]. Against
   literature Cmax, evacuation-based field rations give:
   - cod 0.49-0.82 (central ~0.6);
   - haddock 0.30-0.61 (no haddock Cmax function exists);
   - whiting 0.17-0.71;
   - saithe 0.19-0.54.
   Against the ENGINE benthivore ceiling (~1.6 x cod Cmax at 7 C) cod and haddock sit at
   0.38-0.42. The "0.3" is therefore close to the engine ceiling, not to the literature
   Cmax. Both views say the plateau is well below the ceiling.
4. A ceiling issue found on the way (c, J): an evacuation-limited ceiling scales with prey
   armament. Shelled or ash-rich benthos (crustaceans, brittle stars, bivalves) is
   evacuated more slowly than fish flesh, by about 2x for brown shrimp in whiting (Andersen
   1999 [A]). Cod evacuation stage durations rise with ash and carbonate content
   (Couturier et al. 2013 [A]). On armoured benthos the mass-based ceiling could be about
   half the ceiling on soft prey of the same energy density (factor J).

1. Mechanism (a): foraging time budget
--------------------------------------
Theory.
- Holling with a foraging time share T_f: intake = T_f a N/(1 + a h N), plateau T_f/h.
- The risk version is the activity-mediated growth-mortality trade-off (Werner & Anholt
  1993 [S]; Lima & Dill 1990 [S]). Optimal foraging time falls as predation risk rises or
  as food rises past the need. It gives time-limited plateaus below the digestive maximum.
- The foraging arena version (Walters & Juanes 1993 [S]; Ahrens, Walters & Christensen
  2012 [S]) restricts predator and prey to arenas in space and time:
  V = v B/(v + v' + a P), Q = a V P.
  The EwE chapter text adapted from Ahrens et al.: activity restrictions split each prey
  population into "vulnerable and invulnerable population components", so predation is
  "limited by exchange rates between those components". The refugium note
  ("Refugium och utbyte i bentos"/summary.md) already worked out this algebra and its
  relation to the engine's Beddington-DeAngelis term.

Evidence. Share of 24 h with ingestion, per species and prey:
- Whiting on fish (Rindorf & Gislason 2005 [F]).
  - Ingested "almost exclusively during dawn and dusk".
  - "The ingestion around midday was low" and "Ingestion during darkness was low for all
    species but whiting at location 5".
  - All prey but Norway pout showed two peaks at dawn and sunset.
  - Window length: two twilight windows of about 1-2 h, a share of 0.08-0.17 of the day
    [D, J]. The authors: "data did not allow the duration of the non-feeding periods to be
    estimated".
- Whiting on benthos (Rindorf 2003 [F]).
  - "whiting at 4 of the 5 locations were feeding on bottom dwelling prey at night,
    whereas pelagic and free swimming prey were captured mainly during daylight".
  - She suggests the cause is "the ability of the bottom dwelling prey to avoid the
    predator during daylight", other prey being available by day, or vision being needed
    to capture free-swimming prey.
  - Night in September at 56-58 N is about 11-12 h, so the benthic share is about 0.4-0.5
    of the day (J).
- Whiting, total intake. The feeding peak is at 22-24 h, minimum 08-10 h (Mergardt &
  Temming 1997 [S]). Morning feeding is reported by others (Hall et al. 1995 and
  Pedersen 2000, both cited by Rindorf 2003).
- Cod.
  - Searches actively by day and little at night; the odour response is the same day and
    night (Lokkeborg & Ferno 1999 [A]).
  - North Sea cod ate invertebrates at night and fish by day, with morning and evening
    fullness peaks for herring (Adlerstein & Welleman 2000 [S]).
  - Feeding therefore spreads over much of 24 h; J share 0.5-1.
- Haddock. Bait response rises at dawn and falls at dusk, about 1 h earlier than cod
  (Lokkeborg & Pina 1996, via the earlier note gadoid_diel_feeding.md). Albert 1995 (early
  morning and late afternoon) and Hall et al. 1995 (22-24 h) disagree (both cited by
  Rindorf 2003). J share 0.3-0.6 of the day.
- Whiting and Norway pout on migrating krill. No day/night difference in fullness: the
  predator follows the prey (Onsrud et al. 2004 [S]).
- Plaice, dab, long rough dab. de Groot 1971 and the Gibson tidal-rhythm work were not
  obtained. Daylight visual feeding of plaice is a common statement but unverified here
  (gap).

Magnitude and the key caveat [D] (B_evacuation.py, bout schedule).
- If the fish can fill its stomach in each window, a time window costs little:
  - with n = 1-4 windows a day, daily intake is 0.78-0.98 of continuous feeding (n = 2:
    0.89-0.96);
  - reason: evacuation takes 1-4 days (section c), so the stomach carries the meal
    through the non-feeding hours.
- A window plateau of 0.1-0.2 Wmax therefore needs a second condition: few captures within
  the window.
  - Whiting example: a 250 g whiting caught one 9 g clupeid every 2-4 days; that is 0.27-
    0.55 of Wmax.
  - Captures are discrete and rare. The encounter with VULNERABLE prey inside the window
    is low even when the trawl catch (total prey density) is high.
- This is the foraging arena reading: the plateau is set by exposure windows times the
  vulnerable stock, not by Cmax. The addendum's 2-4 h x Wmax/24 per hour arithmetic
  (0.08-0.17 Wmax) implicitly caps the ingestion rate at Cmax per hour. That cap is the
  engine's logic, not fish physiology (J).

What it changes.
- Neither a nor 1/h. It sets the time share: the engine's eat share x hunger, or the
  vulnerable stock.
- In the engine the time share is a learned action with no cost or constraint other than
  the metabolic action cost and the observation of risk. Nothing makes eat < 1 optimal on
  a benthic pool where predation risk is low (J).
- A 6-h tick cannot resolve a 1-2 h twilight window. dark_ratio is the only existing hook
  and cannot represent twilight peaks (convention 6 in "Attackrater ur motesteori"/
  summary.md).

2. Mechanism (b): prey patchiness below the 1 km cell (scale transition)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Theory: Chesson 1998, 2012 scale transition [S]; Englund & Leonardsson 2008 not obtained.
- Second order:
  E[f(N)] ~ f(Nbar) + 1/2 f''(Nbar) Var(N) + f'(Nbar) Cov(P, N)/Pbar.
- For Holling II, f(N) = a N/(1 + a h N), with x = a h Nbar:
  relative correction = -CV^2 x/(1 + x)^2,
  which goes to -CV^2/x at saturation.
- Occupancy (prey only in a share p of the cell, predator searching at random):
  R = p (1 + x)/(p + x), which goes to p as x grows.
- An aggregative response (Cov > 0) pushes R up.

Magnitude [D] (B_jensen.py, exact integration):
- Saturated benthos pairs, x = 100-500, lognormal CV 0.3-1.0: R = 0.991-0.9998, i.e. less
  than 1 % loss.
- Fish -> fish pairs near the knee, x ~ 1: R = 0.85-0.98.
- Only near-empty sub-areas matter at saturation. Gamma with CV 2-3 at x = 100-500 gives
  R = 0.50-0.82, the same effect as occupancy p.

Sub-km variability data:
- Downing 1979 [F], Taylor's power law over 1462 sets of replicate grabs/cores:
  s^2 = 5.24 M^1.462 (M in ind/m2).
  - Implied CV: 0.66 at 100/m2, 0.36 at 1000/m2.
  - Polychaeta: a 3.24, b 1.561. Pelecypoda: a 7.87, b 1.496.
  - Caveat: mostly freshwater sets, counts rather than biomass.
  - Since b < 2, CV rises as the mean falls, so depletion makes the bed patchier.
- Nephrops: the 2025 Swedish UWTV interim report gives 4 Norwegian Skagerrak stations at
  0.13-0.32 burrows/m2, CV 0.39 [F, D]. These stations lie 5-15 km apart, so the sub-km CV
  is a gap.
- Fish patch use: whiting showed no aggregative response; predator and prey catches were
  "slightly negatively correlated" (Rindorf & Gislason 2005 [F]).
- A fish cruising at 0.4-0.6 BL/s covers 1 km in about 1-2 h, so uniform search over a
  cell within a 6-h tick is plausible (J).

What it changes:
- The response SHAPE near the knee: effectively a lower a at x ~ 1. Nothing at saturation
  unless large parts of the cell are empty of accessible prey.
- Not a, and not the ceiling, for the benthos pairs.
- Relevance to slow benthic pools: low. It grows as a pool is eaten down, because x falls
  and CV rises.

3. Mechanism (c): digestion-limited intake
------------------------------------------
Theory: Jeschke, Kopp & Tollrian 2002 [F], the SSS equation (eq. 13).
- With success rate a, corrected handling time b and corrected digestion time c:
  y(x) = [1 + a x (b + c) - sqrt(1 + a x (2(b + c) + a x (b - c)^2))] / (2 a b c).
- Asymptote 1/max(b, c) (eq. 15). The hunger level is h = 1 - c y (eq. 11), and the search
  probability equals h.
- Check of the premise that SSS saturates more sharply than Holling: it does not. "For b = 0
  (i.e., zero handling time), the SSS equation simplifies to the disc equation but with c
  instead of b". "if one of these two factors is negligibly small, the curve becomes
  virtually identical to that of the disc equation". The two differ only when b ~ c.
- For fish, b/c ~ 1/400: behavioural capture and handling take 15-30 s (Ellis & Gibson
  1997; Persson et al. 2012, via benthivores_encounter.md). The engine's Holling form with
  h = 1/Cmax is therefore exactly the SSS digestion-limited limit. "The vast majority of
  predators seems to be digestion limited".
- Jeschke also notes that predators spend much time resting, "often ... motivated by
  satiation".

Evacuation vs the 6-h tick [D] (B_evacuation.py; WGSAM 2024 Table 3-7 square-root cylinder
model, 7.5 C).
- Model: dS/dt = -R' S^0.5, t_half = 2(sqrt(S0) - sqrt(S0/2))/R', t_empty = 2 sqrt(S0)/R'.
- 2 %BW meal, fish or soft prey:
  - cod 40 cm: t_half 14 h, t_empty 48 h;
  - haddock 40 cm: 16.5 / 56 h;
  - whiting 30 cm: 15 / 51 h;
  - soft benthos (E 3.5): 12-14 / 41-48 h.
- Armoured benthos (armament factor 0.5, J): t_half 24-28 h, t_empty 82-96 h.
- 5 %BW meal: t_half 19-26 h soft, 38-45 h armoured.
- Independent check: dab satiation meals have a gastric emptying time of 49 h at 7.5 C and
  30 h at 10.5 C by a linear fit (Bagge et al. 1995 [F]). Other fits in the same paper give
  25-36 h at 7.5 C.
- So a full stomach is NOT cleared within 6 h. Half a meal takes 2-4 ticks and the whole
  meal 7-16 ticks.

Max meal vs daily Cmax:
- Cod single-day satiation intake is 5.7-7.9 %BW against 2.2-3.1 sustained (lab, 6.3-6.8
  C; earlier note).
- Whiting on demand ate ~3.4 %BW/d in about one meal per 21 h (Seyhan et al. 1998 [A]).
- A fish can therefore eat one to three days of Cmax within a 2-4 h window. Meals are
  bouts, separated by 1-2 days of digestion.
- To sustain FB4 Cmax, a 40 cm cod needs a mean fill of 2.2 %BW [D].

What it changes:
- The evacuation-limited 1/h is already the engine's ceiling, and the shape is unchanged.
- Two refinements:
  (i) the ceiling on armoured benthos should be lower in mass than on soft prey (armament;
      [A] + J factor);
  (ii) intake is bout-structured over days. The per-tick ceiling 1/h forbids eating 1-3
      days of ration in one tick, but the daily mean is the same.
- Neither refinement keeps fish below Cmax on a plentiful pool: an ad-lib fish under
  digestion limitation feeds at Cmax by definition (Jeschke eq. 15).

4. Mechanism (d): prey behaviour (vigilance, escape, refuge)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Already reviewed in "Refugium och utbyte i bentos" (reused, not redone):
- Nephrops emerge 1-2 times a day, exposed share 0.07 (0.03-0.35), burrows re-dug in 1-2 d.
- Northern shrimp spend 0.75 of the day on the bottom.
- Infauna accessible share 0.2 (0.08-0.45); Amphiura arm tips regrow over ~146 d.
- Fish eat 1.3-6.5 % of infauna production (Dauvin 1988, via that note).

New here:
- Juvenile plaice siphon nipping makes Macoma lose siphon mass and bury shallower; recovery
  speed "varied greatly" (de Goeij et al. 2001 [A]).
- In whiting on shrimp, a fuller gut lowers strike success and lengthens handling
  (Staniland thesis [A]).
- Rindorf 2003 [F]: Crangon "spends part of the daylight hours buried"; Pandalus spend the
  day on or near the bottom and ascend at night.

What it changes:
- It sets the exposure windows and the vulnerable stock, i.e. the accessible fraction and
  the window in (a).
- With the accessible fractions already in the engine, x stays at 100-500, because the
  accessible stock is still large relative to 1/(a h).
- Prey behaviour lowers the plateau only through window-limited capture: per-window
  exposure, not a standing accessible stock.

5. Mechanism (e): satiation and state-dependent feeding
-------------------------------------------------------
Engine and SSS gates side by side:
- engine: hunger = min(1, max(0, (1 - s)/(1 - u))), s = reserve fill, u = 0.5;
- SSS: h = 1 - c y, the stomach (empty gut volume), time scale hours.

Evidence favours stomach regulation on the hour-day scale:
- Whiting resume feeding at about 40 % stomach fullness, with a ~21 h rhythm; appetite
  return "follows the gastric emptying curve closely" (Seyhan et al. 1998 [A]).
- Dab feeding intervals match gastric emptying time (except at 6.5 C); "stomach fullness is
  a major factor controlling appetite" (Gwyther & Grove 1981 [A]).
- Dab intake depends on the previous day's meal: "a high food intake is followed by a day
  with a lower one" (Bagge et al. 1995 [F]).
- North Sea whiting: partly digested food lowers the chance of fresh food, while meal size
  is unchanged (Rindorf 2002 [S]).

Reserve (lipostat) regulation acts over weeks:
- hyperphagia and compensatory growth after restriction, refilling reserves (Jobling &
  Johansen 1999 [A]; Ali et al. 2003 [S]);
- cod: Jobling et al. 1994, title only.

Wild feeding level:
- Ursin et al. 1985 [F] give growth-derived satiation levels f = 0.44-0.97 by size class
  (North Sea sets 0.44-0.89). Their Hm is a growth-model parameter, not FB4 Cmax, so this
  is NOT the same quantity as ration/Cmax.
- Wild North Sea cod stomach fill (Ursin Table 1) is 1.2-1.9 %BW. Against the fill that
  sustains FB4 Cmax this implies 0.73-1.05 [D], biased upward (pooled mean fill; large cod
  on large prey).
- Evacuation-based rations give 0.49-0.82 for cod [D].
- No source was found that relates wild gadoid intake to liver index or reserve.

What it changes:
- A stomach gate makes intake bout-like but, with food always available, still averages 1/c
  = Cmax (Jeschke eq. 15). It cannot by itself give sub-Cmax plateaus.
- A reserve gate (the engine's) lowers intake only when the reserve is above u. Evidence
  for slow reserve control exists (compensatory hyperphagia), but its strength in wild
  gadoids is unmeasured (gap).
- Neither changes a. The stomach gate changes the time structure, the reserve gate the
  plateau (hunger < 1).

6. Mechanism (f): interference
------------------------------
Arithmetic [D] (B_feeding_level.py).
- Benthivore w = 0.45 km2/t at B_pred 1.53 t/km2 gives wB = 0.69.
- Against 1 + x = 101-501 the intake ratio is 0.993-0.9986; at B = 6 t/km2 it is still
  0.974-0.995.
- This is the "saturation dilutes interference" argument of the interference note with
  current numbers.

Field evidence:
- Plaice and dab along an Irish Sea trawl gradient (Johnson et al. 2015 [F]).
  - "As trawling frequency increased, both fish and prey biomass declined, such that the
    ratio of fish to prey remained unchanged".
  - "even at frequently trawled sites with low prey biomass, both plaice and dab
    maintained constant levels of stomach fullness and gut energy contents".
  - Plaice shifted toward energy-poor prey.
- Kattegat: plaice and long rough dab condition followed the prey/consumer ratio, rising
  then falling above about 5 passes/yr; dab showed no effect (Hiddink et al. 2016 [A],
  via the earlier addendum).
- Cod density had no effect on capelin consumption at 2 km (Fall et al. 2021 [A]).
- No gadoid or flatfish BD parameter exists.

What it changes:
- Shape near the knee only. Invisible on saturated pairs, so it cannot keep fish below Cmax
  on plentiful benthos.

7. Food limitation at km to 100 km
----------------------------------
- Barents Sea cod (MULTSPEC, Bogstad et al. 1997): field half-saturation 1.57 t/km2 of
  suitable food, fitted on ~10^2 km areas, 6-7x the encounter value at 1 km (earlier note).
- Cod is "gut-limited, not encounter-limited" (Fall & Fiksen 2020 [S]).
- Eastern Baltic cod (Neuenfeldt et al. 2020 [A]): feeding level fell to levels consistent
  with severe growth limitation in small cod. Linked to fewer benthic prey (hypoxia) and few
  pelagic prey, i.e. food limitation at stock scale when benthic prey is lost.
- Johnson 2015: flatfish fullness was flat over a several-fold prey biomass range; prey/fish
  ratio constant.
- Reading (J): wild benthivores are saturated at site and km scale (the plateau does not
  follow prey density) AND feed at 0.4-0.8 of Cmax. This is the signature of a time or
  exposure limit (a + d), not of encounter (b), digestion (c) or interference (f).
  Food limitation appears when benthic prey is lost at large scale (Baltic) or the
  prey/consumer ratio falls (Kattegat).
- Mehl 1989 and Engelhard 2008 were not re-read here; see the earlier notes.

8. Ranking (J)
--------------
By strength of field evidence that the mechanism operates in North Sea demersal fish:
  (a) time budget / exposure windows   - direct diel stomach series (Rindorf 2003, 2005)
  (c) digestion limitation             - strong: evacuation experiments, SSS; sets the ceiling
  (e) stomach-scale satiation          - strong in the lab, indirect in the field
  (f) interference                     - weak and mostly null at fine scale
  (b) sub-cell patchiness              - variance data exist; no fish-specific test
  (d) prey behaviour                   - strong qualitatively; little quantified as fish intake

By relevance to fish on slow benthic pools (what can hold a saturated pair below Cmax):
  1. (a) + (d) together: exposure windows and window-limited capture. This is the only
     combination that lowers the plateau while prey is plentiful.
  2. (c) armament: a lower mass ceiling on shelled benthos (moves 1/h by a factor ~0.5, J).
  3. (e) reserve gate: lowers the plateau only at a high reserve; field strength unknown.
  4. (b), (f): no effect at x = 100-500; they matter only after the pool is eaten down.

9. What the engine lacks (literature-grounded, no fix proposed)
---------------------------------------------------------------
(i) The time share (eat x hunger) is a free learned action. It has no time constraint and
    no exposure window, so it can be 1 every tick. In wild fish the plateau below Cmax
    lives in the time share and the vulnerable stock, not in a or 1/h.
(ii) The per-tick ceiling 1/h acts as an hourly ingestion cap. In real fish, digestion caps
    the daily mean, and meals are bouts of 1-3 days' ration followed by 1-4 days of
    evacuation. A window restriction therefore costs real fish little unless captures in
    the window are rare. A time-share cap in the engine bites one-for-one on intake, which
    does not match the biology (J).
(iii) The 6-h tick cannot represent 1-2 h twilight windows, and dark_ratio cannot
    represent dawn and dusk peaks.
(iv) The evacuation-limited ceiling has no prey armament term (shelled benthos vs fish
    flesh).

10. Gaps
--------
- Hall et al. 1995 (J Anim Ecol 64:39), Albert 1995 (ICES JMS 52:873), de Groot 1971 and
  Gibson's tidal feeding work: not obtained, so there is no measured feeding-hour share
  for haddock, plaice or dab.
- Englund & Leonardsson 2008, Werner & Anholt 1993, Walters & Juanes 1993, Ahrens et al.
  2012: not obtained (theory cited [S]).
- Sub-km CV of Nephrops burrows and of North Sea macrobenthos biomass (only Downing's
  mostly freshwater counts and between-station UWTV).
- Armament factor for cod and haddock on brittle stars, bivalves and polychaetes
  (Couturier et al. 2013 numbers not read).
- Wild gadoid intake vs liver index or condition; Neuenfeldt 2020 and Hiddink 2016 full
  texts (publisher and repository bot checks).
- Window-limited capture rates of gadoids on emerged Nephrops or exposed infauna.
