FIELD FEEDING LEVEL OF NORTH SEA / SKAGERRAK DEMERSAL FISH (agent A)
=====================================================================

Question: how far below Cmax (at ambient temperature and size) do wild cod, haddock, whiting,
saithe and the main flatfish feed? Feeding level f = field consumption / Cmax.

Files
- tables/A_field_feeding_level.csv   one row per number (species, season, size, basis, source, tag)
- tables/A_ration_over_cmax_stdout.txt  output of scripts/A_ration_over_cmax.py (SMS and Daan rations
  against FB4, Jobling and the whiting lab ration)
- tables/A_ices_stomach_summary.csv, A_ices_stomach_stdout.txt  scripts/A_ices_stomach_summary.py
  (ICES online stomach database: empty share, content %BW, whiting content/Smax)
- tables/A_crr219_empty_stdout.txt  scripts/A_crr219_empty.py (ICES CRR 219 Table 7, 1991)
- data/A_*/README.txt  where each download came from

Tags: [F] full text read, [A] abstract only, [S] secondary, [D] my arithmetic. J = judgement.

Units discipline. The project's Cmax (params files) is energy, kJ/kg/d, with 1 g prey = 4.184 kJ in the
FB4 convention. Field rations are wet weight of prey as eaten. A wet/wet ratio and an energy/energy
ratio differ by (diet energy density / 4.184): fish-heavy diets (5-6 kJ/g) raise the energy ratio,
benthos-heavy diets (3-3.5 kJ/g) lower it. The `method` column of the CSV says which basis each
ratio uses and whether the maximum is a lab satiation ration at the same size and temperature.

The project's own ratios (benthivores 0.30, predators 0.49) are against the engine's constructed Cmax
and are NOT literature values; they are not in the CSV.


1. SOURCES, ONE BY ONE
----------------------

1.1 Rindorf & Gislason 2005, J Exp Mar Biol Ecol 324:1-19, doi:10.1016/j.jembe.2005.04.013 [F]
    (postprint already in "Attackrater ur motesteori"/data/gap_validation/rindorf2005; not re-fetched)
- Measured: 48-72 h round-the-clock trawl series at 5 North Sea stations, early September (1988 and
  later), whiting 20-24.9, 25-29.9, 30-34.9 cm; intake per prey by evacuation (Andersen 1998, total
  stomach content evacuation rate), 'very fresh' prey excluded (possible trawl feeding).
- Wmax definition (appendix): the lab ad-libitum sandeel ration of Andersen & Riis-Vestergaard 2003,
  Wmax,sandeel = 0.00122 L^2.28 e^(0.076T) g/d, is converted to a maximum sustained stomach content
  Smax = 0.0352 L^1.68 e^(0.004T) g; then Wmax = 24 rho_tot Smax with the evacuation rate of the
  actual mixed field diet. So Wmax IS a lab satiation maximum at the same size and temperature,
  re-expressed for the field diet (wet/wet). Text: maximum "4.3 to 6.2% body weight" at the warmest
  station, "around 3.1%" at the coldest; overall 2.7-6.1 %BW/d, mean 3.3.
- Total intake / Wmax (read from Fig. 3F; the plot is [F], my reading of points is approximate):
  8.2 C 0.09-0.11; 8.6 C 0.16-0.19; 10.6 C 0.24-0.31; 13.7 C 0.19-0.22; 16.7 C 0.24-0.35.
  The two cold deep stations (73 and 137 m, 8.2-8.6 C) are the Skagerrak-like case: 0.10-0.18.
- Split: "other food" (crustaceans etc.) "between 8 to 22%, 10 to 20% and 8 to 17% of maximum daily
  ration" for the three sizes, rising with temperature; fish prey 0-0.22, varying 17-36 fold between
  stations, uncorrelated with other food. Single-prey saturation plateaus (Table 2): clupeids K =
  0.10-0.17 of Wmax, gadoids 0.06-0.12. Abstract: "none of the levels corresponded to the maximum
  digestive capacity of the predator".
- Diel: fish prey eaten in two peaks, around dawn and dusk; low at midday and in darkness (except at
  the 137 m station, where the afternoon peak extended). Rindorf 2003 (MEPS 249:265) adds that
  bottom-dwelling prey are eaten mainly at night, free-swimming prey by day.
- Caveats: one season (September), 3 size groups, 5 stations; prey patchiness may make apparent
  saturation lower than the physiological maximum (authors' own discussion).

1.2 ICES (1997) CRR 219, Database report of the stomach sampling project 1991,
    doi:10.17895/ices.pub.4626 [F] (figshare PDF)
- Table 7A-E: % empty stomachs per predator size class x roundfish area x quarter. The text layer
  loses the column alignment, so per quarter I take the all-size total (last value of the Total row)
  and the range over size classes (scripts/A_crr219_empty.py). The column position of that total
  is inferred (it is the extra last value in each Total row and lies inside the size-class range),
  not read from an aligned header; the script prints the header line of each table it parsed.
- All-size empty share, Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4/year:
    cod      0.161 / 0.095 / 0.094 / 0.082 / 0.105
    haddock  0.184 / 0.058 / 0.084 / 0.097 / 0.100
    whiting  0.453 / 0.120 / 0.125 / 0.079 / 0.163   (large whiting Q1 0.53-0.57)
    saithe   0.268 / 0.024 / 0.147 / 0.105 / 0.120
    mackerel 0.48 in Q1
  Skagerrak (roundfish area 8), Q4 only: cod 0.084, haddock 0.195, whiting 0.183.
- Report text: Q1 had the highest empty share for all five predators, and Q1 1991 was higher than
  1981 ("small in the case of cod, but large for haddock, whiting, mackerel and saithe"). Caveat in
  the report itself: ships applied different criteria for empty vs regurgitated ("This is indeed
  disturbing").
- No total-content table (Table 10 covers commercial prey only), so no fullness from this report.

1.3 ICES online stomach database (stomachdata.ices.dk), full download 2026-10-26 [D]
- Only a subset of the Year of the Stomach data is online. North Sea + Skagerrak-Kattegat records,
  empty = StomachEmpty / Number; mean content = sum of prey weights / non-regurgitated stomachs
  (empties included); body weight from length-weight fitted to the database's own single-fish
  records (cod 0.00494 L^3.172, whiting 0.00542 L^3.105); groups with unweighed prey are blanked.
- Cod 1981-2023: empty 0.15 (Q1, n 1212), 0.18 (Q3), 0.12 (Q4, 1981); Skagerrak-Kattegat 0.10
  (n 258). Mean content 0.7-1.7 %BW (Q1 peak 1.5-1.7 at 40-69 cm). Daan's 1968-71 relation gives
  1.7-1.9 %BW.
- Whiting: empty 0.30 (Q1), 0.23 (Q2), 0.33 (Q3), 0.24 (Q4); Skagerrak Q1 0.46. Content/Smax (Smax
  from 1.1, the lab satiation stomach content) 0.09-0.20 for < 30 cm, up to 0.39 for 30-39 cm in
  Q3; content 0.9-1.9 %BW. With first-order evacuation (Rindorf's own assumption, Wmax = 24 rho Smax)
  intake/Wmax = S/Smax, so these are feeding levels of 0.1-0.2 (0.4 for the largest in Q3).
  Caveat: survey hauls are mostly daytime; whiting feed at dawn and dusk, so midday stomachs are
  below the daily mean S (bias low), but S/Smax averaged over the day is what matters.
- Plaice 2024 (one survey programme): Q1 0.987 empty (n 547; content 0.00-0.02 %BW); Q3 0.645 empty
  (content 0.09-0.13 %BW). The winter fast is unambiguous. Skagerrak-Kattegat Q1 0.86 (n 14).
- Haddock: Q1 2025 0.62 empty (n 265, prey unweighed), Q3 1982 0.17 (n 76; content 0.3 %BW).
- Saithe 1980-91 (+2026): empty Q1 0.28, Q2-Q4 0.04-0.08, but 17-37 % recorded regurgitated.
- Long rough dab 1991: empty 0.27 (Q1), 0.055 (Q2), 0.14 (Q3); 45-54 % regurgitated (pooled).
- Bad length entries (mm in a cm field, 2021-23 uploads; 139 records) dropped.

1.4 SMS key run 2023 rations (ICES WGSAM 2024, ICES Sci Rep 6:13, Tables 3-7 and 3-8) against lab Cmax [D]
- Rations ration = a_q W^b (kg/quarter, W kg), from Year of the Stomach data by the Andersen & Beyer
  cylinder evacuation model with prey energy density and armament. Wet weight as eaten.
- Cod vs FB4 cod Cmax (Hansson et al. 1996; 0.099 W^-0.195 f(T), Kitchell CQ 2.41 CTO 13.7 CTM 21;
  check 1000 g 7 C = 78.0 kJ/kg/d, as the project table). Wet/wet at 4.184 kJ/g, quarterly mean of
  the manifest's at_50m series (7.2, 6.9, 8.9, 10.5 C):
    1.19 kg: Q1 0.52, Q2 0.72, Q3 0.64, Q4 0.64; annual 0.63 (300 g 0.65; 5 kg 0.61).
  A +1.5 C shift lowers each by ~0.03-0.08.
- Cod vs Jobling (1988) Cmax (as eq. 2 of Neuenfeldt et al. 2020: 0.223 e^(0.104T - 0.000112T^3)
  W^0.802, read as kJ/d): Jobling is 1.42-1.51 x FB4 at 5-10 C and all sizes. Energy/energy at
  diet 4.184 or 5.0 kJ/g: annual 0.42-0.44 / 0.50-0.52; Q1 0.35-0.44, Q2 0.48-0.60, Q3-Q4 0.42-0.53.
- Haddock vs the cod function (no adult haddock Cmax exists): annual 0.27-0.31 (x1.0), 0.21-0.24
  (x1.3, the project's judgement); Q1 0.15-0.22, Q2 0.21-0.31, Q3 0.25-0.36, Q4 0.22-0.33. The SMS
  haddock ration per kg is 0.6 x the cod ration at equal size.
- Whiting vs the Andersen & Riis-Vestergaard sandeel satiation ration (lab, same size and T; energy/
  energy with whiting diet 4.5 kJ/g, range 4.0-5.5): annual 0.27-0.30 (range 0.21-0.36); Q1 0.23-0.26,
  Q2 0.26-0.28, Q3 0.33-0.36, Q4 0.24-0.27. Independent of 1.1 (different data, same ceiling) and in
  the same band (Rindorf Q3 0.10-0.35).
- Saithe: rations Q1 0.44, Q2 1.27, Q3 0.67, Q4 0.79 %BW/d at 1.5 kg; no saithe Cmax exists. Not
  converted to a feeding level (the whiting lab formula would be extrapolated far outside its sizes).
- Caveats. (a) The quarterly SHAPE depends on the temperature series used for Cmax: at_50m is coldest
  in Q2, so cod Q2 comes out highest; in the North Sea proper Q2 is warmer than Q1 and SMS evaluated
  each stratum at its own ambient temperature (not given in the report). A uniform +1.5 C does not fix
  the phase. Robust: the annual level, and Q1 lowest. (b) Evacuation-model choice moves rations by up
  to a factor 2 (Hansson et al. 1996; Bogstad & Mehl 1990), so every ration-based ratio carries that.
  (c) SMS rations are per survivor and include the Q1 empties and spawning.

1.5 Daan 1973, ICES CM 1973(?)/F:38 (preliminary version of Neth J Sea Res 6:479) [F]
- 1968-71, 5705 southern + 1725 northern North Sea cod stomachs. Stomach content w = 0.00016 L^3 g
  (no significant seasonal or regional difference in the feeding coefficient), daily ration R =
  (b/2) L^2 with b 0.06: Table II 0.53 g at 10 cm, 8.5 g at 40, 19 at 60, 34 at 80, 53 g at 100 cm.
- [D] With W = 0.006838 L^3.07: ration 3.1 %BW/d at 20 cm, 1.5 at 40, 0.97 at 60, 0.71 at 80 cm;
  stomach content 1.7-1.9 %BW.
- Against FB4 (wet/wet, 7-10 C): 20 cm 0.80-0.99, 40 cm 0.59-0.72, 60 cm 0.48-0.59, 80 cm 0.42-0.52,
  10 cm 1.1-1.4. Against Jobling (energy 4.184-5.0 kJ/g, 7-10 C): 20 cm 0.53-0.81, 40 cm 0.39-0.59,
  60 cm 0.32-0.49, 80 cm 0.28-0.43. The >1 at 10 cm against FB4 says the FB4 function is too low for
  small cod, or Daan's evacuation model too fast for them; Jobling stays < 1 at all sizes but 10 cm.
- Pattern robust to the basis: feeding level falls with size (ration ~ L^2, Cmax ~ W^0.8 ~ L^2.45).

1.6 Hall 1987, J Fish Biol 31:479-491, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1987.tb05253.x [A] (abstract via marineinfo)
- Lab, food continuously available: haddock ate ~75 % of food by day, dusk peak and inferred dawn peak;
  dab mainly at night, ~40 % less than haddock. "Comparison of haddock maximum daily ration with
  published estimates of consumption in the wild indicate that wild haddock feed at between 15 and 22%
  of their maximum capacity." Lab temperature, fish size and which field estimates were used cannot be
  checked from the abstract; so it is unknown whether the maximum matches field T and size.

1.7 Ajiad, Smedstad & Korsbrekke 1994, ICES CM 1994/O:7 (Barents Sea cod) [F] - out-of-area anchor
- 33 000 stomachs 1984-91; "Cmax" = 68.84 W^0.934 cal/d is a line drawn as the upper envelope of
  field consumption vs weight, NOT a lab satiation ration (Jobling's lab range 7-11 C, 100-700 g was
  judged unsuitable for Barents cod). Actual/maximum by age and quarter, 1990: Q1 0.24-0.38, Q2
  0.11-0.19, Q3 0.18-0.24, Q4 0.05-0.35 (small n). "Cod consumed from 16% to 37% of the maximum
  rations"; maintenance needs 0.22-0.23 of this Cmax. Because the denominator is a field envelope,
  these ratios understate nothing physiological; they say intake varies ~3-fold across quarters
  with a spring (Q2, spawning/migration) low.

1.8 Neuenfeldt et al. 2020, ICES J Mar Sci 77:624-632, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsz224 [F, HTML text]
- Eastern Baltic cod 1963-2014, ~109 000 stomachs 20-80 cm, cylinder evacuation model (rho0 2.43e-3,
  T fixed at 5 C), Cmax = Jobling 1988 (above), f = C/Cmax in energy. Numeric f only in Fig. 4, which
  I could not read (the OA PDF sits behind a Cloudflare check). Text: 20-30 cm cod in 2005-2014
  "among the lowest on record", "at or even below minimum fish maintenance levels"; larger cod rising
  with length. "Previous studies have shown that 15-40% of cod stomachs are empty." Use only as
  qualitative support that small cod can sit near maintenance (f ~0.2) in a depleted system.

1.9 Armstrong & Schindler 2011, Nature 476:84-87, doi:10.1038/nature10240 [A, PubMed abstract]
- 639 estimates, 38 fish species: "piscine predators typically maintain the physiological capacity to
  feed at daily rates 2-3 times higher than what they experience on average" -> mean load:capacity
  0.33-0.5 [D]. Species list and the gadoid entries not seen.

1.10 Hartvig, Andersen & Beyer 2011, J Theor Biol 272:113-122, doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2010.12.006 [F, arXiv]
- Not a measurement. From the von Bertalanffy growth constant (~10 g^0.25/yr, Andersen et al. 2008)
  and lab scalings of Cmax and metabolism they infer "the expected average feeding level of
  individuals in the field ... f ~ 0.4, i.e. around twice the critical feeding level" fc ~ 0.2 (where
  all assimilated food goes to metabolism). f0 = 0.6 (at resource carrying capacity) gave realised
  ~0.4 in their model; feeding level falls with body size.

1.11 Hansson et al. 1996, ICES J Mar Sci 53:107-114, doi:10.1006/jmsc.1996.0010 [A]
- Same North Sea cod stock, 1974-89: standard ICES evacuation model 840 kt/yr, two other evacuation
  models 1020 and 1640 kt, Wisconsin bioenergetics 1420 kt. The ICES model is 0.59 of the
  bioenergetics estimate; the differences come from young cod. Whether their P (proportion of Cmax)
  was fitted to growth or fixed is not in the abstract; I do not infer it. The FB4 cod Cmax used in
  1.4-1.5 comes from this paper.

1.12 Basimi & Grove 1985, J Fish Biol 27:505-520, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1985.tb03198.x [A]
- Inshore plaice, eastern Anglesey (Irish Sea). Seasonal daily rations 5-10 %BW (15-20 cm), 3-12 %
  (20-25 cm), 2.8-4.4 % (30-35 cm); "Plaice ate little food in winter"; bouts of heavy feeding in
  which new prey are moved into the anterior intestine "without full gastric digestion".
- [D] Against Fonds et al. 1985 lab maximum (excess mussel meat; Table 8 via the benthivore evidence
  file) at 14 C and 350 g, 166 kJ/kg/d = 4.0 %BW/d at 4.184 kJ/g: 30-35 cm plaice 0.7-1.1 wet/wet.
  J: the prey (Abra alba, Pectinaria koreni) is probably ~3 kJ/g wet, giving ~0.5-0.8 in energy;
  the field temperature (~14 C) is my assumption for the feeding season.
- Contrast: offshore North Sea Q3 2024 survey stomachs are 64 % empty with 0.1 %BW content (1.3).
  The two methods disagree systematically for a bout feeder that clears its stomach fast: a daytime
  snapshot of a bout feeder sees mostly empty stomachs, while the evacuation ration integrates the
  bouts. Present both; do not average them.

1.13 Others (small)
- Hislop et al. 1991, ICES J Mar Sci 48:139 [A]: of the 1981 whiting stomachs "almost 50% were either
  empty or were judged to have lost all or part of their contents through regurgitation".
- Bogstad & Mehl 1990 (ICES CM, IMR Brage) [S, search summary]: six evacuation models on 1984 Barents
  cod stomachs gave 0.5-1.2 %BW/d (factor 2.4 by model choice).
- Knust 1996 (dab, German Bight / Dogger Bank) [S]: feeding peaks in summer, drops at spring spawning;
  little seasonality on Dogger Bank. No ration found.
- Searched without a usable number: Essington et al. 2001 (CJFAS 58:2129 is about von Bertalanffy-based
  consumption, not P), Kiorboe & Hirst 2014 (maximum rates only), Bjornsson et al. 2001 (abstract has
  growth, not intake), saithe rations or Cmax (none found beyond SMS), dab/witch/LRD field rations
  (none found), Funk et al. 2023 and Maioli 2019 (no feeding level).


2. SYNTHESIS PER SPECIES
------------------------
Every ratio below names its Cmax basis. Seasons: Q1 Jan-Mar, Q2 Apr-Jun, Q3 Jul-Sep, Q4 Oct-Dec.

WHITING - best attested (lab ceiling matched in size and temperature, two independent routes)
- Rindorf & Gislason 2005, field 48-h series, September: 0.10-0.35, ~0.14 at 8 C (Skagerrak-like).
- SMS rations vs the same lab ceiling: annual 0.27-0.30 (0.21-0.36); Q3 highest (0.33-0.36).
- Stomach content / lab satiation content (ICES database): 0.1-0.2 (up to 0.4, largest in Q3).
- Empty: 0.45 in Q1 1991 (large whiting 0.53-0.57), 0.08-0.13 in Q2-Q4; Skagerrak Q4 0.18.
- J: central 0.25 (range 0.10-0.35); Q1 lowest (high empty share; ~0.15-0.25), Q3 highest
  (~0.3-0.35). At 8 C bottom water the field value sits at the low end (0.10-0.2). Quality: good.

COD - basis-dependent by a factor ~1.45
- SMS rations: 0.63 annual against FB4 (Q1 0.52, Q2-Q4 0.64-0.72 with the at_50m series);
  0.42-0.52 against Jobling 1988. Daan 1973 at 40 cm: 0.59-0.72 (FB4) or 0.39-0.59 (Jobling).
- Size: falls with size on both bases (Daan: 20 cm 0.5-1.0, 80 cm 0.3-0.5).
- Empty: 0.16 in Q1, 0.08-0.10 in Q2-Q4 (1991); 0.10-0.15 in the online database; Skagerrak 0.08-0.10.
  Mean stomach content 0.7-1.9 %BW.
- Out-of-area: Barents 0.16-0.37 of a field envelope (Q2 lowest); Baltic small cod near maintenance
  (f ~0.2) in 2005-14.
- J: central 0.5-0.55. Annual SMS level on the two bases: 0.63 (FB4) / 0.43 (Jobling) wet/wet at
  4.184 kJ/g; 0.75 (FB4) / 0.52 (Jobling) in energy at a 5.0 kJ/g diet. Range 0.43-0.75 over basis x
  diet energy (the Q2 FB4 0.72 is a temperature-series artefact and is not used to set the range). Q1 lower (0.35-0.52
  across the two bases), and small cod higher than large. Quality: moderate (rations solid, the ceiling is
  the uncertainty; the FB4 cod function looks low for small cod).

HADDOCK - no adult lab Cmax; all ratios lean on a cod analogue
- SMS rations vs the cod function: annual 0.27-0.31 (x1.0) or 0.21-0.24 (x1.3); Q1 0.15-0.22,
  Q3 0.25-0.36. Hall 1987: 0.15-0.22 of a lab maximum (T and size unchecked).
- Empty: Q1 0.18, Q2-Q4 0.06-0.10 (1991); Skagerrak Q4 0.20.
- J: central 0.22 (range 0.15-0.31); Q1 ~0.15-0.2, Q3 ~0.25-0.35. Quality: low-moderate. The SMS
  haddock ration per kg is 0.6 of the cod ration at equal size, so with any Cmax at or above the
  cod function haddock feed further below their ceiling than cod.

SAITHE - rations, no ceiling
- SMS rations 0.44 (Q1), 1.27 (Q2), 0.67 (Q3), 0.79 (Q4) %BW/d at 1.5 kg; Q2 is 2.9 x Q1.
- Empty: Q1 0.27, Q2 0.02, Q3 0.15, Q4 0.11 (1991); 17-37 % of stomachs regurgitated in the online
  data (midwater fish hauled from depth), so "empty" is uncertain.
- J: no feeding level can be stated from the literature. If saithe Cmax per kg is like whiting's or
  cod's at 1-2 kg (~1.5-2.2 %BW/d at 7-10 C on the FB4 basis), Q2 would be ~0.6-0.8 and Q1 ~0.2-0.3;
  that is an analogy, not evidence. Quality: gap.

PLAICE - strongly seasonal
- Winter (Q1): 0.99 empty, content ~0 (ICES 2024, n 547); "ate little food in winter" (Basimi &
  Grove). Feeding level ~0 in Dec-Mar.
- Feeding season: inshore evacuation rations 2.8-4.4 %BW/d at 30-35 cm = 0.7-1.1 of the Fonds lab
  maximum wet/wet (~0.5-0.8 in energy, J); offshore daytime survey stomachs 64 % empty.
- J: summer 0.5-0.9 inshore (bout feeding), lower offshore (unknown); winter ~0. An annual mean
  would be roughly 0.3-0.5 (J, not measured). Quality: low-moderate; no North Sea offshore ration.

DAB, LONG ROUGH DAB, WITCH - gaps
- Dab: lab intake ~0.6 x haddock (Hall 1987); seasonal feeding with a spring (spawning) drop in the
  German Bight (Knust 1996). No field ration found.
- Long rough dab: empty 0.27 (Q1), 0.06 (Q2), 0.14 (Q3) in 1991 (with ~50 % regurgitated). No ration.
- Witch: nothing found.
- J: treat as plaice-like seasonal feeders with unknown level.

CROSS-SPECIES ANCHORS
- Armstrong & Schindler 2011 (38 species): mean intake 0.33-0.5 of capacity.
- Hartvig et al. 2011: expected field f ~0.4, maintenance fc ~0.2 (growth-based inference).
- The North Sea gadoid numbers above sit inside this band: whiting and haddock at its low end
  (0.2-0.3), cod in the middle to upper part (0.45-0.65 by basis).

EVIDENCE QUALITY, SHORT
- Strong: Rindorf & Gislason 2005 (whiting); CRR 219 empty shares; SMS rations themselves.
- Moderate: the [D] SMS and Daan ratios (rations solid; ceilings and temperatures are mine to match).
- Weak or missing: haddock Cmax, saithe Cmax, all flatfish field rations in the North Sea offshore,
  Neuenfeldt's numeric f (figure not read).


3. GAPS
-------
- No adult haddock or saithe lab Cmax; no flatfish field ration from the offshore North Sea/Skagerrak.
- Cod Cmax basis: FB4 (Hansson et al. 1996) vs Jobling (1988) differ by 1.45x; Bjornsson et al. 2001
  (wild cod 447-2213 g fed capelin and shrimp to satiation) would arbitrate but the intake data are
  behind a paywall.
- Neuenfeldt et al. 2020 Fig. 4 (Baltic cod f by size and decade) not read.
- SMS rations were not available by stratum temperature, so the quarterly ratios use the Skagerrak
  50 m series; the annual level is robust, the quarter shape less so.
- Online ICES stomach data are a subset; fullness (content %BW) by quarter from the full 1981/1991
  projects would need the original data, not the reports.
