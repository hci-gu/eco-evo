# Does bottom trawling lower gadoid juvenile survival or recruitment through habitat change? (Q1)

Labels:
- **[Primary]**: peer-reviewed literature.
- **[Report]**: assessments and grey literature.
- **[Own]**: my computation or inference.

Each entry also gives:
- fetch status: FULL (full text), ABS (verbatim abstract), SNIP (snippet or metadata only);
- evidence type: experimental, field-correlative, modelling or review.

DOIs were checked against Crossref. The trawl depletion and recovery parameters themselves are in
`research_notes/Bottentrålning och bentos/` and are not repeated here.

## Takeaway

- **There are two candidate pathways. Neither is demonstrated for gadoids on mud.**
  1. **Shelter (structural habitat).**
     - Strong experimental and field support for **0-group cod** on shallow cobble, gravel, sponge, eelgrass and
       maerl, mostly at less than 100 m.
     - Dense emergent structure halves predation mortality in tank trials (Lindholm et al. 1999).
     - Juveniles settle everywhere but survive better in complex habitat (Tupper & Boutilier 1995).
     - **No study links mud epifauna (sea pens, burrow mounds) to juvenile gadoid survival.**
     - The one soft-sediment trawl experiment on fish-habitat structure (sand, 170 m) found no effect on
       structure-forming invertebrates (Lindholm et al. 2015).
  2. **Food (benthic prey).**
     - The trawling-gradient studies found clear effects only on flatfish and Nephrops. Those effects are
       hump-shaped: light trawling can *raise* food for benthivores (Hiddink et al. 2008, 2016; van Denderen et al.
       2013).
     - For gadoids the evidence is null or weak:
       - whiting condition showed no effect on an Irish Sea Nephrops mud ground;
       - whiting trophic level was slightly lower where trawling was heavier (Celtic Sea);
       - haddock diets and sizes differed between fished and unfished gravel (Georges Bank).
- **The review that covers exactly this question** (Collie et al. 2017) concludes the following:
  - trawling changes diet composition and prey quality "rather than the amount of prey consumed";
  - flatfish may gain at light trawling on sand and lose at high intensity;
  - models "suggest" a lower K;
  - patchy effort and foraging behaviour "may mitigate" the effect;
  - scaling to population level is still "current research".
- **Evidence by type:**
  - **Experimental:** juvenile cod, shallow hard substrate only.
  - **Correlative:** gadoid diet, size or condition along trawling gradients. The results are mixed, with no
    recruitment endpoint.
  - **Modelling:** Lindholm 2001 (habitat complexity to 0-group cod survival), Fogarty 2005 (habitat-limited K),
    van Denderen 2013 (food web).
  - **No study shows lower gadoid recruitment caused by trawl-induced habitat change at stock scale.**
- **For the park area** (mud; Mareld 180-450 m, Poseidon Nord 55-230 m; the juvenile haddock band at 150-300 m; see
  `nursery_areas.md`):
  - the shelter pathway does not apply to any documented gadoid life stage;
  - the food pathway is of uncertain sign.

## 1. Shelter pathway: juvenile cod and structured habitat

- **Lindholm JB, Auster PJ, Kaufman LS. 1999.** Habitat-mediated survivorship of juvenile (0-year) Atlantic cod
  *Gadus morhua*. *Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser.* 180:247-255. doi [10.3354/meps180247](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps180247).
  [Primary, FULL, experimental (tank)]
  - Design: three 0-year cod (7-10 cm) and one 38-40 cm cod predator, 4-h trials, 5 replicates per habitat. The
    habitats mimic gear impacts on Georges Bank / Gulf of Maine gravel.
  - Mean % of juveniles eaten (SD):

    | Habitat | % eaten (SD) |
    |---|---|
    | Sand | 93.4 (14.8) |
    | Cobble | 66.8 (23.7) |
    | Short sponge, low density | 46.6 (18.6) |
    | Short sponge, high density | 32.2 (23.7) |
    | Tall sponge | 60.2 (15.2) |

  - Habitat effect F = 8.86, p < 0.001.
  - Quotes:
    - "a drop in total mortality of 50 % from cobble to SS max";
    - "Epifaunal density was shown to be more significant than epifaunal height";
    - "No amount of fishing activity will convert cobble to sand."
  - Predator reaction distance was 60 cm over sand and 23 cm over sponge.
  - **Limits:** relative 4-h tank mortality on cobble, not a field survival rate and not mud.
- **Lindholm JB, Auster PJ, Ruth M, Kaufman L. 2001.** Modeling the effects of fishing and implications for the design
  of marine protected areas: juvenile fish responses to variations in seafloor habitat. *Conserv. Biol.* 15:424-437.
  doi [10.1046/j.1523-1739.2001.015002424.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1523-1739.2001.015002424.x).
  [Primary, ABS, modelling] See `quantitative_links.md`.
  - Survival ranks "pebble-cobble substratum with emergent epifauna > pebble-cobble > sand".
  - "habitat change caused by fishing has significant negative effects on juvenile cod survivorship".
- **Tupper M, Boutilier RG. 1995.** Effects of habitat on settlement, growth, and postsettlement survival of Atlantic
  cod (*Gadus morhua*). *Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci.* 52:1834-1841. doi [10.1139/f95-176](https://doi.org/10.1139/f95-176).
  [Primary, ABS, field (shallow Nova Scotia)]
  - "Settlement of cod did not differ between habitat types, but postsettlement survival and subsequent juvenile
    densities were higher in more structurally complex habitats."
  - Growth was highest in seagrass, survival highest on rock reef and cobble.
  - The abstract gives no numbers.
  - Note: the brief's MEPS 118 paper (doi [10.3354/meps118295](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps118295)) is the one on
    priority at settlement.
- **Gotceitas V, Brown JA. 1993.** Substrate selection by juvenile Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*): effects of predation
  risk. *Oecologia* 93:31-37. doi [10.1007/BF00321187](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00321187).
  [Primary, SNIP (metadata), experimental]
  - Cited in Lindholm 1999: 0-group cod shift to cobble or gravel when a predator is present.
- **Gotceitas V, Fraser S, Brown JA. 1997.** Use of eelgrass beds (*Zostera marina*) by juvenile Atlantic cod.
  *Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci.* 54:1306-1319. doi [10.1139/f97-033](https://doi.org/10.1139/f97-033).
  [Primary, ABS, field and lab]
  - With a predator present, 0+ cod hid in cobble or in eelgrass at 720 stems/m2 or more.
- **Gregory RS, Anderson JT. 1997.** Substrate selection and use of protective cover by juvenile Atlantic cod in inshore
  waters of Newfoundland. *Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser.* 146:9-20. doi [10.3354/meps146009](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps146009).
  [Primary, ABS, field, 18-150 m]
  - "80% [of age 2-4] associated with areas of coarse substrate and high bathymetric relief".
- **Lough RG, Valentine PC, Potter DC, et al. 1989.** Ecology and distribution of juvenile cod and haddock in relation
  to sediment type and bottom currents on eastern Georges Bank. *Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser.* 56:1-12.
  doi [10.3354/meps056001](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps056001). [Primary, ABS, field, 70-100 m]
  - Settled 0-group cod and haddock were "abundant only on the gravel bed".
  - The survival advantage is hypothesised, not measured.
- **Kamenos NA, Moore PG, Hall-Spencer JM. 2004.** *ICES J. Mar. Sci.* 61:422-429.
  doi [10.1016/j.icesjms.2004.02.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icesjms.2004.02.004). [Primary, ABS, field, < 7 m]
  - Juvenile cod, saithe and pollack associated with maerl.
- **Elliott SAM, Turrell WR, Heath MR, Bailey DM. 2017.** Juvenile gadoid habitat and ontogenetic shift observations
  using stereo-video baited cameras. *Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser.* 568:123-135.
  doi [10.3354/meps12068](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps12068). [Primary, ABS, field correlative, Firth of Clyde]
  - "G. morhua were most abundant in shallow, sheltered areas composed of gravel-pebble containing maerl. M. aeglefinus
    and M. merlangus predominated over deeper sand and mud."
  - **This is the most site-relevant point:** juvenile haddock and whiting use mud, but nothing ties their survival
    there to structure.
- **Flavio H, Seitz R, Eggleston D, Svendsen JC, Støttrup J. 2023.** Hard-bottom habitats support commercially
  important fish species: a systematic review for the North Atlantic Ocean and Baltic Sea. *PeerJ* 11:e14681.
  doi [10.7717/peerj.14681](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.14681). [Primary, ABS, systematic review]
  - Cod has "enhanced biomass, density, feeding, and spawning on hard-bottom habitats"; most studies were nearshore.
- **Pihl et al. 2006** (`nursery_areas.md`). [Primary, SNIP]
  - Swedish Skagerrak eelgrass fell 60 %, and 0-group cod density was 96 % lower where eelgrass was lost.
  - This is the strongest regional habitat-recruitment signal for cod. It concerns coastal eelgrass, which is not
    trawled.
- **Lindholm J, Gleason M, Kline D, et al. 2015.** Ecological effects of bottom trawling on the structural attributes
  of fish habitat in unconsolidated sediments along the central California outer continental shelf. *Fish. Bull.*
  113:82-96. doi [10.7755/FB.113.1.8](https://doi.org/10.7755/FB.113.1.8). [Primary, ABS, field experiment, sand, 170 m]
  - "no significant differences between trawled and untrawled plots with respect to structure-forming invertebrates
    (e.g., sea whips)".
  - Door scars persisted for at least a year.

## 2. Benthos depletion: the magnitude on the habitat side

- **Kaiser MJ, Clarke KR, Hinz H, Austen MCV, Somerfield PJ, Karakassis I. 2006.** Global analysis of response and
  recovery of benthic biota to fishing. *Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser.* 311:1-14.
  doi [10.3354/meps311001](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps311001). [Primary, ABS, meta-analysis of 101 manipulations]
  - "soft-sediment habitats, in particular muddy sands, were surprisingly vulnerable, with predicted recovery times
    measured in years".
  - Sponges and soft corals take "up to 8 yr".
- **Sciberras M, Hiddink JG, Jennings S, et al. 2018.** Response of benthic fauna to experimental bottom fishing: a
  global meta-analysis. *Fish Fish.* 19:698-715. doi [10.1111/faf.12283](https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12283).
  [Primary, ABS, meta-analysis of 122 experiments]
  - "A gear pass reduced benthic invertebrate abundance by 26% and species richness by 19%".
  - Depletion was larger in muddy, unfished or biogenic habitat.
- **Hiddink JG, Jennings S, Kaiser MJ, Queirós AM, Duplisea DE, Piet GJ. 2006.** Cumulative impacts of seabed trawl
  disturbance on benthic biomass, production, and species richness in different habitats. *Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci.*
  63:721-736. doi [10.1139/f05-266](https://doi.org/10.1139/f05-266). [Primary, ABS, model plus 33 field stations]
  - The North Sea trawl fleet reduced benthic biomass by 56 % and production by 21 % against an unfished state.
  - Impacts are greatest where natural disturbance is low, as on deep, quiet mud.
- **Hinz H, Prieto V, Kaiser MJ. 2009.** Trawl disturbance on benthic communities: chronic effects and experimental
  predictions. *Ecol. Appl.* 19:761-773. doi [10.1890/08-0351.1](https://doi.org/10.1890/08-0351.1).
  [Primary, ABS, field correlative, Irish Sea Nephrops mud]
  - Infauna fell with trawling; epifaunal abundance and richness fell, but "no such effect was evident for epibenthic
    biomass".
- **Hiddink et al. 2017** and **Pitcher et al. 2022** give d and r. See `research_notes/Bottentrålning och bentos/`.
  The Hiddink et al. 2017 abstract gives otter trawls "removing 6% of biota per pass" as the all-habitat mean. The
  model uses d 0.10 for Nephrops otter trawls on mud (Rijnsdorp et al. 2020).

## 3. Food pathway: gadoid diet, condition and size along trawling gradients

- **Collie J, Hiddink JG, van Kooten T, Rijnsdorp AD, Kaiser MJ, Jennings S, Hilborn R. 2017.** Indirect effects of
  bottom fishing on the productivity of marine fish. *Fish Fish.* 18:619-637.
  doi [10.1111/faf.12193](https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12193). [Primary, ABS (full text blocked), review]
  - "Towed bottom-fishing gears typically kill 20-50 per cent of the benthic invertebrates in their path".
  - "Bottom fishing affects diet composition and prey quality rather than the amount of prey consumed".
  - "Flatfish may benefit from light trawling levels on sandy seabeds, while higher-intensity trawling on more
    vulnerable habitats has a negative effect."
  - "Models suggest that reduction in the carrying capacity of habitats by bottom fishing could lead to lower
    equilibrium yield and a lower level of fishing mortality to obtain maximum yield."
  - "This patchiness, coupled with the foraging behaviour of demersal fish, may mitigate the indirect effects".
  - "Current research attempts to scale up these localized effects to the population level."
  - Its references include Lindholm 1999/2001, Tupper & Boutilier 1995, Gotceitas & Brown 1993, Lough 1989 and
    Fogarty 2005. I could not see how the text weighs them.
- **Hiddink JG, Johnson AF, Kingham R, Hinz H. 2011.** Could our fisheries be more productive? Indirect negative
  effects of bottom trawl fisheries on fish condition. *J. Appl. Ecol.* 48:1441-1449.
  doi [10.1111/j.1365-2664.2011.02036.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2664.2011.02036.x).
  [Primary, ABS, field correlative, Irish Sea Nephrops mud, 0.5-11.9 passes/yr]
  - Plaice condition fell with trawling.
  - "Whiting ... feeds primarily on fish, and therefore, no effect of bottom trawling on its condition was expected or
    detected."
- **Johnson AF, Gorelli G, Jenkins SR, Hiddink JG, Hinz H. 2015.** Effects of bottom trawling on fish foraging and
  feeding. *Proc. R. Soc. B* 282:20142336. doi [10.1098/rspb.2014.2336](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2014.2336).
  [Primary, FULL, field correlative, plaice and dab]
  - Prey and fish both fell with trawling, so "the ratio of prey biomass to fish biomass was not reduced".
- **Hiddink JG, Moranta J, Balestrini S, et al. 2016.** Bottom trawling affects fish condition through changes in the
  ratio of prey availability to density of competitors. *J. Appl. Ecol.* 53:1500-1510.
  doi [10.1111/1365-2664.12697](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.12697). [Primary, ABS, field correlative, Kattegat]
  - Food per plaice "increased before decreasing at trawling frequencies >5 times year-1".
  - No gadoids were analysed.
- **Hiddink JG, Rijnsdorp AD, Piet G. 2008.** Can bottom trawling disturbance increase food production for a
  commercial fish species? *Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci.* 65:1393-1401. doi [10.1139/f08-064](https://doi.org/10.1139/f08-064).
  [Primary, ABS, model]
  - Prey production for plaice was "maximal in areas that are trawled once to twice a year".
- **van Denderen PD, van Kooten T, Rijnsdorp AD. 2013.** When does fishing lead to more fish? Community consequences of
  bottom trawl fisheries in demersal food webs. *Proc. R. Soc. B* 280:20131883.
  doi [10.1098/rspb.2013.1883](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.1883). [Primary, FULL, theoretical model]
  - "When susceptible benthos are the most profitable prey, trawling reduces fish abundance, yield and persistence of
    fish in all situations". Otherwise trawling can raise fish biomass.
- **Shephard S, Minto C, Zölck M, Jennings S, Brophy D, Reid D. 2014.** Scavenging on trawled seabeds can modify
  trophic size structure of bottom-dwelling fish. *ICES J. Mar. Sci.* 71:398-405.
  doi [10.1093/icesjms/fst134](https://doi.org/10.1093/icesjms/fst134). [Primary, ABS, field correlative, Celtic Sea]
  - "For whiting ... of all sizes, trophic level was slightly lower in areas of higher fishing activity".
- **Shephard S, Brophy D, Reid DG. 2010.** Can bottom trawling indirectly diminish carrying capacity in a marine
  ecosystem? *Mar. Biol.* 157:2375-2381. doi [10.1007/s00227-010-1502-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-010-1502-9).
  [Primary, SNIP]
  - Plaice length-at-age fell with trawling on gravel and rose on sand. Flatfish only.
  - A repository lists a retraction for a *different* Shephard et al. item ("Benthivorous fish may go hungry on trawled
    seabed"). This was not verified, so do not cite that item.
- **Smith BE, Collie JS, Lengyel NL. 2013.** Effects of chronic bottom fishing on the benthic epifauna and diets of
  demersal fishes on northern Georges Bank. *Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser.* 472:199-217.
  doi [10.3354/meps10031](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps10031). [Primary, ABS, field correlative, gravel]
  - Epifauna was lower where fishing was heavy.
  - "lengths of haddock, Atlantic cod, and winter flounder tended to be larger at the undisturbed sites".
  - Haddock diets shifted with the fishing-sensitive epifauna.
  - This is the only gadoid (haddock) diet signal. It is on gravel, and size is confounded with fishing mortality.

## 4. Searches that found nothing

- Sea pens, burrowed mud or Nephrops grounds as juvenile gadoid habitat in the Skagerrak, Kattegat or Clyde.
  - A Glasgow thesis finds Funiculina scarcer on trawled Clyde grounds, but nothing links sea pens to gadoids.
- Any stock-level estimate of gadoid recruitment lost to trawl-induced habitat change.
- The Collie et al. 2017 main text, Lindholm 2001's functional forms and Gotceitas & Brown 1993's abstract could not be
  fetched.

## [Own] What this means for the proposed K coupling

1. The literature supports "structured habitat raises 0-group cod survival" on shallow hard ground. That ground is
   absent from the grid: it has 25 km2 shallower than 50 m and no coast.
2. For haddock, the one gadoid with juvenile habitat in the trawled 150-300 m band:
   - the habitat link is a food link;
   - its sign under trawling is ambiguous (hump-shaped for benthivores, Hiddink 2008/2016);
   - its gadoid evidence is limited to haddock diet and size on gravel (Smith et al. 2013).
3. So the elasticity of gadoid K to the benthic state of *this* area is not known even in sign for the food pathway.
   For the shelter pathway it is near zero. This sets the "e" factor in `recommendation.md`.
4. The benthic food channel is already explicit in the model: gadoids eat `benthic_community`, which trawling depletes
   per cell. A K coupling through H would count that channel twice.
