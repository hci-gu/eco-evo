# Evidence A - Gadoid morphology and habitat: who can exploit benthos, pelagic fish and zooplankton

Scope: the per-pair parameter phi_j (share of the pooled gadoid BIOMASS that can exploit prey pool j
at the full per-capita rate), scored per species x stage from **morphology and habitat only**: gill
rakers, mouth and gape, body form and swimming, water-column position, diel behaviour, life-stage
boundaries. Diet appears only where a source mixes it in, and is then labelled **diet-derived**.
Diet shares are left to the sibling diet note.
Box: eastern Skagerrak, Norwegian Trench slope, 57.7-58.4 N, 9.9-11.3 E, 44-529 m, mostly
100-300 m mud. Mix (biomass): haddock 46.6 %, saithe 29.1, whiting 17.0, cod 5.7, hake 0.85.

Retrieved partly by four delegated web lookups. Their [F] tags mean that lookup read the full text.

Key ranking from morphology alone:
- **Gape**: hake > cod ~ whiting > saithe > haddock.
- **Small-crustacean equipment (raker count)**: saithe (35-40) >> haddock, cod, whiting (21-28) >> hake (8-12).
- **Pelagic build** (forked tail, schooling, high sustained speed, diel ascents): saithe > whiting > hake > cod > haddock.

## 1. Gill rakers

### 1a. Counts

- FishBase MorphDataSummary pages: the gill-raker fields are **empty** for cod, haddock, whiting
  and saithe. Hake: "on lower limb 7 - 9". Files `data/morphology/fishbase_morph_*.html` [F].
- FAO 1990 (Cohen et al.) prints counts only for hake, Norway pout and blue whiting. European hake:
  "gill rakers short and thick, with blunt tips; total number of gill rakers on first arch 8 to 11
  (mostly 9 to 11)" (`../Torskfiskarnas artsammansättning/data/diet/t0243e.pdf`, p. 339) [F].
- Svetovidov 1962 (English translation of the 1948 "Fauna of the USSR, Gadiformes"),
  https://archive.org/details/gadiformestresko00svet, OCR text in
  `data/morphology/rakers_svetovidov1962_gadiformes_djvu.txt` [F]:
  - Method: "Gill-rakers were counted in the outer row of the first gill arch and all gill-rakers
    were counted including rudiments."
  - Saithe: "gill-rakers 35-40, vertebrae 54".
  - Haddock: "gill-rakers 24-27, vertebrae 52-57".
  - Whiting (*Odontogadus merlangus merlangus*): "gill-rakers 21-22, vertebrae 54".
  - Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua morhua*): "rakers 21-28, vertebrae 49-53" (the word before
    "rakers" is OCR-damaged). Lofoten cod "gill-rakers (average 24.9)"; a White Sea form "gill-rakers
    20-26 (23.3)".
  - Pollack (contrast): "gill-rakers 26-27".
  - Hake: "gill-rakers (7) 8-12 on lower limb of the first gill arch, on the entire arch 10-12."
- FNAM portal (Whitehead et al. 1986, Naturalis), files `rakers_fnam_naturalis_*.html` [S, FNAM]:
  saithe "Gillrakers 35-40."; whiting "Gillrakers 19-26."; Norway pout "Gillrakers 33-43.";
  blue whiting "Gillrakers 27-33."; cod and haddock pages print no count.
- Methven & McGowan 1998, Can J Zool 76:1054, doi:10.1139/z98-046 [A, search snippet]: *G. ogac*
  16-19 rakers, "overlap with those for G. morhua (range = 21–26)".
- Bigelow & Schroeder 1953 and Fahay 2007 (Gadidae chapter) give no raker counts for these species [F].

Contrast: saithe's 35-40 rakers match or exceed those of the two zooplanktivorous gadoids, blue whiting
(26-34) and Norway pout (31-35 FAO, 33-43 FNAM). Cod, haddock and whiting sit at 21-28. Hake's 8-12
short, thick rakers are a piscivore arch.

### 1b. Spacing and retention (copepods vs krill)

- **No measured inter-raker gap was found for any Atlantic gadoid.** No raker length or shape
  statement was found for cod, haddock, whiting or saithe. Wheeler, Muus & Nielsen and Collette &
  Klein-MacPhee 2002 were not freely online.
- Magnuson & Heitz 1971, Fish Bull 69:361 (scombrids), `rakers_magnuson_heitz1971_fishbull69.pdf` [F]:
  "a 50-cm K. pelamis, a 30-cm T. albacares, and a 10-cm S. orientalis all had a mean gill raker gap
  of approximately 1 mm". Gap grows with fork length. Crustacean volume in stomachs was "inversely
  related to mean gaps".
- Budy et al. 2005, TAFS 134:1228, doi:10.1577/T04-159.1 [A] (trout): "inter-gill raker spacing
  averaged 1.09 mm"; the electivity threshold "closely corresponded to the mean interraker spacing".
- Langeland & Nøst 1995, J Fish Biol 47:719 [A]: "The minimum size of cladoceran species found in fish
  stomachs was much smaller than the distance between gill rakers"; the straining hypothesis for
  particulate feeders "is rejected".
- Gibson & Ezzi 1992, J Fish Biol 40:577 [A] (herring, the filter-feeding contrast, fed on
  Calanus): "At low concentrations the fish feed by selective capture of individual particles".
  Filtering costs "1.4 to 4.6 times" as much as biting.
- Lee 1985 (UAF thesis, juvenile Pacific cod vs walleye pollock) [A]: measured "the distances between
  gill rakers"; "gill structures were related to size-selective predation". (Mixed morphology +
  diet.)
- **No study found that cod, haddock, whiting or saithe filter or pump-filter Calanus.** The records
  show copepods eaten (diet), not the capture mode. Bigelow & Schroeder 1953 on pollock (saithe):
  "Large pollock take morsels as small as copepods" [F, diet-derived, no mechanism].

What this means:
- Retention is a constraint only for 1-3 mm copepods. Measured raker gaps of fish that strain are
  ~1 mm. A gadoid that picks copepods one by one does not need rakers to retain them (Langeland &
  Nøst), but it gets a low intake per unit search effort.
- Krill (15-40 mm) and Themisto are retained by any arch. For them the limit is encounter, i.e.
  water-column overlap (section 4 and 6), not rakers.
- So the raker count is a weak-to-moderate marker. It sets saithe apart (the only one with a
  planktivore-like arch) and puts hake firmly out of the copepod business.

### 1c. Juveniles

- Bromley et al. 1997, ICES JMS 54:846, `rakers_bromley1997_icesjms54_0group_gadoids.pdf` [F,
  diet-derived]: "Fish of 1–3 cm in length fed mainly on copepods"; "Calanus finmarchicus were eaten
  by all the 0-group gadoids".
- No raker count vs body length data were found for any gadoid. Juveniles of 87-135 mm SL already
  have adult counts (Methven & McGowan 1998) [A].

## 2. Mouth and gape

### 2a. Direct measurements (morphology)

- Svetovidov 1962 [F], jaw lengths in % of head length and head length in % of body length:
  - Cod: head "24.6-26.6% of the body length"; "upper jaw 39.2-44.2, length of lower jaw 49.0-54.8".
  - Haddock: "Jaw bones greatly shortened, length of the upper jaw 28.6-33.1, length of lower jaw
    35.3-39.1% of head length"; head length 24.6-25.7.
  - Whiting: "length of upper jaw 40.6-44.1, length of lower jaw 50.7-56.0"; head 23.9-25.2.
    Genus: "a row of sharp, well-developed teeth on the premaxillary".
  - Saithe: "jaws shortened, upper 33.0-35.1, lower 48.7 - 52.0% of head length"; head 23.0-24.0.
  - Hake: "length of upper jaw 49.3-52.4, lower jaw 61.3-64.5"; head 24.3-25.8.
  - Svetovidov's lower-jaw length is the mandible bone length and is longer than the upper jaw in
    every species, haddock included. It does not show which jaw projects (FAO's "lower jaw
    shorter" refers to projection).
  - Converted to % BL (midpoints, my arithmetic): UJ cod 10.7, haddock 7.8, whiting 10.4, saithe 8.0,
    hake 12.8; LJ cod 13.3, haddock 9.4, whiting 13.1, saithe 11.8, hake 15.8.
  - FAO 1990 hake: "upper jaw 47.8 to 53.5%" of HL; head 25.1-30.5 % SL [F]. This agrees.
- Scharf, Juanes & Rountree 2000, MEPS 208:229, Table 3,
  https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps/208/m208p229.pdf, `gape_scharf2000_meps208p229.pdf` [F] (mm TL):
  - Atlantic cod: "GW = 0.128PD – 11.689" (r2 0.91, n 18, 300-910 mm); GH = 0.085PD + 1.584 (r2 0.87).
  - Silver hake (*M. bilinearis*, proxy for European hake): "GW = 0.076PD + 1.011" (n 83, 100-400 mm);
    GH = 0.123PD + 2.147.
  - "For 5 of 8 predators examined, gape size appeared to be a good indicator of maximum prey size consumed".
- Rowlands et al. 2006, J Fish Biol 69:1379 [F]: gape of cod, haddock and whiting only at 4-70 mm SL,
  "segmented regression lines in all three species, with an inflection point at c. 10.5 mm". Too
  small for clupeid prey; coefficient table not legible in the scan.
- Johnson et al. 2012, CJFAS 69:191, doi:10.1139/f2011-138 [A]: gape vs maximum prey size for 6 of 9
  demersal species including hake; coefficients not obtained.
- **No adult gape regression was found for haddock, whiting, saithe or *M. merluccius*.**

### 2c. Size thresholds

Prey body depth taken as 0.18-0.20 x TL for sprat and herring (my assumption, not checked against a
source). Gape route: PD = (depth - intercept)/slope on the cod GH line. For the other species the cod
threshold is divided by the ratio of their jaw length to cod's (Svetovidov, % BL): haddock 0.71-0.73,
whiting 0.97-0.98, saithe 0.75 (UJ) - 0.89 (LJ), hake 1.19-1.20. **The jaw scaling is my own
estimate**, assuming gape height is proportional to jaw length. Prey width vs gape width was not checked.

Check against the field (diet-derived, so only a check): Hoines et al. 1992, ICES CM 1992/G:9,
`gape_vliz274776.pdf` [F]: "Cod larger than 50 cm had a very high proportion of adult herring in the
diet, from around 75 to 100 per-cent in terms of weight". This agrees with the gape threshold
(51-57 cm) for a 25-cm herring.

In-box consequence (length shares from `tables/length_biomass.csv`):
- Haddock (mostly 25-40 cm) can at most swallow a sprat; adult herring is out of reach at all
  in-box sizes.
- Whiting (mostly 20-40 cm) can take sprat and small herring, rarely adult herring.
- Saithe 30-60 cm can take sprat and small herring; >60 cm also adult herring.
- Hake (mostly >20 cm, many >60 cm) can take every clupeid size.
- Cod 30-50 cm: sprat and small herring; >50 cm: adult herring.
- Mackerel and horse mackerel in the model pool (deeper-bodied, often >25 cm) are within reach of
  large cod, saithe >60 cm and hake only.

### 2d. Piscivory onset in the literature (all diet-derived)

None of these thresholds was derived from gape; all come from stomachs.
- Cod: FishMap [F]: from ~7 cm demersal juveniles eat mainly crustaceans. Hoines 1992 [F]: "Only the
  small cod (TL < 30 cm) fed mostly on other prey than sandeel". Onset ~30 cm, adult herring from ~50 cm.
- Whiting: FishMap [F]: "Immature whiting (< 20 cm) feed on crustaceans"; "Whiting >30cm feed almost
  entirely on fish".
- Saithe: FishMap [F]: "Adults feed almost entirely on pelagic and demersal fish" (no length).
- Hake: Mahé 2007 [F]: "A piscivorous diet commences at ~11 cm TL, but becomes significant only in
  hake >20 cm TL".
- Haddock: Hoines 1992 [F]: "fish prey were generally unimportant"; Tam et al. 2016, J Fish Biol
  88:2203 [A]: no consistent increase of piscivory with size. No onset length was found.
- The gape thresholds of 2c agree with these lengths: whiting ~20 cm, cod ~20-30 cm, hake ~15-20 cm.

## 3. Body form and swimming

- Body form (FishBase morphology pages [F]):
  - Cod: "body depth about 1/5 of total length"; fusiform; caudal "more or less truncate".
  - Haddock: fusiform, caudal truncate.
  - Whiting: "Body elongate; head small".
  - Saithe: fusiform; caudal "forked".
  - Hake: "elongated". FAO: "Body long and rather slender"; caudal "becoming progressively forked with growth" [F].
- Cod Ucrit: Schurmann & Steffensen 1997 abstract, "U-crit was 1.6, 1.7 and 1.9 at 5, 10 and 15
  degrees C" (BL/s), https://marineinfo.org/id/publication/144544 [A]. Norin et al. 2019, PeerJ,
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6777481: 2.62 BL/s at 20.9 cm and 12 C [F, read via a
  fetch summary].
- Cod in situ: "Swimming speeds below 1.0 body lengths (bl) s-1 were most common (70%)" (Fernö et
  al. 2011, Mar Biol Res 7:310, https://marineinfo.org/id/publication/204763) [A].
- Haddock: "Estimates of Ums ranged from 0.38 ± 0.03 m s−1 and 3.16 ± 0.02 BL s−1 (for a 16.0-cm fish)
  to 0.62 ± 0.04 m s−1 and 1.51 ± 0.07 BL s−1 (for a 42.0-cm fish)" (Breen et al. 2004, ICES JMS
  61:1071, https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/61/7/1071/639547) [A]. Norin 2019: Ucrit 3.02 BL/s
  at 18.7 cm; haddock "closer to a fineness ratio of ~4.5" [F].
- Saithe vs whiting: "The optimum swimming speed (U opt) was 1.4 BL s-1 for saithe and 1.0 BL s-1
  for whiting"; saithe reached the higher critical speed (Steinhausen et al. 2005, Mar Biol 148:197,
  https://marineinfo.org/id/publication/260233) [A].
- Saithe Ums ~3.5 BL/s at 25 cm, 2.2 BL/s at 50 cm, 14.4 C (He & Wardle 1988, J Fish Biol 33:255) [A, snippet].
- Hake swimming speed: not found.
- Reading: haddock is a good sustained swimmer for its size. Its limit for pelagic fish is the
  small mouth, not speed. Saithe is the only one with a forked tail, schooling and the
  highest sustained speeds: the pursuit build. Whiting is a slower, elongate ambusher with sharp teeth.

## 4. Water-column position and diel behaviour

- Cod:
  - Hobson et al. 2007, MEPS 347:101, `vert_hobson2007_meps347_cod_vertical.pdf` [F]: "Across all
    areas, over 55% of their time was spent within 5 m of the seabed".
  - Hobson et al. 2009, Aquat Biol 5:133 [F]: "midwater (43% of total time) and near the seabed (57%)".
  - Righton et al. 2000, ICES CM 2000/Q:09 [F]: in August-September "cod typically left the seabed at
    nightfall and returned to it before dawn".
  - FAO 1990 [S, FAO adapting Bigelow & Schroeder 1953]: "gregarious during the day, forming compact
    schools that swim between 30 and 80 m above the bottom, and scatter at night".
  - Skaret et al. 2020 (Barents Sea bank) [F, abstract page]: cod "remained mostly within 10 m of the
    bottom throughout the diel cycle".
- Haddock: Engås, Jacobsen & Soldal 1988, ICES CM 1988/B:32, `vert_engas1988_icescm_B32_diurnal_trawl.pdf`
  [F, OCR cleaned]: "Aggregations of haddock were observed up to about 150 m above bottom at daytime";
  at night the fish "stayed more close to the bottom"; "haddock has a far greater vertical diurnal
  migration than cod". This is Barents Sea. No Skagerrak or Norwegian Deep data were obtained (Albert
  1994 not read). Michalsen et al. 1996 [F]: "tidal currents affect the vertical distribution of cod
  and haddock".
- Whiting: Onsrud et al. 2004, ICES JMS 61:1278 (Oslofjord, 120-m site) [F, fetched]: "During daytime,
  herring and whiting were foraging in the upper and middle part of the krill assemblage"; at night
  "herring, whiting, and sprat were most abundant in the upper 50 m". FishMap [S]: "commonly found near
  the bottom ... but may move into midwater in the pursuit of its prey". FAO: "Benthopelagic" [F].
- Saithe: FishMap [S]: adults "may be found hundreds of metres above the bottom during night time, as
  well as close to the bottom, down at depths of more than 300 m during day time". Jonsson &
  Armannsson 2006 (Icelandic DST, mean 56 cm) [F]: "Average hourly Δz was highest during daylight hours".
  FAO: "An active, gregarious, pelagic fish" [F]. FishMap [S]: saithe >=30 cm show "a clear zone of
  high abundance along the edge of the Norwegian Deep", i.e. exactly the box slope.
- Hake: FAO [F]: "It lives close to the bottom during day-time, but moves off-bottom at night".
  Carpentieri et al. 2005, Fish Bull 103:411 [F, citing older work]: juveniles move "from near the
  bottom, 100−200 m depth, to midwater at night". No height in metres was found.
- Norwegian Deep gadoids: Bergstad 1991, Sarsia, doi:10.1080/00364827.1991.10413455 [A]: "All the
  species are benthopelagic and epibenthic predators on fish and crustaceans."

## 5. Life-stage boundaries (habitat and morphology)

- Cod: "The eggs and the larvae up to 2.5 months are pelagic; subsequently the postlarvae settle to
  the bottom" (FAO) [F]. North Sea: "the 0-group adopt a demersal way of life in July/August at a size
  of approximately 7 cm" (FishMap) [S]. Skagerrak coast: settlement at 3-5 cm in eelgrass
  (https://www.duo.uio.no/handle/10852/11645) [A]. A pelagic 0-group stage <~7 cm is kept; in the
  box it carries 0 % of the GOV biomass.
- Haddock: "larvae are believed to be pelagic for some 3 months" (FAO) [F]; "the 0-group remain
  pelagic until they reach a size of approximately 7 cm" (FishMap) [S]. **Boundary moved from
  <10 cm to <~7 cm.** It carries 0.4 % of the in-box biomass either way.
- Whiting: "larvae and juveniles are associated with jellyfish, and do not become demersal until
  they reach 5 to 10 cm length" (FAO) [F]. Juvenile/adult boundary set at **20 cm**, where the gape
  first admits a 10-cm sprat (2c, morphology). This coincides with FishMap's diet boundary.
- Saithe: "During their first 2-3 years of age, saithe remain in shallow coastal waters"; "at 1 year,
  ca. 20 cm, 2 years, 35 cm, 3 years, 50 cm" (FAO) [F]. Saithe tagged at 62.5 N left the coast at
  35-40 cm, age 3-4 (Jakobsen 1978) [A]. This explains why the box has no saithe <30 cm. Split at
  60 cm, where the gape admits a 25-cm herring (57-76 cm).
- Hake: FAO [F]: "tips of pectoral fins reaching to level of anal origin in young fish (below 20 cm
  standard length) but not in adults". This is a morphological marker of the 20-cm boundary. Also
  "Up to age 3, juveniles live on muddy bottoms" [F]. Settlement: "age at settlement (62.7 days)",
  juvenile modes 7-11 cm (Morales-Nin & Moranta 2004, Sci Mar 68:399, western Mediterranean) [A].
  The 20-cm split is kept.

## 6. Prey position (overlap with 100-300 m slope gadoids)

- Krill (*M. norvegica*), Oslofjord (120-m site), Onsrud & Kaartvedt 1998, MEPS 171:209,
  `prey_Onsrud_Kaartvedt_1998_MEPS171.pdf` [F]:
  - "Acoustic scattering layers of Meganyctiphanes norvegica in the Oslofjord, Norway, typically
    stayed below ~70 m during the day"; at night "upward migration was mainly arrested at ~10-30 m".
  - The 70 m is shallower than in western Norwegian fjords, "where they mainly stay below 100 m"
    [S, citing Evans & Hopkins 1981 and others].
  - "Norway pout Trisopterus esmarki, whiting Merlangius merlangus and cod Gadus morhua are among the
    potential diurnal predators in this location".
- Schmedling 2021 (UiO thesis) [A]: "The krill layer concentrated near the seafloor during September
  and October, where they became vulnerable to predation by deep-living predators."
- Barents Sea analogue, Skaret et al. 2020, MEPS, doi:10.3354/meps13254 [F, abstract page]: "During
  daylight, krill pushed towards the bottom, sharing habitat with cod".
- Norwegian Deep: Norway pout "generally 5-10 m above the sea bed" fed mainly on euphausiids, more
  at night (Albert 1994, doi:10.1006/jmsc.1994.1005) [A, diet-derived for the pout]. This is
  indirect evidence that krill are near the bottom there.
- Calanus overwintering:
  - Oslofjord (mostly *C. helgolandicus*): "In the course of winter, Calanus became increasingly
    concentrated towards the bottom (120 m)" (Bagøien et al. 2000, Sarsia,
    doi:10.1080/00364827.2000.10414581) [A].
  - Bunnefjorden: "In early winter the bulk of Calanus inhabited near-bottom waters" (Kaartvedt et
    al. 2021, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8009684/) [F].
  - Norwegian Trench: "Dense concentrations occurred in ... the Norwegian Trench" (Heath et al. 2004,
    doi:10.1016/j.icesjms.2004.03.013) [F]. The depth in metres was only in a figure that could not be read.
  - **No measured overwintering depth for *C. finmarchicus* on the Skagerrak/Trench slope was found.**
    The general range is "300–1 500 m" (Head & Pepin 2008) [F]. Over the 100-300 m slope, Calanus
    in diapause likely sits deeper (in the trench, down to the box's 529 m) or near the bottom.
    This is my inference.
- Herring and sprat:
  - Oslofjord: "Schools were located at about 50 m during the day, i.e. above the krill layer"
    (Onsrud & Kaartvedt 1998) [F]; at night in the upper 50 m (Onsrud 2004) [F].
  - Baltic: "aggregated during the day at the bottom" (Cardinale et al. 2003) [A].
  - Shetland, July: herring "5-20m above the sea bed" by day (Bailey et al. 1980) [F].
  - Sprat in a 150-m fjord: "the majority of the population occurred in deep waters during both day
    and night" (Solberg et al. 2012) [A].
  - **No Skagerrak-specific herring/sprat depth data were found.**
- Pasiphaea multidentata: "Most specimens occur at depths of 300–400 m during the day-time", with
  upward migration at night (Matthews & Pinnoi 1973, Korsfjorden) [A]. Adults "mainly present on the
  bottom during the night" (Aguzzi et al. 2007, Mediterranean) [A]. It is a deep, partly epibenthic
  shrimp. It fits the benthos pool by position for the deep box, and zooplankton by habit.
- Reading for overlap:
  - By day, krill and overwintering Calanus sink to or near the bottom in Norwegian fjords. In clearer
    Skagerrak water this probably happens deeper than 70 m. So demersal gadoids at 100-300 m
    plausibly meet krill near the bed by day.
  - Whiting and saithe also meet krill in midwater.
  - Herring and sprat are near the bottom by day in several systems and higher at night, so daytime
    overlap with demersal gadoids is plausible. Gape is then the binding limit.

- Min and max put every stage at its own min or max at the same time. They are envelopes, not
  confidence intervals.
- The pooled values are dominated by haddock (47 %) and saithe (29 %).
  - phi_P is driven by saithe and by the haddock mouth limit.
  - phi_Z is driven by saithe's arch and by whether haddock meets krill near the bed. Haddock's Z
    score is the least supported: it rests on Barents Sea diel ascents.
- Per-capita "full rate" on zooplankton means krill and Themisto. On 1-3 mm Calanus no gadoid except
  perhaps saithe and the 0-groups is equipped to reach a full rate (no raker spacing data). If the pool
  is Calanus-dominated in biomass, phi_Z should be read toward its min.
