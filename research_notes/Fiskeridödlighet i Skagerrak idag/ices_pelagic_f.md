## Q1. North Sea autumn-spawning herring (her.27.3a47d): F, reference points, biomass, and the 3.a share

### Takeaway
Stock-wide F (ages 2-6 winter rings) was **0.191 in 2025** (0.24 in 2024; 0.18-0.24 every year
since 2017). FMSY = FPA = Fmgt = 0.23, and Flim is not defined. SSB in 2025 was 1.68 Mt and TSB
2.96 Mt. Catch was 428,593 t, of which **only 692 t was taken in Division 3.a**. The 3.a herring
fishery has been almost closed since 2022 to protect WBSS herring: the 3.a quota can be
transferred 100 % to the North Sea, and the EU has agreed to take only 969 t of herring in 3.a in
2025-2026. Catch/TSB is **0.145** (computed). Fishing pressure on juveniles in 2025 was "the
highest in the past 10 years". Juveniles dominate the herring in the Skagerrak.

### Inferences
- **Computed** exploitation rate on total biomass: u = 428 593 / 2 956 478 = **0.145 yr-1** (2025).
  Over 2021-2025 it is 0.130, 0.143, 0.131, 0.167, 0.145 (mean 0.143).
- u (0.145) is below Fbar (0.191) because Fbar averages only ages 2-6 wr, while TSB also holds
  0- and 1-ringers that are barely fished: F0-1 is 0.052 against F2-6 0.168 in the 2026
  interim year, a ratio of about 0.31. The section "Instantaneous equivalents" below explains how
  u converts to an instantaneous rate.
- Herring in the Skagerrak are juvenile-heavy (Q3), so the stock-wide u probably *overstates*
  the life-cycle F of the fish that are in the Skagerrak at any time. HERAS 2025 found NSAS mean
  weights of 41, 127 and 48 g and maturity of 0.08, 0.71 and 0.03 in strata 31, 41 and 42. The
  biomass-weighted maturity is 0.36 (**computed**: (22.1x0.08 + 30.3x0.71 + 14.1x0.03)/66.5;
  the proportion mature is by numbers, so this is approximate).

## Q3. Herring mixing in 3.a (NSAS / WBSS / others) and what it means for a Skagerrak-specific F

### Takeaway
In the Skagerrak (SD 20 = 27.3.a.20) the herring are a mixture that changes with age and season.
In Q3 2025 WBSS made up about 5 % of 0-ringers, 12 % of 1-ringers, 38-46 % of 2-3-ringers,
32-39 % of 4-6-ringers and 73-99 % of 7+. In the HERAS July 2025 Skagerrak strata, WBSS was **32 %
of the herring biomass** (31.7 of 98.2 kt; 14 % in 2024). The genetic data also show that the
"NSAS" and "WBSS" indices are "contaminated" by other populations, including a local Skagerrak
spring spawner. So an area-specific F is the mixture-weighted F of a stock fished at F ~0.19 and
one fished at ~0.01. Today both are fished hardly at all *inside* the Skagerrak: all herring
caught in 3.a.20 in 2025 came to **0.46 kt**.

### Inferences
- I assume strata 31, 41 and 42 are the Skagerrak, because stratum 21 is named as the Kattegat
  and 3.a = strata 21/31/41/42. The figure that maps the strata was not checked, so I cannot say
  which of the three strata covers the Mareld box.
- **Computed** HERAS July 2025 Skagerrak herring: NSAS 66.5 kt + WBSS 31.7 kt = 98.2 kt, of which
  WBSS is 0.323. In 2024: 57.4 + 9.1 = 66.5 kt (WBSS 0.14). The share swings by a factor of 2 between
  years, which fits the "variable migration behaviors and differences in year class strength"
  that WGIPS points to.
- **Computed** in-area removal rate for Skagerrak herring: 0.46 kt / 98.2 kt = **0.0047 yr-1**
  (2024: 0.35 / 66.5 = 0.0053). This is a July snapshot of biomass against an annual catch, so it is
  an order of magnitude, not an estimate. It is still at least 20x below the stock-level rates,
  because the 3.a herring fishery has been almost shut since 2022.
- **Computed** stock-level mixture for the Skagerrak with the HERAS 2025 shares:
  0.677 x 0.145 + 0.323 x 0.0087 = **0.101 yr-1**. With the 2024 shares (0.86 / 0.14):
  0.126.
- Which F the model should use depends on what it represents. These are migratory stocks fished
  mostly *outside* the box: juvenile NSAS leave the Skagerrak for the North Sea and are caught there.
  The model's r_max and M1 are also derived at stock / North Sea level. A model population that
  stands for "a representative sample of these stocks" should therefore lose the **stock-level**
  rate. A box that is truly closed (no emigration) would see only the **in-area** rate (~0.005),
  and the fishing those fish meet after they leave would not be in the model at all. A
  model without emigration that uses the in-area rate leaves out a real loss. A model with
  explicit emigration that also uses the stock-level rate counts that loss twice.

## Q4. Sprat (spr.27.3a4): F, Fcap, biomass and the 3.a share

### Takeaway
Sprat is the most heavily exploited stock in the pool, but very little of that fishing happens in
the Skagerrak. F (ages 1-2) was **1.51 in model year 2025** (July 2025-June 2026). The series swings
between 0.67 and 2.09 in 2021-2025. Fcap is 0.71, and FMSY and FPA are not defined. Of the 211.8 kt
caught in calendar 2025, **1.0 kt came from 3.a.20 (Skagerrak)** and 2.5 kt from 3.a.21 (Kattegat).
HERAS found no sprat in the Skagerrak strata in July 2025. Coastal sprat in Norway and Sweden is
excluded from both the catches and the assessment. Model-year yield/TSB is **0.45** (computed;
2021-2025 mean 0.43).

### Cited Findings

"No reference points for fishing pressure have been defined for this stock. - "Fages 1–2 (2025) 1.51 From the assessment based on observed catches until 01 March 2026"; "Total catch (2025) 206 616 Based on observed catches for April 2025 to 01 March 2026". ### Inferences
- **Computed** u = model-year yield / 1 July TSB = 189 709 / 423 028 = **0.448** (2025). With the
  calendar-year catch (211 810, a different time window) it is 0.501. 2021-2025: 0.565, 0.296,
  0.585, 0.256, 0.448 (mean 0.430). I use the model-year pair (same SMS output), because the three
  catch figures (211 810 calendar, 206 616 preliminary model year, 189 709 SMS April-March) follow
  different conventions.
- u (0.45) is far below Fbar (1.51) because Fbar covers ages 1-2 only, and because a large part of
  the 1 July TSB is 0-group that is not yet caught. Sprat also has a high M (SMS), so a large share
  of each cohort dies of other causes before it is caught.
- The in-area rate is uncertain. Sprat in 3.a in July sat in the Kattegat (39.5 kt, HERAS). 3.a
  sprat landings of 3.5 kt against that give ~0.09 (**computed**, order of magnitude), and the
  Skagerrak-only share (1.0 kt) has no Skagerrak biomass to set it against.

## Q5. Mackerel (mac.27.nea), horse mackerel and sandeel: relevance to 3.a

### Takeaway
- **Mackerel**: F (ages 4-8) was **0.285 in 2025**, above FMSY = FPA = 0.191. SSB of 3.07 Mt is
  below MSY Btrigger and BPA. Catch was 802 kt and TSB 3.73 Mt, so catch/TSB is **0.215**
  (computed). Mackerel is a summer feeding visitor to the Skagerrak. ICES reports catches only for
  "Subareas 3 and 4" (290.7 kt in 2025); the 3.a-alone catch was not retrieved.
- **Horse mackerel**: after the 2024 benchmark, 3.a catches belong to the *western* stock, coded
  **hom.27.2a3a4a5b6a7a-ce-k8** (the brief's code without "3a" is outdated). F (ages 1-10) was
  **0.065 in 2025** (FMSY = FPA 0.08), with catch 61.4 kt and TSB 1.60 Mt, so catch/TSB is **0.038**
  (computed).
- **Sandeel**: Sandeel Area 3r (northern and central North Sea and Skagerrak, SD 20) had **zero
  catch and F = 0 in 2025**, and ICES advises zero catch in 2026. Sandeel Area 6 (Skagerrak,
  Kattegat, Belt Sea) had 0.86 t in 2024 under a 140 t TAC. Today's fishing on sandeel in the
  Skagerrak is effectively zero.

## Instantaneous equivalents and why they differ from Fbar

The model removes F/1460 x B every tick, so the yearly catch is F x (mean biomass). If the model
biomass is roughly stationary over the year (production balances losses), the model F that
reproduces an observed catch is **F_model = catch / mean biomass ~ u**, with u = catch / TSB, as
long as TSB is close to the annual mean. Two other conversions are often quoted. Both describe a
cohort with **no replenishment**, so they are upper bounds for the model:
- -ln(1 - u): the instantaneous rate if fishing were the only loss.
- Baranov, solved for F with M = the model's pool M (M1 0.58 + checked M2 0.312 = 0.89):
  u = F/(F+M) (1 - e^-(F+M)).

| Stock (year) | Fbar (ages) | Catch (t) | TSB (t) | u = C/TSB (computed) | -ln(1-u) | Baranov F, M=0.89 | Baranov F, M=0.58 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSAS her.27.3a47d (2025) | 0.191 (2-6 wr) | 428 593 | 2 956 478 | **0.145** | 0.157 | 0.242 | 0.210 |
| WBSS her.27.20-24 (2025) | 0.011 (2-5) | 2 315 | 266 183 | **0.0087** | 0.0087 | 0.013 | 0.012 |
| Sprat spr.27.3a4 (model yr 2025) | 1.51 (1-2) | 189 709 | 423 028 (1 Jul) | **0.448** | 0.595 | 0.999 | 0.840 |
| Mackerel mac.27.nea (2025) | 0.285 (4-8) | 802 265 | 3 728 611 | **0.215** | 0.242 | 0.380 | 0.327 |
| W. horse mackerel (2025) | 0.065 (1-10) | 61 434 | 1 596 010 | **0.038** | 0.039 | 0.060 | 0.052 |
| Sandeel SA3r / SA6 (2025) | 0 (1-2) | 0 / ~0 | - | **~0** | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Why u differs from Fbar:
1. Fbar averages F over the fully selected ages only. TSB also holds young age classes with low
   selectivity (NSAS F0-1/F2-6 ~0.31; sprat 0-group) and old fish, which lowers u.
2. TSB is a biomass at one moment (1 January, or 1 July for sprat), while catch accrues over the
   year as the fish grow. For short-lived, fast-growing species this pushes u up.
3. Fbar is instantaneous and u is a fraction, so they diverge more the larger F + M is (sprat).

### Recommendation
- **Central value: F ~0.10 yr-1 (~7e-5 per 6-h tick).** A (HERAS Skagerrak, 0.101) and B-mix (0.106)
  agree, although they get there differently: A is all herring, while B adds heavily fished sprat
  and mackerel but holds a large unfished sandeel share.
- **Range: 0.005-0.19 yr-1.**
  - The floor is the in-area rate. It applies only if the box is treated as closed, so that
    fishing outside it does not count.
  - The top is the stock-level rate with the North Sea 1991 shares, sandeel removed and all
    herring counted as NSAS.
  - Baranov-type conversions (up to ~0.35) apply only to a non-replenished cohort and overstate
    the model F.
- Two effects push the stock-level value below 0.10 for the Skagerrak:
  - The Skagerrak herring are juvenile-heavy, and juvenile NSAS F is ~0.3x Fbar.
  - The rising WBSS share (0.32 in 2025) carries an F close to zero.
- Two effects push it above 0.10:
  - Mackerel is present in summer (u 0.215, rising).
  - Some sprat lives in the Skagerrak and is fished in the North Sea (u 0.45).
- On the brief's assumption that the starting biomass stands for today under a restrictive fishery,
  0.10 with a sensitivity run at 0.05 and 0.19 covers the defensible span.
- The previous project estimate of "~0.20 pelagics" (resume section 141, subagent inference) is at
  the top of this range. It is consistent only with the North Sea-wide, sandeel-free, all-NSAS
  weighting.

## Side note: harbour porpoise bycatch (numbers only, not verified against primary text)

- Kindt-Larsen et al. 2023 (Proc. R. Soc. B 290: 20222570), Danish and Swedish gillnet fleets,
  North Sea to Western Baltic: "Between 2010 and 2020, yearly total bycatch averaged 2088 animals
  (95% CI: 667–6798)." For the Western Baltic assessment unit, bycatch was above sustainability
  thresholds. No Skagerrak-only figure was found. — [Kindt-Larsen et al. 2023, PMC10336385](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10336385/) (Snippet)
- Greater North Sea (which includes the Skagerrak): the OSPAR Marine Mammal Expert Group's 2020
  estimate of 5 974 porpoises (95 % CI 3 176-10 739) is "1.73% of an estimated North Sea population
  size of 345,000 from SCANS-III, July 2016", above the OSPAR threshold of 1 622 removals per year
  (~0.47 % of 345 000, computed). — [ASCOBANS NSG14 progress report](https://www.ascobans.org/sites/default/files/document/2026-06/ascobans_nsg14_progress-report-nsp.pdf); [OSPAR QSR 2023 marine mammal bycatch](https://oap.ospar.org/en/versions/2230-en-1-0-0-marine-mammal-bycatch/) (Snippet)
- Implication, not checked against the model: a porpoise M1 of 0.10 yr-1 that excludes bycatch
  would leave out a loss of about 0.017 yr-1 (North Sea-wide share). Whether the Skagerrak share is
  higher or lower is unknown.
