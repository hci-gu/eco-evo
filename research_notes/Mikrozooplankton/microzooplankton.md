# Microzooplankton (ciliates + heterotrophic dinoflagellates) for the Mareld site (Skagerrak/Kattegat, ~58.1 N 10.8 E, ~100 m, mixed layer 6-17 m, spring diatom bloom Feb-Mar, summer nitrate at detection limit, copepods 5-26 t WW/km2)

Scope: about 70 tool calls; items Q1-Q7 as requested.
Tags: **[F]** full text read by me; **[A]** abstract only; **[S]** snippet / WebFetch summary,
unverified; "[F] of X" = read X, not the original it cites. **[D]** = my calculation.
Model units: biomass in t WW/km2 (= g WW/m2) for a mixed-layer column, rates per day.
C:WW for protists = **0.1 g C/g WW** (task default), supported by the 0.11 pg C/um3 of NK94 and 0.071-0.11 in
Tiselius 1989 (wet density ~1); Putt & Stoecker's 0.19 is for Lugol-shrunk cells. Phytoplankton prey use
the sibling-note phyto C:WW 0.042 (`Litteraturbaserad growth rate per FG/plankton.md`).

**Pool definition.** Exclude the obligate autotroph *Mesodinium rubrum*: "the most abundant ciliate" in
the southern Kattegat, >50 % of integrated ciliate biomass Feb-Apr (1, 3). Ciliate numbers below exclude
it. Many oligotrichs and some "heterotrophic" dinoflagellates are mixotrophic (1, 6).

## Q1. Biomass by season

### Literature
- **Southern Kattegat 1989 (56 N 12 E, 28 m; source 1).** Mixo-heterotrophic ciliates: "seasonal peak
  in biomass, 19 ug C liter-1 (corresponding to 16% of the phytoplankton biomass), was measured in
  April, after which the biomass decreased to a few micrograms per liter during early summer"; "a
  second and less pronounced peak ... in early autumn". Up to 50 % of phytoplankton C in May.
  Integrated: "peaks during the spring phytoplankton bloom with ~400 mg C m-2"; 2-depth method
  "overestimates the biomass by an average of 50%". June 1988 daily sampling: "surface and
  pycnocline concentrations were below 3 and 1 ug C liter-1". *M. rubrum* alone 30-80 ug C/l in spring. [F]
- **Kattegat heterotrophic dinoflagellates 1989 (4).** HD >20 um abundant in "March/April,
  August/September and November with standing stocks of 46, 42 and 163 ug C l-1, respectively, at
  2.5 m"; mean HD = 15, 21 and 69 % of phytoplankton biomass in spring, late summer, autumn; HD <20
  um 0.5-3.7 ug C/l in May-July. HD/(HD+ciliates) "fluctuated between 7.5 and 99%". [F]
- **Kattegat synthesis (3).** Spring bloom: "ciliates in Kattegat obtained a mean biomass half that
  of the heterotrophic dinoflagellates"; after the bloom both "remained at prebloom biomass levels
  until late summer"; protozooplankton dominate total grazer biomass Jan-Apr, copepods ~75 % in
  May-July, protozoa again late summer-autumn. Values per month are in Fig. 3 only. [F]
- **Skagerrak spring bloom, March, 3.5-4.5 C (5).** Table 2, 0-20 m: ciliates 61-109 mg C m-2, HD
  41-171 mg C m-2 (HD:ciliate 0.72-1.54); peaks at the chl max "up to 17 and 40 mg C m-3"; copepods
  57-188 mg C m-2. [F]
- **Skagerrak late summer (6, thesis).** "Protozooplankton biomass (0-40 m) ... varied between
  459-1216 mg C m-2"; "Heterotrophic dinoflagellates dominated ... with 90+-4%" (incl. *Prorocentrum
  micans*, *Dinophysis*, partly phototrophic); "Ciliate biomass was low <5 ug C l-1 but heterotrophic
  dinoflagellate biomass was higher with up to 50 ug C l-1"; budget 970 mg C m-2 protozoa vs 440
  copepods. DCM held up to 95 % of column PP. [F of thesis]
- **Early summer coastal Kattegat/Skagerrak 1988 (7):** aloricate ciliates "0.06 to 2.9 ug C l-1
  equal to 0.4 to 2.9 % of phytoplankton carbon" (a *Chrysochromulina* bloom year). [F]
- **Dogger Bank, May (8):** ciliates >10 ug C/l on the Bank and in the subsurface peak, 1-5 ug C/l
  elsewhere. [F]

### Calculation [D] (column = concentration x MLD / 0.1; 1 mg C m-3 x 10 m = 0.1 t WW/km2)
| Situation | mg C m-3 | MLD 6 m | MLD 17 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skagerrak spring, ciliates+HD, 0-20 m mean (5) | 5.1-14 | 0.3-0.8 | 0.9-2.4 |
| Skagerrak late summer, 0-40 m mean, 90 % HD (6) | 11.5-30 | 0.7-1.8 | 2.0-5.2 |
| Kattegat April ciliate peak (1) | 19 | 1.1 | 3.2 |
| Kattegat summer ciliates (1, 6, 7) | 0.06-5 | 0.004-0.3 | 0.01-0.85 |
| Kattegat HD peaks, 2.5 m (4) | 42-163 | 2.5-9.8 | 7-28 |
Full-column equivalents: Maar 2002 0-20 m 1.0-2.8; Maar 2004 0-40 m 4.6-12 t WW/km2.

### Inference
- Pro-rating 0-20/0-40 m integrals to the mixed layer is an **upper bound** for the mixed layer:
  protozoa peak at the chl max/DCM below it (4, 5, 6).
- Microzooplankton C ~ copepod C here (spring 102-280 vs 57-188; late summer 970 vs 440 mg C m-2);
  the pool is **HD-dominated** (HD:ciliate ~1-10).
- Recommended mixed-layer column: **central ~1.5 t WW/km2**; winter ~0.3-0.5, spring bloom 1-3,
  early summer 0.3-1, late summer-autumn 1-5 (HD peaks up to ~10).

## Q2. Maximum growth rate, Q10, GGE

### Literature
- **Kattegat in situ (1):** growth "close to maximal growth rates obtained in laboratory cultures"; eq. (1)
  "u = 2.62 V^-0.243 exp(0.095 t)" (V um3, t C); temperature-corrected u10 = 8.32 V^-0.24 (smallest
  group omitted); Q10 "between 2.2 and 3.0 and averaged 2.6". [F]. Montagnes (11) quotes
  ln u = 0.095T - 0.243 lnV + 1.1569 (e^1.1569 = 3.18), and LN02 (3) cites an NK94 erratum (L&O
  39:1423, not retrieved). The u10 fit agrees with 3.18 (3.18 x e^0.95 = 8.2), so 2.62 is probably the misprint. Oct-Nov storm study:
  "instantaneous growth rates of ciliates (0.55-0.85 day-1)", "not food-limited" (9). [A]
- **Mueller & Geller (via 11):** ln umax = 1.52 lnT - 0.27 lnV - 1.44, "best predictor" for oligotrichs. [F of 11]
- **Rose & Caron (13):** upper-envelope fit, herbivorous protists "y = 0.10x - 1.0" (y = ln umax,
  x = T), ciliate-dominated; slopes 0.10 herbivores vs 0.06 phototrophs. [F]
- **Ciliates vs HD:** HD umax lower by "a factor of ~3" (10) [F]; at 13 C ciliates 0.77-1.01, HD
  0.41-0.48 d-1 (12) [A]. Hansen 1997 Table 5 HD: log umax = -0.47 - 0.26 log V. [F] (h-1 and 20 C inferred from Table 9 convention)
- **Q10:** compilation "overall average Q10 of 2.8" (n = 42, range 1.5-4.0) (10) [F]; Barents Sea
  field 1.64 at -1.3 to 4.5 C (17) [S].
- **GGE:** Straile (14): ciliates mean/median 30 %/30 %, dinoflagellates 26 %/26 %; "GGE hardly
  differed between taxa". [F]. Hansen 1997 yield at umax: ciliates 0.36-0.42, HD 0.10-0.40, average
  "0.33". [F]. Verity (16): tintinnids mean 33 %; 36-70 % at 10 C at optimal food. [F]. NK94 used 40 %. [F]

### Calculation [D] at 10 C (5 C)
- NK94 with intercept 3.18 (Montagnes/u10 form): V = 1e4 um3 (ESD 27 um) 0.88 (0.55); 1e5 (ESD 58 um)
  0.50 (0.31) d-1. Printed 2.62 gives 0.72 and 0.41; u10 fit gives 0.91 and 0.52.
- Mueller-Geller: 0.65 (1e4), 0.35 (1e5). Its power law in T (C) gives 0.12-0.23 at 5 C, an
  artefact of the fit (mostly >10 C); use the exponential forms below 10 C.
- Rose & Caron envelope: 1.00 d-1 (0.61). Strom & Morello to 10 C (Q10 2.7): ciliates 0.57-0.75,
  HD 0.30-0.36. Hansen HD regression, V = 1e4: 0.74 d-1 at 20 C -> 0.26 at 10 C.

### Inference
- Ciliates ~0.6-0.9 d-1, HD ~0.3-0.4 d-1 at 10 C. Because the pool is HD-dominated (Q1), a single
  pool's effective umax should be biomass-weighted: **central 0.5 d-1 at 10 C (range 0.3-0.9)**,
  ~0.3 d-1 at 5 C. **Q10 2.6 (1.5-3.0)**; **GGE 0.3 (0.2-0.4)** on carbon.

## Q3. Ingestion and functional response

### Literature
- **Hansen 1997 (10):** ciliate Imax "exceed those of dinoflagellates ... by a factor of ~2"; Table 9
  log-mean: ciliates log V 4.50, log Imax -0.693 (h-1, 20 C, body volumes); HD 3.88, -0.889; common
  exponent -0.23. Mean K for all groups "~2 ppm (vol/vol), corresponding to a prey density of ~240 ug
  C liter-1"; "ciliates have a Km that is lower as compared to the flagellates". Table 3 K (ppm):
  ciliates 0.04-1.63 (n = 14), HD 0.17-7.5 (n = 7). [F]
- **Montagnes (11):** threshold (growth = 0) "10 to 100 ng C ml-1"; growth rises to near max over "20
  to 300 ng C ml-1"; "cells only survive 1 to 3 d" below threshold. [F]
- **Jonsson (15):** ingestion "fitted to a hyperbolic function with no apparent feeding threshold". [A]
- **Verity (16):** ingestion "increased asymptotically with phytoplankton abundance and declined at
  high food concentrations". [F]
- **Field (20):** K from 96 dilution experiments "vary over three orders of magnitude", scale with Chl
  a, "smaller than estimates from laboratory cultures"; Type II, III and rectilinear "perform equally
  well" [A]. A pooled Type III fit gave K = 0.2 ug Chl/l [S].
- **ERSEM Z5 (25):** effective prey = suprey x P x P/(P + minfood), minfood 12 mg C m-3; uptake
  sum x F/(F + chuc), chuc 32 mg C m-3. [F, code]

### Calculation [D]
- Hansen Table 9 to body C per day (assumes equal C:volume of predator and prey): ciliates V = 1e4
  6.3 d-1 at 20 C -> 2.3 at 10 C (Q10 2.8) or 3.2 (Q10 2.0); V = 1e5 1.3-1.9. HD V = 1e4 1.0-1.5;
  1e5 0.6-0.9. PANGAEA data, per species: ciliate median 3.6 d-1 at 10-22 C (n = 14) -> 1.6 at 10 C
  (1.0-9.2); HD median 2.1 -> 0.9 (0.1-2.4).
- Consistency: umax/GGE = 0.5/0.3 = 1.7 d-1. K: ciliate median 0.63 ppm = ~75 ug C/l (5-195); HD 2.5 ppm = ~300 ug C/l (20-900) (120 ug C/l per
  ppm, from Hansen's 2 ppm = 240). ECOSMO 0.5 mmol N m-3 = 40 and NORWECOM 1.0 = 80 mg C m-3 (Redfield
  C:N 6.625). Field Type III 0.2 ug Chl/l x 50 (C:Chl, source 4) = 10 ug C/l.
- In model units (MLD 10 m, phyto C:WW 0.042): K 10 / 30 / 80 ug C/l = 2.4 / 7.1 / 19 t WW/km2 of
  phytoplankton. The model's zoo->phyto half-saturation of 20 t WW/km2 corresponds to ~84 ug C/l.

### Inference
- **Imax 1.7 d-1 at 10 C (range 1.0-3; Q10 2.0 vs 2.8 shifts it 40 %)**; ciliates higher, HD lower.
- **K ~30 ug C/l (10-80) of phytoplankton** for a mixed pool in the field; lab K are higher.
- **Type II ingestion** with a maintenance-driven growth threshold of ~10-100 ug C/l. Montagnes's
  threshold is a numerical-response (growth) threshold, not a Type III feeding threshold. A Type III
  term (or ERSEM's minfood) is a modelling choice for stability, not a measured protist trait.

## Q4. Share of primary production grazed by microzooplankton

### Literature
- **Global (18):** m:u "accounting for 67% of phytoplankton daily growth"; "60% for coastal and
  estuarine", "~59% for temperate-subpolar"; protist respiration "35-43% of daily PP". [A]
- **Kattegat ciliates (1):** "annual consumption of 143 g C m-2, equivalent to 49% of the annual
  primary production of 290 g C m-2" (yield 40 %); "estimated grazing may even exceed estimated
  primary production" in summer; annual ciliate production ~57 g C m-2. [F]
- **Kattegat ciliates + HD (3):** "20-60% of the annual primary production was consumed by ciliates"
  (complete algivory); Table 4 HD ingestion 57 % PP yr-1; HDs "as important grazers as ciliates". [F]
- **Skagerrak spring bloom (5):** copepods "< 3% of daily primary production"; protozooplankton
  "ingested 2 to 4 times more"; total zooplankton 17 %. Norwegian fjords spring: 12-26 % (Archer et
  al. 2000, via 5). [F]
- **Skagerrak late summer (6):** "potentially, consumed 43-166% of daily primary production"
  (published) [A]; pre-publication thesis manuscript 111 % (abstract), 147 % (synopsis) [F].
- **Southern North Sea spring (29):** microzooplankton grazed 15-54 % of daily PP. [F of sibling note]

### Calculation [D]
- Skagerrak spring: 2-4 x <3 % = **~6-12 % of daily PP** by protozoa.
- Kattegat annual (3, Table 4): same-method (clearance, 10^5 body volumes h-1) total = ciliates 20 % +
  HD 57 % = **~77 % of annual PP** (capacity, not realised grazing). The umax-based 60 % for ciliates
  has no HD counterpart, so the two methods cannot be added.

### Inference
- **Annual central 50-60 % of PP (range 30-70 %)**. Seasonally: **spring bloom ~10-25 %** (protists
  lag the bloom at 3-5 C, Q2); **summer/autumn 50-100 %+**, matching the "summer nitrate at detection
  limit" regime.

## Q5. Copepod feeding on microzooplankton

### Literature
- **Global (19):** ciliates average "30% of copepod daily carbon rations"; 49 % at <50, 25 % at
  50-500 and 22 % at >500 ug C/l phytoplankton; diet share "disproportionately high with respect to
  their relative abundance"; copepod grazing on ciliate stock "0 to 200% of the standing stock
  consumed per day". [F]
- **Kattegat (2):** at high egg production "ciliates contribute little (~20%)"; microzooplankton "may
  even dominate the diet during most of summer"; "between May and July estimated ciliate production
  (~170 mg C m-2 ...) exceeds estimated copepod carbon consumption (~100 mg C m-2)"; Acartia max
  clearance on ciliates "~30 ml ug C-1 d-1". [F]
- **Kattegat/Skagerrak early summer (7):** ciliate ingestion "always < 10 % and generally < 1 % of
  total carbon ingestion"; clearance 27.7-52.5 ml female-1 d-1 (A. clausi), 32-125 (C. hamatus); "no
  sign of size-independent preference for ciliates". [F]
- **Skagerrak late summer (6):** "microzooplankton potentially contributed 0-78% to the diet of
  copepods" (thesis) [F]; herbivory 14-85 % (published) [A].
- **Skagerrak spring (5):** Calanus protozoan ingestion "7 to 10% of the phytoplankton ingestion";
  copepods would eat "5 to 6% of daily ciliate and heterotrophic dinoflagellate production". [F]
- **Top-down (1, 3, 8):** copepod clearance "sufficient to check the community biomass of ciliates
  >=50 um throughout the year" (1); summer clearance "equaled or surpassed growth of the larger
  ciliates", HD predation exceeded growth May-July, small cells outgrow copepods (3); Dogger Bank May:
  copepod clearance 25-36 % of water column d-1 vs microzooplankton growth 25-54 % d-1 (8). [F]
- **Behaviour:** *Acartia* switches between suspension and ambush feeding; "clearing of ciliates was
  almost constant and independent of the availability ... (diatoms)"; ~100 ml female-1 d-1 at 20
  ciliates ml-1 (21) [F]. *A. clausi* "displayed a Holling type 3 functional response" on ciliates
  with algae present; stable coexistence in a model only with Type III (22). [A]

### Calculation [D]
- Dogger Bank clearance/growth per region: 36/25, 25/31, 30/54, 32/34 = **0.56-1.44**: copepods remove
  roughly all microzooplankton production in stratified May. Skagerrak spring: only 5-6 %.

### Inference
- **Copepod diet share: central 25-30 %**, <10 % during blooms (Kattegat, Skagerrak spring),
  ~50-80 % in stratified late summer.
- **Copepod predation as share of microzooplankton mortality: ~5 % in the spring bloom, ~60-100 %
  in summer.** No size-independent preference (7), but switching (21, 22) argues for a Type III or
  switching copepod response on this prey.

## Q6. Nitrogen regeneration

### Literature
- **Tintinnids (16):** respiration and NH4+ excretion "averaged 63 and 53% of ingested carbon and
  nitrogen" (line partly garbled in the scan); O:N 4-7; excretion "sufficient to support 11-18% of
  net primary production" (Narragansett Bay). [F]
- **Flagellates (23):** NH4+ regeneration efficiency "15 to 30 %" in exponential growth, "50 %" over
  the whole experiment, "8 %" with N-limited prey; up to "70 %" after prolonged stationary phase. [A]
- **Oligotrophic (24):** protozoa "may account for >50% of the nutrient recycling". [A]
- **Copepods for contrast (19):** "20% of ingested nitrogen is excreted as ammonia"; copepod
  excretion + unassimilated N "could only explain 14% of the nitrogen requirements" for PP. [F]
- **Regional:** Oslofjord 15N dilution: daytime regeneration 0-28 % of NH4 assimilation [S]; no
  Kattegat/Skagerrak microzooplankton-specific value found.

### Calculation [D]
- Stoichiometry: N-GGE = GGE_C x (C:N prey)/(C:N predator) = 0.3 x 6.6/5.0 = 0.40 (Calbet & Saiz
  weight ratios); NH4 excreted = AE_N - N-GGE = 0.75-0.9 - 0.40 = **0.35-0.50 of ingested N**. With
  N-poor summer prey (C:N 10): 0.15-0.30.
- Regional share: protozoan ingestion ~100-150 % of summer daily PP (6) x 0.35 = **~35-50 % of summer
  phytoplankton N demand**. Check: Verity's tintinnids ate 16-26 % of PP x 0.53 = 8-14 %, close to his 11-18 %.

### Inference
- **NH4 return 0.4 of ingested N (0.15-0.55)**, lower when prey are N-limited (summer surface).
  Microzooplankton excretion is the main regenerated-N source in summer (~1/3-1/2 of demand).
  Copepods supply much less.

## Q7. Microzooplankton mortality, respiration and grazing in regional models

### Literature
- **ERSEM 15.06 Z5 microzooplankton (25):** `sum: 1.25` d-1 at 10 C, `q10: 2.0`, `chuc: 32`,
  `minfood: 12` mg C m-3, `pu: 0.5` (assimilated), `pu_ea: 0.5` (of the unassimilated part,
  excreted; rest respired), `srs: 0.02` d-1 rest respiration, `sd: 0.05` d-1 basal mortality,
