# Autumn microzooplankton and the missing October bloom (Skagerrak/Kattegat/North Sea)

Question: is a model microzooplankton (MZ) pool of ~6-8 ug C/l in a 10 m mixed layer in Sep-Oct, grazing
~8 %/d of the phytoplankton, plausible, and why does the real system still get an October bloom (A17 0-10 m
chl 1.8-1.9 ug/l in October vs ~0.8 in September)? Companion to `microzooplankton.md` (same folder).
Tags: **[F]** full text/table/data read by me; **[A]** abstract only; **[S]** secondary (source named);
**[D]** my derivation. "MZ" = heterotrophic ciliates + heterotrophic dinoflagellates (HD), excluding the
autotrophic ciliate *Mesodinium/Myrionecta rubrum*. Model units: 1 t WW/km2 in 10 m = 10 ug C/l at C:WW 0.1.

## 1. Field MZ biomass by season, and MZ:phytoplankton in autumn

- **Southern Kattegat 1989, HD (Hansen 1991).** HD >20 um "especially abundant during 3 periods: March/April,
  August/September and November with standing stocks of 46, 42 and 163 ug C l-1, respectively, at 2.5 m depth
  and 54, 38 and 159 ug C l-1 at the pycnocline". HD mean biomass "15, 21 and 69 %" of phytoplankton in spring,
  late summer and autumn; 13-77 % during large-cell periods, once 134 %. Small HD (<20 um) 0.5-3.7 ug C/l in
  May-July. Phytoplankton C = chl x 50. [F]
- **Same station, Ceratium bloom 24 Aug-10 Sep 1989 (Nielsen 1991).** *Protoperidinium divergens, P.
  pellucidum*, Diplopsalis group "average biomass ... 15-20 ug C liter-1"; ciliate peaks: *M. rubrum* 6.3,
  naked oligotrichs 5.4, tintinnids 3.8 ug C/l. Ceratium up to 185 ug C/l (54 cells/ml). [F]
- **Kattegat ciliates (Nielsen & Kiorboe 1994).** "a distinct spring peak, a less pronounced early autumn
  peak, and lower biomass in summer and late autumn"; spring peak 19 ug C/l (16 % of phytoplankton) in April.
  Autumn values only in a figure. [F]
- **Helgoland Roads, 0.5 m, weekly 2007-2009 (Loder et al. 2011 data, PANGAEA).** HD 0.5-272 and ciliates
  0.2-106 ug C/l over the series; HD > ciliates "especially during the summer months" [F]. Monthly means of
  heterotrophic ciliates + HD, Noctiluca excluded, trophy as in their Table 1 [D from F data]:

  | ug C/l | Apr | May | Jun-Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2007 | 32 | 40 | 24-43 | 29 | 31 | 6 |
  | 2008 | 20 | 21 | 14-25 | 17 | 9.5 | 6 |
  | 2009 | 65 | 35 | 21 (Jun) | - | - | - |

  Ciliates/HD split Sep-Oct: 2007 12-13 / 16-19, 2008 4-10 / 5-7. Mixotrophic dinoflagellates (Ceratium,
  Lepidodinium, Prorocentrum...) were 80-120 ug C/l in Sep-Oct 2007 and 2-5 in 2008. [D]
- **Skagerrak late summer (Maar et al. 2004).** Protozooplankton 0-40 m 459-1216 mg C/m2 (= 11-30 ug C/l
  column mean), 90 % HD, ciliates <5 ug C/l. [S: `microzooplankton.md`, which read the thesis version [F]]
- **Inner Oslofjord, Sep-Oct (Olseng et al. 2002).** Diatom bloom (2-4 ug chl/l) in September, then a
  *Ceratium furca* bloom up to 128 ug chl/l in October; thecate HD biomass "at all times was <1% of that of
  the autotrophic phytoplankton"; *P. steinii* >2000 cells/l, seen feeding on *C. furca*. Only thecate HD
  counted. [A]

**[D] Ratio and level.** Field heterotrophic MZ in Sep-Oct is ~10-30 ug C/l (Helgoland, Skagerrak, Kattegat
HD + ciliates during the Ceratium bloom), with HD peaks of 40-160. Field MZ:phytoplankton in autumn ranges
from <1 % (thecate HD, Oslofjord bloom) through 21 % (Kattegat late summer) to 69 % (Kattegat autumn mean).
The model's 6-8 ug C/l is **at or below the low end of the field level**; its MZ:phyto ratio of ~0.6-0.8 is
**at the high end** of the field ratios. That is because the model's phytoplankton is low, not because its MZ
is high: A17 October 1.8-1.9 ug chl/l x 50 = 90-95 ug C/l, September 0.8 x 50 = 40 ug C/l, against the model's
~10 ug C/l. At C:Chl 50 and C:WW 0.1, 1 t WW/km2 in 10 m = 0.2 ug chl/l; check the model's own chl conversion.
If the model uses the phytoplankton C:WW 0.042 of `microzooplankton.md`, its phytoplankton is ~4 ug C/l and
the gap widens to ~10-23x [D].

## 2. Grazing rates and share of production, autumn vs spring/summer

- **Global (Calbet & Landry 2004):** m:mu "60% for coastal and estuarine", "~59% for temperate-subpolar". [A]
- **Helgoland 2012, dilution (Yang et al. 2021).** Spring (4 Apr-9 May, 6-9 C): g 0.23-0.25 d-1, 36-47 % of
  production. "Late summer/autumn" bloom: g 0.14-1.53 d-1, 24-103 % of production. Phytoplankton k 0.80-1.44
  (diatoms) and 1.10-1.86 d-1 (flagellates). Ciliate growth 0.73-0.94 d-1. **The "autumn" experiments are
  1, 15 and 22 August at 16-18 C**, a diatom bloom with Ceratium and Tiarina rising, not October. [F]
- **Kattegat Ceratium bloom (Nielsen 1991).** Ceratium growth 0.15-0.18 d-1. HD max ingestion ~9.1 ug C/l/d,
  "equivalent to 9 and 6% of the biomass and 50 and 33% of the production per day". Crustacean grazing 30-70 %
  of Ceratium production per day. Grazing by the ciliate *Tiarina fusus*: "0.3-0.5% and 1.8-3% of the
  biomass and production d-1". [F]
- **Kattegat annual (Nielsen & Kiorboe 1994; Levinsen & Nielsen 2002):** ciliates 49 % of annual PP; HD
  potentially 57 %. [S: `microzooplankton.md`, [F] there]
- **Southern North Sea, spring only (Stelfox-Widdicombe et al. 2004):** 15-54 % of daily PP [S:
  `microzooplankton.md`]. No Sep-Nov dilution experiment from the Skagerrak, Kattegat or Oslofjord was found.

**[D]** The model's 8 %/d removal is g ~ 0.08 d-1: below every field g above (0.14-1.53 in late summer) and
the same as HD grazing on the Kattegat Ceratium bloom (6-9 %/d), which still bloomed. A grazing loss of
0.08 d-1 can only stop a bloom when phytoplankton net growth (after M1 and other losses) is below ~0.1 d-1.
The model's October phytoplankton growth rate is therefore the number to check, not the MZ grazing rate.

## 3. What the autumn bloom is, and who can eat it

- **Kattegat succession (Nielsen & Kiorboe 1994; Nielsen 1991):** "an autumn bloom of large dinoflagellates
  (mainly Ceratium spp.) in August-September, and a mixed dinoflagellate-diatom bloom in November-December",
  all dominated by cells >11 um; the second bloom forms "during destratification", fed by nutrient-rich
  bottom water mixed upward. >50 um fraction = 57-62 % of chl during the Ceratium bloom. [F]
- **Swedish west coast, AlgAware (SMHI):** 9 Oct 2008, Slaggo (Skagerrak coast) "A diatom bloom ... many
  species in high cell numbers" (*Pseudo-nitzschia, Skeletonema, Leptocylindrus, Cylindrotheca*); A17 the
  same day low diversity, diatoms and dinoflagellates equal. Early Oct 2009: "probably the remains after an
  autumn bloom". Dec 2011: "The genus Ceratium that has been found in high amounts during autumn"; A17
  *Skeletonema marinoi* and *C. lineatum*. [F] So the autumn bloom is a **mixed chain-diatom +
  Tripos/Ceratium bloom**, varying between years [D]. Caveat: the bloom evidence is mostly coastal (Slaggo);
  at A17 itself the reports say Oct 2008 "low diversity", Oct 2013 cell numbers "very low" [F].
- **Size refuge from ciliates:** oligotrichs/tintinnids take prey up to ~45 % of oral diameter, so the
  largest ciliate (*Favella*, 80 um mouth) takes ~35 um at most; "this is why the ciliates do not respond to
  the spring bloom of colonial diatoms or to the late summer bloom of large dinoflagellates" (Hansen 1991);
  the only ciliate eating Ceratium (*Tiarina*) removed <=3 % of production (Nielsen 1991). [F]
- **No size refuge from HD:** pallium feeders (*Protoperidinium*, Diplopsalis) eat diatoms in spring and
  ">20 um dinoflagellates in late summer"; *Polykrikos schwartzii* "mixed microplankton ... in late autumn";
  predator:prey size ~1:1 for HD vs 1:10 for ciliates (Hansen 1991) [F]. *P. steinii* eats *C. furca* in
  the Oslofjord (Olseng et al. 2002) [A]. HD max growth 0.029-0.035 vs ciliates 0.17-0.23 h-1 at 20 C (Hansen
  1991) [F], i.e. 5-8x slower [D] (~3x in the Hansen et al. 1997 compilation [S: `microzooplankton.md`]), so
  they **lag** the bloom: the Kattegat HD peak (163 ug C/l) came in
  November, after the Aug-Sep Ceratium peak.
- **Copepods:** only *Centropages* (>=C4) and cladocerans grazed Ceratium in the Kattegat (Nielsen 1991) [F];
  *Calanus helgolandicus* ate *C. furca* and *Dinophysis* in the North Sea in August, *Acartia* could not
  (Jansen et al. 2006) [F]. Copepod clearance "sufficient to check the community biomass of ciliates >=50 um
  throughout the year" (Nielsen & Kiorboe 1994) [F], but Kattegat copepod biomass declined steadily "between
  early August and late October" (Kiorboe & Nielsen 1994) [F]. Hansen (1991) puts the November HD peak down
  to low copepod numbers. Copepod control of MZ weakens through the autumn.
- **Autumn ciliate growth is not low:** Kattegat ciliate growth followed temperature (Q10 2.6) and was
  "independent of various measures of food availability" (Nielsen & Kiorboe 1994) [F]; Oct-Nov 1988 growth
  0.55-0.85 d-1, not food-limited (Nielsen & Kiorboe 1991) [S: `microzooplankton.md`, [A] there].

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Biomass: plausible, at the low end.** Field heterotrophic MZ in Sep-Oct is ~10-30 ug C/l (HD-dominated);
   6-8 ug C/l is not too high. Lowering MZ biomass would move the model away from the data. The high MZ:phyto
   ratio comes from model phytoplankton that is 4-9x too low against A17 chl at C:Chl 50 [D].
2. **Rate: not excessive.** 8 %/d is below field dilution g and equal to HD grazing on a Kattegat Ceratium
   bloom that still developed. If this grazing alone stops the bloom, the model's October phytoplankton net
   growth is too low (light, nutrient supply at destratification, M1). Check that before changing MZ.
3. **Mechanism, ranked by evidence:**
   a. *Size refuge from ciliates + slow, lagged HD response* (strong: Hansen 1991, Nielsen 1991, NK94, Olseng
      2002). The autumn bloom formers (Tripos/Ceratium, chain diatoms) are out of ciliate reach. The HD that
      eat them grow 5-8x slower and peak after the bloom. In one well-mixed pool, the model lets the whole MZ
      stock graze the whole bloom at full pool Imax from the first day.
   b. *Nutrient-fuelled growth burst at destratification* (strong, bottom-up: NK94, Nielsen 1991): the autumn
      bloom needs phytoplankton growth well above 0.1 d-1 in Sep-Oct.
   c. *Copepod predation on ciliates* (moderate in Aug-Sep, weakening through Oct as copepods decline): it
      cannot explain a larger October bloom by itself.
   d. *Lower autumn ciliate growth* beyond temperature: **not supported**.
   How to represent (a), e.g. an edibility/preference factor for the large-cell part of the phytoplankton or
   slower HD-like kinetics in Sep-Nov, is a modelling choice. The literature gives no single parameter for it.
