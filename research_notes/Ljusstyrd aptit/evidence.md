# Light-cued appetite: evidence trail (2026-11-12)

Tags as in summary.md. "[F via tool]" = full text read through a web-fetch extraction, not by me line by line.

## E1. Metcalfe & Thorpe 1992, J Anim Ecol 61:175, doi:10.2307/5520 [A, OpenAlex abstract]

- "an index of appetite dropped by an average of 96% between early September and late December" (lab, excess
  food). The abstract does not say whether temperature and photoperiod were ambient or controlled.
- "This depletion was experimentally accelerated by depriving fish of food for 3 weeks. The fish responded by
  increasing their appetite; appetite was negatively correlated with the estimated energy reserves".
- "made up their lost fat within 4 weeks of resuming feeding; their appetite then decreased to the level of
  control fish".
- "The degree and duration of anorexia ... is not preprogrammed but is regulated by energy reserves, the salmon
  effectively having a 'defended energy level' below which appetite is increased".

## E2. Bull 1996, PhD thesis, Univ. Glasgow (theses.gla.ac.uk/38968) [F, local `papers/bull1996_thesis/thesis.txt`]

Ch. 3.4 (salmon parr < 70 mm; controls with "excess food (dispensed every 10 min by automatic feeder)", ambient
temperature, simulated natural photoperiod):
- "body lipid levels consequently dropped from 5.32 ± 0.42% of body weight (n = 18) in September to 2.63 ± 0.40%
  (n = 18) in April (figure 3.13), despite food always being in excess."
- Deprivation losses: "1.12 ± 0.22% ... 1.00 ± 0.33% ... and 1.94 ± 0.31%" (early, mid, late winter).
- "Following deprivation in Early Winter, experimental fish exhibited a marked elevation in appetite relative to
  controls over the first 14 days of refeeding. However no such effect was apparent after periods of
  deprivation in Mid- or Late Winter ... despite their body lipids being lower by this time."
- "fat levels ... increasing during the refeeding period in Early Winter (mean increase of 0.49 ± 0.23% ...) but
  not following Mid- and Late Winter deprivations (mean reduction 0.05 ± 0.39% ...; 0.37 ± 0.34%)".
- "the fish facultatively responded not to their current reserve level, but to their projection of whether they
  would be above or below the target level at the end o[f] the winter".
- "the intake by control fish appears much lower than that predicted from existing published data relating food
  intake by salmonids to temperature" (so not a capacity limit).
- p. 39: "This natural anorexia commences in early autumn independent of water temperature or food availability
  (Metcalfe et al, 1986)".

Ch. 3.5 (photoperiod):
- Design: "food was provided every twenty minutes ... ad lib. rations 24 hours a day to every group ... the same
  seasonally changing water temperatures ... but differed only in perceived daylength".
- Table 3.3 schedule: accelerated 7.75 h (27 Oct, "13 Dec") to 16.75 h (14 Feb, "27 May"); delayed held at ~10-14
  h ("Aug-Nov").
- After the November deprivation: restored "1.02% ± 0.13, 1.27% ± 0.11, and 1.91% ± 0.13 for accelerated, delayed
  and control groups".
- After the January deprivation: "both the delayed and control groups responded by increasing body fat ...
  whereas the accelerated group did not restore losses".
- "photoperiod can act to synchronise the timing of the appropriate restoration of energy deficits, but proved
  inconclusive in terms of the feeding intensity".
- Arithmetic: `tables/light_appetite_numbers_stdout.txt` section 3.

## E3. Sæther, Johnsen & Jobling 1996, J Fish Biol 48:1113, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1996.tb01808.x [A, OpenAlex]

- "held at constant temperature (4° C) under either simulated natural photoperiod (nLD) or constant photoperiod
  (12:12 LD) ... Food was supplied in excess during a 5-h period each day, and daily consumption was determined
  using X-radiography."
- "Food consumption and growth of both immature and maturing fish showed distinct seasonal cycles irrespective of
  photoperiod treatment. Feed intake and growth rates were highest in mid-summer and lowest in autumn."
- "circannual rhythms of food consumption and growth exist in the absence of seasonal changes in these
  environmental cues."
- "Reductions in feeding and growth in autumn occurred about 1 month earlier in the maturing males".
- %BW/d values: not obtained (closed).

## E4. Tveiten, Johnsen & Jobling 1996, J Fish Biol 48:910, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1996.tb01486.x [A, OpenAlex]

- "constant temperature (4°C) under conditions of liberal feed supply. Feed intake and growth remained low in the
  period December-April, with only 20-60% of the fish having fed on each occasion".
- "The fish appeared to reduce feeding once a 'threshold condition' of 1.4-1.5 had been attained. ... the fish
  may become anorexic once there has been replenishment of the energy reserves required for overwintering".
- The abstract does not state the photoperiod.

## E5. Nordgarden et al. 2003, Aquac Nutr 9:287, doi:10.1046/j.1365-2095.2003.00256.x [A, OpenAlex; PDF blocked]

- "simulated natural photoperiod (SNP) for 12 months or continuous light (LL) from January to June followed by
  SNP ... Feed was given to excess and uneaten feed pellets were collected after every meal".
- "SGR, condition factor and feed intake displayed strong seasonal variation in both groups ... the seasonal
  variation in SGR was independent of temperature and fish size. Continuous light treatment resulted in
  increased growth from spring".
- Temperature regime and intake values: not obtained.

## E6. Finstad et al. 2010, Proc R Soc B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2009.1874 (PMC2842767) [F via tool]

- Stream channels at 2.8 C, daylength ~6 -> 9 h.
- "a positive scaling of feeding activity with decreasing energy levels of northern populations and no
  relationship for the more southern populations".
- Proposed rule: "increase energy intake when approaching critical storage energy levels" (not tested).

## E7. Striberny et al. 2015, PLoS ONE (PMC4589418) [F via tool]

- Charr, simulated natural light at 69 N, AMBIENT temperature, "fed in excess".
- Stomach contents (% body mass): "0.0 ± 0.0 in May, 3.5 ± 0.4 in July, and 0.0 ± 0.0 in January".
- Not a constant-temperature result.
- Intro: offspring held "at constant temperature ... fast voluntarily for several months during winter even
  though feed is available".

## E8. Berg, Seljestad & Folkvord 2024, MEPS 741:203, doi:10.3354/meps14521 [F, `../Krillparen mot vintermagar/papers/berg2024_meps741/paper.txt`; supplement `papers/berg2024_supp/`]

- Design: "temperature (7°C or 10°C) and light (natural or offset)"; "The offset light refers to light cycle out
  of phase by 6 mo"; "During mid-summer, the light intensity was approximately 10 times higher compared to
  mid-winter".
- Feeding: "herring were weaned onto dry feed (AgloNorse 600-900 μm; 58-60 % protein, 17-20 % fat). The size of
  the dry feed was increased over the experimental period and was fed in excess based on previously observed
  growth rates."
- Feeding hours: no statement in the paper or supplement (grep for feed/fed/ration/hour/automat/meal/satiat).
- Condition: "Residuals of the following linear length-weight model were used as proxy for individual somatic
  condition: ln(Weight) = a + b1 ln(SL) + b2 Temp + b3 Light"; "Only post-metamorphic fish (SL >55 mm)".
- "The residuals ... clearly followed a seasonal pattern induced by different light regimes ... Under the natural
  light regime, the lowest somatic condition was observed during winter, whereas it was observed during spring
  for herring reared under 7°C and offset light conditions. For herring reared under offset light conditions and
  10°C, the season with lowest somatic conditions was not conclusive".
- Maturity: "For adult fish, sex and maturity stage were determined"; no maturity term in the condition model.
- Stunting: "a stunting effect occurred in fish from all tanks after the first year"; final SL ~180-190 mm (Fig.
  S4).
- Fig. 6 read-off (GAM lines, +-0.005): 10 nat -0.050..+0.030; 7 nat -0.035..0.000; 7 off -0.030..+0.045; 10 off
  -0.040..+0.005. Fig. S5: individuals span about -0.3..+0.3 [D, `tables/` section 1].

## E9. dos Santos Schmidt et al. 2022, JEMBE 552:151737, doi:10.1016/j.jembe.2022.151737 [F, `papers/dossantosschmidt2022/`]

- Sister experiment: hatched 2016, ended October 2019, Matre.
- "all individuals experienced the same temperature, from 8.5 to 9.3 °C (mean ± sd: 8.9 ± 0.2 °C)".
- "Herring were fed ad libitum throughout the entire experiment and the amount of food was adjusted to the size
  of the fish."
- "we could not find any differences between the two groups in terms of body length, weight or condition. Here it
  should be noted that all individuals were fed ad libitum and received the same amount of food independent of
  day length ... individuals gathered relatively high body reserves throughout the year, even during the winter
  period."
- "the Offset Group showed a displacement in vitellogenesis of 6 months".
- Table 1 Kn and GSI by sample and experienced month are reproduced in `scripts/light_appetite_numbers.py`;
  n-weighted Kn winter 0.976 (n 21), spring 1.022 (86), summer 0.944 (10), autumn 1.001 (60) [D].
- Natural GSI: February 1.92 (F) / 1.05 (M), May 2.72 / 3.63.

## E10. Varpe & Fiksen 2010, Ecology, doi:10.1890/08-1817.1 [A]

- "a mechanistic model of ingestion rate, including both solar irradiance and prey abundance, predicted seasonal
  food intake in good accordance with observed herring body condition. This suggests that the seasonality in
  herring foraging and energy storage is closely linked to the return of longer days in spring".
