# Seal and porpoise floors - evidence (2026-11-11)

Sources for summary.md. Grades: [F] field, [L] lab, [A] abstract or search snippet only, [S]
secondary (review, press, or an earlier project note), [D] derived here, [J] judgement.
PDFs read in `papers/` (Unpaywall): E2, E7b (Hoekendijk), E14, E15. E3 and E6 were read as full

## A. What "rest" is for each mammal, and what fish can sense of it

**E1. Harbour seal haul-out near the box** [S, project note
research_notes/"Parametervarden for salar i Skagerrak"/distribution_and_impacts.md (a-umlaut)].
- 10-30 % of time hauled out outside the moult (Cunningham et al. 2009: daily means 11-27 %, bouts
  ~5 h); Norwegian Skagerrak Aug-Nov ~17 % (Riaz et al. 2026, product of means); ~57 % ashore at the
  late-August moult (Harkonen & Harding 2001); breeding and moult peaks June-August.
- The box has no land; the nearest haul-outs are 5-30 km outside the east edge. That note already
  concluded that Rest in an offshore cell is at-sea rest, not haul-out, and that the start density
  assumes 75-90 % of the population at sea; the seasonal onshore absence is not represented.

**E2. Vance et al. 2021, Sci. Rep. 11:6331, doi 10.1038/s41598-021-85376-2** [F, PDF read].
German Wadden Sea; 8 harbour seals, 12 offshore trips; DTAG accelerometer and magnetometer.
- "Tagged seals rested for a median of 205.8 min/day, both at the surface and during distinctive low
  activity dives" (= 14.3 % of the day at sea [D]); resting rose by 36.9 min per trip day.
- Rest was identified as "extended intervals at the surface or sequences of dives with slow descents
  and exceptionally low stroking rates at the bottom"; resting dives were 7.5 % (SD 3.6) of dives.
- "harbour seals forage almost continuously throughout the trip"; dive shape "consistent with
  continuous benthic foraging". Seabed 10-20 m.
- Citing Brasseur et al.: seals prevented from hauling out later hauled out more, "suggesting that
  resting in the water is not equivalent to hauling out".
- Uses 1.4 m/s as the typical forward speed of harbour seals.

**E3. Mikkelsen et al. 2019, Ecol. Evol. 9:2588-2601, doi 10.1002/ece3.4923** [F, full text via PMC
PMC6405890]. North Sea DTAGs, 3 harbour and 2 grey seals, up to 21 d.
- Hauled out 6.6-42.3 % of the time (harbour seals).
- Resting on the sea bottom 8.1-12.4 % of at-sea time (harbour seals), mean resting-dive submersion
  4.0-5.3 min; resting "down to 35 m"; a seal "lying still or rocking in the current on the bottom"
  (camera); surface resting also seen, not quantified.
- [D] E3 bottom rest over E2 total rest: 0.57-0.87 of at-sea rest is on the bed in shallow North Sea
  water (two studies, two areas; a range, not a measurement).

**E4. Bjorge et al. 1995 (Froan, central Norway)** [A, via research_notes/"Salars jakt i ljus och
morker"/diel_foraging.md]. 13 seals "typically foraging at or close to the sea bed", foraging sites
from kelp to 150-200 m muddy basins; mean dive 3.3 min.

**E5. Wright et al. 2017, Anim. Behav. 133:211-222, doi 10.1016/j.anbehav.2017.09.015** [A].
Seven tagged harbour porpoises, Danish waters, 53-72 h each, 1884-2755 dives per animal. "Parabolic"
dives: shallow, low-energy, stereotyped, nearly half with no echolocation clicks - proposed as sleep.
Press summaries say up to 18 % of underwater time [S, not in the abstract seen].

**E6. Rojano-Donate et al. 2024, Sci. Adv. 10:eadj7132, doi 10.1126/sciadv.adj7132** [F, full text
via PMC PMC11318689]. 20 tagged porpoises, Kattegat and Belts (mean depth 23 m / 13 m).
- Foraging dives 62 % of diving time: "37% in pelagic-feeding mode and 25% engaged in bottom feeding";
  the rest (~38 %) non-feeding (travel, nursing, sleep).
- Mean maximum depth of non-feeding dives ~4.4 m, pelagic feeding 8.2 m, bottom feeding 11.6 m (day);
  95 % of dives < 25 m. Feeding dives 65 % nocturnal.

**E7. Wisniewska et al. 2016, Curr. Biol. 26:1441-1446, doi 10.1016/j.cub.2016.03.069** [A].
Five porpoises; forage "nearly continuously day and night", up to 550 prey (3-10 cm) per hour,
capture success > 90 %.
**E7b. Hoekendijk et al. 2018, Mar. Mamm. Sci. 34:258-264, doi 10.1111/mms.12446** [read]: the five
were four juveniles and one adult, tagged 15-23 h after 24 h in a pound net; "may offer a biased and
extreme view"; prey encounters 0-200 /h by day and 50-550 /h at night.

**E8. Schack, Malte & Madsen 2008, J. Exp. Biol. 211:2079-2086, doi 10.1242/jeb.015081** [A].
Unconditioned Atlantic cod: no heart-rate change at 210 dB re 1 uPa pp; no flight from ultrasonic
clicks and echosounder pulses above 204 dB; no swimming impairment above 213 dB. Conclusion: biosonar
clicks "probably play no ecophysiological role in wild cod populations".

**E9. Astrup & Mohl 1993, J. Exp. Biol. 182:71-80** [A]. Conditioned cod (n 15) detected 38 kHz
pulses at a mean 194.4 dB re 1 uPa (SD 5.4); later reviews cite 203-204 dB pp; possibly cutaneous.

**E10. Kastelein et al. 2008, Mar. Environ. Res. 65:369-377, doi 10.1016/j.marenvres.2008.01.001**
[A]. Startle thresholds to tones 0.1-64 kHz in eight North Sea species: no 50 % threshold for cod and
pollack; pout (Trisopterus luscus) 0.1-0.25 kHz; horse mackerel 0.1-2 kHz; Atlantic herring 4 kHz.

**E11. Villadsgaard, Wahlberg & Tougaard 2007, J. Exp. Biol. 210:56-64** [A]; Miller & Wahlberg 2013,
Front. Physiol. 4:52 [S]. Wild porpoise clicks: source level 178-205 dB re 1 uPa pp @ 1 m (mean 191),
centred 130-140 kHz, duration ~44-113 us (57-175 us at -10 dB in another definition).

**E12. Mann, Popper & Wilson 2005, Biol. Lett. 1:158-161, doi 10.1098/rsbl.2004.0241** [A]. Pacific
herring did not detect ultrasonic tones up to 185 dB re 1 uPa; hearing 100-5000 Hz; earlier responses
to simulated echolocation clicks (Wilson & Dill 2002) are explained by lower-frequency energy.

**E13. Foote 1980, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 67:2084-2089, doi 10.1121/1.384452** [A]. Swimbladder =
90-95 % of dorsal backscatter in gadoids (29-42 cm, 38 and 120 kHz), by comparison with mackerel,
which has none.

**E17. Doksaeter et al. 2009, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 125:554-564, doi 10.1121/1.3021301** [A].
Overwintering herring (Vestfjorden): no escape from 1-2 or 6-7 kHz sonar at 127-209 dB; killer whale
FEEDING sounds (low-frequency) "induced vertical and horizontal movements".

**E18. Remage-Healey, Nowacek & Bass 2006, J. Exp. Biol. 209:4444-4451** [A]. Wild Gulf toadfish
cut calling by ~50 % and raised cortisol after playback of low-frequency bottlenose dolphin foraging
"pops"; dolphin whistles (high frequency) had no effect.

**E19. Morisaka & Connor 2007, J. Evol. Biol. 20:1439-1458** [A]. Narrow-band high-frequency clicks
(porpoises among them) as acoustic crypsis against killer whales, which hear poorly < 2 and > 100 kHz.

**E20. Van Parijs et al. (Moray Firth); Van Parijs & Kovacs 2002, Can. J. Zool. doi
10.1139/z02-088** [A]. Harbour seals are among the least vocal pinnipeds; underwater roars
(78-1300 Hz) are made by males in a short mating season (Moray Firth 1 July - 12 August).

**E21. Grey seal - cod telemetry** [A/S]. Lidgard et al., MEPS 501:157-168: in 4 years 9 of 64 seals
with receivers detected 17 tagged adult cod, timing suggesting 2 eaten; encounters brief. A DFO
analysis (Harvey & Hammill, via search snippet) reports higher densities of several fish including cod
where grey seals did not forage much - a distribution pattern, not a measured avoidance.

**E24. Smell and predator avoidance** [S, project note research_notes/"Lukt och
rovdjursundvikande"]. Predator cues: cm-m ranges, minutes-hours persistence, downstream filaments; no
marine fish km-scale predator-odour avoidance found; odour does not vanish when the emitter rests.

## B. What a hiding fish is to a seal or a porpoise

**E15. Bowen et al. 2002, Mar. Ecol. Prog. Ser. 244:235-245, doi 10.3354/meps244235** [F, PDF read].
Sable Island; 39 adult male harbour seals with Crittercam; 1094 capture attempts.
- Flounders (American plaice, yellowtail; 15-30 cm) were "usually (14 of 15 cases) hidden ... within
  the sandy bottom and only became visible after the seal had attempted a capture"; "In several cases,
  the flounder was not seen until the male quickly drove his muzzle into the sand".
- Capture success (% of attempts, per video unit, mean +- SE): flounder 79.9 +- 10.6, cryptic (buried)
  sand lance 63.7 +- 5.2, schooling sand lance 37.5 +- 6.5 (schooling lower, p < 0.05).
- Capture attempts per video unit: cryptic sand lance 5.5, flounder 1.9, schooling 9.2 [D: captures
  per unit ~3.5 buried vs ~3.45 schooling sand lance].
- Schooling: seals split the school; isolated fish swam to the bottom and "sat motionless"; the seal
  then pursued them. Depth 30-37 m.
- [J] Burial gives no refuge against harbour seals; schooling gives some (success ~0.6 of buried).

**E16. Harbour seal vibrissae** [L, via research_notes/"Salars jakt i ljus och morker"/seal_senses.md
and prey_refuge.md]. Blindfolded seals follow hydrodynamic trails up to 30-35 s old (Dehnhardt et al.
2001; Wieskotten et al. 2010); locate artificial flatfish breathing currents of 3.7-4.2 cm/s
(Niesterok et al. 2017a,b; n 2-3). No seal light dependence (resume 167.2).

**E22. Skagerrak seal diets and models** [S, project notes as E16]. Norwegian Skagerrak 2020 scats:
sandeel 39.6 % of otoliths, gadids 50.3 % (Kappa et al. 2026, via summary); flatfish 46 % of diet
energy in a northern Skagerrak thesis; haddock, pollack and saithe 32.7 %, plaice 12.4 % (Sorlie et
al. 2020); Baltic EwE gives grey seal fish prey "no refuge" (Costalago et al. 2018). Basis of the 0.85
floor of resume 167.3.

**E14. Schneider von Deimling et al. 2023, Commun. Earth Environ. 4:478,
doi 10.1038/s43247-023-01102-y** [F/D, PDF read]. German Bight, 20-40 m: > 40 000 seabed pits, mean
depth 0.11 m; the authors "conclude that harbor porpoises excavate sediments during benthic foraging.
By grubbing the seabed, they cause sandeels to escape from the sediment". An inference from pit
morphology, habitat and porpoise distribution, not a direct observation.

**E23. Porpoises in the box** [S/D, project note research_notes/"Tumlarens byten"]. Skagerrak maximum
dives 132-152 m (Teilmann 2007, van Beest 2018); prey < 30 cm, limit ~40 cm; water-column part
inside the window (< 35 cm: saithe 0.70, hake 0.30; < 40 cm: saithe 0.73, hake 0.27;
tables/wc_window_stdout.txt). Resume 149.3: no study tests seabed masking of porpoise sonar;
porpoises aim clicks downward when feeding on bottom fish.

**E25. Group composition and windows** [D, project]. Benthivores (research_notes/"Fiskgrupper i
rutan"/tables/long_run_species_by_group.csv): haddock 38.6 %, cod 31.4 %, long rough dab 12.6, dab
4.5, plaice 3.8, witch 1.7, lemon sole 0.9, other flatfish 0.4, rays and others ~6.5. Water column:
saithe 0.797, spurdog 0.097, hake 0.048, anglerfish 0.027, ling, velvet belly, pollack. Mammal prey
windows: harbour seal 7-35 cm, porpoise 0-40 cm (research_notes/"Arsungarnas andel" (A-ring),
parameters_B.md); length-biomass by 1-cm class from its tables/stage_length_biomass.csv.

## Derived here (scripts/mammal_floors.py, stdout in scripts/mammal_floors_stdout.txt)

- D1. Box beds: min 44 m, median 143 m (east third 130 m); no cell <= 35 m; 23 % <= 100 m.
- D2. Seal bed rest: D_max = v (T - 1 min)/2 = 54-181 m for T 4.0-5.3 min (E3), v 0.6-1.4 m/s;
  cells where it is feasible: 4-87 % of the east third. Observed bed rest only to ~35 m (E3).
- D3. A surface cue spreads 0.7-21 m vertically in a 6 h tick (Kz 1e-5 to 1e-2 m2/s): it does not
  reach a 100-300 m bed.
- D4. A porpoise click reaches cod's conditioned threshold only within 1-3 m on axis at the maximum
  wild source level, never at the mean (and that threshold is at 38 kHz, the click at 130 kHz).
- D5. Porpoise sonar bed dead zone c tau/2 = 3-13 cm, plus 1-5 cm of beam footprint at 1-5 m range:
  a gadoid on the bed is above it in the final approach, a buried
  flatfish inside it.
- D6. Inside the seal window, benthivores are 0.57 gadoids / 0.43 flatfish; inside the porpoise
  window 0.64 / 0.36 (vs 0.70 / 0.24 of the whole group).

## Added after review

**E26. Deecke, Slater & Ford 2002, Nature 420:171-173** [A]. Harbour seals reacted to playbacks of
mammal-eating killer whale calls and habituated to fish-eating killer whale calls: the SEAL is the
listener. Relevance to fish sensing seals: none.

**E27. Wilson et al. 2011, J. Exp. Biol. (allis shad, Alosa alosa)** [A]. Directional escape from
ultrasonic clicks mimicking an approaching toothed whale - in an ultrasound-detecting Alosinae, not a
model species (herring, sprat, pout and the gadoids do not detect ultrasound, E8-E12). Relevance: low.

**Breathing** [J, gap]. Porpoise and seal blows are in air; their underwater sound is low-level and
no study was found of fish detecting a surfacing or breathing marine mammal.
