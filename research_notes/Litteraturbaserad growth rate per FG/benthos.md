# Benthic macrofauna community: P/B, logistic growth rate r and biomass (K) - literature values for a 6 h tick model of Skagerrak/Kattegat

Conversion convention used throughout: model tick = 6 h, 1460 ticks/yr.
- Per-tick rate = per-year rate / 1460. This is the correct conversion for (a) continuous-time logistic intrinsic rates r (as fitted by Hiddink et al. 2017/2019) and (b) P/B ratios (annual production flux divided by mean biomass, i.e. a mean specific production rate).
- ln(1+x) would only apply if a value were an annual *discrete* growth factor (B_next_year = (1+x) B). None of the sources below report that, so ln(1+x) would only give a lower-bound sensitivity check. Example: P/B = 1.3 /yr -> 1.3/1460 = 8.9e-4 /tick (correct), whereas ln(2.3)/1460 = 5.7e-4 /tick; for r = 0.82 -> 5.6e-4 vs 4.1e-4; for r = 1.46 (current) -> 1.0e-3 vs 6.2e-4. The difference grows with x and is large for meiofauna (P/B 10.8: 7.4e-3 vs 1.7e-3).
- 1 t/km2 = 1 g/m2 (exactly).
- Current model: r = 0.001 /tick = 1.46 /yr, K = 10 t/km2.

## Q1. Community-level macrobenthos P/B in Kattegat, Skagerrak and the North Sea

### Takeaway
The only fully verified, community-resolved North Sea numbers retrieved are the Mackinson & Daskalov (2007) Ecopath tables: biomass-weighted P/B is ~1.26-1.3 /yr for infaunal macrobenthos, ~0.9 for small infauna (polychaetes), ~0.39 for mobile epifauna, ~0.26 for sessile epifauna, ~1.4-1.9 for small mobile epifauna (swarming crustaceans) and ~11-35 for meiofauna; the biomass-weighted whole-macrobenthos P/B is ~0.8 /yr (data) and ~0.77 /yr (balanced model, excl. meiofauna). No Kattegat- or Skagerrak-specific community P/B value could be retrieved and verified.

## Q2. Typical macrofauna biomass in Skagerrak/Kattegat and the North Sea (for K) and conversion factors

### Takeaway
Literature standing stocks are an order of magnitude or more above the model's K = 10 t/km2: North Sea macrofauna ~7 g AFDW m-2 (~45-120 g WW m-2 depending on conversion), Kattegat soft bottoms ~70-150 g WW m-2 (search snippet, unverified), Baltic Ecopath macrozoobenthos 27 t/km2, North Sea Ecopath total benthos ~500-925 t/km2 WW (including heavy echinoderms and shelled bivalves).

### Cited Findings
- Heip et al. (1992), ICES North Sea Benthos Survey (spring 1986): average total macrofauna biomass over all stations is 7 g AFDW m-2, decreasing from south to north. — [search summary of Heip et al. 1992, ICES J. Mar. Sci.](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/221010.pdf)
- Mackinson & Daskalov (2007) restate Heip et al. (1992): infaunal macrobenthos ~7 g AFDW m-2 (SE = 7.6); with a WW:AFDW conversion of 15:1 (Christensen 1995) this is 105 g WW m-2; sampling in April (seasonal biomass minimum) likely underestimates; it is twice Rachor's (1982) 3.2 g AFDW m-2. — [Mackinson & Daskalov 2007](https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf)
- Mackinson & Daskalov (2007) Ecopath biomasses (t km-2, wet weight, North Sea mean): infaunal macrobenthos 274.8, small infauna 256.0, epifaunal macrobenthos 158.0, sessile epifauna 210.6, small mobile epifauna 23.9, meiofauna 1.8 (data, total ~925); balanced model 136 + 150 + 78 + 105 + 30 + 4.1 (~503). — [Mackinson & Daskalov 2007](https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf)
- Duineveld et al. (1991), as cited in Mackinson & Daskalov (2007): potential food demand of North Sea benthos averages 50 g AFDW m-2 or 20 g C m-2 yr-1, and 40, 10 and 3 g C m-2 yr-1 for the southern, central and northern North Sea (this is a food demand/production figure, not standing stock). — [Mackinson & Daskalov 2007](https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf)
- Kattegat: mean macrofauna biomass of 146 vs 71 g wet weight m-2 in different study areas; densities on silt-mud bottoms can exceed 1000 ind. m-2; total biomass highly variable and not a sensitive indicator of trawling (UNVERIFIED: from a search-engine summary that appears to refer to Sköld et al. 2018; the full text could not be opened to confirm which areas the two numbers refer to). — [search result: Sköld et al. 2018, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321363181_Effects_of_chronic_bottom_trawling_on_soft_seafloor_macrofauna_in_the_Kattegat)
- Sköld et al. (2018), Kattegat (verified abstract): the soft-bottom community over a trawling gradient (including an MPA closed to trawling) was dominated by burrowing brittle stars; Amphiura chiajei increased in abundance from low to medium trawling intensity, interpreted as release from predation by benthivorous flatfish and Nephrops; abundance of dominant brittle stars fell after the MPA was enforced. — [Sköld et al. 2018, MEPS 586:41-55 (abstract via OpenAlex)](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps12434)
- Baltic Sea Ecopath (Tomczak et al. 2012, balanced for 1974): macrozoobenthos B = 27.30 t km-2, CV = 0.95, uncertainty range 0-79.17 (2.55-35.86) t km-2; highest CV of all groups because of scarce, spatially variable data. The macrozoobenthos P/B used could not be retrieved. — [search summary of Tomczak et al. 2012, Ambio, PMC3428477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428477/)
- Josefson, Jensen & Aertebjerg (1993) and related work report a general increase in macrofaunal biomass and density in the Skagerrak-Kattegat during the 1970s-80s, attributed to increased supply of degradable organic matter (no numbers retrieved). — [search summary; Josefson et al. 1993 JEMBE 172:31-45](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0981(93)90087-5)
- Conversion factors, Ricciardi & Bourget (1998), 28 taxonomic groups: mean AFDW as % of WW = 16% for polychaetes, 7.5% for prosobranch gastropods, 5.8% for bivalves (whole wet weight incl. shell), 16% for amphipods, 16.5% for decapods. — [Ricciardi & Bourget 1998, MEPS 163:245-251](https://www.int-res.com/abstracts/meps/v163/meps163245)
- Generic Ecopath conversion used by Mackinson & Daskalov (2007) for plankton: 0.1 g C = 0.2 g dry weight = 1 g wet weight; for benthos they use WW:AFDW = 15:1 (Christensen 1995). — [Mackinson & Daskalov 2007](https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf)

### Inferences
- 7 g AFDW m-2 converts to ~44 g WW m-2 (polychaete-like, 16% AFDW/WW), ~105 g WW m-2 (15:1) or ~120 g WW m-2 (bivalve-like, 5.8%). So a North Sea mean macrofauna stock is roughly 45-120 t/km2 WW; Kattegat 70-150 t/km2 WW (unverified); Baltic 27 t/km2.
- The model's K = 10 t/km2 is 3-15x below these standing stocks (and 50-90x below the Mackinson & Daskalov totals). K = 10 t/km2 is defensible only if "benthic_community" represents the fish-available prey fraction (small infauna/epifauna, excluding large echinoderms and shelled bivalves) or a dry/AFDW-like currency; otherwise K ~ 50-150 t/km2 WW would match the literature. Note that equilibrium predation load scales with K, so K and r should be chosen together against gadoid consumption.

### Gaps
- No verified Kattegat/Skagerrak biomass table (g WW m-2 by depth) was retrieved; Swedish national monitoring (SMHI/HaV "bottenfauna") and Danish NOVANA data would give these but were not accessed.
- Whether Mackinson & Daskalov biomasses include shell weight was not stated in the retrieved text.

## Q3. Maximum community growth / recovery rates after disturbance (direct estimates of logistic r)

### Takeaway
The best direct estimate of a logistic r for whole-community macrofauna biomass is Hiddink et al. (2017): r = 0.82 /yr (5-95% interval 0.42-1.53) at no trawling, rising to 1.73 /yr (0.89-3.23) at 10 trawl passes/yr as the community shifts to faster taxa. Hiddink et al. (2019) give r = 5.31/longevity (yr) for taxa of known longevity. In per-tick terms: 5.6e-4 (range 2.9e-4 to 1.05e-3), i.e. the current 1.0e-3 /tick sits near the upper 95% bound for untrawled communities.

### Cited Findings
- Hiddink et al. (2017) PNAS 114:8301-8306: logistic growth (identical to the Schaefer model) fitted to recovery of benthic invertebrate communities; equilibrium B/K = 1 - F d / r (F = trawling frequency /yr, d = depletion per pass). Mean community biomass r increased with trawling frequency "from 0.82 y-1 when there was no trawling (5-95% uncertainty intervals = 0.42-1.53) to 1.73 (0.89-3.23) y-1 when the trawling frequency was 10 y-1". — [Hiddink et al. 2017, PNAS (NOAA repository PDF)](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/24380/noaa_24380_DS1.pdf)
- Same paper: with r = 0.82 /yr, recovery from D = 0.5K to 0.95K takes 3.6 yr (5-95%: 1.9-6.4 yr). Estimates of r for community *numbers* (abundance) range from 0.18 /yr (towed dredge on 45% gravel) to 4.47 /yr (beam trawl on 0% gravel), giving recovery times 0.7-16.6 yr. — [Hiddink et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/24380/noaa_24380_DS1.pdf)
- Same paper: "Recovery in numbers is driven more strongly by recruitment than recovery of biomass, which is driven by increases in the size and age structure of the population through growth of individuals. We recommend the use of recovery rates for community biomass when modeling trawl impacts and their consequences." Biomass recovery times are similar to empirical recovery after trawling stopped in three areas (4-5 yr) but longer than small-scale experiments (25-500 d); recovery is faster close to unimpacted areas. Studies are mostly temperate, so estimates "are likely applicable to trawled shelf seas in general". — [Hiddink et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/24380/noaa_24380_DS1.pdf)
- Same paper: depletion per pass d = 0.06 (otter trawl), 0.14 (beam trawl), 0.20 (towed dredge), 0.41 (hydraulic dredge); each unit increase in swept-area ratio reduced community biomass by 15.5% on average. — [Hiddink et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/24380/noaa_24380_DS1.pdf)
- Hiddink et al. (2019) J. Appl. Ecol. 56:1075-1084: intrinsic rate of population increase r is linearly related to the reciprocal of longevity, matching theory; biota living >10 yr show a 2-3x larger trawling effect than biota living 1-3 yr. — [Hiddink et al. 2019 (abstract)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2664.13278)
- The fitted relationship is r = H/longevity with H = 5.31 (from a search-engine summary of Hiddink et al. 2019; not checked against the full text). — [Hiddink et al. 2019, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327978337_Assessing_bottom_trawling_impacts_based_on_the_longevity_of_benthic_invertebrates)
- Pitcher et al. (2017) Methods Ecol. Evol. 8:472-480: relative benthic status RBS from the equilibrium of the logistic growth equation, with depletion and recovery rates taken from meta-analyses; experimental-scale d and r must be adjusted to grid scale (D, R). The specific r values used could not be retrieved. — [Pitcher et al. 2017 (abstract via Semantic Scholar)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.12705)
- Rijnsdorp et al. (2018), 401 box-core/grab stations in the English Channel and southern North Sea: the biomass share of long-lived taxa rises with gravel and falls with mud content and shear stress; trawling (gears penetrating >2 cm) shifts the community toward shorter-lived taxa; longevity composition "can also be used to estimate the recovery rates of the benthos". — [Rijnsdorp et al. 2018, Ecol. Appl. (Europe PMC full text)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6055734)
- Kattegat hypoxia (Sept 1988): at moderately affected stations species composition and abundance recovered almost completely within two years; at the most severely affected site recolonisation was incomplete two years later. Generally after oxygen deficiency ends, species richness and density rise rapidly (months to a year) but biomass comparable to undisturbed areas, and large individuals, appear only after several years (search-engine summary; primary source not opened). — [search summary; Rosenberg, Bonsdorff & Karlson review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228505943_Temporal_and_spatial_large-scale_effects_of_eutrophication_and_oxygen_deficiency_on_benthic_fauna_in_Scandinavian_and_Baltic_waters-a_review)
- Sköld et al. (2025), Kattegat (verified abstract): macrofauna followed for 12 years in a 426 km2 no-take zone in the south Kattegat after demersal trawling stopped, compared with a nearby trawled area. Burrowing brittle stars Amphiura filiformis and A. chiajei dominated abundance and biomass and *decreased* in the closed area (abundance effect size A. filiformis 48%, A. chiajei 45%) while benthivorous flatfish increased; brittle stars were the staple food in flatfish stomachs. "Food web effects thus appear to override the decrease in mortality of the dominant macrofauna caused by the cessation of trawling." No biomass recovery rate is given in the abstract. — [Sköld et al. 2025, Conservation Science and Practice (abstract via OpenAlex)](https://doi.org/10.1111/csp2.70037)
- In Gullmar Fjord (Skagerrak coast) the macrofauna disappeared after oxygen deficiency and was not re-established until 1.5 years later (Josefson & Rosenberg 1988, as summarised by search engine; unverified). — [same search summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231032810_Ecosystem_impacts_of_hypoxia_Thresholds_of_hypoxia_and_pathways_to_recovery)

### Inferences
- Per-tick conversion (r/1460) of Hiddink et al. 2017 biomass r: 0.42 -> 2.9e-4; 0.82 -> 5.6e-4; 1.53 -> 1.05e-3; 1.73 -> 1.18e-3; 3.23 -> 2.2e-3.
- From r = 5.31/L (computed by me): L = 1 yr -> 5.3 /yr (3.6e-3 /tick); 2 yr -> 2.65 (1.8e-3); 3 yr -> 1.77 (1.2e-3); 5 yr -> 1.06 (7.3e-4); 10 yr -> 0.53 (3.6e-4); 20 yr -> 0.27 (1.8e-4). The community value r = 0.82 corresponds to an effective biomass-weighted longevity of ~6.5 yr.
- Consistency check with the logistic model: a hypoxia/defaunation recovery where biomass takes "several years" to come back, and Hiddink's 3.6 yr from 0.5K to 0.95K, are both consistent with r ~ 0.8 /yr. With r = 1.46 /yr (current), the same recovery would take ~2 yr (3.6 x 0.82/1.46).
- The Kattegat NTZ result (Sköld et al. 2025) shows that fish predation can hold the dominant Kattegat macrofauna well below the level reached under trawling, i.e. the community is top-down regulated by benthivores there. This supports modelling fish predation explicitly and keeping r gross of it, and it implies that the observed Kattegat biomass is a predation-limited B*, below K.
- Hiddink's r is fitted to field biomass that is already subject to natural predation (including fish), so it is a *net* intrinsic rate. A logistic r that must be gross of explicit fish predation should be at or above it; the current 1.46 /yr is within the range 0.82-1.73 /yr and inside the 5-95% interval of Hiddink's untrawled estimate (0.42-1.53).

### Gaps
- Pitcher et al. (2017) numeric r values not retrieved.
- Lambert et al. (2014) "Quantifying recovery rates and resilience of seabed habitats" (cited in Hiddink 2017) not retrieved.
- No Kattegat-specific fitted logistic r was found; the Kattegat MPA/NTZ studies (Sköld et al. 2018, 2025) show faunal change after closure, but no biomass recovery rate was extracted (2025 full text not accessed; it may contain biomass time series).

## Q4. Benthos P/B in Ecopath models (North Sea, Kattegat, Skagerrak, Baltic), split by infauna/epifauna/meiofauna

### Takeaway
North Sea Ecopath (Mackinson & Daskalov 2007) is fully retrieved (see Q1): infauna 1.0-1.3, small infauna 0.9, mobile epifauna 0.39, sessile epifauna 0.26, small mobile epifauna 1.4-1.9, meiofauna 10.8-35 /yr. A Kattegat EwE model exists (ICES WGINOSE 2020, 29 biota groups, Ecopath year 1982, Ecosim 1982-2008), but its benthos parameters were not retrievable; the Baltic model's macrozoobenthos biomass is 27.3 t/km2 with P/B not retrieved.

### Cited Findings

Gross efficiency P/Q assumed 0.15 for macrobenthos groups (0.2-0.35 in the balanced model), Q/B estimated by the model: infaunal macrobenthos Q/B = 3.33, small infauna 3, epifaunal macrobenthos 1.94, sessile epifauna 1.3, small mobile epifauna 5.43, meiofauna 125. — [Mackinson & Daskalov 2007](https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf)
- Kattegat EwE model: 39 nodes, 257 links, 29 biota groups; Ecopath base year 1982, Ecosim calibrated 1982-2008; described in ICES WGINOSE (2020) report (ICES Sci. Rep. 2:68). — [search summary citing ICES 2023 ICES J. Mar. Sci. 80:218](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/80/1/218/6965515)
- Baltic Sea (Tomczak et al. 2012): 22 functional groups, Ecopath balanced for 1974; macrozoobenthos B = 27.30 t km-2 (5-yr median 1974-1978). — [search summary of Tomczak et al. 2012, Ambio, PMC3428477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428477/)

### Inferences
- An Ecopath-style split would be: fish-available "benthic_community" ~ infauna + small infauna + small mobile epifauna with biomass-weighted P/B ~1.0-1.1 /yr (computed from Table 11.9: (347 + 231.5 + 32.6) / (274.8 + 256 + 23.9) = 1.10 /yr; balanced model (136 + 135 + 57) / (136 + 150 + 30) = 1.04 /yr). Per tick ~7.1e-4 to 7.5e-4. Including slow epifauna drops it to ~0.8 /yr.
- The low EE of infaunal macrobenthos (0.28) and sessile epifauna (0.04) in the North Sea model means most of their production is not eaten by modelled predators, i.e. fish predation removes only a fraction of P; that fraction is higher for small infauna (EE 0.91) and small mobile epifauna (0.92).

## Q5. Does P/B include recruitment (gross production), and how does it relate to logistic r?

### Takeaway
Empirical P/B (Brey, Tumbiolo & Downing) estimates annual somatic production per mean biomass, i.e. all tissue growth including growth of recruits after they enter the sampled population, but normally excluding gamete output; at steady state it equals the total mortality (eaten + other deaths). In a logistic model, observed P/B corresponds to r(1 - B*/K) at the observed biomass B*, so r >= P/B, and r ~ 2 x P/B if B* ~ K/2.

### Cited Findings
- Brey's virtual handbook (empirical models): P/B models rest on the negative exponential relation of metabolic rate to body mass; Tumbiolo & Downing (1994) model for marine benthos: log P [g DM m-2 y-1] = 0.24 + 0.96 log B [g DM m-2] - 0.21 log Mmax [mg DM] + 0.03 T [deg C] - 0.16 log(D+1) [m] (N = 125, r2 = 0.86). Brey et al. (1996)/Brey (1999) unified models estimate P/B from body mass, taxon, mode of living, temperature and depth. The page is titled "Somatic Production". — [Brey, Virtual Handbook: Empirical models for estimating production and P/B](http://www.thomas-brey.de/science/virtualhandbook/somatic/soempmod.html)
- Tumbiolo & Downing (1994): production rises with biomass ~B^0.96 and falls with maximum body mass; temperature Q10 of secondary production is 2-2.5; depth effect strongly negative; enriched regions are more productive than oligotrophic waters. — [Tumbiolo & Downing 1994, MEPS 114:165-174](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps114165)
- Hiddink et al. (2017) distinguish recovery of numbers (driven by recruitment) from recovery of biomass (driven by individual growth and size/age structure) and recommend the biomass r for modelling. — [Hiddink et al. 2017](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/24380/noaa_24380_DS1.pdf)
- Brey's model is reported to perform best among empirical P/B models (Cusson & Bourget 2005; Dolbeth et al. 2005), but is known to fail for communities dominated by large bivalves. — [search summary; Estuar. Coast. Shelf Sci. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272771422000956)

### Inferences
- In this model, dB/dt = r B (1 - B/K) - predation. At a fish-predation-limited equilibrium B*, specific production r(1 - B*/K) equals the specific predation rate. Literature P/B (~0.8-1.3 /yr community; ~1.1 /yr for the fish-available fraction) is measured on predated field populations near steady state, so it estimates r(1 - B*/K) including losses to all predators and non-predation mortality.
- Hence: (a) setting r = P/B (~1.0-1.3 /yr, 6.8e-4 to 8.9e-4 /tick) is a lower bound and only allows B* near 0 under the same predation; (b) if the community sits near K/2, r ~ 2 x P/B ~ 2.0-2.6 /yr (1.4e-3 to 1.8e-3 /tick); (c) Hiddink's net logistic r (0.82, upper 95% 1.53 /yr) is fitted to biomass recovery and already includes background predation, so a gross-of-fish r somewhat above 0.82 is justified. A defensible range is r = 1.0-2.6 /yr = 7e-4 to 1.8e-3 /tick; the current 1.0e-3 /tick (1.46 /yr) is inside it, at the low-mid end, and close to the North Sea infauna P/B (1.26-1.3).
- The non-predation part of mortality (P/B minus EE x P/B) is not represented in a logistic term unless K absorbs it; with North Sea EE values of 0.28-0.91 a sizeable share of production is not consumed by modelled predators.
- The meiofauna P/B (10.8-35 /yr) should not be mixed into the macrofauna r unless meiofauna is part of the group; including it would raise r far above the macrofauna values.
- Allen's (1971) steady-state result P/B = Z (total mortality) is the standard justification for treating P/B as a mortality/turnover rate; it was not retrieved here and is stated from general knowledge.
