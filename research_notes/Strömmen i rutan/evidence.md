# Evidence - near-surface currents in the Mareld box (eastern Skagerrak)

Each item: citation, link, what kind of evidence it is, and the exact quoted
sentence(s) or table values used in `summary.md`. Tags: **[M]** measured,
**[Mod]** numerical model, **[S]** secondary (a source quoting another).
Quotes are copied from the PDF text layer (pdftotext) or, where that was garbled,
read off the rendered page; page numbers are the journal's.

The box: 60 x 60 km, SWEREF 99 TM E 211000-271000, N 6414000-6474000
(NW 58.3116 N 10.0651 E, SE 57.8093 N 11.1450 E).

## E1. Rodhe (1987) - repeated current sections across the Skagerrak [M]

Rodhe, J. 1987. The large-scale circulation in the Skagerrak; interpretation of
some observations. Tellus 39A: 245-253.
doi:[10.3402/tellusa.v39i3.11757](https://doi.org/10.3402/tellusa.v39i3.11757)
(open PDF at tellusjournal.org).

Method: gelatine pendulum current meters, 10-15 per profile, section 1
(Kristiansand-Hanstholm, ~110 km, west of the box) crossed 22 times and
section 2 (off the Swedish coast, ~80 km; from Rodhe's Fig. 1 it starts on the
Swedish coast north-east of the box, i.e. the measured section nearest the box,
position read off a small map) crossed 9 times, 1975-1977. The top meter was
"less than 10 m" (shallow stations) to "20 m or more" (deep stations) below the
surface (p. 246) - so the surface layer is under-sampled at the deep stations.

- Abstract (p. 245): "The mean currents show a cyclonic circulation. Apart from a
  narrow baroclinic coastal current, the mean velocities generally increase
  towards the bottom. ... In the upper layers, the direction of the circulation
  occasionally changes, whereas in the deeper layers, the cyclonic circulation is
  present in all the observations. The advective residence time for the water
  down to 400-500 m in the Skagerrak is some 100 days."
- p. 245: "The tidal currents are very weak, of the order of 1 cm/s, in this area."
- p. 245: "The main part of the low saline water follows the Swedish coast in the
  Baltic Current and the Norwegian coast in the Norwegian Coastal Current. Outside
  this current, there is a cyclonic circulation (on the average) with North Sea
  and Atlantic water entering along the Danish coast and leaving along the
  Norwegian coast."
- p. 248 (mean velocities; these are the DEEP maxima near the bottom, not surface
  speeds): "The maximum velocity in the deep part of the circulation gets smaller
  following the flow around the Skagerrak. It is 15-20 cm/s at the Danish slope of
  the Trench, 10-15 cm/s outside the Swedish coast and 5-10 cm/s where the water
  leaves the Skagerrak along the Norwegian coast."
- p. 248: "The velocities outside the Swedish coast are highest away from the coast
  where this coastal current adds to the basic circulation of high saline water.
  ... Close to the Swedish coast, there is, at the bottom, a south-going counter
  current".
- Fig. 3b caption (p. 248, section 2): "the north-going basic current with local
  maximum in the velocity at the bottom and further, the high velocity in the
  surface in the outer part of the Baltic current". Mean surface contours on that
  panel reach 20-25 cm/s (read off the figure).
- p. 248-249: "The circulation in the upper part can change to an anticyclonic
  direction (see Fig. 4d)."
- p. 249 (surface variability): "The observations in the surface layer reflect the
  Ekman velocity forced by the transient wind. The magnitude of the transient
  Ekman velocity is about 20 cm/s and the depth of the Ekman layer is some
  10-20 m." and "The magnitude of the transient Ekman transport can then be
  estimated to 2-4 m2/s."
- p. 251: at station 6 of section 1 (100 m depth) the along-topography velocity
  standard deviation is "u = 17 cm/s" and the spin-up "duration time ... is then a
  day, which is of the same order as the typical duration time of the wind in
  this area."
- Fig. 2d (section 1, standard deviation of the in-out velocity, read off the
  figure): 15-20 cm/s in the upper ~20 m, about 20 cm/s at the surface on the
  Danish side, 5-10 cm/s in the deep interior.
- Table 1c (p. 251), section 2, transport per unit width, positive northward,
  9 observations, stations 1-7 from the Swedish coast outward:
  mean 4, 8, 16, 18, 8, 8, -6 m2/s; standard deviation 3, 7, 10, 14, 12, 12, 13 m2/s.
- p. 252: "The integrated transport is the same at the 3 places, ~5.10^5 m3/s. This
  figure is the mean value of only 9 observations and is probably not valid as a
  true mean value"; Furnes (1980) "report a mean northward flow of ~10.10^5 m3/s"
  for 1.5 months in spring 1976 [S].

## E2. Danielssen et al. (1997) - SKAGEX I, May-June 1990 [M]

Danielssen, D.S., Edler, L., Fonselius, S., Hernroth, L., Ostrowski, M.,
Svendsen, E., Talpsepp, L. 1997. Oceanographic variability in the Skagerrak and
Northern Kattegat, May-June, 1990. ICES Journal of Marine Science 54: 753-773.
doi:[10.1006/jmsc.1996.0210](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmsc.1996.0210) (open).

Moored current meters on section G (Kristiansand-Hanstholm, WEST of the box),
ship ADCP, Argos drifters; 24 May-20 June 1990 only.

- Abstract (p. 753): "the main outflow from the Skagerrak, represented by the
  Norwegian Coastal Current, was barotropic with daily mean velocities varying
  from 10-40 cm s-1. During the second half a clear baroclinic current component
  developed, giving rise to near surface velocities of up to 100 cm s-1. A
  pronounced feature in the Skagerrak during the study was the counter-clockwise
  circulation of the Norwegian Coastal Current at times of strong northwesterly
  winds. During such conditions this surface water reached as far as the Danish
  coast south of 57 N ... During northerly winds upwelling also occurred along the
  Swedish coast." (The 10-40 and 100 cm/s are the NCC "10 nmi from the Norwegian
  coast on section G", Fig. 6 caption, p. 759.)
- p. 754: "during weak local wind situations the surface waters mainly follow the
  general circulation (Fig. 1). Unpublished modelling results indicate, however, a
  clear tendency for the anticyclonic circulation of the surface water in the
  eastern Skagerrak in contrast to the deeper cyclonic circulation." [Mod,
  unpublished, as cited]
- p. 755: "The drifting buoys, showing speeds up to 1 knot on the Danish side and
  sometimes up to 2 knots on the Norwegian side, confirmed the general pattern as
  well. In the area north of 57 N the tidal currents are negligible".
- p. 755: "a total volume transport into and out of the Skagerrak in the upper
  100 m of about 1 (+-0.5) Sverdrup ... during the first half of SKAGEX I."
- p. 755-756: "an imbalance between in- and outflows of the order of 0.5 SV ...
  can last for about a day at maximum before an unrealistic sea-surface height
  will occur. This means that the total volume transport variability in one
  direction must rapidly be compensated by a counterflow."
- p. 757: the Jutland Coastal Water "never reached section F" (a section in the
  eastern Skagerrak); its intrusions were set by the wind: a north-westerly gale
  "forcing the NCW to flow across to Denmark", and after "strong southerly wind on
  3-4 June ... the JCW again appears at transect H (5 June ...), progressing to
  section G by 8 June".
- p. 764-765: "explanation for the 'twin peaks' feature may be that there is an
  anticyclonic surface circulation on top of the general cyclonic circulation.
  This has been found recently in unpublished modelling experiments and needs
  further investigation."

## E3. Fonselius (1990) - SMHI Oceanography 38, review [S], incl. drift cards [M]

Fonselius, S.H. 1990. Skagerrak - the gateway to the North Sea. SMHI
Oceanography No. 38, Norrkoping.
[PDF](https://www.smhi.se/download/18.18f5a56618fc9f08e832a49d/1717801532204/Oceanografi_38%20Skagerrak%20-%20the%20gateway%20to%20the%20North%20Sea..pdf)

- p. 9-10: "The total transported amount is thus 15 000 km3/year influx from the
  North Sea plus 2500 km3/year influx from the Kattegat, of which about 2000
  km3/year return, making the total outflow of 15 500 km3/year. The turnover time
  for the Skagerrak's water has been calculated as being a few weeks in areas of
  strong currents and somewhat longer in coastal areas or in the water at a lower
  depth than 270 m in the Norwegian Trench (Grimas and Svansson 1985). Rodhe
  (1987) gives about 100 days as being the extent of the Skagerrak water's
  turnover time." (15 000 km3/yr = 0.48 Sv.)
- p. 9: "A surface current of about 2500 km3/year of Baltic (brackish) water from
  the Kattegat is thus formed. This current, called the Baltic current, continues
  northwards along the Swedish coast until it joins the Norwegian Coastal current".
- p. 10: "As in most sea areas the surface currents of the Skagerrak are heavily
  dependent on the wind, though there is a permanent circulation where the centre
  is formed by the Baltic and Jutland currents. Because of these the Skagerrak
  water circulates in an anti-clockwise direction."
- Fig. 6 (p. 11), drift cards (Engstrom 1967) released in a line across the
  Skagerrak: "Recovered 11-21 days later" [M, Lagrangian, surface].
- p. 12: "Normally the Baltic current follows the Swedish coast both in the
  Kattegat and the Skagerrak ... The Baltic current can also vary in strength and
  can occasionally spread itself out over nearly all the surface of the Skagerrak.
  The current is strongest some nautical miles from land and off the coast of
  Norway it can have a westward speed of more than three knots."
- p. 12: "The Baltic current's volume of water is largest during Summer and lowest
  during Winter."
- p. 12: the Jutland current "can have a very high speed and it is not unusual to
  measure speeds of between 2 to 3 knots. The Jutland current can change course
  depending on the strength and direction of the wind from the Skaw. It can force
  it's way deep into the Kattegat, steer straight for the Swedish coast or veer to
  the north east towards the Vaderoarna." (A north-eastward path from Skagen to
  Vaderoarna crosses the box.)
- p. 12: "This upwelling has been estimated to be in the region of 8000 km3/year
  (Grimas et Svansson 1985)."
- p. 13: "Tidal currents are weak, about 1-2 cm/s, except in narrow sounds."

## E6. Christensen, Sperrevik & Brostrom (2018) - reanalysis with HF radar [Mod]

Christensen, K.H., Sperrevik, A.K., Brostrom, G. 2018. On the variability in the
onset of the Norwegian Coastal Current. Journal of Physical Oceanography 48:
723-738. doi:[10.1175/JPO-D-17-0117.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/JPO-D-17-0117.1)
(paywalled; abstract via Crossref only).

- Abstract: "A high-resolution reanalysis of the circulation in the Kattegat and
  Skagerrak is used ... use surface current observations from two coastal
  high-frequency radars that were ideally placed to monitor the exchange between
  the two basins. This study finds a strong correlation between the variability in
  the wind forcing in the Skagerrak and the transport in the Norwegian Coastal
  Current through the Torungen-Hirtshals section. ... For winds out of the
  Skagerrak, strong outflows of Baltic Sea Water associated with a deflection of
  the Kattegat-Skagerrak Front may disrupt local processes in the Skagerrak".

## E7. Falkenhaug et al. (2022) - Calanus at Arendal Station 2 [M]

Falkenhaug, T., Broms, C., Bagoien, E., Nikolioudakis, N. 2022. Temporal
variability of co-occurring Calanus finmarchicus and C. helgolandicus in
Skagerrak. Frontiers in Marine Science 9: 779335.
doi:[10.3389/fmars.2022.779335](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2022.779335)

- Abstract: "The results suggest that the proximity to the Norwegian Trench
  influences the demography and abundance of C. finmarchicus in this coastal area,
  both as a gateway for the advective supply, as well as a habitat for local
  overwintering."
- Discussion: "We suggest that the proximity to the overwintering site in the
  Norwegian Trench enables the early spring invasion of G0 in February to this
  coastal area, and further that the observed spring cohort G1 of C. finmarchicus
  is the offspring of the overwintering stock originating from the Norwegian
  Trench."
- Discussion: "Moreover, Skagerrak is an advective system and some of the observed
  CV and adults may have been advected from the northern North Sea, or from nearby
  fjords."
- Discussion (C. helgolandicus): "The July peak in CI-CIV may represent a single
  cohort, indicating local production of C. helgolandicus in the area. ... Yet, the
  large overlap in timing of the copepodite stages and adults strongly suggests
  that the population is also subject to advective transport from other areas, and
  there is probably considerable generational 'smearing' from both local and
  various up-stream locations."
- Discussion: "Young copepodites of C. finmarchicus have been shown to be
  transported into the North Sea from the north in surface layers during spring
  (Heath et al., 1999; Madden et al., 1999) while diapausing individuals enter the
  area during winter via the deep Norwegian Trench Atlantic Inflow (Gao et al.,
  2021)." [S]

Also see the existing note `research_notes/Den pelagiska poolens reglering/
zooplankton_consumption.md`, section 4 (Gao et al. 2021: inflow from the north
"constitutes on average 41% of the annual mean biomass in the North Sea", the

## E8. Kiorboe et al. (1990) - food web along a Skagerrak transect, May 1987 [M]

Kiorboe, T., Kaas, H., Kruse, B., Mohlenberg, F., Tiselius, P., Ertebjerg, G.
1990. The structure of the pelagic food web in relation to water column structure
in the Skagerrak. Marine Ecology Progress Series 59: 19-32.
doi:[10.3354/meps059019](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps059019)

- Discussion: "while specific growth rates depend on the currently prevailing chemical,
  physical and biological conditions, absolute production rates also depend on the
  biomass of the considered organisms themselves that, in turn, may be the result
  of biotic and/or physical processes in the past. This is perhaps particularly
  relevant for copepods, since they have long generation times in comparison to
  their algal food, and advection, predation etc. may, therefore, obscure potential
  copepod-phytoplankton distributional relationships".
- Results: subsurface lenses at 30-60 m "may be interpreted as water horizontally
  advected from elsewhere, presumably from the surface of the North Sea", and the
  low-salinity surface water at the last station "is the Norwegian Coastal Current
  ... promoted by the onset of northeastrely winds (> 10 m s-1) from 20 May."

## E10. NorKyst-800 operational ocean model, MET Norway - our extraction [Mod]

Dataset: NorKyst-800m (ROMS, 800 m grid, hourly output on z-levels), daily
analysis files `NorKyst-800m_ZDEPTHS_his.an.YYYYMMDD00.nc`,
https://thredds.met.no/thredds/catalog/fou-hi/norkyst800m-1h/catalog.html
(OPeNDAP, no login; licence "CC-BY-4.0"; creator "Norwegian Meteorological
Institute (MET Norway)"; global attributes: title "NorKyst-800m - ROMS", ROMS
3.7, tides on (SSH_TIDES, UV_TIDES), radiation-nudging open boundaries, bulk
atmospheric fluxes). Variables used: `u_eastward`, `v_northward` (rotated to
east/north at rho points), `Uwind`, `Vwind` (the 10 m wind forcing in the same
files). Depth levels used: 0, 10, 25, 50 m.

What was extracted (scripts/fetch_norkyst.py, fetch_wind.py, fetch_point.py):
- 7 points, hourly, 0/10/25/50 m (Y row, X col in the NorKyst grid; distance from
  the box's W and N edges): NW (97,326) 7 km/7 km, h 448 m; N (65,344) 36/10 km,
  h 232 m; NE (43,358) 57/10 km, h 101 m; C (58,318) 29/30 km, h 165 m;
  SW (70,279) 4/50 km, h 73 m; S (41,296) 31/52 km, h 118 m; SE (15,312)
  55/53 km, h 67 m.
- the whole box at stride 4 (~3.2 km), 0 and 25 m, at 00/06/12/18 UTC;
- 10 m wind at C, hourly;
- 0 m current at (119,335) = 58.388 N 10.002 E, hourly, for the HF radar check.
Years: 2023 (360 of 365 days; full statistics) and 2022 PARTIAL (198 days:
Jan-May, Nov 1-17 and Dec; only the monthly tables `points_monthly_2022.csv` and
`box_monthly_2022.csv` are kept, the fetch was stopped for time).

Caveat found in the grid: the model's SOUTHERN OPEN BOUNDARY runs across the
northern Kattegat from 57.38 N 10.73 E to 58.35 N 11.84 E (grid row 0). Shortest
distances to it: box SE corner 3.5 km, SE point 11.9 km, S point 32.5 km; the
eastern ~15 km of the box's south edge and the SE corner area lie within ~20 km
(~25 grid cells, i.e. plausibly inside the boundary nudging zone). The Kattegat
outflow into the SE part of the box and the eastern part of the south-edge inflow
are therefore boundary-forced, not NorKyst's own dynamics.

All derived numbers are in `tables/` (see the table list in summary.md).
