# Evidence: sources and derivations

Tags as in summary.md. "Read" states exactly what was read. Model values are context only.

## 1. Selectivity and diet against availability

**Rindorf A, Gislason H, Lewy P 2006. Prey switching of cod and whiting in the North Sea. MEPS 325:243-253.
doi:10.3354/meps325243.** [F]
- Read: full text, Inter-Research open PDF, kept in the scratchpad.
- Data: stomachs from 1981, 1985-87 Q1 and all quarters of 1991; 11 899 cod (30-100 cm, 4 length groups) and
  24 660 whiting (20-40 cm) (p. 245).
- Prey: haddock, herring, Norway pout, sprat, whiting, 5-30 cm (p. 245).
- Prey field: IBTS delta-estimator by area. Diet and prey are compared in 60 x 60 nmi areas per quarter, "a time
  scale of a few months" (p. 246).
- Table 1 (p. 248), switching exponent b by reference prey (1 = no switching, < 1 = negative switching):

  | reference prey | b | 95 % interval | n |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | pout 7.5 cm | 0.350 | 0.310-0.390 | 563 |
  | pout 12.5 cm | 0.332 | - | 794 |
  | pout 17.5 cm | 0.280 | - | 248 |
  | whiting 12.5 cm | 0.214 | - | 300 |
  | whiting 17.5 cm | 0.218 | - | 312 |
  | whiting 22.5 cm | 0.145 | 0.081-0.209 | 349 |

  The relative survey catch alone explains ~20 % of the variation (p. 249).
- Sample size: b rises by 0.091 per ln(number of stomachs) for the pout references, giving 0.297 at the mean of 20
  stomachs and 0.442 at the maximum of 99 (p. 249, Table 2 p. 250). Predator density has no significant effect on
  b (p. 250).
- On scale (p. 251): "the scale was far greater than the perceptive abilities of the predators"; the results
  "cannot be used to determine the underlying biological mechanisms acting at the local scale" (p. 246).
- Mechanisms offered (p. 251):
  - saturation through limited stomach volume;
  - lower prey vulnerability at high density (schooling);
  - prey avoidance was judged unlikely (no link to predator density).
- The introduction cites predator physiological state as a factor in general (Werner & Hall 1974 and others) [S].
- Applies: North Sea, 20-100 cm gadoids, 1980s-1991. Mechanism-free.

**Kempf A, Floeter J, Temming A 2008. Predator-prey overlap induced Holling type III functional response in the
North Sea fish assemblage. MEPS 367:295-308. doi:10.3354/meps07555.** [P]
- Read: abstract, methods (pp. 297-298), discussion (pp. 303-305).
- Data:
  - stomachs from 1981, 1985-87 and 1991, with cod 30-50 cm and whiting 25-40 cm;
  - 5 prey (cod, whiting, haddock, pout, clupeids) x 3 length classes 5-20 cm;
  - prey field and overlap per ICES rectangle (30 x 30 nmi), Schoener index, area = 27-114 rectangles.
- Findings:
  - the GAM explains 65.0 % of deviance;
  - stomach shares fall fast once overlap drops below ~0.1;
  - after a steep rise at relative prey abundance or overlap 0.1-0.2, shares increase only moderately. This "early
    saturation effect may has caused the observation of negative prey switching in former analyses" (p. 305);
  - the refuge at low prey abundance comes "to a large extent by a passive change in the availability of the prey
    due to changes in predator-prey overlap" (p. 305).
- Consumption rates were assumed constant (p. 305).

**Floeter J, Temming A 2003. Explaining diet composition of North Sea cod (Gadus morhua): prey size preference vs.
prey availability. CJFAS 60:140-150. doi:10.1139/f03-007.** [A]
- Also read as manuscript 1 of Floeter 2005 (below): its abstract and discussion, thesis p. ~34.
- "More than 75% of fish found in the stomachs of North Sea cod originated from the least preferred quartile of the
  prey size range, indicating that prey abundance is the main determinant of the diet composition."
- Discussion: "prey size avoidance is by far more pronounced than positive preference".
- Prey field: IBTS number spectra corrected for gear efficiency and weighted by local predator abundance.

**Floeter J, Temming A 2005. Analysis of prey size preference of North Sea whiting, saithe, and grey gurnard. ICES
JMS 62:897-907. doi:10.1016/j.icesjms.2005.03.004.** [A]
- Also read: the thesis conclusions.
- The preferred predator-prey weight ratio of whiting and grey gurnard rises with predator size. "From a
  species-specific body size onwards they increasingly shift their prey preference towards relatively smaller prey
  sizes".
- Saithe: non-constant ratios that vary by season. The model "could not be applied". Saithe turns to fish prey
  "only during winter time" (thesis conclusions).

**Floeter J 2005. An investigation of key processes affecting trophic interactions in the North Sea fish
assemblage and their significance for multi species fisheries assessment. PhD thesis, Univ. Hamburg,
ediss/1034.** [P]
- Read: abstract, the manuscript 1 discussion, Conclusions and perspectives (pp. ~160-162).
- "prey size preference may be of minor importance for the realized diet composition ... spatio-temporal
  predator-prey overlap and local abundances seem to be of greater importance."
- Fish respond to fronts at "several 10s of nautical miles". Large whiting (> 15 cm) are more abundant in
  non-frontal rectangles (factor 0.48 for > 15 cm abundance, 0.45 for > 25 cm biomass).

**Ludwig KE, Singer A, Kröncke I, Sell AF 2024. Predator-prey trait associations and feeding preferences of
demersal fishes in the southern North Sea. MEPS 739:173-190. doi:10.3354/meps14597.** [P]
- Read: abstract, methods 2.2.3 and 2.3, results 3.3, discussion 4.1 (open access).
- Chesson alpha per station, with stomachs matched to the same haul's fish catch (NS-IBTS / GSBTS, Q3, 1991-2020).
  Fish prey only: 150 hauls, 8 predator-stage combinations, minimum 8 per combination (pp. 179-180).
- Results 3.3 (p. ~181), alpha +- 95 % CI:
  - adult grey gurnard: sandeel 0.45 +- 0.32;
  - adult whiting: sandeel 0.63 +- 0.43, clupeids 0.80 +- 0.30;
  - juvenile cod: gobies 0.56 +- 0.41;
  - adult cod: gadoids 0.42 +- 0.30, among them whiting 0.18 +- 0.06 and dab 0.13 +- 0.05;
  - adult turbot and juvenile whiting: no significant preference.
- Per-panel n and h are in Fig. 5. The figure was not read; only two strings, "n = 17" and "n = 52", were
  extracted, unassigned.
- Southern North Sea, not the Skagerrak.

**Rindorf A, Gislason H 2005. Functional and aggregative response of North Sea whiting. JEMBE 324:1-19.
doi:10.1016/j.jembe.2005.04.013.** [F]
- Read: the cached DTU postprint at `../Attackrater ur mötesteori/data/gap_validation/rindorf2005/postprint.txt`
  (abstract, results excerpt, discussion; postprint line numbers).
- 5 North Sea stations, 48 h round-the-clock sampling, whiting 20-35 cm.
- Fish prey saturate at ~10 % and 17 % of maximum daily ration on clupeids (25-29.9 cm and 30-34.9 cm whiting) and
  ~6 % on gadoids (lines 428-431). "The predator population was thus never limited by gastric processing rate".
- Other-food intake is "uncorrelated with that of fish". There is no fish / other-food specialisation
  (lines 434-437, 541-543).
- Prey and predator log catches correlate negatively in all but 2 of 9 combinations; only herring x whiting
  25-29.9 cm is significant (-0.92, p 0.03) (lines 414-418).
- The modal lengths of fish prey in stomachs equal those in the trawl (lines 497-499).
- Not saturating at Cmax may be "a generally occurring phenomenon in piscivores" (lines 506-510).
- Patchiness within the tow could make saturation look low (lines 463-475).

**Trenkel VM, Pinnegar JK, Dawson WA, Du Buit MH, Tidd AN 2005. Spatial and temporal structure of predator-prey
relationships in the Celtic Sea fish community. MEPS 299:257-268. doi:10.3354/meps299257.** [P]
- Read: abstract, methods (p. 259), extracted statements.
- Cod, hake, megrim and whiting, 1977-94. The prey field is the CEFAS survey (no catchability correction).
- "opportunistic predators and general independence between predator and prey distributions"; whiting matches
  Trisopterus spp.
- Reports earlier Chesson forage ratios (Pinnegar et al. 2003 [S]): saithe, whiting and hake "seemed to prefer
  clupeoids".

**Link JS, Garrison LP 2002. Trophic ecology of Atlantic cod on the northeast US continental shelf. MEPS
227:109-123. doi:10.3354/meps227109.** [P]
- Read: abstract, preference method, discussion excerpts.
- More than 15 000 stomachs over 25 years; preference from occurrence ranks.
- "Atlantic cod diet generally reflects prey availability"; "In this sense cod are opportunistic feeders".
- Preference for sand lance, Cancer crabs and herring "regardless of the abundance or spatio-temporal overlap".
- Other region.

**Mahé K et al. 2007. Ontogenetic and spatial variation in the diet of hake in the Bay of Biscay and the Celtic Sea.
ICES JMS 64:1210-1219. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsm100.** [A]
- "The diet did not generally reflect fish prey availability". Horse mackerel and blue whiting, the most abundant
  prey, were not positively selected. Cannibalism 19.2 %W.

**Neuenfeldt S, Beyer JE 2006. MEPS 310:151. doi:10.3354/meps310151.** [A]
- Baltic cod. The herring:sprat ratio in the diet rises faster than the overlap ratio and slower than the abundance
  ratio.
- That is overlap-driven, with a negative-switching-like response to abundance.

## 2. Fine-scale overlap and avoidance

**Rose GA, Leggett WC 1990. The importance of scale to predator-prey spatial correlations: an example of Atlantic
fishes. Ecology 71:33-43. doi:10.2307/1940245.** [A] (not open)
- Acoustic transects, Newfoundland, cod and capelin.
- In phase at > 4-10 km; out of phase below the aggregation size (3.5 km); maximum negative correlation at 2-3 km;
  in phase at 3.5 m when cod feed.
- In thermal refuges, coherent only above the refuge scale.

**Temming A, Floeter J, Ehrich S 2007. Predation hot spots: large scale impact of local aggregations. Ecosystems
10:865-876. doi:10.1007/s10021-007-9066-3.** [S]
- Not open; no abstract obtainable.
- Via Fauchald 2009 p. ~147: "an aggregation of > 50 million juvenile cod within an area of only 18 km2 was entirely
  wiped out in 5 d by predatory whiting".

**Fauchald P 2009. Spatial interaction between seabirds and prey: review and synthesis. MEPS 391:139-151.
doi:10.3354/meps07818.** [P]
- Read: extracted passages on the predator-prey game.
- Whichever side has a "spatial anchor" loses: a constrained prey gives a positive association, a constrained
  predator a negative one.
- Schooling and local enhancement are small-scale processes. Rose & Leggett is the example of a prey refuge.

**Fall J, Johannesen E, Englund G, Johansen GO, Fiksen Ø 2021. Predator-prey overlap in three dimensions: cod
benefit from capelin coming near the seafloor. Ecography. doi:10.1111/ecog.05473.** [A]
- ~2000 cod stomachs, 2 km resolution, Barents Sea.
- Capelin abundance has small effects; capelin depth has large ones. "Spatial overlap ... needs to be evaluated in
  three dimensions".

**Hunsicker ME et al. 2013. PLoS ONE 8:e66025. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0066025.** [A]
- Overlap of arrowtooth flounder and age-1 pollock, driven by the predator's density-dependent habitat selection
  and by temperature.

**Kempf A et al. 2010. ICES JMS 67:1989-1997. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsq114.** [A]
- Survey overlap indices parametrise SMS. Overlap is "a sensitive parameter".
- The PDF download returned HTML, so this is not read beyond the abstract.

**Derived, GOV box and region** [D], `scripts/overlap_scale.py` -> `tables/overlap_scale_stdout.txt`.

  - NS-IBTS GOV Q1/Q3 2000-2025 from the cached extract `../Sejens djupläge/data/gov/` (collision-free key);
  - biomass from length-weight relations;
  - Lloyd L = mean(xy)/(mean x mean y) at the haul, at 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 deg cell-survey means, and at cell
    climatologies;
  - interval: bootstrap over years;
  - L_within: hauls relative to their cell-survey mean.

  | box Q3 | 180 | 1.59 (0.50-2.81) | 1.69 | 2.02 | - | 0.55 (20) |
  | Skagerrak 57.3-59.2 N, 7.5-12 E, Q1 | 779 | 1.04 | 0.97 | 0.95 | 1.06 | 1.29 (198) |
  | Skagerrak Q3 | 771 | 1.63 | 1.59 | 1.67 | 1.07 | 1.08 (138) |

  | NNS Q3 | 3064 | 0.38 | 0.39 | 0.41 | 0.36 | 0.88 |
