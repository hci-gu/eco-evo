Acoustic blind zones (surface and near-bottom) and acoustic vs bottom-trawl herring estimates
============================================================================================
Compiled 2026-10-24. Tags: [F] full text, [A] abstract/snippet, [S] secondary.

1. Survey-specific settings in the eastern Skagerrak (HERAS, RV Dana) [F] - see heras_methods_allocation_ts.txt
 - Towed body 3-7 m (mostly 4-5 m); integration from 3 m below transducer (absolute start 7-9 m in 2013;
   manual 2025: upper integration limit 8-10 m). -> upper ~8-10 m not integrated.
 - Bottom: "Bottom margin (backstep) 1.0 m" (Dana 2013 Table 1); integration "to 1 m above the bottom".
 - Depth: max 500 m (2016+); max 300 m and layers below 150 m excluded (2002-2013 reports).
 Derived illustration (this agent, not from a source): on a flat bottom at 168 m with the ES38-7 (7 deg
 beam) and 1.024 ms pulse, the acoustic dead zone is ~ c*tau/2 + D(1 - cos 3.5 deg) = 0.77 + 0.31 = ~1.1 m,
 plus the 1 m backstep = ~2 m above the seabed not integrated; more on slopes (Norwegian Trench flanks).

2. Ona, E. and Mitson, R.B. 1996. Acoustic sampling and signal processing near the seabed: the deadzone
 revisited. ICES Journal of Marine Science 53: 677-690. [S - full reference and the term "bottom dead
 zone" via Scalabrin et al. 2009; the paper itself not read]

3. Scalabrin, C., Marfia, C. and Boucher, J. 2009. How much fish is hidden in the surface and bottom
 acoustic blind zones? ICES Journal of Marine Science 66: 1355-1363. Open access:
 https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00000/11093/7637.pdf [F]
 Abstract: AUV vs RV Thalassa, Bay of Biscay, Sept 2007. "The AUV provided higher sA measurements than did
 the vessel. For particular environmental and fish-distribution patterns, the biomass estimated by the AUV
 was more than ten times that estimated by the vessel alone."
 Introduction (p. 1355): the "surface blind zone ... can extend to 15 m below the surface, depending on the
 acoustic frequency, the draft of the vessel, and the sea state"; "it has not been possible to take full
 account of the upper 10 m in the abundance estimates" (anchovy); "useful detections of fish, such as hake
 or cod, may not be possible, if they are located within 2 m of the bottom. This region is known as the
 'bottom dead zone' (Ona and Mitson, 1996) ... Its volume depends on the beam pattern, pulse duration, and
 bottom slope, and it increases with depth."

4. ICES IPS manuals [F]
 SISP 9 (2015), p. 15-16: "The blind zone, which is the transducer draft + the nearfield zone may therefore
 be larger on some vessels than others." IESSNS caveats (p. 22): "The acoustic dead zone at the surface
 (0-15 m depth)".
 IPS manual 2025 (TIMES 71) ch. 7.8 (German GERAS, PDF p. 128): night survey because "a certain fraction of
 target species being distributed within the bottom dead zone during daytime (Bethke, 1993)"; near-surface
 clupeids at night "are not quantified, since the integration start depth has been set to 10 m".
 IPS manual 2025, HERAS (printed p. 64): in Kattegat/Skagerrak herring "is found in dispersed layers mixed
 with other fish at all times" (so day/night surface-rise is a smaller issue there than elsewhere).

5. Diurnal acoustic vs trawl differences (demersal, Barents Sea) [A]
 Hjellvik, V., Godo, O.R. and Tjostheim, D. 2004/2005. "Diurnal variation in acoustic densities: why do we
 see less in the dark?" (ICES conference contribution, figshare 25349773; journal version Can. J. Fish.
 Aquat. Sci. ~2004-2005): echo abundance ~40-50 % higher by day in the winter Barents Sea demersal survey;
 migration into/out of the bottom dead zone "did not appear to be the main cause"; behaviour/tilt-driven TS
 changes more plausible. [A, via search summaries of IMR Brage records 11250/108193, 11250/106609]
 Hjellvik, Godo & Tjostheim 2002, Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci. 59: 33-48: diurnal variation in Barents Sea
 bottom-trawl catches of cod, stronger for small fish and at greater depth [A].

6. North Sea herring: acoustic (HERAS) vs IBTS index comparison [F]
 ICES 1997. Report of the Planning Group for Herring Surveys (PGHERS). ICES C.M. 1997/H:5. figshare
 19263092, https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/41288400 . Section 2.1.3: "The ratio of the acoustic index
 with the IBTS from 1987 to 1994 shows considerable fluctuation with a low point in 1988, resulting in a
 factor of 1.7 or 1.2 between observations at the ends of this period ... The differences over the full
 available time series from 1984 to 1994 indicates a factor between 1.4 to 0.7 from the mid 1980s to the
 early 1990s ... the acoustic series provides a more precise estimate of year-class strength at 2 to 4
 ring." Appendix C: "The IBTS exhibits much steeper selection (faster apparent reduction of numbers at age)
 than the acoustic survey".
 This is a relative (index) comparison; no absolute catchability of the GOV for herring was found.
 WBSS stock annex 2026 (section 2.3.3): IBTS Q1/Q3 herring indices in 3.a were "deemed unfit" in 2002 and
 included again from the 2013 benchmark; combined IBTS+BITS GAM indices since 2018.

7. Simple absolute comparison for the model box (derived by this agent from the files in this folder):
 GOV bottom-trawl estimate supplied by the caller: 1.3 t/km2 planktivores (herring ~0.9 t/km2).
 HERAS July herring in stratum 31 (covers ~60-65 % of the box): 0.5-8.7 t/km2, mean 3.9 (2016-2025).
 Danish national old stratum D (~90 % of box): 1.5-14.2 t/km2, mean 7.1 (2004-2015, six years).
 Argos August 1989, eastern Skagerrak: 13.2 t/km2 over all strata, but only 0.7 t/km2 in stratum D5 and 2.7 in
 C5, which (inferred, see argos_1989_skagerrak_kattegat.txt; E0 does not fit the depth scheme) are the
 100-300 m depth strata, i.e. herring were concentrated in the shallow coastal 20-60 m strata.
 So the summer acoustic herring density is typically 3-5x the GOV figure for the whole stratum, but the
 (inferred) depth-stratified 1989 data suggest the deep (>100 m) part of the box holds far less than the stratum mean.
