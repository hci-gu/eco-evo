# Horizontal net displacement of gadoids (cod first; haddock, saithe, whiting, pollack, Norway pout) at 1 h, 6 h and 24 h - for the Mareld gadoid group (1 km cells, 6 h tick)

Labelling used throughout: **[LIT]** = value stated in the cited source; **[CALC]** = my own calculation from cited values; **[2nd]** = value seen only as a citation inside another paper (not verified in the original). Regions: NNS/CNS/SNS = northern/central/southern North Sea, CHA = eastern English Channel.

## Q1. Atlantic cod - resident / home-range behaviour (Skagerrak, Kattegat, North Sea)

### Takeaway
Acoustic telemetry on the Norwegian and Swedish Skagerrak coast shows that resident cod use home ranges of only ~0.01-0.25 km2 (median ~0.05-0.08 km2), i.e. far smaller than one 1 km x 1 km model cell; net displacement over 1, 6 and 24 h while resident is sub-cell (order 0.1-0.5 km). North Sea data storage tag (DST) studies classify >55% of fish-days as "resident" (<2 km net per day), which supports a median 24-h net displacement below 2 km even offshore.

### Cited Findings
- [LIT] Sandnesfjord, southern Norwegian Skagerrak coast (Kristensen, Olsen, Moland, Knutsen et al. 2021, Ecol Evol): fixed-receiver positioning of tagged cod (~40-56 cm in the table rows extracted) from May-Nov 2017; 95% MCP home ranges "varied from 1 to 25 hectares (mean: 7.1 ha, SD: 5.9 ha, median: 5.0 ha), with no clear difference between the two ecotypes" (fjord vs North Sea ecotype); no effect of fish length (p = 0.637) - [Kristensen et al. 2021, PMC8366838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8366838/)
- [LIT] Same study: about 42% of North-Sea-ecotype fish left the fjord system (possibly to offshore spawning grounds) versus 7% of fjord-ecotype fish - [Kristensen et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8366838/)
- [LIT] Same study cites Villegas-Rios et al. (2017) for the adjacent Tvedestrand fjord: individuals range "from almost completely sedentary to highly migratory"; home ranges in Sandnesfjord were "generally smaller" than at Tvedestrand - [Kristensen et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8366838/)
- [2nd] Tvedestrand fjord (Norwegian Skagerrak, fjord area 3.8 km2): typical cod behaviour is "limited horizontal movement, with a median home range of 0.08 km2" (citing Freitas et al. 2016; Villegas-Rios, Reale et al. 2017); home-range repeatability 0.43. Not verified in the 2017 original (Animal Behaviour 124:83-94, 303 cod over 3 years) - [Frontiers in Marine Science 2021 (citing Villegas-Rios 2017)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.674756/full); [Villegas-Rios et al. 2017 abstract page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003347216303335)
- [LIT] Norwegian Skagerrak coast (IMR, 10 coastal cod tracked 45 days with a stationary positioning system): most fish stayed within about 2 km of the release point; three moved 2-6 km from it - [IMR Brage: Long-term movement patterns of coastal cod](https://imr.brage.unit.no/imr-xmlui/handle/11250/106213)
- [LIT] Swedish Skagerrak coastal fjord system (sites I and II; site name not confirmed from the retrieved text; Staveley et al. 2019, Ecol Evol; 39 juvenile/subadult cod, ~26-36 cm TL in the table rows extracted, released Aug 2015, ~5 months): "movement of fish gradually decreased from large-scale (km) linkages in the summer to more localized movement patterns in the winter (limited to 100s m)"; cooling sea-surface temperature significantly reduced movement; residency index 0.38 +/- 0.06 (range 2-153 days detected) - [Staveley et al. 2019, PMC6706200](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6706200/)
- [LIT] Norwegian Skagerrak summer study (44 receivers, 80 cod, 2012): home-range size and cumulative distance travelled changed by month; body size mainly affected vertical range (thesis, numbers not retrieved) - [DUO UiO thesis 10852/36593](https://duo.uio.no/handle/10852/36593)
- [LIT] North Sea offshore analogue (Belgian C-Power wind farm, southern North Sea; Reubens et al. 2013): 41 cod tagged 2010-2011; "strong residency and site fidelity" in summer and autumn, aggregating around turbine foundations (artificial reefs) - [Reubens et al. 2013, Mar Environ Res](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141113613001050?via=ihub%3D); [dataset](https://obis.org/dataset/63061840-e725-4c74-bdbc-65f31a8e28c8)
- [LIT] Note on source weight: Righton et al. 2008 is an ICES CM working paper headed "Not to be cited without prior reference to the authors" (grey literature); it is the only source here for the RES/ELM/EDM thresholds and day fractions.
- [LIT] North Sea DST programme (Righton, Quayle, Neat, Pedersen, Wright, Armstrong, Svedang, Hobson, Metcalfe; ICES CM 2008/P:04): 1126 cod (37-110 cm) tagged 1999-2008, 263 returned, >31,000 days of data; daily positions by tidal location + Fokker-Planck geolocation; 35 fish fully reconstructed (6935 daily positions). Daily classes: **RES = <2 km between successive days; ELM (extended localised movement) = 2-15 km/day; EDM (extended directed movement) = >15 km/day** - [Righton et al. 2008, ICES CM 2008/P:04](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [LIT] Resident cod in a northern Norwegian fjord, high-resolution stationary positioning (fixes every 17 s, summer diel cycle; Mar Biol Res 7(3), 2011, abstract/snippet only): most swimming-speed recordings were below 1.0 body length per second and only 4-7% exceeded the species' sustained swimming speed - [Variable swimming speeds in individual Atlantic cod (T&F abstract)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17451000.2010.492223)
- [LIT] Same: "RES behaviour was the most frequently observed, accounting for over 55% of all observations"; RES bouts lasted ~13 days on average (max 534 days); ELM bouts ~7 days; EDM bouts ~2.5 days; direct EDM<->RES transitions were rare - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [LIT] Same, share of days by class (Table 4 as extracted; columns CHA/CNS/NNS/SNS): EDM 5.7% / 3.2% / 1.7% / 14.5%; ELM 56.9% / 25.9% / 5.3% / 64.0% (overall ELM 38.4%); RES = remainder (~37% / ~71% / ~93% / ~21%) - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [LIT] Same: 10 of 11 NNS cod and 4 of 5 CNS cod were "resident" (>90% of days RES); 8 of 9 SNS cod were migrants (moved >150 km from release); CHA cod were migrants or "wanderers" - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [LIT] Same, Table 1b "Distance moved (km)" per day (mean +/- SD), as extracted from the PDF: 6 +/- 6; 8.5 +/- 14.9; 10.6 +/- 13.3; 16.4 +/- 13.4; overall 12.5 +/- 12.4. Column order is ambiguous in the text extraction (most likely NNS, CNS, CHA, SNS, overall; the text says SNS and CHA cod "exhibited the greatest daily rate of movement" and NNS the lowest). **Caveat:** these means do not reconcile with the class fractions above (e.g. NNS ~93% of days <2 km cannot give a 6 km/day mean); see Inferences - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [LIT] Geolocation resolution: tidal location used a 12 km tidal/bathymetry grid - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf); an HMM geolocation validation for cod (Gulf of Maine, moored tags) gave median error 4.93 km - [Liu et al. 2017 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312279777_Validation_of_a_hidden_Markov_model_for_the_geolocation_of_Atlantic_cod)
- [LIT] North Sea cod DST (Pedersen et al. 2008, CJFAS 65:2367-2377; tag 2255, 311 days at liberty, two-state HMM): diffusivity MLEs **17.4 km2/day (low activity) and 149 km2/day (high activity)** (SD 2.69 and 28.5); one-state model 57.6 km2/day. Convention in the model: per-axis displacement variance = 2 D dt - [Pedersen PhD thesis, DTU (includes the 2008 paper)](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/5429384/phd243_mwp_rev_net.pdf)
- [LIT] Neat et al. 2014 (J Appl Ecol; 252 cod with temperature/depth loggers around the British Isles): individuals showed "migration, site fidelity and limited home ranging"; northern offshore North Sea cod did not mix with central/southern North Sea cod - [Neat et al. 2014 (Europe PMC record / journal)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2664.12343)

### Inferences
- [CALC] Righton 2008 percentiles from class fractions (24 h net displacement, North Sea + Channel pooled): >55% of days <2 km -> **median 24-h net displacement < 2 km**; ~94% of days <15 km overall (NNS ~98%, SNS ~85%) -> **p94 ~ 15 km/day** (NNS ~p98, SNS ~p85); p90 lies somewhere inside the 2-15 km ELM band and is not resolved by the class data; maximum observed ~70 km/day. For a cod in the northern/central North Sea (closest analogue to the offshore Skagerrak), the median is well below 2 km/day and the 15 km/day threshold sits at ~p97-p98.
- [CALC] Reconciliation check on Table 1b: with NNS fractions (93% RES, 5.3% ELM, 1.7% EDM) and generous class means (1.5, 7, 30 km/day) the implied mean is ~2-2.3 km/day, not 6; the overall 12.5 km/day is likewise ~2x what the pooled fractions imply. Likely causes: geolocation noise in daily positions inflating straight-line day-to-day distances, and/or table mis-extraction. Use the class thresholds and fractions, not the Table 1b means.
- [CALC] Rayleigh conversion of the Pedersen 2008 diffusivities (sigma = sqrt(2 D dt) per axis; median = 1.177 sigma, mean = 1.253 sigma, p95 = 2.448 sigma). These are **upper bounds** because daily-step HMM diffusivity also absorbs position uncertainty (tidal grid ~12 km, typical errors ~5 km), and because movement at sub-daily scale is not diffusive:

| D (km2/day) | dt | median (km) | mean (km) | p90 (km) | p95 (km) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17.4 (low activity) | 1 h | 1.4 | 1.5 | 2.6 | 2.9 |
| 17.4 | 6 h | 3.5 | 3.7 | 6.3 | 7.2 |
| 17.4 | 24 h | 6.9 | 7.4 | 12.7 | 14.4 |
| 149 (high activity) | 1 h | 4.1 | 4.4 | 7.6 | 8.6 |
| 149 | 6 h | 10.2 | 10.8 | 18.5 | 21.1 |
| 149 | 24 h | 20.3 | 21.6 | 37.0 | 42.3 |

- The low-activity D=17.4 gives a 6-h median of 3.5 km, which contradicts the acoustic home ranges (median 5 ha = ~0.2-0.25 km across; 0.08 km2 = ~0.3 km across). Acoustic telemetry is therefore the authority for resident scale: [CALC] a fish confined to a 0.05-0.25 km2 home range cannot have a net displacement over 1, 6 or 24 h larger than the home-range diameter, i.e. **~0.1-0.5 km**, regardless of how much it swims within it.
- [CALC] Resident table for the model (cod, Skagerrak coast/offshore analogue):

| Behaviour | 1 h | 6 h | 24 h | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resident (home range) median | <0.2 km | ~0.1-0.3 km | ~0.1-0.5 km | acoustic home ranges [LIT->CALC] |
| Resident upper (p90) | ~0.5 km | ~0.5-1 km | <2 km | RES class threshold [LIT] |
| Localised search (ELM, 2-15 km/day) | ~0.5-1.5 km | ~1-4 km | 2-15 km | Righton class [LIT]; sub-daily by sqrt(t) [CALC] |

- Swimming speed does not discriminate behaviour: resident cod mostly swim <1 BL/s (Mar Biol Res 2011) and migrating cod 0.3-0.9 BL/s (Arnold 1994) - the ranges overlap. What differs is directional persistence, i.e. net displacement. Saithe (~1 m/s field speed, Pedersen 2001) is the gadoid outlier.
- In model units the current movement_speed 0.25 cell per 6 h fits **resident** cod (and the home-range scale is sub-cell); ELM-type days (~38% of days pooled North Sea, but only ~5% in NNS) would justify a local reach up to ~1 cell per 6 h.

### Gaps
- No acoustic study of cod in the offshore Skagerrak (deep basin, 57.8-58.3 N, 10.1-11.1 E) was found; all Skagerrak telemetry is from coastal fjords (Norway, Sweden), where topography constrains movement. Offshore North Sea analogues are the Belgian wind-farm study (residency, no km values retrieved) and DSTs.
- Villegas-Rios 2017 home-range distribution (percentiles) and Freitas et al. 2016 were not retrieved directly; the 0.08 km2 median is second-hand.
- The full text of the high-resolution swimming-speed study (Mar Biol Res 7(3), 2011) could not be opened (paywall), so hourly net displacement from it is unknown - [T&F abstract page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17451000.2010.492223).
- Kattegat: no acoustic telemetry study on Kattegat/Oresund cod movement rates was found.

## Q2. Atlantic cod - directed movement / migration (spawning migrations, selective tidal stream transport, Skagerrak-North Sea exchange)

### Takeaway
Directed cod movement covers ~10-20 km per 6 h in the tide-swept southern North Sea (selective tidal stream transport, ground speed 50-90 cm/s), but through-water swimming speed is only 20-60 cm/s (0.3-0.9 body lengths/s), i.e. ~4-11 km per 6 h of sustained directed swimming in a near-tideless sea such as the Skagerrak. Directed days (>15 km/day) are rare (2-14% of days) and come in short bouts (~2.5 days), concentrated around spawning (Q1 and spring dispersal).

### Cited Findings
- [LIT] Southern North Sea, sector-scanning sonar tracking of 24 cod with 300 kHz transponding tags (Arnold, Greer Walker, Emerson & Holford 1994, ICES J Mar Sci 51:207-232): tracks lasted up to 52 h and covered up to 72 km; five fish off East Anglia moved net 15-20 km offshore across the tidal axis; three fish SE of the Norfolk Banks moved net 40-70 km north by selective tidal stream transport (STST); one fish north of the Banks moved 54.7 km east - [Arnold et al. 1994 (VLIZ PDF)](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/249248.pdf); [abstract record](https://marineinfo.org/doc/publication/227734)
- [LIT] Same: "Average swimming speeds in midwater were 0.3-0.9 L s-1" (cod 4, 6, 7, 9: 20-60 cm/s through water), similar speeds estimated near the bottom; ground speeds were proportionately greater because they included the tidal current - [Arnold et al. 1994](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/249248.pdf)
- [LIT] Same, per-tide numbers: cod 10 moved 20 km north between 1100 and 1700 h (one 6-h transporting tide); cod 20 covered net 21.1 km (0900-1600 h) and 16.5 km (2200-0400 h) per north-going tide vs predicted tidal excursion 9-9.4 km, implying through-water swimming of 0.3-0.5 m/s (0.5-0.7 L/s); cod 20 also gained 5.8-7.9 km north during opposing tides; cod 7 midwater ground speed 50-90 cm/s; cross-tide directed movement 1-8 km per single tide (ground speeds 4-40 cm/s); cod 23 moved net 35.4 km in ~20 h (0400-2400 h) - [Arnold et al. 1994](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/249248.pdf)
- [LIT] Same: undirected (surface-released) fish made little net movement but were carried back and forth: cod 7 total ground distance 65.8 km vs net 16 km; cod 1-3 total 78, 162 and 112 km vs net <20 km. One fish held a heading within +/-40-45 deg for up to 8 h - [Arnold et al. 1994](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/249248.pdf)
- [LIT] North Sea DSTs: cod "sometimes moving up to 70 km in a single day"; SNS cod "moved rapidly in spring from presumed spawning areas in the south and east North Sea to feeding grounds in the central and western North Sea"; EDM (>15 km/day) bouts typically ~2.5 days (EDM mean event length 1.5-3.1 days by region, max 34 days) - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [LIT] North Sea DSTs (Turner, Righton & Metcalfe 2002; 58 cod tagged off Lowestoft, 22 returned): "highly active mid-water swimming while moving north from spawning grounds between March and June", mostly inactive on the seabed July-September, renewed activity in Oct-Nov (southward spawning migration) - [Cefas publication abstract](https://www.cefas.co.uk/data-and-publications/publications/list/publication-abstract/?id=12066)
- [LIT] Eastern Skagerrak / Kattegat DSTs (Svedang, Righton & Jonsson, ICES CM 2006/Q:06; cod >40 cm tagged off the Swedish Skagerrak coast, Gullmar Fjord and Kattegat 2003-2005): cod "migrated towards the North Sea, in particular, during the spawning period"; of recaptured Skagerrak-tagged fish "about 48% were either classified as resident or showing undefined movements in the deeper part of the Skagerrak, whereas 20% were recaptured in the western part of the Skagerrak or in the North Sea, and 20% were geolocated" there; migratory fish were older (3.4 vs 2.9 yr) and more often mature/maturing (54% vs 22%) - [Svedang et al. 2006, ICES CM 2006/Q:06](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/2006/Q/Q0606.pdf)
- [LIT] Published version: Svedang, Righton & Jonsson 2007, MEPS 345:1-12, "natal homing is the prime stock-separating mechanism"; genetic follow-up shows 2-5 yr cod residing at or migrating toward Kattegat and North Sea spawning areas "display philopatric behaviours" - [Andre et al. 2016, BMC Res Notes](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13104-016-1878-9)
- [LIT] North Sea DSTs: cod recaptured on average 96 km (+/-127; max 680 km) from release after mean 187 days at liberty - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)
- [2nd] Atlantic cod (Liu et al. 2017, Gulf of Maine geolocation): mean ~4 km/day, cited as similar to pollack - [Gonse et al. 2024, J Fish Biol (PMC12120336)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12120336/)

### Inferences
- [CALC] Unit conversions: 0.2 m/s = 0.72 km/h = 4.3 km/6 h; 0.3 m/s = 6.5 km/6 h; 0.5 m/s = 10.8 km/6 h; 0.6 m/s = 13 km/6 h. Arnold's STST examples: cod 10 ~3.3 km/h, cod 20 ~2.8-3.0 km/h over a transporting tide (tide-assisted); cod 23 ~1.8 km/h averaged over 20 h.
- [CALC] Transferability: the Skagerrak has small tidal currents (inference; no source retrieved here), so tide-assisted ground speeds of 15-21 km per 6-h tide are an **upper bound** there. The transferable quantity is through-water directed swimming of 0.2-0.5 m/s, giving **~4-11 km net per 6 h of sustained directed swimming**, consistent with Righton's EDM threshold (>15 km/day) and a typical EDM day of ~15-30 km.
- [CALC] Migration table (cod; values per step spent travelling):

| Behaviour | 1 h | 6 h | 24 h | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Directed swimming, near-tideless (median-ish) | ~1-2 km | ~5-8 km | ~15-30 km | 0.3-0.9 L/s, 20-60 cm/s [LIT]; EDM >15 km/day [LIT] |
| Directed, tide-assisted (STST, southern North Sea) | ~3 km | 15-21 km | 35-70 km | Arnold 1994; Righton 2008 max 70 km/day [LIT] |
| Population-wide share of days that are directed | - | - | ~2% NNS, ~3% CNS, ~6% CHA, ~15% SNS | Righton 2008 [LIT] |

- [CALC] Seasonality: directed movement concentrates in the spawning period (Q1) and the spring post-spawning dispersal (Mar-Jun), and in the eastern Skagerrak roughly half the tagged cod stayed resident while ~40% moved to the western Skagerrak/North Sea - so transit is a seasonal, sub-population behaviour, not a year-round rate.
- Because EDM bouts last ~2.5 days, a fish in transit moves consistently in one direction across ~10 consecutive 6-h steps; a k-cell jump per step is a reasonable discretisation of that.

### Gaps
- No direct per-hour or per-6-h displacement distribution (median/percentiles) for migrating cod in the Skagerrak or Kattegat was found; the Svedang DST work reports classifications, not speeds.
- Neat et al. 2014 home-range and seasonal-movement numbers were not accessible (paywall; only the abstract was read).
- Pedersen et al. 2008 "high activity" diffusivity is from a single North Sea fish.

## Q3. Other gadoids - haddock, saithe, whiting, pollack, Norway pout

### Takeaway
Saithe is the most mobile gadoid (field swimming speed ~1 m/s, long seasonal migrations), pollack moves ~3.5 km/day on average (2-7 km/day; DST geolocation, Iroise Sea/English Channel) within ~30 km of release, whiting rarely makes long migrations (<110 km over years), and no electronic-tag displacement rates were found for haddock or Norway pout.

### Cited Findings
- [LIT] Saithe, North Sea, split-beam echosounder tracking (Pedersen J. 2001, J Fish Biol 58:1073-1085): mean swimming speed 1.05 m/s (+/-0.09), median 0.93 m/s; 25-29 cm fish 1.08 m/s at night vs 0.72 m/s by day; max ping-to-ping 3.34 m/s; speed independent of size; method bias (stationary target apparent speed 0.15 m/s) discussed - [DTU Orbit record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/hydroacoustic-measurement-of-swimming-speed-of-north-sea-saithe-i/)
- [LIT] Saithe, Northeast Atlantic conventional tagging synthesis (Homrum et al. 2013, ICES J Mar Sci 70:782-792): displacement grows with length/age up to ~80 cm and ~5 years at liberty; Faroese/Icelandic fish level off at ~200-300 km; Norwegian-tagged fish moved farthest (longest in Feb-Mar, shortest in summer); ~42% of adults tagged at the Faroes recaptured outside Faroese waters vs 6.6% of Norwegian-tagged adults outside continental waters; no km/day values - [Homrum et al. 2013](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/70/4/782/726798)
- [LIT] Saithe, Ryfylke (SW Norway), acoustic tags for up to 2 years (Otterå & Skilbrei 2014; 62 fish, 38-59 cm): many stayed in the release area for 1-2 years and moved frequently among salmon farms (719 receiver changes; one fish 74 moves); ~32% of T-bar recaptures outside the release area incl. North Sea and Skagerrak; no km or speed values reported - [Otterå & Skilbrei 2014](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/71/9/2484/2798173)
- [LIT] Saithe, short-term acoustic tracking in Norway: nine fish tracked 16.6-264 h were active by day and relatively inactive at night, restricting movement to a core area - [ICES library: Local movements of saithe near fish farm cages](https://ices-library.figshare.com/articles/report/Local_movements_of_saithe_Pollachius_virens_L_in_the_vicinity_of_fish_farm_cages/19270859)
- [LIT] Pollack, Iroise Sea / English Channel (Gonse et al. 2024, J Fish Biol; 83 acoustic tags, 48 also DST): "On average, pollack traveled 3.5 km/day (ranging between 2.0 km/day ... and 7.1 km/day)" from geolocation; no relation with size; up to ~1800 km in about a year; mostly detected within ~30 km of release; residency periods mean 24.2 h; earlier Norwegian west-coast external tagging (Jakobsen 1985, 28-52 cm) recaptured within ~30 km after up to 5 years - [Gonse et al. 2024, PMC12120336](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12120336/)
- [LIT] Whiting, North Sea / northern European shelf tag-recapture: most recaptured <110 km from release; Tobin et al. 2010 reported long-term displacements of 53-123 km; little mixing across the Dogger Bank - [ICES FishMap whiting factsheet](https://www.ices.dk/about-ICES/projects/EU-RFP/EU%20Repository/ICES%20FIshMap/ICES%20FishMap%20species%20factsheet-whiting.pdf); [Tobin et al. 2010, Mar Biol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00227-009-1387-7)
- [LIT] Haddock: older tag-recapture reviews indicate some haddock stay local year-round, the largest fish migrate furthest, and North Sea adults spawn in deeper water and feed in shallower water to the west and south - [FAO haddock synopsis](https://www.fao.org/4/c3584e/c3584e.pdf); [NOAA Mar Fish Rev 60(4) 1998](https://spo.nmfs.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-content/mfr6041_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- [CALC] Saithe 1.05 m/s is a cruise/foraging speed, not net displacement; if sustained in one direction it would be 3.8 km/h = 23 km per 6 h, an upper bound for gadoid transit. Saithe is pelagic-schooling and better fits a large transit k than demersal cod, but it is a minority of the gadoid biomass the model group represents.
- [CALC] Pollack 3.5 km/day (2-7) is close to the reported cod mean (~4 km/day, second-hand) and both sit between the RES (<2 km/day) and ELM (2-15 km/day) classes - i.e. a pooled-day average that mixes resident and moving days, and is inflated by geolocation error.
- Whiting and haddock tag-recapture displacements (tens to ~100 km over months to years) imply low mean daily net displacement (well below 1 km/day averaged over time at liberty), but those are not per-step rates.

### Gaps
- Haddock: no acoustic or DST study giving horizontal displacement rates was found.
- Norway pout: no telemetry study found (small, short-lived species; likely untaggable with current tags) - no data.
- Whiting: no electronic tagging rates found; only conventional recapture distances.
- Saithe: no km/day displacement from electronic tags (Homrum et al. 2013 and Otterå & Skilbrei 2014 report no km/day values); Icelandic DST work (Armannsson et al., vertical migrations) not retrieved.
- Flatfish (which the model gadoid group also stands in for as seal prey) were not researched, per the assignment.

## Q4. Synthesis for the model - local movement rate, transit reach k, and scaling to a 1-h step

### Takeaway
For the gadoid group (cod-dominated) on 1 km cells: keep the local step at about **0.25 cell per 6 h** for resident behaviour (acoustic home ranges are sub-cell), allow up to ~1 cell per 6 h for localised search; set the transit jump to **k ~ 5 cells per 6 h (plausible range 3-8; 15-20 km/6 h only with tidal assistance)**, to be used on a small, seasonal fraction of steps. If the modeller wants gadoids to stay strictly local, k = 1 is defensible for non-spawning seasons. All values in this section are my own inferences from the cited findings above.

### Cited Findings
- [LIT] Resident cod home ranges 1-25 ha (median 5 ha) in a Skagerrak fjord - [Kristensen et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8366838/)
- [LIT] >55% of North Sea cod-days are RES (<2 km/day); EDM (>15 km/day) 1.7-14.5% of days by region, bouts ~2.5 days - [Righton et al. 2008](https://www.ices.dk/sites/pub/CM%20Doccuments/CM-2008/P/P0408.pdf)

### Inferences
- [CALC] Recommended parameters (model: 1 km cells, 6 h step, engine cap today 1 cell/6 h):

| Parameter | Suggested | Range | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local movement (resident) | 0.25 cell / 6 h (current value) | 0.1-0.5 | net displacement of home-ranging cod ~0.1-0.5 km per 6-24 h |
| Local movement (search / ELM) | up to 1 cell / 6 h | 0.5-1 | ELM 2-15 km/day -> ~1-4 km per 6 h if diffusive |
| Transit jump k | 5 cells / 6 h | 3-8 | directed swimming 0.2-0.5 m/s in a near-tideless sea = 4-11 km/6 h |
| Transit upper bound | 15-20 cells / 6 h | - | tide-assisted STST (southern North Sea) or saithe at ~1 m/s; not appropriate for offshore Skagerrak cod |
| Share of steps in transit | ~2-6% (up to ~15%) | - | EDM day fractions NNS/CNS/CHA vs SNS; seasonal (Q1, Mar-Jun) |

- [CALC] Scaling to a 1-h step: resident / search movement is roughly diffusive, so it scales with sqrt(t): 1-h reach = 6-h reach / sqrt(6) = 6-h reach / 2.45 (e.g. 0.25 cell/6 h -> ~0.1 cell/h; 1 cell/6 h -> ~0.4 cell/h). Directed transit scales linearly: 1-h reach = 6-h reach / 6 (e.g. k = 5 cells/6 h -> ~0.8-1 cell/h; i.e. at a 1-h step, transit and local step merge into ~1 cell per tick). Note: summing six 1-h diffusive steps reproduces the 6-h spread only if direction is uncorrelated; real fish have correlated headings over hours (one cod held a heading +/-45 deg for 8 h, Arnold 1994), so for transit the linear rule is the right one.
- [CALC] If the model can only express a single speed for gadoids, the time-averaged expectation is dominated by resident days: mean net displacement over 24 h of order 1-3 km (cod), which is ~0.25-0.75 cell per 6 h - consistent with the current setting plus occasional transit.
- Choosing k = 1 (no transit) would remove the ~2-15% of directed days that carry Skagerrak cod to North Sea spawning areas; within a closed 60 x 60 km box that migration largely leaves the domain anyway, so k = 1 is a defensible simplification if the model does not represent spawning migration out of the box.

### Gaps
- No source gives the full distribution (median/p90/p95) of 1-h or 6-h net displacement for offshore gadoids; all sub-daily values above are derived [CALC] from swimming speeds, daily classes, or home ranges.
- The near-tideless character of the Skagerrak is stated from general knowledge, not from a source retrieved here; the transit recommendation assumes it.
- Thermal effects (Staveley 2019: movement shrinks to 100s of m in cold winter water) suggest the local step could be seasonal; no quantitative per-degree relationship was found.
