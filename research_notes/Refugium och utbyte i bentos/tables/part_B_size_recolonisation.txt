Q2.3 Share of Nephrops biomass inside the vulnerable window (J, scripts/nephrops_size_window.py)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method (J): steady-state population with constant recruitment at the first-postlarva size
(3.08 mm CL, Smith 1987, see Q2.5), annex VBGF per sex (females switch to 65/0.10 at 29 mm), annex
M (0.3; 0.2 mature females), gear selectivity derived iteratively from the 2023 catch LFD
(s(L) proportional to C(L)/N(L)), F scaled so that catch/abundance(>=17 mm) = the 2021-23
harvest rates (4.2-5.1 %). (The model-vs-observed catch shape printed in stdout D agrees by
construction of the selectivity fit and is NOT a validation.)
Independent checks (J): (i) digitised LFD x annex L-W = 6739 t of catch 2023 vs WGNSSK 2023
landings 6175 t + discards 975 t = 7150 t (6 % low; checks digitisation + L-W); (ii) modelled mean
weight of animals >= 17 mm is 28-29 g vs the 30 g (23-45) adopted earlier in
"../Bottensamhallets epifauna/evidence_A2_nephrops.md" - a consistency check only, since that 30 g
was itself a judgement. The population shape between 17 and ~28 mm is set almost entirely by the
VBGF and M, because the catch LFD carries no information there (fitted gear selectivity ~0 below
25 mm).
Results, share of the BIOMASS of the >= 17 mm (UWTV-counted) stock:
    CL <= 25 mm: 0.07     CL <= 30 mm: 0.15-0.16     CL <= 35 mm: 0.28-0.30
    CL <= 40 mm: 0.43-0.47     CL <= 45 mm: 0.59-0.63
  (share of NUMBERS: <= 30 mm 0.49-0.50; <= 40 mm 0.78-0.80)
  Biomass below 17 mm (settlement to UWTV floor) = ~2 % of the >= 17 mm biomass (M 0.3 or 0.6).
Selectivity-weighted (Armstrong 1991 Table 11 SEL applied to that population, J):
  effective vulnerable biomass share 0.59-0.60 (cod age 6+), 0.72-0.73 (cod age 4),
  0.23-0.25 (cod age 2). I.e. large cod "see" ~60 % of the Nephrops biomass at full rate.
Catch-LFD version (gear-selected, under-represents < 30 mm; J): CL <= 30 mm 4.7 % of catch weight,
  <= 40 mm 40 %, <= 45 mm 64 %.
De Clerck & Sterk window (J): 96 % of prey of cod < 66 cm and 81 % of prey of cod >= 66 cm were
  <= 39 mm CL -> a window "<= 40 mm" holds ~45 % of the >= 17 mm biomass; for small/medium cod a
  window "<= 35 mm" (90th pct of their prey) holds ~29 %.
J synthesis: there is no hard size refuge; the refuge is partial and predator-size dependent.
  Biomass in the main window (17-35/40 mm) ~0.3-0.45 of the stock; selectivity-weighted ~0.6 for
  big cod, ~0.25 for 2-yr cod. Caveats: Irish Sea / North Sea prey sizes (populations of smaller
  Nephrops than FU 3-4); the steady state assumes constant recruitment and the annex growth.

Q2.5 Juveniles: settlement size, burrows, emergence
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Smith 1987, PhD thesis, Univ. Glasgow, "The biology of larval and juvenile Nephrops norvegicus (L.)
in the Firth of Clyde" [F]  https://theses.gla.ac.uk/3511/  (data/smith1987_thesis/)
  - Table 4.1a carapace length (mm): Z1 1.74, Z2 2.18, Z3 2.56, PL1 3.08, PL2 4.00, PL3 4.74,
    PL4 5.75, PL5 7.15, PL8 9.64, PL11 14.12, PL12 16.00 (reared).
  - [F of Smith; the 10-15 mm emergence size is [S] via Smith, Chapman 1980 not read]
    "Field evidence suggests that most Nephrops settle on the sea bed soon after metamorphosis and
    remain within the confines of their burrows, which in the past have only been discovered in
    association with adult burrows. It seems that they do not emerge from their burrows until the
    end of their first year, when they have reached a carapace length of 10 - 15mm (Chapman, 1980)."
  - Abstract: "the 1st postlarval stage is a transitional form between the planktonic and benthic
    environments. Final recruitment to the benthos may be delayed until sometime after the 1st
    postlarval stage." "Juveniles beyond the 1st postlarval stage are capable of constructing their
    own burrows but will enter inhabited adult burrows through surface openings. If the inhabitant
    does not evict the recruiting juvenile it excavates an adjoining burrow through one of the
    walls of the adults burrow to form a juvenile / adult association. These associations are
    known to occur in the field".
Tuck et al. 1994 (Ophelia, doi:10.1080/00785326.1994.10429547): not open access, not read.
J: juveniles < ~15 mm CL live in burrows (own or attached to adult burrows) and are not in the
  emerged, visible fraction; their biomass is ~2 % of the >= 17 mm stock (Q2.3), so this refuge
  matters little for biomass.

Q6.1 Nephrops: larval duration, settlement, larval dispersal
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Smith 1987 thesis (Firth of Clyde) [F]  https://theses.gla.ac.uk/3511/
  - "Under the culture conditions applied in this study 12C was the lowest temperature for which
    complete larval development (to 1st stage postlarvae) was accomplished, taking 46 days."
  - "From laboratory observations involving a single individual Hillis (1974) reported that the
    full larval phase was completed in 39 days at 12C" [S via Smith].
  - Table 4.2 intermoult periods (zoeae from the 14 C trial, postlarvae at 13.5 +- 1.5 C):
    Z1 6.96 d, Z2 9.33 d, Z3 12.75 d (zoeal phase ~29 d at 14 C, J sum), PL1 13.70 d, PL2 15.90 d.
  - "During the 3rd zoeal stage the light response changed from photopositive to photonegative and
    Nephrops may first come into contact with the sea bed towards the end of this stage."
  - Settlement size: first postlarva CL 3.08 mm (Table 4.1a), see Q2.5.
  - "The results from tagging experiments (Chapman and Bailey, 1987) suggest that these regions of
    'unsuitable' sediment form boundaries to the dispersal of Nephrops. As a consequence the
    Nephrops communities within each mud patch may be self-contained forming a separate
    'stocklet'. This isolation, however, will probably not be maintained during the pelagic larval
    phase" [S for Chapman & Bailey 1987, via Smith].
* McGeady, Lordan & Power 2019, MEPS 631:141-155, doi:10.3354/meps13142 [F]
  (data/twilight_migrators_ulster/)
  - "Observed developmental rates of Stage I and II larvae (combined) range from 33 d at 9C to 13 d
    at 15C (Thompson & Ayers 1989, Dickey-Collas et al. 2000). However, the larval period is further
    prolonged when the duration of Stage III is accounted for."
  - Particle tracking over 60 d (ROMS): western Irish Sea "the no DVM scenario resulted in the
    highest retention (32.3%) and an advection distance of 99.8 +- 68.1 km. Retention was reduced
    by more than half for the twilight (15.1%) and nocturnal (15.0%) DVM scenarios. Advection
    distances were 125.0 +- 81.4 km for the twilight and 120.5 +- 80.3 km for the nocturnal DVM";
    Aran grounds "181.2 +- 98.9 km for twilight DVM and 199.5 +- 106.4 km for nocturnal DVM. The
    maximum distance advected ... was > 580 km."
* ICES stock annex FU 3-4 [F]: "No information is available on the extent to which larval mixing
  occurs between Nephrops stocks, but the similarity in stock indicator trends between FUs 3 and 4
  for both Denmark and Sweden indicates that recruitment has been similar in both areas."
J: pelagic phase ~1-2 months (13-33 d for stages I-II, ~30-46 d to postlarva at 12-14 C; longer at
  Skagerrak spring temperatures); larvae are advected ~100-200 km, i.e. further than the 60 km grid.
  Recolonisation of a depleted patch by settlement is therefore not limited by local spawners; it
  is limited by growth: settlers need ~1.6-2 yr to 17 mm and ~3.5-4 yr to 30 mm (Q2.4).

Q6.2 Nephrops: adult movement
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Power et al. 2019, Sci. Data 6:7, doi:10.1038/s41597-019-0013-x [F, via PMC6437642]
  Clew Bay (Ireland), 1177 PIT-tagged animals released 2013, 205 recaptured 2014 (~340 d at
  liberty), 36 in 2015 (~725 d):
  "Mean distance moved away from the release point of 231.47 +- 82.97 m by female tagged
  individuals." "Mean distance moved away from the release point of 232.41 +- 83.15 m by male
  tagged individuals." (pooled over recaptures after 1 and 2 years; ground left fallow)
* Vigo Fernandez 2023, PhD thesis Univ. Barcelona, ch. 2.3 (MEPS 674:173-188 in published form),
  315-475 m depth, NW Mediterranean 10 km2 no-take reserve [F]  https://hdl.handle.net/2445/204581
  (data/vigo2023_thesis/)
  - "The total distance travelled per day for these individuals ranged from 1.05 to 266.97 m d-1"
  - "once settled in a place, their home ranges reached approximate sizes of 17.75 to 736.25 m2"
  - "most individuals dispersed a few hundred metres"; "Tag-recapture data indicated minimal
    spillover of biomass, implying that Norway lobsters are site settled and do not perform large
    movements." Recapture 5 of 216 coded tags (2.32 %) by trawlers outside the reserve.
  - acoustic tags on animals 31-50.5 mm CL.
* MarLIN BIOTIC: adult dispersal potential "10-100m" [S, search snippet; page not read].
J: adult net displacement ~0.2-0.3 km/yr (straight-line, release to recapture); daily paths of
  1-270 m stay inside a home range of < 0.001 km2. Adult immigration into a depleted patch is
  therefore an edge effect of a few hundred metres per year, negligible on a 1 km cell.

Q6.3 Nephrops: recovery after closure / hypoxia
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Vigo et al. 2023, ICES J. Mar. Sci., doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsad130 (thesis ch. 2.4) [F, thesis]
  - "After 4 years of closure, the Norway lobster population increased in abundance, biomass, body
    size, and trophic level in the no-take reserve. Our approach also revealed an increase in Norway
    lobster biomass beyond its boundaries, suggesting a spillover effect."
  - "Here, we report that the Norway lobster population increased by 60% compared to the baseline
    state before the closure. Moreover, we observed a decrease in abundance and biomass in the CA
    during the study period, amounting to about 80% relative to the baseline state"
  - "These results corroborate a previous evaluation conducted 2.5 years after the closure using
    ROV video surveys".
  (Recovery from a fished, not an emptied, state; Mediterranean slope.)
* Baden, Pihl & Rosenberg 1990, MEPS 67:141-155 (SE Kattegat hypoxia) [F]
  https://int-res.com/articles/meps/67/m067p141.pdf (data/baden1990/)
  - "During the study period (October 1984 to September 1989) lobster biomass decreased in the area
    from 10.8 kg h-1 (catch per unit effort) to zero (estimated during the last 12 mo of the
    investigation)."
  - "In September 1988 the mean biomass at the 12 stations was 3.5 kg h-1, but in the following
    3 mo and in April and September 1989 no lobsters were caught at these stations."
  - "Survival of N. norvegicus exposed to 15 and 10 % O2-saturation was 4 wk and 2 to 4 d"
  - In 1985-86 autumns "large quantities of dead lobster (up to 50 % of the total catch) were
    caught"; "In the autumn of 1987 a higher biomass (3.3 to 5.0 kg h-1) was observed compared to
    the 2 previous years" (partial recovery within 1 yr after 1986, J reading).
  No data on recolonisation after 1989 in this paper.
* Smith & Jensen 2008 (ICES, closed-area modelling): "Implementation of a closed area also led to
  oscillations in stock biomass, recruitment, and yield over several years after the closure"
  [S, search snippet].
J: a 1-km cell emptied of Nephrops refills by settlement (annual, from outside) and growth; the
  biomass needs at least the 3-5 yr it takes settlers to reach 30-40 mm (Q2.4); a 4-yr closure
  gave +60 % (Vigo). Recolonisation of biomass: years (3-10), not months.

Q6.4 Pandalus borealis (Skagerrak / Norwegian Deep)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* DNV GL 2015, MSC assessment report, Swedish Pandalus fishery [F of the report, underlying studies
  S] (file "../Bottensamhallets epifauna/data/lit/msc_se_pra.pdf"):
  - "In Skagerrak and the North Sea, spawning and mating takes place in October/November and
    females carry the fertilized eggs under the abdomen until hatching takes place in March the
    following year (Bohle, 1977). The species has five pelagic larval stages which drift with ocean
    currents for 45-90 days depending on the ambient sea temperature before settling on the bottom
    (Aschan and Ingvalsen, 2009; Shumway et al., 1985). Particle tracking models reveal that the
    larvae of P. borealis may be transported as far as 300km during the pelagic phase (Pedersen et
    al. 2003)."
  - "Migration of egg-carrying females into shallower waters in connection with egg-hatching has
    been observed (Horsted, 1978), juveniles may migrate from shallower to deeper water (Smidt,
    1981)"
  - Knutsen et al. 2014: "spatial genetic structure among oceanic samples from the Skagerrak and
    eastern North Sea was weak and non-significant".
* Sovik & Thangstad 2021, NAFO SCR Doc. 21/001 [F] (file "../Bottensamhallets epifauna/data/
  pandalus/scr21-001.pdf"): "The high abundance of 1-year old shrimp in Skagerrak indicates that
  these waters constitute a nursery area for the stock. ... a good correlation between the number of
  1-year old and 2-year old shrimp in respectively Skagerrak and the Norwegian Deep suggests that
  there is a westward migration or drift of small shrimp from Skagerrak to the Norwegian Deep"
  (i.e. redistribution on a ~1-yr, 100-km scale; J).
* Pedersen et al. 2003 (Barents Sea particle tracking; Nofima record): "the distance travelled by a
  shrimp larvae varied between 0 and 330 km and the annual mean distance varied between 74 and
  122 km" [S, search snippet].
J: Pandalus is pelagic for 1.5-3 months, adults are swimmers with diel vertical migration; a 60 km
  grid is re-stocked each year by settlement and by juvenile/adult redistribution. Recolonisation
  time ~1 yr (one settlement season; recruits reach the fishery at ~2 yr).

Q6.5 Crabs, hermit crabs, Munida, Crangon allmanni, mysids
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Hermit crabs: Ramsay, Kaiser & Hughes 1996, MEPS 144:63-72 [F]
  https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps/144/m144p063.pdf (data/ramsay1996/)
  Site ~40 m depth off Anglesey, Irish Sea, April 1995. Table 2, P. bernhardus caught per 1000 m2
  (control / treatment): before 9.7 / 4.5; 1 d after 5.0 / 12.8; 2 d after 7.1 / 81.8 (p<0.001);
  3 d after 7.6 / 60.5 (p<0.001); 4 d after 6.0 / 40.6 (ns). J: a ~10-fold local aggregation within
  2 d, decaying over ~4+ d (control waylines 250 m away).
  "Catch numbers of P. bernhardus were significantly higher on the treatment wayline 2 and 3 d
  after fishing, whilst on the fourth day they were no longer significantly different." "These
  results suggest that P. bernhardus migrate into recently trawled areas" (4 m beam trawl).
  Ramsay et al. 1998, JEMBE 224:73-89: P. bernhardus "moved into the areas fished" at one site but
  "no detectable increases" at two others [S, search snippet].
