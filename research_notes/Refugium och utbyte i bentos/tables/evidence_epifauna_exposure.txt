Munida 26 %; Pandalus borealis 7 %; small shrimps 4 %; mysids 3 %.
Predators considered: cod, haddock, whiting, saithe, flatfish (long rough dab), rays, gurnards.

S1. Nephrops emergence (Q1) -- the main number for the group
------------------------------------------------------------
- Timing at 100-300 m: two bouts (dawn, dusk) at ~100-150 m, one midday bout at ~200+ m.
  N Kattegat 120-135 m "Sunrise and sunset", 205-220 m "Middle of day" (Hoglund & Dybern 1965 in
  Farmer 1975 FAO Fish. Synop. 112, Table XXI) [S]; Sarda 1995: single peak "at around noon in the
  vicinity of 150 m" [F]; Aguzzi et al. 2021 (UWTV, 51-160 m) "diurnal with a plateau shape" [F].
- Fraction out at once (undisturbed population, UWTV): 0.06-0.12 visible per burrow at peak windows
  (Aguzzi 2021 Table 3) [F]; fully emerged (visible minus door-keepers) 0.037-0.066 at 101-160 m (J).
  Door-keepers are 18-41 % of visible animals on the shelf, 32-54 % on the slope [F], 53 % in Biscay
  (Bahamon et al. 2025, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsaf205) [F].
- Trawl catch per swept area is 0.4-2.5 % of burrow density (Tuck et al. 1997, Clyde) [F, ratio J];
  "only 0.5 percent of all N. norvegicus occurring in the path of the trawl were captured" (DAFS 1966a
  in Farmer 1975) [S].
- Hours out per animal: UWTV implies ~0.4-0.7 h/day fully out (+0.2-0.4 h at the burrow mouth) (J).
  Acoustic telemetry (Aguzzi et al. 2023 Rev Fish Biol Fish 33:1465, doi:10.1007/s11160-023-09787-2,
  NW Mediterranean 315-475 m, 25 creel-caught animals tagged and released): burrow-holding day-night
  subset (n = 12) fully out 34.8 % of the time (8.4 h/d; range 6.5-73.8 %), door-keeping 16.2 %,
  in burrow 49.0 % (J, recomputed from Table 2 [F]; the paper's Table 3 does not add up).
  THE TWO METHODS DISAGREE BY ~5x. The telemetry animals were displaced and released at high density,
  and "no detection" is only a proxy for "in burrow"; the UWTV value is from undisturbed populations.
  J: central exposed fraction 0.07 (0.03-0.35). The current 0.10 lies inside the UWTV-based range
  (near its top) and well below the telemetry value.
- Exchange time scale: 1-2 bouts per day; "the same animals do emerge constantly over consecutive days"
  (Aguzzi 2023) [F]. Hidden <-> exposed cycle 12-24 h (J), i.e. the hidden pool turns over within 2-4
  ticks; it is a diel phase, not a permanent refuge. Home range of settled animals 17.75-736 m2
  (radius 2-15 m; Vigo et al. 2021, thesis 2023) [F]; displaced adults settle within ~1 month [F].
- Berried females: egg laying Aug-Sep, hatching Apr-Jun in the Norwegian Deep (IMR 2021 narrative,
  citing Farmer and Powell & Eriksson) [S]; incubation 8-10 months in UK waters (Farmer 1975 Table V)
  [S] -> 7-10 months in the burrow, mostly unfed ("berried females can go without eating for nearly a
  year", Sarda 1995) [F]. Counter-evidence: in the Mediterranean berried females emerged rhythmically
  like the rest (Aguzzi 2003) [F]. Annual vs biennial spawning in Scandinavia is UNRESOLVED (Sarda 1995
  "probably almost entirely biennial" [F]; IMR 2021 "once a year" [S]).
  Kattegat/Skagerrak catches 2005-13: ovigerous ~5 % in trawls vs ~23 % in creels; females 32 % of
  trawl catches vs 40 % in creels (Hornborg et al. 2017, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsw153) [F]. The assessment
  uses M 0.2 for mature females vs 0.3 for others "based on the assumption of reduced burrow emergence
  while ovigerous" (stock annex nep.fu.3-4) [F]. Female share of the population: not measured (J ~0.5).
- Day/night trawl catch ratio as a number: NOT FOUND for Nephrops.

S2. Size refuge from fish (Q2)
------------------------------
- No hard size refuge. Cod 26-105 cm ate Nephrops 7-57 mm CL; median prey CL 20 / 23 / 32 mm in cod
  26-45 / 46-65 / 66-105 cm; 96 % of prey <= 39 mm in cod < 66 cm, 81 % in cod >= 66 cm (De Clerck &
  Sterk 1980 ICES CM 1980/G:43, Table 5 [F]; medians J). Slope ~0.25 mm CL per cm cod (J). Whiting
  21-60 cm took 9-38 mm [F]. Firth of Forth cod <= 64 cm took 8-30 mm, mean 20 mm vs 28 mm in the trawl
  (Kinnear & Livingstone 1979) [F]. Irish Sea cod: mode 22-26 mm in every cod age; selectivity on the
  >41 mm class still 0.48-0.62 of the peak in cod age 4-6+ (Armstrong et al. 1991, ICES CM 1991/G:15
  Table 11) [F]. Haddock, rays, gurnards, long rough dab: no prey sizes found.
- Share of the >= 17 mm FU 3-4 biomass (steady-state model from stock-annex growth/M and the digitised
  2023 catch LFD, J): <= 30 mm 0.16, <= 35 mm 0.29, <= 40 mm 0.45; Armstrong-selectivity weighted ~0.6
  (large cod), ~0.25 (2-yr cod). Animals < 17 mm hold ~2 % of the biomass.
- Years in the window (VBGF, J): 17 -> 30 mm ~2 yr; 17 -> 40 mm 3.8 (males) - 5.4 yr (females).
- Juveniles: settle at 3.1 mm CL [F], live in their own burrows or ones attached to adult burrows
  (Smith 1987 thesis) [F]; first emerge at 10-15 mm (Chapman 1980 via Smith) [S]. Small for biomass.

S3. Northern shrimp (Q3)
------------------------
- Males and small / non-ovigerous shrimp leave the bottom dusk to dawn; ovigerous females stay
  (Shumway et al. 1985, FAO Fish. Synop. 144 -- not 142) [F]; time off bottom scales with night length
  (Barr 1970) [A]. Day/night bottom-trawl ratios 1.8-3.5 (Davis Strait; Jones & Parsons 1978 2.47,
  midnight catch 0.404 of noon) [F]; 1.9-6.33 (Frechette 1983 via Baker 2021) [S]; 1.7-2.6 W Greenland,
  largest in autumn, smallest near the solstices (Smidt 1976, J from [F] table).
- 24-h mean on-bottom availability relative to the day level: 0.68 (Aug, Davis Strait), 0.51-0.75
  (Oct-Dec, W Greenland) (J from [F]); ~1 in the Skagerrak Jan-Feb survey (no sun-height effect).
  J: annual mean 0.65-0.85, central 0.75 (current 0.7 at the low end). Exchange time scale 24 h.
- Height of ascent: whole water column in a shallow bay (Barr) [A]; P. montagui > 200 m off bottom
  (Crawford et al. 1992) [A]. No Skagerrak measurement. Off-bottom shrimp are exposed to pelagic fish
  (saithe, blue whiting) instead; no study compares the risk on vs off the bottom.

S4. Small shrimps, crabs, Munida, hermits, mysids (Q4)
------------------------------------------------------
- Mediterranean 24-h trawl series (Aguzzi & Bahamon 2009, doi:10.1016/j.fishres.2009.08.010 [F];
  factors rounded to orders of magnitude): night/day 100 m shelf L. depurator 5, Goneplax 10, Processa
  100; 400 m slope L. depurator 2.5, Goneplax 2, Pontophilus norvegicus 3, Munida tenuimana 2,
  M. intermedia 0.5 (diurnal). Measured densities (Bahamon et al. 2009) give L. depurator 3.4-3.5 on the
  shelf and 0.9-1.3 on the slope (J from [F]). J: Skagerrak 100-300 m light is closer to the slope case.
- Goneplax "was not seen on the mud surface during the day but was common at night"; L. depurator "was
  seen on the mud surface during the day but specimens were inactive" (Atkinson, Lundy, 17 m) [F].
- L. holsatus is day-active in the sublittoral (Abello et al. 1991) [A], which contradicts the
  "buries by day" note in evidence_B; L. depurator is the crab of the Skagerrak mud (J).
- Munida rugosa does not dig; it uses vacated Nephrops/Goneplax/Cepola burrows, stones and crevices and
  stays at the opening (Zainal 1990 thesis) [F]; more full stomachs in the morning (0.41 vs 0.19, J).
- Mysids: migrating mysids "are not abundant at the shelf and trench locations" (Kaartvedt 1989 MEPS
  57:119, Brattegard pers. comm.) [F] -> the Skagerrak stock is probably mostly bottom-bound (J).
  Lophogaster buries by day and the whole population is off the bottom ~10 h/night [F]. Bay of Biscay
  91 m: near-bottom mysids at night ~0.22 of the day number (J from Mees & Jones 1997 review [F/S]).
- Hermit crabs, Crangon allmanni, Pontophilus spinosus, Eualus: no activity or burial data found.
- J exposure (time mean): crabs 0.6 (0.5-0.8), Munida 0.5 (0.3-0.7), small shrimps 0.6 (0.5-0.75),
  mysids 0.6 (0.4-0.9). Exposure of buriers is nocturnal, when visual fish feed less (J).

S5. Refuge saturation (Q5)
--------------------------
- Burrows need 10-100 % silt-clay (stock annex; Campbell et al. 2008/2009) [F]; Fladen density
  0.13 + 0.0056 x (silt-clay % - 10) burrows/m2 [F]; Clyde maximum 1.48 burrows/m2 (Tuck 1997) [F];
  FU 3-4 0.21 (2024), i.e. ~1/7 of the observed maximum (J).
- Territorial, exclusive home ranges, evictions by dominants, fights over burrows even when burrows are
  not limiting (Sbragaglia 2017; Vigo thesis) [F]; male L-inf = 73.0 - 16.3 x burrow density (Tuck 1997)
  [F]; density-independent survival (Power et al. 2020) [F]. Destroyed burrows re-excavated within 1-2
  days (Marrs et al. 1996 via CRR 340) [S]. Burrows up to 1 m2 and 30 cm deep (Stewart 1974) [F].
- J: no evidence of a hard burrow ceiling; at FU 3-4 density the Nephrops refuge is not saturated.
  Munida depends on burrows dug by others and stones, a refuge that can saturate, but no numbers exist.
  Burial in soft mud (Liocarcinus, Processa, Lophogaster) is not space-limited (J).

S6. Recolonisation (Q6)
-----------------------
- Nephrops: larval phase ~1-1.5 months (46 d to first postlarva at 12 C, Smith 1987 [F]; stages I-II
  13-33 d, McGeady et al. 2019 [F]); larvae advected ~100-200 km in 60 d [F], i.e. beyond the grid.
  Adults move ~0.2 km/yr (Power et al. 2019 Sci Data, release-recapture) [F]; "Adult Nephrops only
  undertake very small-scale movements (a few 100 m)" (stock annex) [F]. A 4-yr no-take closure gave
  +60 % abundance (Vigo et al. 2023) [F]; Kattegat NTZ biomass "more than doubled" in ~12 yr (Skold
  et al. 2022) [F]; SE Kattegat catches fell to zero after the 1988 hypoxia (Baden et al. 1990) [F],
  recovery not documented. J: an emptied cell refills in 3-10 yr (settlement plus growth), not months.
- Pandalus: pelagic larvae 45-90 d, transported up to ~300 km (MSC 2015 report citing Shumway,
  Pedersen 2003) [S]; Skagerrak is a nursery with westward drift of 1-2-yr shrimp (Sovik & Thangstad
  2021) [F]. J: ~1 yr.
- Hermit crabs aggregated ~10-fold in trawled lanes within 2 d, gone by day 4 (Ramsay et al. 1996) [F].
  L. depurator has no long-term site attachment; local residence weeks (Glass 1985 thesis) [F].
  Crangon allmanni lives ~1.5 yr, recruits each summer (Blahudka & Turkay 2002) [F]. J: <= 1 yr.
  Munida, mysids: not found.

S7. Measured predation and depletion (Q7, plausibility check)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Nephrops, W Irish Sea 1981-84 (cod abundant): cod ate 1.7-5.4 kt/yr (base 3.05 kt) of a 17-24.7 kt
  2+ stock (Armstrong et al. 1991) [F] -> consumption/biomass 0.10-0.23 /yr (J); M2 by cod 0.04-0.25
  /yr by age vs F up to 0.83 [F]; cod ate ~1/3 of the landings [F].
- Pandalus, Skagerrak/Norwegian Deep: assessment M 0.75 assumed, best fits 0.9-1.2 (Jorgensen et al.
  2014 NAFO SCR 14/066) [F]; predation >= 2 x catch (Munch-Petersen et al. 2010 SCR 10/70) [F]; cod
  stomachs 22 %W Pandalus, blue whiting > 35 cm 19 %W, haddock 2.2, saithe 2.7, whiting 1.0 %W [F].
  Barents Sea: cod eats 15-58 % of the stock per year (Berenboim et al. 1999) [F], 3-5 x the catch
  [F], yet stock change barely tracks consumption (Hvingel 2006; Aschan et al. 2006) [F].
- Crangon crangon (small-shrimp analogue): predation M 4.75 (1971) -> 1.26 /yr (2010) as cod and
  whiting consumption fell 64-72 % (Temming & Hufnagl 2015) [F].
- Worm & Myers 2003: inverse cod-shrimp correlations in 8 of 9 regions [S]; 3 significant after
  autocorrelation correction (Baum & Worm 2009) [F]; Skagerrak is one of the nine, weakest relations at
  the warm southern edge [F]; Skagerrak r not extracted. Lags 1-2 yr (W Greenland, Wieland et al. 2007)
  [F]; 0-5 yr, peak 2 yr for snow crab (Boudreau et al. 2011) [F].
- J: group-weighted M2 by fish ~0.2-0.4 /yr (crabs and mysids unmeasured).

S8. Group-level exposure (J)
----------------------------
Biomass-weighted time-mean exposed fraction: 0.60 x 0.07 + 0.26 x 0.6 + 0.07 x 0.75 + 0.04 x 0.6 +
0.03 x 0.6 = 0.29 (0.27 with Nephrops 0.03; 0.46 with the telemetry value 0.35). Nephrops dominates
the biomass but contributes only ~0.04 of it; the crab component carries most of the exposed biomass
and is the least measured. Exposure is diel for every taxon (12-24 h exchange); the slow refuges are
berried Nephrops females (7-10 months) and the growth through the size window (years), and neither is
absolute.

Q1. EMERGENCE AND BURROW RESIDENCE

Q1a. Timing of emergence and depth dependence (relevant band 100-300 m)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
* Kattegat, Hoglund & Dybern 1965 (in FARMER1975 Table XXI) [F, secondary]:
  "Hoglund and Dybern, 1965   Northern Kattegat  120-135  May   Sunrise and sunset"
  "Hoglund and Dybern, 1965   Northern Kattegat  205-220  May   Middle of day"
  "Jensen, 1962, 1963, 1965a  Skagerrak and Kattegat  >70  Summer  Night"
  At 58 m in the northern Kattegat the times of increased catches shift with month (Jan "Sunrise and
  sunset"; Mar "Night"; Aug "Night and sunset"). -> In the Mareld band the timing is crepuscular at
  ~100-150 m and midday at ~200+ m (J: two old trawl series only).
* Scottish/Irish series, same table [F, secondary]: Sound of Jura 103 m Apr-May "2h after sunrise and
  5h before sunset"; Simpson 1962/65 North Minch 120-130 m Sep "12.00 to 16.00"; South Minch 145-160 m
  Sep "08.00 to 18.00"; Hillis W Irish Sea 130 m Aug-Sep "Midday".
* FARMER1975 p.51 [F]: "in shallow water (30 m) animals are active during the middle of the night,
  Whereas in deeper water (40-90 m) there are two periods of activity which tend to move further apart
  (from midnight) with increasing depth ... It has been suggested that in very deep water N. norvegicus
  is active during the middle of the day." "animals only become active when the light intensity is
  within the range 1 to 10-5 lx".
* SARDA1995 p.83 [F]: "The highest catches are made at dawn or dusk in relatively shallow water. There
  is variability with depth, with the above two periods of activity becoming a single peak at around noon
  in the vicinity of 150 m."
* AGUZZI2003 (W Mediterranean, 100 m and 400 m, 24-h trawl cycles) [F]: "nocturnal with crepuscular peaks
  on the continental shelf, and diurnal on the continental slope." At 100 m "2 peaks of catch were observed
  per day, approximately coinciding with the sunset and the sunrise timing ... Catches attained their
  lowest values during daylight hours. Animals were continuously captured at night time"; at 400 m "a
  single peak at around midday" and "Low but constant levels of sampled organisms were reported during
  the night." Day/night ratio not readable from the text (figures only) - see Not found.
* AGUZZI2021 (Irish UWTV, 3055 transects, May-Sep) [F]: GAM: 15-50 m "nocturnal bimodal ... (hour
  intervals: 2 to 7 and 17 to 23)"; "On deeper shelf areas (from 51 to 160 m depth), the emergence pattern
  becomes diurnal with a plateau shape"; slope 340-570 m "single peak (hour interval: 7 to 17)".
  Table 3 peak windows used for 101-160 m: 05-07 h and 18-20 h.
* BAHAMON2025 (Bay of Biscay UWTV 28-133 m) [F via WebFetch]: peaks "around dawn (04:00-07:00) and about
  2 h before dusk (17:00-19:00)"; tidal modulation: "At low tide (below -1 m), total density declined
  markedly".
* Sex/size: AGUZZI2003 [F]: "Emergence patterns were almost identical for males, females and berried
  females, and these were not size-dependent." SARDA1995 [F]: "Chapman and Howard (1979) noted that the
  amount of time an individual spent outside its burrow was proportional to its size." FARMER1975 [F]:
  "usually only large males were observed during the day in the burrow entrances, whereas the small
  animals (including most of the females) remained completely hidden."
  Lab: Sbragaglia et al. 2013 Sci Mar 77:641 (doi:10.3989/scimar.03902.28C) [A, abstract via search summary;
  full text download blocked]: 52 animals, 12:12 LD; 87 % rhythmic, 13 % arrhythmic; of rhythmic: 54 %
  dusk, 4 % dawn, 10 % diurnal, 19 % nocturnal.
  J: for 100-300 m Skagerrak expect one (midday, >~150-200 m) or two (dawn+dusk, ~100-150 m) emergence
  bouts per day, i.e. bouts_per_day 1-2.

Q1b. Hours out per day / fraction emerged at once
------------------------------------------------
(i) Field individual tracking - AGUZZI2023 (acoustic telemetry, NW Mediterranean slope 315-475 m, 25
    creel-caught adults tagged and released June 2019, up to 107 d) [F]:
  "The individuals showed a relative time of 12.92% and 11.81%, respectively, presenting a door-keeping
   behavior identified. The mean time spent inside the burrows, when detections and movement were 0,
   represented 38.31% of their time series, while the mean relative time in activity was about 49.88%."
  "the day-night periodicity was the rhythm in which individuals presented less activity outside the
   burrows (35.94%)". 12 of 25 animals (48 %) were day-night rhythmic, "with a major activity phase at
   daylight hours"; e.g. ID 14468 "significant increment in displacement ... from 6:00 to 19:00".
  Caveat in the paper itself [F]: "In animals with no burrows (for unknown reasons impairing the digging
   capability), behavioral activity can be measured under the form of a constant wandering on the seabed
   at a periodicity that matches inertial currents." and "Inertial periodicity occurs in animals with no
   clear burrowing-oriented activity (always present on the seabed)." Released animals at "a transitory
   high density" around the release point.
  My recomputation from Table 2 (scripts/telemetry_subset_means.py) [J from F]:
     All 25:              door-keeping 12.9 %, in burrow 38.3 %, fully emerged (>0.5 m/h) 48.8 %
                          (reproduces the authors' 12.92 / 38.31 means)
     Day-night subset (n=12, the burrow-holding animals): door-keeping 16.2 % (3.9 h/d), in burrow 49.0 %
                          (11.8 h/d), fully emerged 34.8 % (8.4 h/d; median 30.1 %, range 6.5-73.8 %)
     Arrhythmic/inertial/other (n=13): fully emerged 61.7 % (14.8 h/d).
  Table 3 inconsistency: its Day-Night row (16.18 / 15.06 / 10.25 / 35.94) does not sum to 100 %, and its
  "0 (D)" (in-burrow) mean 10.25 % does not match Table 2 (mean 49.0 % for the same 12 animals). Table 2
  rows sum to 99.8-100 %, so Table 2 is trusted here (J).
  Detection caveat (J): "in burrow" = no acoustic detection; an animal outside the hydrophone range also
  gives 0 detections, and position error inflates hourly displacement, so the emerged share is uncertain
  in both directions.

(ii) Population snapshot - UWTV visible animals per burrow at peak emergence (AGUZZI2021, Table 3) [F]:
  visible (fully emerged + door-keeping) per burrow 0.06-0.12 at peak windows; depth means 0.08 (15-50 m),
  0.11 (51-100 m), 0.08 (101-160 m), 0.09 (340-570 m). Door-keeping share of visible animals 18-41 %
  (shelf), 32-54 % (slope). "a high proportion of the population remains cryptic even during periods of
  peak emergence"; "the proportion of animals not emerging from their burrows on a daily basis is still
  largely undetermined".
  Fully emerged per burrow at peak = visible x (1 - door share) [J from F, per window]:
     15-50 m 0.041-0.078; 51-100 m 0.075-0.092; 101-160 m 0.037-0.066; 340-570 m 0.034-0.068.
  BAHAMON2025 Table 1 [F via WebFetch]: door-keeping fraction of visible animals 0.315-0.750 by year,
  0.531 overall; mean densities 0.006 ind/m2 door-keeping, 0.009 ind/m2 outside (no burrow density given).
  J, hours-out implied by UWTV: if ~0.04-0.07 of burrow holders are fully out through a ~10 h plateau
  (AGUZZI2021 deep-shelf/slope window 7-17 h) and few are out otherwise, mean time fully out per animal
  ~0.4-0.7 h/day (+0.2-0.4 h door-keeping). Higher if occupancy is < 1 animal per burrow.

(iii) Trawl evidence of a small exposed share (catch per swept area vs burrow density):
  TUCK1997 (Clyde, 47-60 m, annual-mean catches, 22 mm trawl) [F]: "The catch m-2 values for each station
  (Table 3) are nearly two orders of magnitude lower than the burrow density estimates (Table 2)".
  Catch density 0.0033-0.037 m-2 vs burrows 0.46-1.48 m-2. Ratio per station [J]: 0.005, 0.004, 0.014,
  0.025, 0.008, 0.012 (median ~0.01). Includes trawl efficiency < 1 and mixed hours of day.
  FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: "assuming each burrow represents one animal, only 0.5 percent of all
  N. norvegicus occurring in the path of the trawl were captured (DAFS, 1966a)."

(iv) DISCREPANCY (not averaged; for the modeller) [J]:
  Telemetry (burrow-holding subset): ~0.35 of the day fully out (~8 h/d), ~0.5 including door-keeping.
  UWTV: ~0.04-0.09 of burrow holders fully out at the daily peak (< 1 h/d implied).
  Trawl: ~0.5-2.5 % of burrow density caught per pass.
  Candidate reasons: telemetry animals were displaced and released at high density (burrow search and
  evictions raise surface time); "no detection" is a proxy, not observed concealment; telemetry site is
  400 m Mediterranean, UWTV Irish shelf; UWTV "visible" counts only animals inside the camera swath at
  that minute and may miss animals lying flat or far from burrows; burrow:animal ratio may exceed 1
  (AGUZZI2021 questions 1:1 occupancy). The UWTV value is the one measured on undisturbed populations.

Q1c. Excursion distance, single-excursion duration, burrow switching, exchange time scale
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Home range of settled animals, VIGO_THESIS ch. 2.3 (= Vigo et al. 2021) [F]: "We obtained an average
  home range size (UD90) ranging from 17.75 to 736.25 m2, which could correspond to the usual area where
  the species carries out its behavioural, burrow-centred, activities." First month after release: June
  UD90 11223 +- 16023 m2 (n=19), then July 170 +- 317 m2, Aug 1414 +- 2680, Sep 1341 +- 2910.
  "The home range area became significantly smaller within 1 mo, as an indication of the establishment of
  the individuals in their burrows". "the territorial behaviour ... centred in small exclusive individual
  areas"; UDOI: "no overlap between the areas estimated with UD90, nor with UD50 or core areas."
  Radius equivalent of 17.75-736 m2 [J]: sqrt(A/pi) = 2.4-15 m.
* Daily displacement, AGUZZI2023 [F]: "The average displacement distance was 216.0 m/day (min: 90.7 m/day,
  max: 424.7 m/day)." (Table 1 header says "Walked per Day (cm)"; J: metres is consistent with the 0.5 m/h
  door-keeping threshold and ~13 % door-keeping, so the "cm" label is likely a typo; acoustic position
  error inflates the sum.)
* Burrow fidelity, Chapman & Rice 1971 in FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: "some N. norvegicus continue to return
  to the same burrow for several days. However, it was also found that some individuals occupied different
  burrows within a few days, indicating a somewhat loose attachment to a particular burrow".
* Adult movement scale: ANNEX34 [F]: "Adult Nephrops only undertake very small-scale movements (a few
  100 m)". POWER2020 (inshore W Ireland, released tagged animals) [F] Table 1: median distance from
  release site after 1 yr 213-223 m (range 21-536 m, n=204), after 2 yr 182-308 m (n=36); "no differences
  in distance travelled from the point of release between sexes, starting sizes, or amount of time spent
  at liberty". DAFS tagging in FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: "less than 5 miles, with the single exception of
  an individual which appeared to have moved 22 miles."
* Same animals emerge daily, AGUZZI2023 conclusion [F]: "indicating that the same animals do emerge
  constantly over consecutive days".
* Single-excursion duration: not measured as such (see Not found). Population activity window, Chapman &
  Rice 1971 Loch Torridon 30 m June, "21.10 to 03.30" (FARMER1975 Table XXI) [F, secondary] = 6.3 h [J].
  Activity in the entrance before/after foraging [F, FARMER1975]: "animals are present in the burrow
  entrance most frequently before and after foraging".
* Exchange time scale [J]: one (midday) or two (crepuscular) emergence bouts per day, the same individuals
  emerging every day -> a hidden<->exposed cycle of 12-24 h; per-animal exposed bout ~1-8 h depending on
  which estimate in Q1b(iv) is believed (UWTV-implied <1 h vs telemetry 8.4 h/day in one bout). With a
  6-h tick the exposed set turns over within 1-4 ticks.

Q1d. Berried (ovigerous) females
--------------------------------
* Concealment: FARMER1975 [F]: "The general absence of berried females throughout most of the year is
  explained by their tendency to remain within their burrows when incubating eggs." "Attempts to entrain
  ovigerous females to a 12 h:12 h light/dark cycle were unsuccessful ... ovigerous females are usually
  rare in trawl catches, since they probably remain in their burrows and do not emerge to feed".
  Dunthorn 1967 in FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: "berried females eat less than non-berried females,
  particularly during the latter stages of embryonic development, and that in the natural environment they
  may not feed at all." SARDA1995 [F]: "Fasting was observed by Farmer (1975), who reported that berried
  females can go without eating for nearly a year." AGUZZI2021 [F]: "berried females do not emerge".
  Counter-evidence AGUZZI2003 [F]: "present results show that berried females may perform rhythmic
  emergence phased to light intensity cycles like the all other individuals" (Mediterranean slope).
  CRR340 [F]: "Mature female Nephrops have lower burrow emergence when carrying eggs (Thomas and de
  Figueiredo, 1965; Redant, 1987) and, therefore, are typically subject to lower mortality rates";
  ANNEX34 (FU 3-4) [F]: M "0.3 ... for males and immature females, with a value of 0.2 for mature females
  ... based on the assumption of reduced burrow emergence while ovigerous".
* Timing and duration:
  IMR2021 (Norwegian Deep, citing Farmer 1974/75 and Powell & Eriksson 2013) [F, secondary]: "Egg-laying
  takes place over a period of a couple of months (August-September). Females carry the roe until hatching
  takes place in the following year. Hatching of the eggs of one female occurs at night-time over several
  successive days (5-20 days), while egg hatching within a local population takes place over a couple of
  months (April-June)." -> berried ~7-10 months (Aug/Sep to Apr/Jun) [J].
  FARMER1975 Table V [F, secondary]: Scottish waters egg laying "Aug.-Nov.", hatching "May-Aug.", incubation
  "9 months (270)"; NE England "Aug.-Sep.", "May-Aug.", "10 months (300)"; Irish Sea "Aug.-Sep.",
  "Apr.-June", "8 months (240)"; Faroes "9 months (270)". No Skagerrak/Kattegat row.
  SARDA1995 [F]: "The duration of egg incubation varies from 6 months in the Mediterranean to more than 10
  months in Iceland, where spawning takes place biennially."
* Annual vs biennial (CONFLICT, left open):
  SARDA1995 [F]: "Spawning in more northerly latitudes (Faroe Islands, Scandinavia, and Iceland) is
  probably almost entirely biennial."  IMR2021 [F, secondary]: "Nephrops spawns once a year ... In more
  northern waters, egg production may be biannual."  FARMER1975 [F]: share of mature females laying eggs
  each year: Karlovac "84 percent" (Adriatic), Thomas "90 percent" (Scotland), Farmer "91.5 percent"
  (Irish Sea).
* Catch evidence of concealment:
  FARMER1975 Table IX, % ovigerous of all females in catches [F, secondary]: Scottish (Thomas & Figueiredo)
  1.0-18.7 % by month (peak Sep), Irish Sea (Farmer MS) 0-12 %, NE England (Symonds) 0-4 % - far below the
  84-91 % of mature females that spawn, i.e. berried females are mostly not caught.
  FARMER1975 Table VIII, % females in monthly catches [F, secondary]: Scottish 12.2 (Mar) - 46.7 (Aug);
  Irish Sea (O'Riordan) 8 (Mar) - 52 (Jul); Irish Sea (Hillis) 21-69; NE England 4-54 depending on series.
  "throughout most of the year there is a strong preponderance of males in the catches, although during the
  period from egg-hatching to egg-laying the [page break] proportion of females generally increasea [sic,
  OCR] to approximately 50 percent."
  HORNBORG2017 (Swedish Kattegat/Skagerrak observer data 2005-2013) [F via WebFetch]: ovigerous females
  "~23% compared with 5% in trawls" (creels vs trawls, annual average); "40% are females in creels whereas
  32% in trawls".
  TUCK1997 [F]: "During this phase, females tend to remain in their burrows and are seldom caught
  (Chapman, 1980)"; Q2 (Apr-Jun) catches "significantly lower than in the first and fourth quarters".
* Female share of the population: FARMER1975 [F]: "Sex ratio of the population - No data are available."
  J: catches in the hatching-to-spawning window approach 40-60 % female (Table VIII), suggesting a near-even
  population sex ratio if non-berried females are as catchable as males; not a measured value.

Q5. REFUGE SATURATION: IS THE BURROW REFUGE SPACE-LIMITED?
=============================================================================
* Sediment window. ANNEX34 [F]: Nephrops "requires sediment with a silt & clay content of between 10-100%
  to excavate its burrows". CAMPBELL2008 [F]: "Nephrops burrows were not found in sediments composed almost
  entirely of sand (i.e. <10% sediment below 63um)"; North Minch burrows in 10-100 % silt-clay, Fladen
  10-90 %.
* Density vs silt-clay. CAMPBELL2008 Fladen GLM (identity link) [F]: Burrow density ~ (Percentage Clay - 10)
  + Year; intercept 0.1300, % clay slope 0.0056 per %. J: 0.13 burrows/m2 at 10 % -> 0.35 at 50 % -> 0.58 at
  90 % silt-clay (2002 year level). North Minch closer to the "dome-shaped" form; Afonso-Dias 1998 mechanism
  quoted in CAMPBELL2008 [F, secondary]: "in very fine grained, soft muds, Nephrops are able to excavate
  extensive burrow complexes, and competition for space once more reduces population density".
  TUCK1997 [F]: 6 Clyde stations 30-96.5 % silt-clay, burrow density 0.460-1.479 m-2 (Table 2); "Three
  stations (numbers 3, 4 and 5) had similarly high burrow densities (about 1.4 burrows m-2)"; peak "on mixed
  sediments of sand, silt and clay and the decline in density on the finest silt and clay sediments";
  alternative: density "may simply reflect general hydrographic conditions rather than sediment type
  directly" (larval supply).
* Observed maxima (for scale). TUCK1997 1.48 burrows m-2 (Clyde). FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: Loch Torridon
  burrows 1/2.1 m2 (1968) and 1/1.4 m2 (1969) with Nephrops 1/7.8 and 1/5.5 m2; Irish Sea burrows 1/2.1 to
  1/9.3 m2, animals 1/2.5 to 1/8.9 m2; "Densities of burrows reported by DAFS (1966a, 1968b) ranged from
  9/m2 to 12/m, although ... these figures refer to numbers of openings and not burrow systems."
  J: FU 3-4 2024 mean 0.21 burrows/m2 (earlier note) is ~1/7 of the Clyde maximum.
* Burrow size. STEWART1974 [F]: "The burrows are often extensive, with many side branches, and can cover an
  area of up to 1 m2 and penetrate to 30 cm below the surface of the mud."
* Spacing. STEWART1974 (Little Loch Broom, 20-25 m) [F]: "from 20 m downwards the burrow density was greater,
  with a burrow every 2 to 3 m along a straight line" (a line-transect spacing, not a nearest-neighbour
  distance). Dybern & Hoisaeter 1965 in FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: burrows "occurred in groups of 5-10, with
  each group well separated from the next"; Chapman & Rice 1971: "not evenly spaced ... nor were they
  distributed at random ... clumped to some extent"; Atkinson 1974: "the spatial distribution of
  N. norvegicus burrows in Loch Aline is random."
  J (random-pattern expectation, mean NN = 0.5/sqrt(D)): 1.1 m at 0.21 m-2; 0.41 m at 1.48 m-2.
* Territoriality / burrow competition. Farmer 1974a in FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: "strongly territorial ...
  there is a critical distance at which agonistic responses to other N. norvegicus occur"; Rice & Chapman
  1971: "little indication that the burrows act as centres for territorial behaviour"; claw damage
  "males 62 percent, females 41 percent" (Loch Torridon). SBRAGAGLIA2017 (lab, 4 males per tank, one
  burrow per animal) [F via WebFetch]: fighting over burrows "occurred even if these were not a limited
  resource"; hierarchies "within 1-3 days"; "Dominant lobsters are more likely to evict subordinate lobsters
  from their burrows and more successful in defending their own burrows. They also spend more time in
  burrows". VIGO_THESIS [F]: home ranges did not overlap (UDOI); "dominant Norway lobsters tend to show
  burrow-oriented behaviour, remaining close to the entrance most of the time and evicting other animals
  from their burrows". AGUZZI2023 [F]: "animal burrowing capability is influenced by a balance between
  aggressive territorial interactions and overall local demography"; wandering animals possibly "those with
  no burrowing capability due to their subordinate social ranking level".
* Occupancy and sharing. FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: "About 70 percent of the large burrows were found to be
  occupied by N. norvegicus; many of the smaller burrows contained L. friesii." AGUZZI2021 [F]: "two adult
  lobsters are rarely found in the same shelter". CRR340 [F]: "Juvenile burrows tend to be found in close
  proximity to adult systems (Marrs et al., 1996). Some burrow systems are conjunctions of the tunnels of
  adults and one or more juveniles." IMR2021 [F, secondary]: "Juvenile specimens are rarely found outside
  burrows of adults (Tuck et al., 1994)".
* Density-dependent growth. TUCK1997 [F]: "L-inf was found to be inversely correlated with burrow density,
  suggesting that growth may be density-dependent"; Fig. 5 "Y=73.042-16.307X (r2 =0.715, p<0.05)" (X =
  burrows m-2, Y = male L-inf mm); "competition for food could limit growth"; L-inf "also positively
  correlated with infaunal biomass". POWER2020 [F]: "a density-dependent suppression of growth (in body
  size) was observed in male lobsters"; "Growth rates for females did not seem to be associated with the
  local density"; "This suggests density-independent survival rates for Nephrops on the fished grounds."
  IMR2021 [F, secondary]: "Intraspecific competition for food and space is demonstrated by negative
  relationships between size and density (Johnson et al., 2013)."
* Burrow (re)construction time. Marrs et al. 1996 in CRR340 [F, secondary]: "burrows that were destroyed
  experimentally were rapidly re-excavated by the occupants; showing signs of re-excavation within one day,
  and by two days, any amelioration appeared complete" (repair by the resident, not a new burrow).
  Crnkovic 1968 in FARMER1975 [F, secondary]: post-larva "constructed a burrow with 5 openings in 5 days".
  Settlement of displaced adults: VIGO_THESIS [F] home ranges shrank "within 1 mo" after release.
  STEWART1974 [F]: aquarium burrows "were in a state of constant modification".
  CAMPBELL2008 [F]: "the time that a burrow survives after its occupying Nephrops has died, been caught or
  moved on is unknown, as is the speed with which burrow entrances are re-opened after being filled in by
  trawl nets."
* Verdict on saturation [J]: evidence for space limitation is indirect - dome-shaped density vs sediment
  (hypothesised burrow interference in fine mud), exclusive home ranges, evictions, density-dependent male
  growth. No study shows a hard burrow ceiling, and survival was density-independent (POWER2020). At the
  FU 3-4 density (~0.2 m-2) the refuge is unlikely to be saturated relative to observed maxima of ~1.5 m-2.

Q2.3 Share of Nephrops biomass inside the vulnerable window (J, scripts/nephrops_size_window.py)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method (J): steady-state population with constant recruitment at the first-postlarva size
(3.08 mm CL, Smith 1987, see Q2.5), annex VBGF per sex (females switch to 65/0.10 at 29 mm), annex
M (0.3; 0.2 mature females), gear selectivity derived iteratively from the 2023 catch LFD
(s(L) proportional to C(L)/N(L)), F scaled so that catch/abundance(>=17 mm) = the 2021-23
harvest rates (4.2-5.1 %). (The model-vs-observed catch shape printed in stdout D agrees by
construction of the selectivity fit and is NOT a validation.)
Independent checks (J): (i) digitised LFD x annex L-W = 6739 t of catch 2023 vs WGNSSK 2023
landings 6175 t + discards 975 t = 7150 t (6 % low; checks digitisation + L-W); (ii) modelled mean
weight of animals >= 17 mm is 28-29 g vs the 30 g (23-45) adopted earlier in
"../Bottensamhallets epifauna/evidence_A2_nephrops.md" - a consistency check only, since that 30 g
was itself a judgement. The population shape between 17 and ~28 mm is set almost entirely by the
VBGF and M, because the catch LFD carries no information there (fitted gear selectivity ~0 below
25 mm).
Results, share of the BIOMASS of the >= 17 mm (UWTV-counted) stock:
    CL <= 25 mm: 0.07     CL <= 30 mm: 0.15-0.16     CL <= 35 mm: 0.28-0.30
    CL <= 40 mm: 0.43-0.47     CL <= 45 mm: 0.59-0.63
  (share of NUMBERS: <= 30 mm 0.49-0.50; <= 40 mm 0.78-0.80)
  Biomass below 17 mm (settlement to UWTV floor) = ~2 % of the >= 17 mm biomass (M 0.3 or 0.6).
Selectivity-weighted (Armstrong 1991 Table 11 SEL applied to that population, J):
  effective vulnerable biomass share 0.59-0.60 (cod age 6+), 0.72-0.73 (cod age 4),
  0.23-0.25 (cod age 2). I.e. large cod "see" ~60 % of the Nephrops biomass at full rate.
Catch-LFD version (gear-selected, under-represents < 30 mm; J): CL <= 30 mm 4.7 % of catch weight,
  <= 40 mm 40 %, <= 45 mm 64 %.
De Clerck & Sterk window (J): 96 % of prey of cod < 66 cm and 81 % of prey of cod >= 66 cm were
  <= 39 mm CL -> a window "<= 40 mm" holds ~45 % of the >= 17 mm biomass; for small/medium cod a
  window "<= 35 mm" (90th pct of their prey) holds ~29 %.
J synthesis: there is no hard size refuge; the refuge is partial and predator-size dependent.
  Biomass in the main window (17-35/40 mm) ~0.3-0.45 of the stock; selectivity-weighted ~0.6 for
  big cod, ~0.25 for 2-yr cod. Caveats: Irish Sea / North Sea prey sizes (populations of smaller
  Nephrops than FU 3-4); the steady state assumes constant recruitment and the annex growth.

Q2.5 Juveniles: settlement size, burrows, emergence
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Smith 1987, PhD thesis, Univ. Glasgow, "The biology of larval and juvenile Nephrops norvegicus (L.)
in the Firth of Clyde" [F]  https://theses.gla.ac.uk/3511/  (data/smith1987_thesis/)
  - Table 4.1a carapace length (mm): Z1 1.74, Z2 2.18, Z3 2.56, PL1 3.08, PL2 4.00, PL3 4.74,
    PL4 5.75, PL5 7.15, PL8 9.64, PL11 14.12, PL12 16.00 (reared).
  - [F of Smith; the 10-15 mm emergence size is [S] via Smith, Chapman 1980 not read]
    "Field evidence suggests that most Nephrops settle on the sea bed soon after metamorphosis and
    remain within the confines of their burrows, which in the past have only been discovered in
    association with adult burrows. It seems that they do not emerge from their burrows until the
    end of their first year, when they have reached a carapace length of 10 - 15mm (Chapman, 1980)."
  - Abstract: "the 1st postlarval stage is a transitional form between the planktonic and benthic
    environments. Final recruitment to the benthos may be delayed until sometime after the 1st
    postlarval stage." "Juveniles beyond the 1st postlarval stage are capable of constructing their
    own burrows but will enter inhabited adult burrows through surface openings. If the inhabitant
    does not evict the recruiting juvenile it excavates an adjoining burrow through one of the
    walls of the adults burrow to form a juvenile / adult association. These associations are
    known to occur in the field".
Tuck et al. 1994 (Ophelia, doi:10.1080/00785326.1994.10429547): not open access, not read.
J: juveniles < ~15 mm CL live in burrows (own or attached to adult burrows) and are not in the
  emerged, visible fraction; their biomass is ~2 % of the >= 17 mm stock (Q2.3), so this refuge
  matters little for biomass.

  - "Observed developmental rates of Stage I and II larvae (combined) range from 33 d at 9C to 13 d
    at 15C (Thompson & Ayers 1989, Dickey-Collas et al. 2000). However, the larval period is further
    prolonged when the duration of Stage III is accounted for."

    highest retention (32.3%) and an advection distance of 99.8 +- 68.1 km. Retention was reduced
    by more than half for the twilight (15.1%) and nocturnal (15.0%) DVM scenarios. Advection

* ICES stock annex FU 3-4 [F]: "No information is available on the extent to which larval mixing
  occurs between Nephrops stocks, but the similarity in stock indicator trends between FUs 3 and 4
  for both Denmark and Sweden indicates that recruitment has been similar in both areas."
J: pelagic phase ~1-2 months (13-33 d for stages I-II, ~30-46 d to postlarva at 12-14 C; longer at

  Recolonisation of a depleted patch by settlement is therefore not limited by local spawners; it
  is limited by growth: settlers need ~1.6-2 yr to 17 mm and ~3.5-4 yr to 30 mm (Q2.4).

Q6.4 Pandalus borealis (Skagerrak / Norwegian Deep)

* DNV GL 2015, MSC assessment report, Swedish Pandalus fishery [F of the report, underlying studies

  grid is re-stocked each year by settlement and by juvenile/adult redistribution. Recolonisation
  time ~1 yr (one settlement season; recruits reach the fishery at ~2 yr).

Exposure convention used below (J): a bottom-trawl catch ratio measures the share of the population inside

(saithe, blue whiting, herring, mackerel), i.e. to a different model group.

Q3 SUMMARY (J)
  - Who migrates: males and small / non-ovigerous shrimp; ovigerous females stay on the bottom (Shumway;
    Hudon 1992: 75 % of pelagic P. montagui migrants were males). Size effect conflicting (Barr: small migrate
    more; Carlsson 1979 W Greenland: large more).
  - When: dusk to dawn; time off bottom scales with night length (Barr). Day/night contrast largest in autumn
    (equinox), smallest at the solstices at high latitude (Smidt 1976).
  - How much: bottom-trawl catch at night = 0.3-0.55 of the day/noon catch (ratios 1.8-3.5 Davis Strait, up to
    6.33 Canada; P. montagui males 1.52). 24-h mean on-bottom availability relative to the day maximum: 0.51-0.75
    (W Greenland Oct-Dec), 0.68 (Davis Strait August). Large (fishable, mostly female) shrimp: small effect
    (Baker 2024). Skagerrak survey in Jan-Feb (berried females, long nights): no effect (evidence_A1).
  - How high: P. borealis up to the whole water column in shallow bays (Barr; Kachemak Bay), limited by the
    thermocline (Apollonio & Dunton); P. montagui > 200 m above bottom (Crawford 1992). Jones & Parsons: some
    shrimp above the 11 m trawl opening at all hours.
  - Exposure: on the bottom by day = reachable by benthivores; in the water column at night = exposed to
    pelagic/semi-pelagic fish instead. No study found that measures predation risk on vs off the bottom.
  - Implication for f 0.7 (J): a year-round mean of ~0.65-0.85 is consistent with the evidence (lower in
    autumn, ~1 in winter when females are berried); 0.7 sits at the low end of that.

REFUGE SATURATION - what was found (J summary)
=====================================================================================================
  - Burrowers that dig their own refuge (Goneplax, Alpheus, Nephrops): no limit on refuges implied; Goneplax
    burrows 0.93 /m2 at 17 m (Atkinson, Lundy) - occupancy unknown.
  - Munida: does not dig (Zainal 1990); depends on vacated burrows of Nephrops/Goneplax/Cepola, stones and

    sediment is not limited by space (J).
  - Processa, Solenocera, Pontophilus: bury in sediment; unlimited substrate (J).
  - Mysids: Lophogaster burrows; others stay in the bottom 0.5 m by day (refuge = proximity to bottom /
    darkness, not a countable shelter).
  - Hermits: portable shell; shell supply is limiting in P. bernhardus (intertidal/shallow, search snippets),
    unquantified, and not a refuge from fish that crush shells.

    Also: Nephrops "yield of Nephrops declined by 0.8 to 2.4 thousand tons when fishing

D3.2 North Sea Ecopath (Mackinson & Daskalov 2007) - stomach-based diet matrix, mass-balanced
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    Mackinson, S. & Daskalov, G. 2007. An ecosystem model of the North Sea to support an
    ecosystem approach to fisheries management: description and parameterisation. Cefas Sci.
    Ser. Tech. Rep. 142. https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf  [F]
    Nephrops (group 55), 1991 balanced model: B 1.1 t/km2 (input 0.98), P/B 0.37 /yr, Q/B 1.85,
    EE 0.9937. "In the model an initial estimate of P/B of 0.35 y-1 is used based on M=0.25 and
    F=0.1." "ICES (WGNNSK, 2005) assume a natural mortality rate of 0.3 for males and immature
    females, with a value of 0.2 for mature females." "Cod have been identified as a predator of
    Nephrops in some areas, and the generally low level of the cod stock is likely to have
    resulted in reduced predation." Nephrops landings+discards in 1991 model: 10,317 t.
    Shrimp (group 58; Crangon allmanni 14 %, P. montagui 10 %, Processa 6 %, P. borealis 4 % of
    the group's survey biomass, C. crangon 36 %): B 0.50 t/km2, P/B 3 /yr, Q/B 10, EE 0.4486;
    catch 24,738 t.
    -> J (Ecopath identity M2 = P/B x EE - F, model area ~570,000 km2):
       Nephrops F = 10,317/(1.1 x 570,000) = 0.016; M2 ~ 0.37 x 0.994 - 0.016 = 0.35 /yr.
       Shrimp F = 24,738/(0.50 x 570,000) = 0.087; M2 ~ 3 x 0.449 - 0.087 = 1.26 /yr.
       These are balance outputs that inherit the ASSUMED P/B (M = 0.25 for Nephrops), so they
       are not independent measurements; they show only that stomach-based consumption in the
       North Sea model is compatible with M2 ~0.3 /yr on Nephrops and ~1.3 /yr on shrimps.

D3.3 Kattegat no-take zone (cod closure area), Nephrops response
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    Skold, M. et al. 2022. A no-take zone and partially protected areas are not enough to save
    the Kattegat cod, but enhance biomass and abundance of the local fish assemblage. ICES J.
    Mar. Sci. 79: 2231-2246. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsac152  [F, read via WebFetch extraction of the
    OUP full-text page]
    - 426 km2 year-round NTZ from 2009; survey 2008-2021.
    - Nephrops: "significantly increased in biomass in the NTZ" relative to control areas;
      "the biomass of N. norvegicus in the NTZ has more than doubled over time"; abundance
      "more variable", main effect a larger mean size.
    - Cod: "neither cod abundance nor biomass showed a significant increase".
    - The paper does not discuss cod predation on Nephrops (per the extraction).
    -> J: Nephrops > x2 in ~12 yr when trawling stopped and cod did NOT recover; this is a
       release from fishing, not a predator test. It does show that Nephrops biomass on a
       Kattegat mud bottom responds on a ~decade scale to removal of one mortality source of
       order F, and that no compensating cod predation appeared.

D4. FURTHER PREDATOR-CHANGE -> PREY-CHANGE EVIDENCE (shrimps)
=====================================================================
(a) NE Newfoundland Shelf (SFA6), Pandalus after the cod collapse.
    Lilly, Parsons & Kulka 2000. Was the increase in shrimp biomass on the Northeast
    Newfoundland Shelf a consequence of a release in predation pressure from cod?
    J. Northw. Atl. Fish. Sci. 27: 45-61. https://journal.nafo.int/Portals/0/2000-2/lilly.pdf [F]
    Cod: "the 3+ biomass declined from about 3 000 000 tons in the early-1960s to less than
    500 000 tons in the mid-1970s, increased to about 1 000 000 tons in 1984-85 and then declined
    to less than 20 000 tons by 1993".
    Consumption: "The highest consumption calculated in this manner was 55 000 tons in 1983
    (Table 4; Fig. 5). For most years prior to 1990, the consumption tended to lie in the range
    10 000-25 000 tons." "The contribution of shrimp to the total food consumption of cod in SFA6
    peaked at 7.4% in the 45-53 cm length-group and averaged 5.9% in the population."
    Shrimp: Hawke Channel survey "biomass estimates increased from an average of about 3 000 tons
    in 1979-81 to about 7 800 tons in 1988-90"; SFA6 survey index "about 400 000-500 000 tons in
    1996-98" (catchability "expected to be less than 1").
    Conclusion: "Results are equivocal ... it appears that an initial increase in shrimp biomass
    must have occurred during the early to mid-1980s and was not related to changes in the cod,
    whereas a larger increase in shrimp biomass in the 1990s was related at least in part to the
    collapse of the cod."
    -> J: cod -98 % (1984-85 to 1993); shrimp index rose by more than an order of magnitude over
       ~1980-1998, but the cod consumption (10-25 kt/yr) is only ~2-6 % of the late-1990s shrimp
       index, so the predation term alone is small relative to the later stock; the 1980s rise
       preceded the cod decline.

(b) West Greenland, Pandalus vs cod.
    Wieland, Storr-Paulsen & Sunksen 2007. J. Northw. Atl. Fish. Sci. 39: 21-33.
    doi:10.2960/J.v39.m579  https://journal.nafo.int/Portals/0/2007-2008/2-wieland.pdf [F]
    "Survey estimates of total stock size of Northern shrimp were negatively correlated with
    Atlantic cod biomass lagged by one and two years until the beginning of the 1990s.
    Thereafter, the Atlantic cod stock in West Greenland offshore waters collapsed, but no
    immediate response of Northern shrimp to the released predation pressure was observed.
    Recruitment and subsequently stock biomass of Northern shrimp increased first about 10 years
    later following a moderate increase in the temperature conditions".
    "highly significant (P <0.01) negative correlations between the CPUE for Northern shrimp and
    Atlantic cod biomass with time lags of one or two years were found when excluding the period
    after 1991 or 1992"; over the whole series no significant correlation at any lag.
    Secondary in the same paper: "Short-term responses, i.e. with time lags of less than 2 or 3
    years, of stock size of Northern shrimp to changes in Atlantic cod biomass have been
    documented in the Barents Sea and Icelandic waters (Stefansson et al., 1998; Berenboim et
    al., 2000)."  Greenland halibut consumption of shrimp "about 10 fold lower than those
    computed for Atlantic cod in West Greenland waters (Grunwald, 1998)". [S for the cited works]
    -> time scale of shrimp response to cod: 1-2 yr lags where a link is seen; the link can
       break down (spatial overlap index fell from 0.28-0.64 to 0.09).

(c) North Sea brown shrimp (Crangon crangon) - analogue for the model's small shrimps
    (C. allmanni, Pontophilus, Processa, P. montagui).
    Temming & Hufnagl 2015. Decreasing predation levels and increasing landings challenge the
    paradigm of non-management of North Sea brown shrimp (Crangon crangon). ICES J. Mar. Sci.
    72: 804-823. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsu194  [F, read via WebFetch extraction of the OUP
    full-text page (first ~45 % of page text); numbers below as extracted]
    - Consumption of shrimp >50 mm, 1971-1980 averages: cod 28,345 t/yr (RFA6 approach) /
      30,131 (North Sea approach); whiting 13,427 / 15,535. 2001-2010: cod 759 / 6,229;
      whiting 14,389 / 6,738.
    - Juveniles (<50 mm) consumed in 1991: whiting 65,000 t, cod 18,000 t.
    - Natural (predation-dominated) mortality, RFA6: M = 4.75 /yr (1971, F 0.68),
      3.15 (1995, F 3.3), 1.26 (2010, F 4.4); 2001-2010 mean M = 1.7 /yr.
    - Landings 13,689 t/yr (1971-80) -> 31,210 t/yr (2001-10); landings/consumption ratio
      0.12-0.14 (1971) -> 3.4-5.6 (2005). Abstract: "the fishery has become the main mortality
      source of adult brown shrimp (>50 mm)"; landings since 2000 ~40 % higher than in the
      1980s-1990s.
    -> J: cod+whiting consumption fell ~64-72 % between 1971-80 and 2001-10 while landings
       (a stock proxy) rose ~2.3x; predation M on a small shrimp fell from ~4.8 to ~1.3 /yr.
       Shallow coastal species; use only as an order of magnitude for small-shrimp M2.

  J (interpretation for the plausibility check, not a source value): measured/assessed M2 on
  Nephrops by fish is low, ~0.1-0.35 /yr, and cod removed roughly a third of the landings in the
  Irish Sea when cod were abundant; on Pandalus it is high, ~0.5-1.2 /yr, and exceeds fishing;
  on small shrimps (Crangon) it can be several per year. A biomass-weighted M2 for
  mobile_epifauna (60 % Nephrops at ~0.2, 26 % crabs unknown, 7 % Pandalus at ~0.8, 4 % small
  shrimps at ~1.5-4, 3 % mysids unknown) would be of order 0.2-0.4 /yr if crabs/mysids sit near
  the Nephrops level; crabs and mysids have no measured M2 in what was found. Field evidence
  that fish predation change alone shifts the prey biomass is strong in the meta-analysis
  (Worm & Myers; 8 of 9 regions inverse, 3 significant after correction) but weak or
  absent in several single-stock tests (Barents Sea, West Greenland after 1991, NE
  Newfoundland 1980s), and is weakest at the warm southern range edge, where the Skagerrak
  lies.
