#####################################################################################
Q1. FORAGING ARENA THEORY AND ECOSIM
#####################################################################################

1(a) Vulnerable-pool dynamics, exchange rates, symmetry
-------------------------------------------------------
* Continuous-exchange model, Ahrens, Christensen & Walters, EwE book ch. 15 eq. 2 [X of Ahrens et al.
  2012; attribution: "The chapter is adapted from Ahrens, R.N.M., Walters, C.J. and Christensen, V.
  (2012), Foraging arena theory. Fish and Fisheries, 13: 41-59"; equation read from the rendered page,
  PDF p. 114]:
      "prey exchange between the vulnerable and invulnerable states at instantaneous rates v and v',
       so that Vi gains individuals at rate v(Bi-Vi) and loses them at rates v'Vi and aViBj."
      dVi/dt = v (Bi - Vi) - v' Vi - aij Vi Bj                                   (eq. 2)
      Qj = aij Vi Bj                                                             (eq. 1, mass action in the arena)
  "the predator rate of effective search aij has units of area or volume per time searched by the
   predator divided by the area or volume (A) of the foraging arena."
* Same flow diagram: Christensen & Walters 2004 Fig. 1 [F]; 2005 guide Exhibit 39 [F]; 2008 guide
  Fig. 3.3 [F]: flows v(Bi - Vi) (unavailable -> available), v Vi (back), aij Vi Bj (to predator);
  caption: "Fast equilibrium between the two prey states implies Vi = vBi/(2v + aBj). Based on
  Walters et al. (1997)."
* With handling time (2008 guide, section "Predator satiation and handling time" [F], p. 38-39):
      "Vij is calculated by solving the 'fast dynamics' equation
       dVij/dt = v(Bi-Vij) - v'Vij - aijPjVij / (1 + hj Si aij Vij) for Vij,
       while assuming the vulnerability-exploitation dynamics are fast enough to keep dV/dt near zero
       (vulnerability exchange rates v, v' large)."
  and: "providing an exact value for the Vij ... at each simulation time step would require excessive
  computer time" (an iterative update of the search-time ratio Psj = 1/(1 + hj Si aij Vij) is used).
* SYMMETRY - yes by default:
  - 2005 guide p. 86 [F]: "it is assumed that there is a flow (vij) of organisms from the invulnerable
    to the vulnerable stage, and conversely (vji), with the assumption v = vij = vji."
  - Shared-arena chapter (EwE book ch. 27, [X] of Walters & Christensen 2007): "The back-exchange
    parameter v' is set equal to v since it cannot be estimated separately from the alpha_a parameter."
  - Cefas TR 142 Appendix 1 [F]: "vij and v'ij are prey vulnerability parameters, with default setting
    vij = v'ij."
  - Consequence (J, follows directly from eq. 2 with Bj = 0): unexploited equilibrium V/B = v/(v+v') = 0.5.
  - Foraging-time exception [F, 2008 guide s.3.6 and book ch. 18]: with prey relative feeding time T_i,
    "time spent vulnerable to predation, as measured by v'ij ... is inversely related to Tit, i.e.,
    v'ijt = v'ij / Tit" (Ti0 = 1, v'ij = vij at baseline). So v' departs from v during a run whenever the
    feeding-time adjustment is on.
* V IS NOT A STATE VARIABLE IN ECOSIM (J, from the quotes above): every EwE source solves V at
  quasi-equilibrium (CW2004 "Fast equilibrium"; 2008 guide "assuming ... fast enough to keep dV/dt near
  zero"). Ecosim is therefore the fast-exchange limit of the planned A/R scheme.

1(b) Fast-equilibrium V and the consumption equation
----------------------------------------------------
* Ahrens et al. (book ch. 15, [X]):
      Vi = v Bi / (v + v' + aij Bj)                                              (eq. 3)
      Q/Bj = a v Bi / (v + v' + aBj)                                             (eq. 5)
  "If v is small and aij is large, Qj approaches the 'donor controlled' limiting rate vBi as Bj
   increases; but as v increases, Qj approaches the mass action rate aijBiBj."
  "instantaneous prey mortality rate (Qj/Bi) should increase in a hyperbolic pattern toward a maximum
   rate (v) as Bj increases".
* Original Ecosim form (2005 guide Eq. 48 [F]; book ch. 18 eq. 1 [F]):
      Cij = aij vij Bi Pj / (2 vij + aij Pj)
  (2005 guide indexes predator i, prey j in this passage; book ch. 18: "aij is the effective search rate
   for predator j feeding on a prey i ... Bi prey biomass, and Bj predator abundance").
* Elaborated form, Christensen & Walters 2004 eq. 15-16 [F, read from rendered page 117]:
      Qij = aij vij Bi Bj Ti Tj Sij Mij / Dj
            / ( vij + vij Ti Mij + aij Mij Bj Sij Tj / Dj )                      (15)
      Dj  = hj Tj / ( 1 + SUM_k akj Bk Tk Mkj )                                  (16, as printed in 2004)
  Ti prey relative feeding time, Tj predator relative feeding time, Sij seasonal/long-term forcing,
  Mij mediation, hj predator handling time. The 2005 guide (Eq. 49-50) prints the same.
* Current form, EwE book ch. 18 eq. 2-3 [F, rendered PDF p. 130]:
      Qij = vij aij Bi Bj Ti Tj Sij Mij / Dj
            / ( vij + vij Ti Mij + aij Mij Bj Sij Tj / Dj / A ) * f(Envt)         (2)
      Dj  = 1 + hj SUM_k akj Vk Tk Mkj                                           (3)
  "A is foraging arena size, f(Envt) is an environmental response function ... Dj represents effects of
   handling time as a limit to consumption rate (1/Dj is proportion of time spent feeding)";
  "Vk is the vulnerable density of prey type k to predator j (Vk is estimated numerically in the Ecosim
   code)." (J: the 2004/2005 Dj with hj in the numerator is evidently a typesetting variant; the 2008
   guide text and the book give the multispecies disc equation Qbij = aij Vij / (1 + hj Si aij Vij),
   i.e. handling acts on VULNERABLE densities.)
* Walters & Christensen (book ch. 28 [X of WC 2007]): "it is sometimes appropriate to account for
  handling time effects, but only if these are predicted using arena-scale vulnerable prey densities V,
  i.e. Q = alpha V P / (1 + hV)". They also object to reading Q = (alpha v B P)/(v + v' + alpha P) as a
  functional response on mean prey density B.
* Predator-density term: the aij Pj (or aij Bj) in the denominator of V is the predator-dependence
  ("ratio dependence") of the arena; Ahrens: "these models do not depend on specific assumptions about
  predator behaviour, such as interference or contest competition". (J: algebraically Q/(Bi Bj) =
  a v / (v + v' + a Bj) is Beddington-DeAngelis in predator density without a prey-density term;
  the existing note "Tathetsberoende och interferens hos torskfiskar/predator_interference.md" already
  records this equivalence.)

1(c) How v is set from the vulnerability K and baseline M2 - VERIFIED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
* 2005 guide s.12.3 [F] (identical text in CW2004 s.6.3 [F] and 2008 guide):
  "if M(o)ij = C(o)ij/B(o)i is the base instantaneous natural mortality rate for prey type i caused by
   predator j base (Ecopath estimate) consumption rate C(o)ij on prey base biomass B(o)i, we assume that
   the maximum possible rate for very high predator j abundance would be vij Bi where vij = K M(o)ij,
   K>1, represents the rate at which prey become vulnerable to predator j."
* EwE book ch. 30 [X of Bentley et al. 2024 ICES JMS 81:260, CC BY]: "one cannot adjust vulnerability
  exchange rates (vij) directly. Instead, this is done via vulnerability multipliers (kij), which can be
  more easily interpreted as the maximum increase in predation mortality rate that a predator can exert
  on a prey if the predator were to grow to its carrying capacity. The increase is relative to baseline
  Ecopath predation mortality rates (M2, where M2=Qij/Bi) The vulnerability exchange rates (vij) are then
  set to the vulnerability multiplier (kij) multiplied by the baseline predation mortality (M2), i.e.
  vij=kijM2. Multipliers can range from one to infinity with two as the default value."
  So v_ij = K_ij x Q_ij/B_i: CONFIRMED.
* Shared arenas (book ch. 27 [X of WC 2007]): Ma(0) = SUM_k Qak(0) / Bi(a) (eq. 3); "we simply set
  va = Ka (and va' = va)" - the printed "va = Ka" has evidently lost the factor Ma(0) (the preceding
  paragraph defines Ka as a multiple of Mij(0)); the bout chapter writes it in full: "va* = Ka Ma(0)".
  Base vulnerable density (eq. 4): Va(0) = (va Bi(a) - SUM_k Qak(0)) / (va + va');
  search rates: alpha_ak = Qak(0) / (Va(0) Pk(0)).
* DERIVED (J): with v = v' = K M0 and SUM Q(0) = M0 B:
      V(0)/B = (K - 1) / (2K)       -> 0.25 at the default K = 2; -> 0.5 as K -> infinity; 0 at K = 1
      a P(0) = 2 K M0 / (K - 1)
      M2 = a v P / (v + v' + a P) -> v = K M0 as P -> infinity (the cap that defines K).
* Two conventions (do not mix): CW2004 s.3.1 [F]: "The input vulnerability rates (vij) in EwE are scaled
  to range from 0 to 1, with 0.3 serving as default for mixed control ... The actual vulnerabilities
  (vij) used in the computations are rescaled from the entered vij's as: vij = exp[2 x (exp(vij) - 1)]."
  The 2005/2008 guides, the book and Cefas TR 142 use the ratio K >= 1, default 2 (Cefas TR 142:
  "The default value of 2 indicates 'mixed control'", ">28 ... totally top-down").

1(d) Interpretation and time scale of v
---------------------------------------
* Behavioural statements [X, Ahrens]: arenas are "spatially limited interactions between predator and
  prey occurring on time scales of minutes/hours and at the spatial scale of meters"; "It is likely that
  interactions between predators and prey are occurring at the scale of hours and meters."
* But the operational v is an EFFECTIVE, fitted rate:
  - Book ch. 28 [X WC 2007]: "the Ka are not purely 'behavioural' or ecological parameters; rather,
    they depend as well on how large the Ecopath initial predator abundances Pk are compared to what the
    ecosystem might naturally support"; Newfoundland cod "must have very high Ka values (1000+)".
  - Book ch. 30 [X Bentley 2024]: "vulnerability multipliers do not only reflect the ecological limits
    caused by prey and predator behaviour, but also how depleted the exploited species is".
  - Cefas TR 142 App. 1 [F]: "'Vulnerability' in the model is largely a theoretical concept, and direct
    estimates of this parameter are not available from the literature."
  - Mechanisms listed for low v (2008 guide s.3.4 [F]): risk-sensitive prey behaviour; risk-sensitive
    predator behaviour ("three to tango"); size-dependent graduation into/out of the vulnerable size
    range; passive differential spatial depletion ("'Flow' between low and high risk areas (vij) is then
    created by any processes that move organisms").
  - Bout feeding (book ch. 28 [X WC 2007], eq. 9-10): va* = nb fa "represents a total prey 'fraction'
    that would become vulnerable over a one-year time scale" (nb bouts per year, fa = vulnerable
    fraction per bout). So v [1/yr] = (bouts per year) x (fraction exposed per bout).
* Typical magnitudes (J, computed from Cefas TR 142 1991 Ecopath, Table 3.3 biomass and the flows table
  "consumed as food"; total M2 over ALL predators, per pair it is smaller):
      group                       B t/km2   M2_total /yr   2 x M2   6 x M2 (cod/haddock K 1991)
      Large crabs                  1.354     0.51           1.02     3.05
      Nephrops                     1.1       0.36           0.73     2.18
      Epifaunal macrobenthos      78         0.17           0.33     1.00
      Infaunal macrobenthos      136         0.28           0.56     1.67
      Shrimp                       0.5       1.11           2.22     6.67
      Small mobile epifauna       30         1.79           3.59    10.8
      Small infauna (polychaetes) 150        0.82           1.63     4.90
  -> Ecosim-implied exchange rates for North Sea benthos are of order 0.1-10 per YEAR (exchange time
     scales of weeks to years), i.e. 1e-4 to 1e-2 per 6-h tick. With v = v', the relaxation rate of V at
     baseline is v + v' + aP = 2 K^2 M0/(K - 1) (J): for Nephrops, K = 2, M0 = total M2 0.36: 2.9 /yr
     (about 4 months) - an UPPER bound; per-pair M2_ij < M2_total, so a per-pair V relaxes even slower. So
     with its own parameter values Ecosim's V would NOT be fast relative to its monthly step; the fast
     equilibrium is an assumption about the functional form (v acts as the cap of per-pair M2), not a
     consequence of fast behavioural exchange. (J)
  -> Contrast with the engine (J): a fixed accessible fraction f recomputed every 6-h tick is exactly
     the "bout feeding" model with no carryover (book ch. 28: "renewal mechanisms between bouts make
     Va(0) = fa Bi(a) ... no carryover effects from previous bouts (extreme opposite of continuous
     feeding assumption)"), with nb = 1460 bouts/yr. Its implied v* = nb f = 1460 x 0.2 = 292 /yr
     (benthivores -> infauna, f = 0.2), i.e. ~100-500 times the Ecosim-implied K x M2 above. The refuge
     as implemented therefore caps nothing on time scales longer than a tick; the Holling ceiling does
     the limiting. Caveat from the source: the Ka are partly a measure of depletion, not behaviour.

1(e) Arenas per pair; mass conservation with shared prey
--------------------------------------------------------
* Default = unique arena per linkage. 2005 guide s.12.4 [F]: "The default assumption in Ecosim is to
  treat each predation rate linkage as occurring in a unique 'foraging arena' defined by the behaviors
  of the specific prey and predator. In this formulation, elimination of one predator will result in a
  decrease in total prey mortality rate equal (at least initially) to the Ecopath base estimate of that
  predator's component of the prey total mortality rate." Book ch. 27 [X]: the rationale is that
  "multiple predators can feed on the same prey type in different ways (at different depths, times of
  day, spatial microhabitats) without competing directly for prey".
* Mass: the prey state is B_i only; dB_i/dt loses SUM_j Q_ij (CW2004 eq. 14 [F]). Each V_ij is computed
  from the full B_i independently of the other arenas (J: there is no constraint SUM_j V_ij <= B_i; the
  V_ij are not disjoint sub-pools of B_i but "the instantaneous density of prey type i vulnerable to
  predator j" (2008 guide). Mass is conserved because only the Q_ij are subtracted from B_i, not the V's.
  At baseline each V_ij <= B_i/2, so with three or more predators the sum can exceed B_i.)
* Shared-arena extension (book ch. 27 [X WC 2007], eq. 2):
      Va = va Bi(a) / ( va + va' + SUM_k alpha_ak Pk )
  with base diet proportions p_ak of predator k's consumption of prey i taken in arena a (columns sum to
  1; default = identity = unique arenas). "This case implies maximum possible impact of predators k on
  availability of prey i to one another, and will cause competitive exclusion of at least some predator
  types in Ecosim unless the predators are well-differentiated in terms of overall diet composition";
  with one shared arena and one prey "there is not even a unique equilibrium point for predator
  abundances" (neutral stability). Handling: Qak = (alpha_ak / Hk) Va Pk.
* Non-additivity caveat (2005 guide s.12.4 [F]): if mortality reflects prey entering states vulnerable
  to predators in general, "removal of any one predator may simply result in the vulnerable prey
  individuals being taken just as fast, but by other predators."
  (J for the engine: a single explicit A per prey shared by all fish groups is the shared-arena
  limit; per-pair A's are the Ecosim default but then the A's are virtual, not partitions of B.)

1(f) Ahrens et al. 2012: measuring v; benthic invertebrates
-----------------------------------------------------------
* [X, book ch. 15] "A variety of approaches have been tried for estimating vulnerability exchange rates
  from field data. None of these has been fully satisfactory ... arena structures are 'theoretical
  entities' that are practically difficult or impossible to directly observe." Four methods: "1. Direct
  assessment of exchange rates for spatially simple arena structures 2. Empirical relationships between
  prey mortality rates and predator abundances 3. Fitting ecosystem models to time series data 4. Using
  complex individual-based spatial models" (details only in the full paper - NOT read).
* Mechanism 2.b [X]: "drift of benthic stream insects that spend most of their time in interstitial
  microhabitats where they are safe from most fish predation ... the V of Eq. 1 is literally the
  concentration of drifting (and emerging) insects, and the drift entry rate v can be limiting".
* Mechanism 2.d [X], partial predation: "Some predators take only parts of their prey without normally
  killing the prey ... In such cases, the v process represents prey body growth ... even with animal-
  animal interactions like fishes that nip at the siphons of buried molluscs or 'graze' on parts of
  corals." (This is the only benthic-invertebrate statement in the extract.)
* Fitting vs biology: book ch. 28 [X WC 2007]: "such information can only come from either fine-scale
  analysis of spatial arena structures, from data collected at different times and/or places about how
  Q's have varied with predator and prey abundances, or from assumptions about or estimates of where
  populations are relative to their carrying capacity. Indeed, this is why we emphasize the importance
  of fitting Ecosim models to time series data by varying the Ka parameters." Meta-pattern: "fitted Ka
  values tend to be small (<2.0) for most trophic linkages in temperate and tropical systems, and for
  feeding by juvenile stanzas in all systems"; larger in high-latitude systems.
  Book ch. 31 [X Bentley 2024]: Chagaris et al. 2020 cap K so a predator's maximal M2 is a share M2cap
  of the prey's natural mortality ("M2cap values between 0.75 to 1.0 led to more reasonable estimates for
  FMSY"); "It can indeed be difficult to specify reasonable vulnerability multipliers for non-exploited
  species." (J: for the project rule "parameters from biology, not fit", the only non-fitted route the
  sources offer is K from where the predator stands relative to its carrying capacity, or a cap from the
  prey's M.)

Q2. CLASSICAL REFUGE MODELS
#####################################################################################

Krivan 1998, Theor Pop Biol 53:131 (doi 10.1006/tpbi.1997.1350) [F]
* Two patches, open (1) and refuge (2); both have prey growth: a1 > a2, "a2 may also be negative"
  (refuge lacks resources). Prey move "infinitely fast" (ideal free distribution, fitness = per-capita
  growth). Model (1):
      x' = (a1 - lam1 p1 y) v1 x + (a2 - lam2 p2 y) v2 x
      y' = (e lam1 v1 x - m1) p1 y + (e lam2 v2 x - m2) p2 y,      v1 + v2 = 1
  vi = proportion of time in patch i; lam_i attack rate; p_i predator preference for patch i;
  complete refuge: p2 = 0 or lam2 = 0. Switching line y* = (a1 - a2)/(lam1 p1 - lam2 p2).
* Complete refuge with a2 > 0: "the population of prey in the refuge will grow exponentially since they
  are not being preyed upon ... trajectories are unbounded".
* SATURATING REFUGE: constraint v2 <= K/x (refuge carrying capacity K). For x > K and y > y*:
      x' = (a1 - lam1 p1 y)(x - K) + K (a2 - lam2 p2 y)
      y' = (e lam1 (x - K) - m1) p1 y + (e lam2 K - m2) p2 y              (14)
  (J: i.e. a full refuge becomes a CONSTANT-NUMBER refuge K.) Result: "Low refuge carrying capacity
  leads to stability of predator-prey dynamics while stability is lost when the carrying capacity of
  the refuge is high"; E14 locally asymptotically stable if K < (m1 p1 + m2 p2)/(e lam2 p2).
* On the classics (Krivan's summary, i.e. [S] for them): "refugia which protect a constant number of
  prey lead to a stable equilibrium and have a stronger stabilizing effect on population dynamics than
  refugia which protect a constant proportion of prey. However, McNair (1986) and Collings (1995)
  showed ... refugia may exert a locally destabilizing effect due to the emergence of a stable limit
  cycle." "neither a constant number nor a constant proportion of prey refuges has been reported"
  (quoting Sih 1987b). McNair 1986 "assumes that both open habitat and refuge are limited and he shows
  that for low density dependence in the refuge a limit cycle appears." Sih 1987b: "if the proportion of
  prey in refugia is decreasing with increasing prey abundance, or increasing with both increasing
  predator density and increasing predation pressure then the corresponding ecological equilibrium is
  locally stable." Ives & Dobson 1987: "Prey investment in antipredator behavior was modeled by a
  parameter. They showed that adaptive change of the parameter speeds the convergence of trajectories
  to an equilibrium." Ruxton 1995: "the rate prey move to a refuge is proportional to predator density.
  It was shown that antipredator behavior has a stabilizing effect." Holt 1983/1985/1987 cited for
  "passive diffusion of prey between patches that alone could lead to a stable equilibrium".

Krivan 2013, J Theor Biol 339:112 (doi 10.1016/j.jtbi.2012.12.016) [F]
* Constant-number refuge (Gause model dR/dt = rR - C f(R), dC/dt = (g(R) - m)C):
      f(R) = 0                                   if R < Rc
      f(R) = l (R - Rc) / (1 + h l (R - Rc))     if R >= Rc                  (2)
  "Presence of a refuge changes the shape of the Holling type II functional response to an (extreme)
   sigmoid type"; equilibrium R* = Rc + m/(l(e - hm)) stable if Rc > h m^2/(l (e - hm)^2); smaller Rc
  gives a limit cycle; below a critical size coexistence fails. "a refuge alone can lead to coexistence
  of predators and exponentially growing prey either at a population equilibrium, or along a limit
  cycle." Some trajectories still escape regulation.
* Adaptive refuge use, fast movement:
      dR/dt = r1 u1 R - C f(u1 R) + r2 u2 R,   dC/dt = (e1 f(u1 R) - m) C      (3)
  u1 share of time out of the refuge, r1 > 0 > or < r2; "Model (3) does not describe mechanistically
  the transition process of moving in and out of the refuge. Instead, it assumes that this process
  occurs very fast when compared to demographic processes". Result: hysteresis and coexistence.

Berardo, Geritz, Gyllenberg & Raoul 2020 (arXiv 1907.07249) [F]; Geritz & Gyllenberg 2012 [S via it, A]
* Prey states exposed/hiding with fast transitions; Beddington-DeAngelis derived "in terms of prey
  refuges instead of the usual interference between predators" (G&G 2012 abstract via search [A]).
  In Berardo et al. eq. 28-29: transition into the refuge proportional to predator density (A1 Y),
  emergence at constant rate A12:
      x1_hat = A12 X / (A12 + A1 Y)     (exposed),  x2_hat = A1 Y X / (A12 + A1 Y)   (protected)
  with c2 = 0 (protected prey uncatchable) the response is BD, a X/(1 + bX + cY); the paper prints
  "a = c1 A12, b = c1 d1 and c = A1/A12" (text extraction partly garbled). By direct algebra (J):
  f = c1 x1_hat / (1 + d1 c1 x1_hat) = c1 X / (1 + c1 d1 X + (A1/A12) Y), i.e. a = c1, b = c1 d1,
  c = A1/A12 (c1 = capture rate of exposed prey; d1 = the handling parameter as defined in the paper).
  (J: the engine's BD interference w can thus be read as a predator-induced refuge with
   w = (hiding rate per unit predator)/(emergence rate) - a fast-exchange refuge, already in the code.)
* Opposite case (their section 6, eq. 49-50): exposed prey seek refuge at constant rate a, predators
  flush protected prey at rate b S P: dE/dt = bSP - aE, dP/dt = -bSP + aE (+ capture). Leads to an Allee
  effect in the predator. "If we assume that only the protected prey in class P give birth ... the prey
  per capita birth rate is proportional to P_hat(X,Y)/X" (eq. 60) - an explicit option where only the
  refuge class reproduces.

Gonzalez-Olivares & Ramos-Jiliberto 2003, Ecol Model 166:135 [A] (abstract via investigadores.anid.cl)
* Two "simplest forms" of refuge use (constant proportion and constant number) added to a continuous
  biomass-conversion model; "there is a trend from limit cycles through non-zero stable points up to
  predator extinction" as hidden prey increase or the predator mortality / conversion ratio rises;
  stability domains differ between the two modes. Equations NOT verified.

Abrams & Walters 1996, Ecology 77:1125 [A via search snippets]: prey transitions between vulnerable and
  invulnerable states/locations; transitions make enrichment raise equilibrium prey density and reduce
  destabilisation by enrichment, and can stabilise limit cycles. Equations, which class reproduces and
  the transfer form NOT verified.

Persistence condition asked for ("prey persists under any predation if exchange out of the refuge is
slower than prey growth"): NOT found stated in any source read. Derivation (J): with growth in the
refuge (or on total B) at per-capita rate r at low density, exchange R -> A at rate e, A -> R at e', and
the worst case that predators remove everything entering A, dR/dt -> (r - e) R. The prey cannot be
driven extinct by predation if r > e (for growth only in A, no such guarantee). In Ecosim terms the cap
on per-pair M2 is v_ij, so prey with r > SUM_j v_ij cannot be eaten out (J). Krivan 1998 (a2 > 0,
complete refuge -> unbounded prey) and Krivan 2013 (constant-number refuge -> coexistence) are the
nearest verified statements.

Which compartment grows - summary of what the sources state:
  Krivan 1998/2013: both, with lower (possibly negative) growth in the refuge; refuge capacity K optional.
  Berardo et al. 2020: option "only the protected prey in class P give birth"; deaths may differ by class.
  Noy-Meir 1975 (Q3): growth depends on TOTAL biomass V including the ungrazable reserve.
  Ecosim: growth on total B_i; V is only a sub-state for predation.
  StrathE2E2, Atlantis (Q4): no refuge state at all.

StrathE2E2 v4.0.1 (Heath & Speirs 2023 ecology model document) [F]
* Predation: "the general equation for the flux of ingestate to a predator (v1) from prey (v2) is":
      U_v1(v2) = v1 v2 rho_v1(v2) Umax_v1 / (v2 + h_v1)                         (eqn 26)
  rho = preference (sum over prey = 1); Holling II on total prey; quadratic density-dependent losses on
  most guilds; Beddington-DeAngelis g = v2 Umax/(v2 + gamma v1 + h) only for birds, pinnipeds and
  cetaceans (eqn 27). No predation refuge, threshold or accessible fraction for benthos as fish prey.
  The text names "sheltering in refuges with increasing predator density (Hill & Weissburg, 2013)" as one
  justification of consumer density dependence.
* Accessibility to FISHING only (new in StrathE2E2): "we specify two additional terms ... a threshold of
  biomass density (m-2) below which none is accessible to the fishery ... [and] a maximum proportion of
  the guild biomass which is potentially accessible to fishing in an unfished system":
      p(w) = max{0, pmax (1 - a_w Blim / B(w))}                                  (eqn 31)
      B(w)_harvestable = p(w) B(w) = max{0, pmax (B(w) - a_w Blim)}               (eqn 32)
  "For the benthos ... we expect the threshold values to represent a high proportion of typical guild
  biomass, and the maximum accessible fraction to be relatively small"; Blim for benthos from the ICES
  North Sea Benthos Survey excluding exploited species; pmax "treated as a fitting parameter".
  (J: an instantaneous constant-number + proportion refuge, recomputed every step - no exchange dynamics.)
* Heath 2012 (StrathE2E1, Prog Oceanogr 102:42): NOT read (publisher blocked); the 2023 document says
  the prototype assumed "all of the biomass of each fishery resource guild was accessible to fishing".

Atlantis (Fulton et al. 2007, Atlantis SE, AFMA AMS stage 2 report) [F]
* "these trophic connection matrices are ... the set of maximum potential proportion of the prey
  population that a predator can access at any one time ... defined as the maximum proportion of prey
  that are accessible by predators"; realised availability further reduced by spatial overlap ("both
  predator and prey must be present in the cell"), habitat usage (on/off per habitat; "only in effect in
  the bottom water column layer (where it contacts the epibenthic and sediment layers)") and gape limits.

* Habitat refuge: "A simple theoretical non-linear curve (Figure 2.16) relates the quality of the habitat

Q5. NUMERICS (6-h STEP, POSSIBLY FAST EXCHANGE)

Precedents in the sources [F]:
* Ecosim: RK4 (book ch. 17 "An introduction to Ecosim": "The set of differential equations is solved in Ecosim using a 4th order
  Runge-Kutta routine ... For groups like phytoplankton and small zooplankton that turn over (have P/B)
  greater than 10 ... the numerical integration prediction is replaced with a prediction based on the
  equilibrium of the Ecosim rate equation of the likely average over the month."); CW2004: Adams-
  Bashforth default or RK4. V solved at quasi-equilibrium with an iterated search-time ratio (2008 guide).
* Ecospace (CW2004 s.4.3 [F]): "If the rate constants were constant over time ... the biomass would change
  as a linear dynamical system, and would move exponentially towards an equilibrium ... the Ecospace
  computations can be greatly increased by using a variable time splitting, where moving equilibria are
  calculated for groups with high turnover rates (e.g. phytoplankton), while the integrations for groups
  with slower turnover rates (e.g. fish and marine mammals) are based on a Runge-Kutta method.
  Comparisons indicate that this does not change the resulting time patterns for solutions in any
  noticeable way". Book: Ecospace now BDF2 on one-month steps.
* Bout model (book ch. 28 [X]): within a bout, dVa/dt = -Va SUM_k alpha_ak Pk (eq. 3) integrates exactly
  to Va(d) = Va(0) exp(-d SUM alpha P); consumption is apportioned among predators by
  alpha_ak Pk / SUM alpha P (eq. 4).
Recommended scheme (J - derivation, not from a source):
* Exchange alone, with B (hence R = B - A) held fixed during the substep:
      dA/dt = e (B - A) - e' A    ->    A(t+dt) = A* + (A(t) - A*) exp(-(e + e') dt),  A* = e B/(e + e')
  Exact, positive, mass-conserving (R = B - A). Unconditionally stable for any e dt.
  (Without predation A + R = B is conserved, so holding B fixed is exact here.)
* Exchange + linear predation in one exact, mass-exact step (per-capita predation m = SUM_j a_j P_j / D_j
  frozen at the start of the tick, i.e. handling-time denominators lagged one tick; growth excluded):
      d/dt [A, R] = M [A, R],   M = [[ -(e' + m),  e ],
                                     [   e',      -e ]]
      tr M = -(e + e' + m),  det M = e m > 0,
      discriminant = (e + e' + m)^2 - 4 e m = (e - m)^2 + e'^2 + 2 e'(e + m) > 0
      -> two real negative eigenvalues l1 > l2, and (Sylvester)
      exp(M dt) = [ exp(l1 dt) (M - l2 I) - exp(l2 dt) (M - l1 I) ] / (l1 - l2)
      [A, R](t+dt) = exp(M dt) [A0, R0];   eaten = (A0 + R0) - (A + R)(t+dt)
      (split among predators in proportion a_j P_j / D_j).
  Checked numerically against RK4 (e 0.3, e' 0.5, m 2.0, dt 1.7, A0 0.4, R0 1.1: identical to 1e-14).
  Cheaper first-order variant when e dt << 1 (the Ecosim-like regime below): hold R fixed during the
  step, dA/dt = e R - (e' + m) A, k = e' + m, A_inf = e R / k,
      A(t+dt) = A_inf + (A0 - A_inf) exp(-k dt),
      eaten   = m [ A_inf dt + (A0 - A_inf)(1 - exp(-k dt))/k ],
      R(t+dt) = R0 - e R0 dt + e' [ A_inf dt + (A0 - A_inf)(1 - exp(-k dt))/k ]
  (mass-exact by construction: B loses exactly 'eaten'; the error is only in treating R as constant).
  Fast limit: quasi-steady A given R is A = e R/(e' + m), i.e. A = e B/(e + e' + m) with B = A + R -
  Ecosim's eq. 3 with v = e, v' = e', a B_j = m.
* Regime test: with Ecosim-like e of order K M2 (0.1-10 /yr) e dt ~ 1e-4 to 1e-2 per tick, so A is a
  slow state variable and even explicit Euler would be accurate; the exponential form is still preferable
  because the predation term m can be large. If e + e' >> 4 /day, A relaxes within a tick and the state
  variable is redundant (fast-equilibrium = Ecosim / current-engine limit).
* Splitting order: exchange (and predation) first on A, then growth on the compartment(s) that grow,
  then any loss terms; or symmetric Strang splitting (half exchange - predation - growth - half exchange)
  for second-order accuracy (Strang 1968, SIAM J Numer Anal 5:506 - standard reference, not read here).
  With lagged handling-time denominators the scheme is first order in the predation coupling regardless.
