  and wet weight (WW) according to biometrical relationships of individuals and arms (Josefson & Jensen
  1992) gives 2.59 g AFDW m-2 yr-1 and 29.75 g WW m-2 yr-1".

- UNIT FLAG. Skold & Gunnarsson 1996 (MEPS 142:203, Table 3 [F, earlier folder data/skold_gunnarsson1996_
  meps142/, page image checked]) prints "Mean regenerated dry wt (g)" = 0.19, 0.25, 0.29, 0.18 and the
  caption formula "Y = 0.010564 X^1.2878 (Skold et al. 1994)". Skold 1994 gives the same relation for
  regenerated arms, "Y = 0.01056X^1.288 (r2 = 0.63 ...)" (log10 DW per arm on log10 arm length).
  Y is in mg DW per arm, X in mm, NOT g. Check with Skold 1994's own numbers: Andreasson's regrowth
  "9.68 mm 41 d-1" -> 0.01056 x 9.68^1.288 = 0.197 mg -> /41 d = 0.0048 mg d-1, exactly their stated
  "G_arm of 0.0048 mg DW arm-1 d-1". So 0.19 "g" in S&G Table 3 is 0.19 mg.

========================================================================================================
1. AMPHIURA ARM TIPS (A. filiformis; A. chiajei; Amphilepis norvegica)
========================================================================================================
(a) Density, biomass, individual size
- Skold & Gunnarsson 1996, Table 1 [F]: offshore site "on the western slope of a 90 m deep trench in the
  Skagerrak": A. filiformis 3349 (SD 507) ind m-2, 264.7 (109.7) g wet wt m-2; A. chiajei 267 (196) ind
  m-2, 34.9 (21.3) g WW m-2; total macrofauna 6576 ind m-2, 517.9 g WW m-2 (Polychaeta 37.3, Mollusca
  18.8, Crustacea 10.9 g WW m-2). Coastal 21 m: A. filiformis 357 ind m-2, 18.9 g WW m-2.
  "The dense population of Amphiura filiformis (3300 ind. m-2) at the offshore site is among the highest
  abundance observed in the Kattegat-Skagerrak." Kattegat below the halocline "densities of around 100
  to 400 ind. m-2 are common (Pearson et al. 1985)" [S]; "280 ind. m-2 as an approximate mean" [S].
  J: mean individual mass 0.079 g WW (offshore), 0.053 (coastal); A. chiajei 0.131 g WW.
  J: at 90 m A. filiformis = 51 % and A. chiajei 7 % of macrofaunal WW; polychaetes 7 %, molluscs 4 %,
  crustaceans 2 %.
- Rosenberg et al. 1997, MEPS 159:121 [F, earlier folder data/rosenberg1997_meps159/]: 86 m Skagerrak
  Deep Trench, field density ~2250 ind m-2; "mean oral width ... of 2.5 (SD = 0.11) mm, equal to a disk
  mean diameter of 8.0 mm"; individual wet weight "mean 0.202 g ind.-1" (moderate) and "0.201" (high
  density) after 63 d (fed and starved pooled in that statement).
- Skold et al. 1994 [F]: 40 m station (Gullmarsfjord area), density "stable around 280 ind. m-2";
  "individual mean biomass was 0.020 g AFDW ind.-1"; adult size group 3 "0.021 g AFDW ind.-1"; oral-width
  regression "Y = 0.00218 X^4.344" (DW per individual; fitted on the small size groups SG 1-2 and
  extrapolated here; J: at OW 2.4 mm gives 0.098, i.e. g DW, which agrees with the two masses below).
  J: 280 ind m-2 x 0.020 g = 5.6 g AFDW m-2.
- Josefson 1998, Hydrobiologia 375:297 [A, VLIZ record 98495]: at 100 m "abundance of Amphiura ...
  showed logistic population growth ... with carrying capacity reached in 1986 (ca. 3000 ind m-2)".
- Josefson & Conley 1997, MEPS 147:49 [F, data/A_infauna/josefson_conley1997_meps/]: grid across the
  Skagerrak-Kattegat front (25-150 m): A. filiformis "reached a maximum density of >3000 ind. m-2 in the
  northern part of the area".
- Josefson & Jensen 1992, Mar Biol 112:615 [A, VLIZ 26698]: 3 sites 55-100 m, biomass rose 1970-89;
  "maximum disc diameter growth rate was ca. 0.4 mm mo-1".
- Arm length: "very long arms (10x disc diameter)" (MarLIN review [S, data/A_infauna/marlin_afiliformis/]).
  J: ~80 mm per arm at an 8 mm disc.
- Reference adult mass, three sources agreeing (J): 0.020 g AFDW = 73 mg DW (Skold factor 0.274) to 98 mg
  DW (oral-width regression; Ricciardi AFDW/DW 0.20); Rosenberg's 0.20 g WW x 0.32-0.49 = 64-98 mg DW.
  Use 64-98 mg DW (0.20 g WW) for an 8 mm disc adult; the dense 90 m population is smaller (0.079 g WW
  -> ~25-39 mg DW).
- Amphilepis norvegica (300 m): nothing found in this round (no density, size, exposure).

(b) Exposed mass at a moment, as a share of the animal
- Mass per mm of regenerated (thin, distal) arm, from the Skold 1994 relation (J arithmetic):
    5 mm 0.084 mg DW; 10 mm 0.205 mg (0.0205 mg/mm); 15 mm 0.35 mg; 20 mm 0.50 mg (0.025 mg/mm);
    30 mm 0.84 mg.
  Intact distal arm is thicker than a regenerate (Duineveld & Van Noort: recent regenerations
  "distinguishable by their smaller diameter" [A]), so intact tips weigh more per mm by an unknown factor.
- Raised length: "Arms engaged in passive suspension feeding extended 10 to 20 mm" and "ca 2 arms
  were extended at a time" (Loo 1996, known); Solan & Kennedy 2002 (in situ, Galway Bay 22 m) [F, data/
  A_infauna/solan_kennedy2002_meps228/]: in low current "feeding 'arms' were positioned vertically and
  extended > 20 mm into the overlying water column"; MarLIN "3-4cm" [S]. Rosenberg 1997 [F]: "One or 2
  arms are stretched up above the sediment for feeding" and "about 3 arms of each individual are also kept
  in the sediment".
- J: 2 arms x 10-20 mm x 0.020-0.025 mg/mm = 0.4-1.0 mg DW exposed = 0.4-1.6 % of a 64-98 mg DW adult
  if the tips are regenerate-thin; perhaps 1-5 % if intact thickness is 2-3x. For the small dense 90 m
  animals (25-39 mg DW) 1-4 % (thin). THIS REVISES DOWNWARD the earlier accessibility note's J of 2-8 %.
  The 22 % (12-30 %) standing regenerated tissue (Skold 1994) is a months-long cumulative of past
  cropping/breakage, not an instantaneous exposure.
- Arm share of body mass: still not measured in print. Skold 1994 weighed "discs, whole arms, cut arms and
  regenerated arms" separately [F] but did not report the shares. J bounds: the thin-regenerate relation
  applied to a whole 80 mm arm gives 3.0 mg x 5 = 15 mg = 15-23 % of 64-98 mg DW, a lower bound (proximal
  arm is thicker); the 22 % regenerated share is also a lower bound. Arms are probably >= 25-50 % of DW.
- Burrow geometry (Solan & Kennedy [F]): "1 arm burrow (8.95 cm long and 0.23 cm wide) terminating in a
  disc chamber (with a volume of 0.27 cm3)"; disc chamber "at approximately 6 to 10 cm depth" (Galway;
  Rosenberg 1997: "disk at about 4 cm depth").

(c) Time share exposed
- Flow (Galway, tidal, 22 m; Solan & Kennedy [F]): "suspension feeding activity by A. filiformis commenced
  at approximately 1 to 2 h following high or low tide, peaked during the mid-tide, and ended at
  approximately 1 to 2 h before the next tide"; "During slack tidal periods, individuals remained below
  the sediment surface and either rested (i.e. arms largely retracted in the disc chamber) or maintained
  the integrity of the burrow structure (i.e. arms just short of the sediment-water interface)." At rising
  flow the tips "first kinked ... and proceeded to adopt an inverted L shaped posture". Counts of visible
  arms (25-35 maximum vs <5) are for a 15 x 22 cm vertical profile window, so NOT convertible to arms per
  individual. J: in a semidiurnal tide this is roughly 2-4 h of each 6.2 h half-cycle, ~35-65 % of time.
  Loo 1996 (known): "In still water many Amphiura filiformis did not extend their arms".
  J caveat: the Mareld slope has near-bottom currents (Rosenberg 1995 via Skold & Gunnarsson: "important
  near-bottom particle transport") but not a strong tidal cycle of the Galway kind; the share raised
  depends on the local current record, not measured at 100-300 m.
- Light: Rosenberg & Lundberg 2004, Mar Biol 145:651 [S, search summary + S2 TLDR; abstract elided by
  publisher]: time-lapse at 30 m, Gullmarsfjord; activity of suspension-feeding arms "clearly related to
  the photoperiod with high activity at night and low or no activity during daytime"; shifted lab
  photoperiods shifted the rhythm (exogenous circadian). No percentages obtained. J: may not transfer to a
  dark 100-300 m bottom; if it does, the raised share is lowest by day when visual fish feed.
- Food: Rosenberg 1997 [F]: "When food was introduced, Amphiura filiformis immediately became active and
  their arms dragged food into the sediment"; Skold & Gunnarsson [F]: raised arms "observed during the
  whole experiment in all treatments".
- Deposit-feeding posture: arms sweep across the surface in slack water (Buchanan 1964 via MarLIN [S];
  earlier note: Duchene & Rosenberg 2001, arms 40-80 % of surface faunal activity [F, earlier]).

(d) Mass yielded per strike
- Duineveld & Van Noort 1986 [A, abstract only]: "Annual consumption of Amphiura arms by dab was
  estimated to be 0.84 g wet weight m-2, which is equivalent to 420 arms or 6% of the arm population."
  J: 840 mg / 420 = 2.0 mg WW (0.64-1.0 mg DW at DW/WW 0.32-0.49). UNRESOLVED - two readings:
  (i) if 420 is a count of arm pieces in stomachs, ~2 mg WW per strike = ~24-34 mm of regenerate-thin
      arm (inverting Skold's relation), more than the 10-20 mm raised part;
  (ii) if 420 is whole-arm EQUIVALENTS (the wording "equivalent to 420 arms or 6% of the arm population"
      favours this), 2 mg WW is the mean mass of a whole Oyster Ground arm and the mass per strike is
      unknown. (ii) implies small animals there (5 x 0.6-1 mg DW = 3-5 mg DW of arms), which fits the
      abstract's "disk size > 4 mm" adults.
  The abstract cannot discriminate; full text not obtained. Do not use 2 mg as a per-strike value.
  J: arm population = 420/0.06 = 7000 arms m-2 (~1400 ind m-2) at the Oyster Ground station.
- Derived per-strike mass if a strike takes only the raised tip (J): 0.2-0.5 mg DW per 10-20 mm thin tip
  (section 1b), more if intact thickness.
- Skold 1994 [F] cross-check of the flux: regeneration production 0.34 g AFDW m-2 yr-1 at 280 ind m-2
  = 1.2 mg AFDW = ~4.4 mg DW per individual per year (J), ~5-7 % of body DW per year, matching the dab
  6 % of arms per year (all croppers together, not dab alone).

(e) Capture and withdrawal evidence
- Discs are out of reach unless the bottom is disturbed: Kaiser & Ramsay 1997, MEPS 152:307 [F, data/
  A_infauna/kaiser_ramsay1997_meps152/], Irish Sea 40 m mud/sand, dab ~19 cm, n = 40 per sample: "In
  undisturbed sediments Amphiura spp. are positioned with their oral discs buried within the sediment
  while their arms protrude ... dab graze the exposed arms, while leaving the oral disc intact". Before
  trawling "The arms of Amphiura spp. occurred in a smaller proportion of stomachs ... (<15%)"; on the
  trawled line fish "ate large numbers of the oral discs of Amphiura spp., whereas they would normally
  graze the arms". J reading of Fig. 2: ~620 discs in 40 dab on day 1 after trawling (~15 per fish) vs
  ~0-30 discs per 40 dab on control lines; arms in ~7-24 of 40 control stomachs.
- Withdrawal: Solan & Kennedy [F]: crab disturbance "appeared to elicit a predation avoidance response
  throughout the local A. filiformis population. When subjected to a disturbance from above, individuals
  immediately retracted to the safety of their burrows for extended periods of time, even in times of peak
  feeding potential." MarLIN [S]: "can withdraw its arms into its burrow when disturbed by noise
  vibrations". Rosenberg & Selander 2000, Mar Biol 136:43 [S, search summary]: in a flume, arms "dropped
  toward the sediment, and some partly sank into it" when dab fed on conspecifics upstream; weaker
  response to dab eating A. chiajei; response to arm pinching "less clear". Solan & Battle 2003 [S]
  found no intraspecific alarm signal. Rosenberg 1997 [F]: animals placed on the surface "were able to
  burrow into the sediment within a few seconds". NO measured arm-retraction speed was found.
- Does a strike get the tip? Indirect yes: 96 % of individuals carry regenerations and 20 % of arms have
  recent distal regenerations (Duineveld & Van Noort [A]); dab, haddock and Nephrops "do not generally
  consume the entire brittle star but crop only the arms" (MarLIN [S], citing Duineveld 1986, Baden 1990).
  Skold 1994 [F] caution: "Lost arms were collected during sorting and constituted 30% ... of the cut
  arms" (handling breakage inflates 'regenerating' counts).
- Haddock: Mattson 1992, Sarsia 77:33 [A, OpenAlex]: "The species-specific feeding method of the haddock
  is aimed primarily at the entire fraction of small to medium-sized endobenthos, and secondarily at
  ophiuroids." "The haddock feeds heavily on ophiuroids and echinoids largely because these form its most
  effective grinding elements." "The prey size and the feeding depth in the sediment increased with fish
  size." Stomach contents "highly particulate and rich in hard fragments". Whether haddock took Amphiura
  discs or arms is NOT in the abstract. Schuckel et al. 2010, J Fish Biol [A, OpenAlex]: northern North
  Sea haddock; "High abundances of echinoderm species with hard calcareous shells ... such as the ophiurid
  Ophiocten affinis and the echinoid Echinocyamus pusillus, indicated their use as grinding elements."
  J: these "grinding" ophiuroids are whole surface ophiurids (Ophiocten, Ophiura), not buried Amphiura
  discs, so they do not settle arms vs discs.
- Nephrops (not a fish, for context): Baden et al. 1990, MEPS 67:141, Table 2 [F, data/A_infauna/
  baden1990_meps67/]: SE Kattegat, % occurrence Oct 1986-Apr 1988 average: Amphiura/Ophiura 31.4,
  Amphiura spp. 10.2, Abra spp. 32.5, Diastylis rathkei 9.3.

========================================================================================================
2. SHALLOW BIVALVES VIA SIPHONS (Abra nitida, A. alba; Thyasira, Nucula)
========================================================================================================
(a) Density/biomass: Skold & Gunnarsson [F] 90 m Skagerrak: Mollusca 2048 ind m-2 but only 18.8 g WW m-2
  (3.6 % of macrofauna WW). Dauwe 1998 (known): Abra nitida and Thyasira in 0-2 cm at 280 m.
(b) Exposed mass share:
- van der Veer et al. 2011, MEPS 434:143, Table 2 [F, data/A_infauna/vanderveer2011_meps434_oa/; the
  values are S of de Vlas]: potential food of juvenile plaice/flounder at Balgzand: adult bivalves "Only
  siphon tips" = "3% of total biomass ... (De Vlas 1985)"; Arenicola marina "Only tail tips" = "15% of
  total biomass (De Vlas 1979b)"; juvenile spat and other polychaetes eaten whole. "Body parts were found
  to consist mainly of siphon tips in bivalves, tail tips from the polychaetes Arenicola marina and
  Heteromastus filiformis, and heads and tentacles of Pygospio elegans."
- Zwarts et al. 1994 (known): extended inhalant siphon 0.6 % of body mass per cm. J: 3 % = ~5 cm of
  siphon, so the two numbers are mutually consistent (Macoma, intertidal). Abra nitida siphon ">0.5 cm"
  above the surface (Gremare 2004, known) -> ~0.3-1 % of body mass exposed while active (J, Macoma
  scaling borrowed).
(c) Time share: Gremare 2004 (known) 24.9 % at 10 C, 54.6 % at 15 C. J: the 10->15 C ratio (2.19 per 5 C,
  Q10 ~4.8) extrapolated to 6 C gives ~13 % active; not measured at 5-8 C. Wikander (1980s) also found a
  positive temperature effect on A. nitida siphonal activity [S, search summary]. Abra alba particle
  mixing far higher at 18-22 C than 14-17 C (PLOS One 2016, PMC4846090 [S, WebFetch extract]); no data
  below 13.7 C. Thyasirids expose no siphon (earlier note [F]); Nucula has no siphons (earlier note).
(d) Mass per nip: no per-nip mass obtained. Regrowth: Trevallion 1971 Tellina tenuis siphons regrow
  "0.3 mg wk-1" DW [S, search summary citing a 2006 Nuttallia paper]; Hodgson 1982 ~20 % of initial
  siphon weight per week in Scrobicularia and Donax [S, same]; Trevallion estimated each Tellina "lost a
  part of its siphons at least once" during the study [S, search summary].
(e) Capture/retraction: Zwarts & Wanink 1993 (earlier [F]): attacked bivalves "withdrew their foot and
  siphon(s) within the shell". de Goeij et al. 2001, Oecologia 126:500 [S, search summary of abstract]:
  three experiments with juvenile plaice; Macoma "lost siphon mass and came closer to the surface".
  Peterson & Quammen 1982 [S]: Protothaca growth rose ">100%" when siphon-nipping fish were excluded
  (citing paper, search summary); per-individual cropping "30 to 92% lower in the muddy sand, in part
  because of a switch to the more abundant deposit-feeding bivalve" (Semantic Scholar auto-TLDR [S]).

========================================================================================================
3. SURFACE POLYCHAETES (palps, tentacles, heads, tail tips)
========================================================================================================
(a) Skold & Gunnarsson [F] 90 m: Polychaeta 832 ind m-2, 37.3 g WW m-2 (7 % of WW). Dauwe (known): at
  280 m polychaetes dominate 10-20 cm.
(b) Exposed parts: heads/tentacles (Pygospio), tail tips (Arenicola, Heteromastus) are the cropped parts in
  Wadden Sea flatfish (van der Veer 2011 [F] citing de Vlas 1979a). Arenicola tail tips 15 % of biomass
  (de Vlas 1979b [S]). No palp/tentacle mass found for cirratulids, spionids or terebellids.
(c) Time share: Lindsay & Woodin 1995, MEPS 125:159, Table 2 [F, data/A_infauna/lindsay_woodin1995_
  meps125/]: intact intertidal spionids, still water, 13-14 C, 2 h video: "Total activity outside tube"
  1748.8 s (Rhynchospio glutaeus) and 5699.3 s (Pseudopolydora kempi japonica) of 7200 s. J: 24 % and
  79 % of time with palps/body exposed. "approximately 15 to 20% of collected individuals of both species
  are missing 1 or both feeding palps" (field, False Bay). Lindsay & Woodin 1992, Biol Bull 183:440 [A]:
  palp loss "significantly increased the amount of tissue exposed, and the frequency of exposure, but not
  the duration of each exposure". Palp regrowth 3-6 d (earlier note).
(d) Mass per crop: not found.
(e) Schuckel et al. 2010 [A]: haddock did not take tube-dwelling spionids (Spiophanes kroyeri, S. bombyx)
  or Phoronis, "potentially avoided due to their ability to withdraw below the feeding depths or due to
  chemical deterrents". Prey density, not sediment position, best predicted haddock prey selection.

========================================================================================================
4. SMALL INFAUNAL CRUSTACEANS (amphipods, cumaceans)
========================================================================================================
(a) Skold & Gunnarsson [F] 90 m: Crustacea 37 (SD 51) ind m-2, 10.9 (18.8) g WW m-2 (~2 % of WW,
  patchy); coastal 11 ind m-2, no biomass. Earlier note: "generally restricted to the surface layer"
  (Dauwe, Frisian Front).
(b)/(c) Emergence: Anger & Valentin 1976, Helgol Wiss Meeresunters 28:138 [S, search summary of the
  abstract; PDF behind a bot challenge]: Diastylis rathkei "never leaves the sediment while light
  remains"; pelagic migrations (Aug, Oct, Kiel Bay) start ~1 h after sunset, peak 22-23 h, end at
  midnight; "Fishes and other predatory animals are almost inactive during this period. Population loss
  by predation is thus negligible". Mattson 1990, Sarsia 75:247 (cod, 55-75 m) [A]: "The cod temporarily
  specialized its feeding to a very high degree on such prey that were temporarily most abundant, such as
  nocturnally swarming males of cumaceans and amphipods." J: emergence makes them takeable whole, but
  mostly at night and mostly males.
(d) Whole animals (cumacean AFDW/WW 7.5 %, amphipod 16.5 %, Ricciardi & Bourget [F]).

========================================================================================================
5. HADDOCK / COD REACH INTO MUD
========================================================================================================
- Brawn 1969, J Fish Res Board Can 26:583 [A, OpenAlex]: "Large pieces of food on the bottom were found by
  sight but smaller pieces were taken after being detected by taste buds on the trailing barbel and pelvic
  fin rays." "Food below gravel or stones was located by smell and uncovered by carrying away gravel in
  the mouth or by rolling stones aside with the head. Food covered by sand was not found." "Cod groups fed
  more effectively than single cod as food deeply buried was uncovered by several fish digging in the
  same place". Distances/depths: not in the abstract (full text not obtained).
- Mattson 1990 [A]: cod "adapted to feed mainly near the bottom, and for catching prey on and below the
  bottom surface. It captures prey mainly by suction"; feeding habitat widened "vertically" with size.
- Mattson 1992 [A]: haddock feeding depth in the sediment increased with fish size (quote in 1e).
- Kaiser & Ramsay 1997 [F]: dab reach arms, not discs, in undisturbed sediment (1e).
- No measured digging depth, pit size or sediment fraction in stomachs for haddock or cod on mud was
  found (also not in the earlier notes).
