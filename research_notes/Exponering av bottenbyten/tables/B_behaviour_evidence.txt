B_behaviour_evidence.txt
Behavioural evidence for the benthic encounter rate a = v x w x p (per fish, per 6 h tick)
of demersal fish (gadoids: haddock, cod; flatfish: plaice, dab, long rough dab, witch,
lemon sole) on infauna and mobile epifauna. Offshore Skagerrak box: 100-300 m mud, bottom
light by day ~1e-3 lux (median), ~1e-10 lux at night, 6-8 C.

Compiled 2026-10-26. Research only; nothing here is applied to the library.

Tags: [F] full text read; [A] abstract only; [S] secondary (citing source named);
J = judgement / arithmetic by the compiler. "Holmes 1979" lines carry
[F-internal]: the thesis states that "no quotation from it and no information derived from
it may be published without the author's prior consent" - internal use only, do not
publish derived values.

Assumed model values under test:
  gadoids   v 0.4 BL/s, w 0.15 L (barbel / pelvic-ray contact), p 0.5 infauna, 0.3 epifauna
  flatfish  v 0.12 BL/s incl. pauses, w 0.6 L (visual), p 0.7
  v x w for a 17.5 cm gadoid = 7.0 cm/s x 2.6 cm = 18.4 cm2/s; x p 0.3 = 5.5 cm2/s;
  x p 0.5 = 9.2 cm2/s (J).
  v x w for a 15 cm flatfish = 1.8 cm/s x 9 cm = 16 cm2/s; x 0.7 = 11 cm2/s;
  for 25 cm: 45 cm2/s, x 0.7 = 32 cm2/s (J).

General caveat: every lab rate below was measured at a stated prey density, in small
lit tanks, mostly with juveniles. Encounter scales with density, so lab results test
p, w and h (and the order of magnitude of a), not absolute strikes per 6 h in the box.

1.1 Persson et al. 2012, MEPS 456:245-253 (juvenile cod functional response) [F]
    Local: ../Attackrater ur mötesteori/data/benth_validation/persson2012_meps456.pdf
    Set-up [F]:
      - Cod: "similar-sized cod representing 1 yr old fish"; caught at 2-5 m, the Sound.
        Experimental cod TL is NOT stated; the 1-yr cod fed to the cannibal were
        "total length 15 to 20 cm" (so ~15-20 cm is the likely size, J).
      - Held individually in 150 l tanks, 8-9 C, 10-15 psu, "light:dark cycle 12:12 h".
        Light intensity: NOT given (unknown). Arena floor area: NOT given.
      - Habitats: sand, artificial eelgrass (514 shoots m-2), natural Fucus, all on sand.
      - Prey: Neomysis integer TL 13.0 +- 2.0 mm, Crangon crangon TL 40 +- 5 mm,
        Palaemon elegans TL 35 +- 5 mm.
      - "5 initial prey densities (12 to 430 prey m-2)"; field: shrimps < 100 m-2,
        small crustaceans < 500 m-2. Cod starved 48 h; "time between the first and the
        fifth prey consumed was recorded"; Holling II fitted (Type III not better).
    Table 1, attack rate a (cm2 s-1) and handling h (s J-1), +- 1 SE [F]:
                         sand        Zostera     Fucus      | h sand   Zostera  Fucus
      Neomysis integer   57 +- 22    8.8 +- 4    13 +- 7    | 0.026    0.022    0.024
      Crangon crangon    1.8 +- 0.7  0.86 +- 0.3 1.2 +- 0.5 | 0.026    0.038    0.019
      Palaemon elegans   21 +- 32    2.4 +- 2    1.6 +- 1   | 0.033    0.045    0.041
      Quote: "Handling time (h) was prey- but not habitat-dependent, being highest for
      P. elegans and lowest for N. integer (approximately 0.041 and 0.026 s J-1)".
    Crangon buried [F]: the low a on Crangon despite "similar size as P. elegans" is
      explained by "the different anti-predator strategies of P. elegans and C. crangon,
      the former relying on camouflage in the vegetation and the latter burying and
      hiding within the sandy sediment." (Crangon burial was not scored directly.)
    Predator cue: consumption "reduced to 22%" in sand, "to 44%" in vegetation [F].
    Per-item handling (J): the paper gives no J per prey. With literature-typical wet
      weights and ~4 kJ/g ww: Neomysis ~10 mg -> ~40 J -> h ~1 s; Crangon 40 mm ~0.6 g ->
      ~2.2 kJ -> h ~58 s; Palaemon 35 mm ~0.35 g -> ~1.4 kJ -> h ~46 s. These Holling h
      values come from first-to-fifth prey of starved fish and probably include more
      than strike + swallow (J).
    Test of the model (J): sand a for a ~15-20 cm cod: exposed mysids 57, cryptic
      Palaemon 21 (+-32), burying Crangon 1.8 cm2/s. Model gadoid v.w.p on epifauna
      5.5 cm2/s: 4x (Palaemon 21) to 10x (mysids 57) BELOW the exposed visible prey, about
      3x ABOVE the burying shrimp. Buried-vs-exposed penalty on epifauna in the same arena: 57/1.8 = 32x
      (mysids, swimming) and 21/1.8 = 12x (shrimp of equal size). Lab cod hunted by sight
      in light; the w for visible epifauna is a visual reaction distance, not 0.15 L.

1.2 Holmes 1979, PhD thesis, Univ. Stirling, "Studies on the feeding behaviour of

    Local: ../Attackrater ur mötesteori/data/benth_flatfish/holmes1979_thesis_stirling.pdf
    Set-up [F-internal]: fish 8-23 cm (I-II group); observation tanks 175 x 30 cm,

      plaice/worms          -            59 %            41 %
      plaice/corophiids     -            26 %            74 %
      flounder/worms        -            20 %            80 %
      flounder/corophiids   4 %          28 %            68 %
      sole/worms            1 %          75 %            24 %
      all pleuronectids     1 %          33 %            66 %
      ("bottom activity" includes searching locomotion, approach, bite and chew.)
    Locomotion [F-internal]: SHUFFLE, the searching gait, "has an upper limit of about
      10 cm per second" with "eyes mov[ing] independently scanning"; SKIM lower limit
      ~15 cm/s; CREEP (stalking) "would not exceed 1 cm per second but commonly ... 1 mm
      per second". Pleuronectid strike = FORWARD (< half a body length); a raised-head
      ARCH, "attack is made from this position after one or two seconds", "well-suited
      to 'browsing' on sedentary worms that were partly emerged from the sand".
    Handling (J): the median 8 s interval for plaice on worms is an upper bound on
      strike + bite + chew per small worm (it includes search).
    Implied realised search rate (J): plaice on Corophium at ~190 m-2 with 56 s mean
      interval -> a ~ 1/(N t) = 0.9 cm2/s over all observed time (2.5 cm2/s on the
      median), i.e. 3.5 cm2/s per active time. Model flatfish 11 cm2/s (15 cm). The
      lab rate includes satiation pauses and timidity; read as "model not low" (J).

1.3 Burrows 1994, MEPS 115:31-39 (0-group plaice, light vs dark, Artemia prey) [F]
    Local: ../Attackrater ur mötesteori/data/benth_flatfish/burrows1994_meps115_31.pdf
    Plaice 14-35 mm (mean 24 mm), Artemia nauplii in excess, 16:8 L:D, IR video, 10.4-12.6 C.
    "The juvenile plaice were in motion for 2 to 5 % of the total time observed in each
      hour in the light, with the remaining 95 to 98% spent motionless on the bottom. In
      the dark, the plaice were typically active for 10 to 35% of the time" (activity in
      the dark = pelagic swimming, not benthic foraging) [F].
    "Stomachs of experimental plaice that had fed contained between 126 and 890 Artemia
      nauplii" per 24 h trial [F]. Satiation is acknowledged ("may have produced
      satiation, reducing activity in well-fed fish").
    Light: "If feeding is visually mediated, benthic swimming should only be used in
      sufficient light to allow prey capture"; field TV saw "many juvenile plaice ...
      swimming in the light but none ... in the hours of darkness" [F].
    Caveat: tiny fish on pelagic nauplii; not a benthic-item test (J).

=====================================================================================
2. SYNTHESIS PER FACTOR
=====================================================================================

2.1 v and search share
  Gadoids:
  - Cod in situ (all behaviour): modes 0.1-0.4 BL/s, hourly means 0.7-0.9 BL/s, > 70 %
    of fixes < 1 BL/s [F, Winger 2004]; active through 24 h, slightly slower during a
    5-h night period [A, Lokkeborg 1998]; odour plume -> "moderately higher" speed,
    by day slower, restricted search after the plume [A/S].
  - No in situ haddock speed and no measured share of time "searching" vs "travel/
    resting" for cod or haddock on soft bottom was obtained.
  - Verdict (J): 0.4 BL/s is the modal speed; as a search-only speed it is plausible.
    The unknown is the share of the 6 h spent searching on the bottom, which is
    outside v (eat share / hunger in the model).
  Flatfish:
  - Moving speed while searching <= 10 cm/s (Holmes [F-int.]); time active on the
    bottom 59 % (plaice, surface worms) / 26 % (plaice, Corophium) / 33 % pleuronectid
    mean [F-int.]; 0-group plaice in excess food moved only 2-5 % of the time in light
    [F, Burrows 1994]. Plaice "search more efficiently ... when stationary" [A, Hill
    2000].
  - v_moving x active share: if the moving speed is 3-10 cm/s and the active share
    (incl. bites) is 25-60 %, the time mean is 0.75-6 cm/s = 0.05-0.4 BL/s at 15 cm.
    The assumed 0.12 BL/s (1.8 cm/s at 15 cm) sits inside that bracket (J).

2.2 w by detection mode, incl. darkness
  Gadoids, infauna: contact by barbel and trailed pelvic rays (taste buds) [A, Brawn
    1969; A, Harvey & Batty 2002]; no distance exists (contact). "Food covered by sand
    was not found" [A]: so buried infauna counts only where exposed (siphon, arm,
    feeding tentacle). w 0.15 L is consistent with a contact sense; light-independent.
  Gadoids, epifauna: visual and chemical detection at distance in light: Persson
    sand a = 57 (mysids) and 21 (Palaemon) cm2/s vs model 5.5 [F] - w for VISIBLE
    epifauna in light is 4-10x the contact width. Chemical cues give longer reaction
    distance than vision for juvenile cod [A, Meager 2005]; dark predation lower than
    lit (no number) [A]. At box light (1e-3 lux day) visual range is not measured;
    Meager 2010 stops at 0.01 umol (~0.5 lux) [A].
  Flatfish: plaice, dab, lemon sole, witch are visual (large optic lobes) with
    chemical back-up [F, de Groot 1969]; plaice can feed on chemical cues alone in
    darkness, but became less active when any sense was removed [A, Batty & Hoyt 1995].
    Visual foraging of three Pacific flatfish "decreased rapidly below 1e-4 umol photons
    m-2 s-1" (~5e-3 lux) [A, Hurst 2007]; box daytime bottom light (~2e-5 umol) is below
    this knee (J). Siphon cropping by plaice in the lab was independent of light [A,
    Burrows et al. 1994b], 0-group plaice stomach items similar day and night with
    higher night fullness [F, Gibson 1998], dabs grazed Amphiura arms at 40 m with
    "little if any light penetration at the seabed" [F, Kaiser & Ramsay 1997].
  Verdict (J): a visual w 0.6 L for flatfish at 100-300 m in the Skagerrak is not
    supported by the light evidence: the daytime bottom is at or below the visual
    knee and the night is dark. The field evidence shows flatfish still crop siphons
    and arms with little light, i.e. by a short-range chemo-tactile mode; a w nearer
    the gadoid contact/short-range value (0.1-0.3 L) fits better. No measurement of
    the flatfish chemical reaction distance exists beyond de Groot's 5-10 cm tube
    tests (plaice localized, dab did not) [F].

2.3 p (capture per encounter)
  - Free surface worms / Corophium: 99-100 % per attack (plaice, flounder, sole)
    [F-int., Holmes]; mobile mysids 84-89 %, Crangon 72-73 % (turbot, brill)
    [F-int.]. Juvenile cod on fish prey 1.0 at small size ratios, 0.05-0.3 at ratios
    0.35-0.45 [F, Ellis & Gibson 1997].
  - Strike on a retracting part (siphon, arm, worm in burrow): NO per-strike success
    was found. Indirect evidence: 55 % of Amphiura carry recent arm regenerations
    [A]; 2.5-18 % of Donax have flatfish siphon damage [A]; plaice siphon attacks
    sometimes pull the whole Donax out of the sand [A, Ansell 1999].
  - Buried vs exposed epifauna (cod, same arena): Crangon (burying) a = 1.8 vs
    Palaemon (exposed, same size) 21 cm2/s = 12x [F, Persson 2012]; the paper attributes
    it to burying. This is a combined w x p effect, not p alone.
  - Verdict (J): p 0.7 (flatfish) and 0.5 (gadoid) on infauna parts cannot be tested;
    Holmes gives only the free-prey ceiling (~1.0). p 0.3 on epifauna: the buried
    Crangon result says the effective a on burying shrimp is ~1/10 of exposed shrimp,
    which is larger than p alone would carry.

2.4 Handling / strike time per item
  - Small free worms, plaice: median 8 s between attacks incl. search -> h <= ~8 s
    [F-int.]; raised-head strike "after one or two seconds" [F-int.].
  - Cod on crustaceans, Holling h 0.019-0.045 s/J [F]; per item (J) ~1 s (13 mm mysid),
    ~45-60 s (35-40 mm shrimp).
  - Cod on 2-5 cm fish: pursuit 1-2 s, handling 15-30 s [F].
  - mg-sized items: arm piece ~2 mg ww [A/J]. No measured strike time per siphon or arm
    tip was found; by the Holmes upper bound, h per mg item is seconds, so handling
    does not limit intake on infauna parts; the encounter does (J).

2.5 Strike rates observed
  - Plaice (8-23 cm, 50 lux, 15 C) at ~190 prey m-2: ~64 h-1 (mean) on Corophium;
    ~225 h-1 on surface worms (density not stated) [F-int.].
  - Sole on worms ~120 h-1 (mean 30 s) [F-int.]; turbot on mysids ~95 h-1, brill on
    shrimps ~12 h-1 [F-int.].
  - 0-group plaice: 126-890 Artemia per stomach per 24 h trial [F].
  - Field items per stomach, 0-group plaice: 17-95 total, molluscs (siphons, J) 2-16
    [F, Gibson 1998].
  - No field strike rate for adult flatfish or gadoids was found.
