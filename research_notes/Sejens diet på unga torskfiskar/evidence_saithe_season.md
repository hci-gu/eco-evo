# Saithe annual cycle and the Q1 zero of haddock in the diet (item 3)

Literature agent, 2026-10-29. Question: is the ~0 % juvenile haddock in Q1 saithe stomachs (ICES
1991) a PREY effect (juvenile haddock unavailable: too large, too few, elsewhere) or a PREDATOR
effect (spawning migration, reduced feeding, saithe distributed away from the haddock grounds)?

Marks: [S] stated in source, [J] my derivation. Files: `data/settlement/` (PDFs, excerpts),
`data/settlement/datras/` (own survey extraction), existing `data/excerpts/crr219_*` and
`data/sms_keyrun2023/` (read, not modified). Companion: `evidence_settlement.md`.

---

## A. Verdict [J]

Both, and the 1991 Q1 zero is not structural:

1. Q1 is not always zero. In 1981 Q1, saithe 40-70 cm had 6-11 % haddock by weight (1-group,
   125 mm), in a similar number of haul samples (139 in 1981 vs 150 in 1991) (entry 2). But 1991
   is the typical case and 1981 the exception: haddock age 1 in Q1 was 1,580 million in 1991 vs a
   1974-2022 median of 1,826 million (SMS), and 3,492 million (~2x median) in 1981. A low Q1 share
   is normal; 1981 shows the ceiling when a strong year class is still ~12-13 cm in January.
2. Prey effect (explains 1981 vs 1991): the 1990 year class was 45 % as abundant as the 1980 one
   at age 1 in Q1 (SMS: 1,580 vs 3,492 million) AND ~5 cm longer in January (NW 182 vs 129 mm),
   putting it above saithe's preferred prey size (11-22 cm for 40-60 cm saithe) and 27-51 % of it
   at >= 20 cm (entry 3). In 2005-2023, Jan-Feb 1-group average 17-18 cm, 24-34 % >= 19 cm.
3. Predator effect (applies every winter, reduces but does not zero the rate): 26-50 % of Q1
   stomachs are empty at every age (Q2 0-3 %, Q3 8-20 %, Q4 7-33 %), including the mostly immature
   ages 3-4, so it is a winter ration depression rather than a spawning fast alone (entry 1). Its
   size depends on predator length: Q1/Q3 prey weight per stomach is 0.35 / 0.34 at ages 3-4
   (39-45 cm, the fish that carry the Q3 haddock peak) but 0.76 / 1.0 at ages 5-6 (55-62 cm); vs Q4
   it is 0.24-0.32 at ages 3-5. For 55-60 cm saithe the Q1 zero must therefore come mainly from
   prey size / abundance and depth separation, not from ration. Q1 saithe sit deeper (141-154 m) than the 1-group haddock
   (77-98 m) and overlap with them ~2x less than in Q3 in the NE and the northern North Sea as a whole
   (per-year medians; not clearly in the NW) (entry 4). The Q1 diet is
   Norway pout + herring + squid + krill: a deep, pelagic winter prey field (entry 1).
4. Spawning (Jan-Mar, shelf edge) and the associated migration affect only mature fish (majority
   mature at age 6, ~60 cm+) (entries 5-7); the 30-45 cm saithe that drive the Q3 peak are mostly
   immature, so spawning is not the main cause of their Q1 zero.

Model implication [J]: the Q1-Q2 low is produced without a calendar switch by (i) a winter ration
depression of the predator (temperature/activity, entries 1, 8), (ii) a winter depth separation
(saithe on the slope, 1-group haddock on the plateau), and (iii) the prey growing through the
preferred size during winter; the Q3 peak by settlement of a new 0-group at 7-9 cm in July
(`evidence_settlement.md` E).

### 4. DATRAS NS-IBTS 2005-2023 - saithe vs juvenile haddock, Q1 vs Q3 (own extraction)

### 5. ICES FishMap saithe factsheet

- Citation: ICES FishMap species factsheet - saithe (EU-RFP repository, ~2006). PDF:
  `data/settlement/ices_fishmap_saithe.pdf`, text `excerpts/ices_fishmap_saithe.txt`.
- [S] "Spawning takes place from January (in the southern part of the distribution area) to May
  (further north), and generally occurs along the edge of the continental shelf."
- [S] "The majority has reached maturity at age 6" (IBTS Q1 2000-2004, Fig. 2).
- [S] distribution: concentrated "along the shelf edge", "a clear zone of high abundance along the
  edge of the Norwegian Deep" (Q1 IBTS 1977-2005); "IBTS data show that density in the northeastern
  North Sea is markedly higher during summer than during winter, suggesting dispersal during the
  feeding season" (Fig. 3, quarterly 1991-1995).
- [S] vertical: "Adults sometimes display extensive diel vertical migrations and may be found
  hundreds of metres above the bottom during night time, as well as close to the bottom, down at
  depths of more than 300 m during day time."
- [S] food: "Adults feed almost entirely on pelagic and demersal fish, such as herring, Norway pout,
  haddock and sandeel, though euphausids and other invertebrates are also consumed."

### 7. Homrum et al. 2013 - migration of saithe in the NE Atlantic (tagging)

- Citation: Homrum, E.I., et al. 2013. Migration of saithe (Pollachius virens) in the Northeast
  Atlantic. ICES J. Mar. Sci. 70: 782-792. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fst048. PDF:
  `data/settlement/homrum2013_saithe_migration.pdf`.
- [S] tagging mostly in summer, "outside of the main spawning season in February-March";
  "For adult saithe tagged in Norwegian waters (length at tagging > 60 cm) there was a seasonal
  pattern in the distance from tagging site, with the farthest distances observed in February-March
  and the shortest in the late summer months ... saithe come back to the same feeding grounds";
  "the southwards spawning migration from northern Norway often extends into the North Sea".
- Use [J]: adults (> 60 cm) leave summer feeding grounds in Feb-Mar to spawn; no equivalent for
  30-45 cm immature saithe.

### 8. Armannsson & Jonsson 2012 - vertical migrations of saithe from data storage tags (Iceland)

- Citation: ICES J. Mar. Sci. 69: 1372-1381. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fss121. PDF:
  `data/settlement/armannsson2012_saithe_dst_iceland.pdf`.
- Data [S]: 51 DSTs, hourly depth, 268,018 records.
- [S] "a movement from deep to shallow water during summer (May-July) and back to deeper water in
  winter (November-March)"; "The median depth was largest during March-April (140 m) but shallowest
  during summer and autumn (< 50 m)"; "diel and seasonal differences in hourly depth changes of
  saithe, indicating reduced activity during night-time and in winter"; trawler catch proportion
  highest in darkness, "especially evident in the first 2 months of the year".
- Use [J]: winter = deeper and less active, independent of spawning; supports a winter ration
  depression. Icelandic waters, not the North Sea.

### 9. Neilson et al. 2003 - pre-spawning aggregations of pollock (Scotian Shelf)

- Citation: ICES J. Mar. Sci. 60: 860-871. doi:10.1016/S1054-3139(03)00068-7. PDF:
  `data/settlement/neilson2003_pollock_prespawning_acoustics.pdf`.
- [S] pre-spawning aggregations form each autumn at fixed sites; "while pollock can occur up to 30 m
