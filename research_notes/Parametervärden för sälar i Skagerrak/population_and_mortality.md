# Harbour and grey seals in Skagerrak-Kattegat: population size, demography, mortality and anthropogenic losses (for the Mareld seal FG)

Scope note: model box 57.81-58.31 N, 10.07-11.15 E (60 x 60 km = 3,600 km2). Its NE corner (58.31 N, 11.15 E) lies a few km SW of Hallo/Smogen (~58.34 N, 11.21 E); the Lysekil archipelago (~11.3-11.45 E) is just east of the box, Vaderoarna (~58.55 N, 11.05 E) ~25-30 km north, Koster ~65 km north. (Coordinates of the places are my own approximate reading, not from a cited source.)

Current model values under review: initial biomass 15 t (range 5-30 t, "~190 seals at 80 kg"); min_split_biomass 80 kg; r_max 0.11 /yr; M1 0.10 /yr; no predator on seals.

## 1. Current counts and population size (2015-2025), correction factor, local haul-outs

### Takeaway
The Kattegat-Skagerrak harbour seal metapopulation counted ~12,500 seals hauled out in August 2023 (~20,000 animals after correction), having peaked in 2016-2017 and declined since. Swedish Skagerrak holds ~6,000-7,000 animals (HaV 2025), Bohuslan/Vastra Gotaland ~3,300 counted in its Skagerrak part, Norwegian Skagerrak ~900; no published per-colony counts for Vaderoarna/Lysekil/Smogen were found. The 15 t starting biomass is plausible only as a low-end value and depends strongly on how much shallow (<50 m) feeding ground the box contains.

### Cited Findings
**Metapopulation (Swedish + Danish + Norwegian Kattegat-Skagerrak)**
- Estimated combined Kattegat-Skagerrak moult count in 2023: 12,507 (95% CI 11,558-13,456); aerial surveys 2003-2023 at the August moult, usually 2-3 replicate surveys per haul-out per year; counts peaked in 2017 (Kattegat, combined) and 2016 (Skagerrak) - [Carroll et al. 2025, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- Applying the previously assumed hauled-out fraction of 57-65% (from Olsen et al. 2010 and Silva et al. 2021), the 2023 population is "close to 20,000"; the authors call these "coarse estimates only" - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- Pre-2002 PDV mean counts ~10,000; an 1890 population estimate from bounty statistics was 16,500 - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- Population grew from ~2,500 animals in the late 1970s to ~25,000 in 2015 (Backlin et al. 2016); ~10,000 at the 1988 PDV epidemic and ~18,000 at the 2002 epidemic - [Frontiers Ecol. Evol. 2018, 10.3389/fevo.2018.00059](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- HELCOM 2023: hauled-out counts in the Kattegat unit ranged 7,500-11,000 during 2016-2021, "the lowest estimated population size for Kattegat is 12 500 individuals"; Kattegat + SW Baltic minimum 14,500 (above the 10,000 Limit Reference Level). The 2020-2021 drop was "mainly driven by a drop in counted seals in the Swedish waters of the Kattegat" - [HELCOM 2023 harbour seal abundance indicator](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf)

**Swedish Skagerrak / Bohuslan**
- HaV 2025: "Totala antalet i respektive population uppskattas till 6 000-7 000 individer i Skagerrak och 13 000-14 000 i Kattegat (svenska och danska vatten)"; counts of both fell over the last five years (-2.4%/yr Kattegat, -3.51%/yr Skagerrak; Carroll pers. comm. 2024); pup counts in Kosterhavet NP have fallen since 2014 - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- HaV 2025: the Skagerrak population is "today below" the HELCOM 10,000 threshold, Kattegat "close to" it - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- (Snippet only) Lansstyrelsen Vastra Gotaland 2025: mean August haul-out count in the county ~4,800 seals, ~3,300 in the Skagerrak part and ~1,500 in the Kattegat part, corresponding to ~7,400 animals in total (citing Harding & Carroll 2025); county subpopulations are Koster, Vaderoarna, Lysekil, Marstrand and Onsala - [Lansstyrelsen VG redovisning, regeringen.se (2025-10-20)](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/110d77cdff8649baaea613df22549277/lansstyrelsen-vastra-gotalands-redovisning-oversyn-av-salskyddsomraden-som-inte-langre-ar-nodvandiga-for-att-uppratthalla-gynnsam-bevarandestatus-for-sal.pdf)
- Kosterhavet NP colony ~3,000 seals (older figure; annual late-summer surveys since 1978) - [Frontiers Ecol. Evol. 2022 (search snippet)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2022.905309/xml/nlm?isPublishedV2=false)

Per-subregion counts are only in the paper's supplementary S4 Table (not retrieved). Vaderoarna, Hallo, Smogen, Tjorn and Orust are not named in the text - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- Regional final rates of change (individuals/yr): Skagerrak -228 (SE 102, CI -430 to -27); Kattegat -236 (SE 111); combined -408 (SE 242, CI -882 to +67) - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)

- "det totalt er rundt 900 steinkobber i hele norsk Skagerrak" (steinkobbe = harbour seal; a search summary mislabelled this as grey seals). GPS-tagged harbour seals from the outer Oslofjord migrate to both Swedish and Danish areas; immigration "sannsynligvis stor", explaining the large increase at Hvaler and Vestfold/Telemark in August 2022 - [Fiskeridirektoratet, horing regulering av jakt pa kystsel 2025](https://fiskeridir.no/hoeringer/regulering-av-jakt-pa-kystsel-i-2025/horing-regulering-av-jakt-pa-kystsel-i-2025.pdf)

**Danish Kattegat / Limfjord**
- HaV 2025 puts Kattegat (Swedish + Danish) at 13,000-14,000 animals - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)

**Correction factor (count -> population)**
- 57-65% hauled out during moult surveys (Olsen et al. 2010; Silva et al. 2021) -> multiply counts by ~1.54-1.75 - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- HaV 2025: "Uppskattningsvis observeras cirka 60-80% av populationen (varierar mellan populationer)" (Harkonen et al. 1999) -> factor 1.25-1.67 - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- HELCOM 2023 implicitly uses 7,500 counted -> 12,500 animals (factor ~1.67, i.e. 60% hauled out) - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf)
- 1988: from dead-seal numbers vs counts, "about 77% of the population" was hauled out during surveys - [Harkonen & Heide-Jorgensen 1990, Z. Saugetierkunde 55](https://archive.org/download/biostor-183335/biostor-183335.pdf)

**Density / space use (for scaling to the model box)**
- Seals per km2 of feeding ground shallower than 50 m, Skagerrak: 0.26 (1988) -> 1.18 (2002); Kattegat 0.39 -> 0.54; Skagerrak total area ~32,200 km2 of which ~8,890 km2 shallower than 50 m (Kattegat ~21,800 / 20,170 km2) - [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- Harbour seals are highly site-faithful; telemetry home ranges are largest for yearlings (6,414 km2) and smallest for adults (Dietz et al. 2013) - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- Harding (2022): seals tagged in the Oslofjord swim to the Kattegat and back within weeks; harbour seals hunt on open bottoms, not in the kelp belt or the free water column - [Harding, Vara Rovdjur 2/2022](https://goteborg.naturskyddsforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/94/2024/09/Presentation-Karin-Harding-knubbsalar.pdf)

### Inferences
- (Own calculation) Current model 15 t = 3,600 km2 x ~4.2 kg/km2. At 80 kg/seal that is ~190 seals (0.05 seals/km2); at the age-structured mean mass of ~55 kg (section 3) it is ~270 seals (0.075 seals/km2).
- (Own calculation) Skagerrak shallow-ground density today: if the Skagerrak (Swedish 6,000-7,000 + Norwegian ~900) holds ~7,000-8,000 animals over ~8,890 km2 of <50 m ground, that is ~0.8-0.9 seals per km2 of shallow ground (vs 1.18 in 2002). A defensible box estimate is N_box ~ 0.85 x (km2 of the box shallower than 50 m) + a smaller contribution from deeper water. If e.g. 300 km2 of the box (the eastern coastal strip) is <50 m, N_box ~ 250 seals ~ 14 t at 55 kg - i.e. the current 15 t. If 1,000 km2 is <50 m, ~850 seals ~ 47 t. The modeller should compute the shallow area from the project's depth layer.
- (Own calculation from the repo's `djup.png`, EMODnet mean depth on the 60x60 model grid.) The PNG is scaled 0-255 relative to the box's maximum depth, which is not stored in the repo, so the <50 m area depends on that maximum: if max = 200 m -> 1,588 km2 <50 m; 300 m -> 538 km2; 400 m -> 104 km2; 500 m -> 42 km2. The shallowest cell is at 20/255 of max (16-39 m for max 200-500 m), i.e. only the NE corner near Hallo is coastal-shallow. Re-running `make_djup.py` prints the max depth and settles this. For a plausible max of 300-500 m, the <50 m area is ~40-540 km2 -> ~35-460 seals from shallow-ground density alone (~2-25 t at 55 kg), plus some use of deeper water. This supports keeping 15 t as the central value, with 5-30 t as the sensitivity range.
- (Own inference) Colony-share cross-check: the box is adjacent to the Hallo/Smogen and Lysekil haul-outs and ~25-30 km from Vaderoarna. With ~5,000-7,000 seals in Swedish Skagerrak spread over Koster, Vaderoarna and Lysekil subpopulations, a few thousand animals live within one yearling home-range of the box; if they spend 10-30% of their at-sea time inside it, that gives roughly 250-900 seal-equivalents (14-50 t at 55 kg). The 5-30 t range in the library is thus reasonable, with 15 t at the low-to-mid end; 30 t would be a reasonable upper sensitivity value. This is a coarse reasoning chain, not a literature estimate.
- The population is at or slightly below carrying capacity (2023 count 12,507 vs logistic K 13,965 counted seals, ~90%), so the seal FG should start near its equilibrium (net growth ~0 or slightly negative), not in a recovery phase.

### Gaps
- No per-haul-out counts for Vaderoarna, Hallo/Smogen, the Lysekil archipelago or Tjorn/Orust were found; they exist in Carroll et al. 2025 S4 Table / S2 Fig (not retrieved) and in Swedish NRM/GU monitoring data (Harding & Carroll 2025, cited by Lansstyrelsen VG). The Lansstyrelsen VG 2025 PDF could not be fetched (404/HTML), so its 4,800/3,300/1,500/7,400 figures are from a search summary.
- No telemetry-based estimate of how much time Bohuslan seals spend >15-20 km offshore in the deep Skagerrak was found.
- No Danish (DCE) 2023-2025 numbers broken out per Danish subregion were retrieved.

## 2. Population trend, epidemics, density dependence and r_max

### Takeaway
Maximum intrinsic growth of this population is ~11-13 %/yr (lambda ~1.12; HELCOM uses 12%), observed after 1988 when the population was far below K; growth slowed to 5-6.5%/yr after 2002 and has been ~0 or negative since ~2016-2017 (logistic fit, K ~14,000 counted). r_max = 0.11 /yr is well supported (it equals ln(1.117)).

### Cited Findings
- History: c. 2,500 in Kattegat-Skagerrak in the late 1970s after hunting; hunting banned in the 1960s (Sweden, protection 1970s) - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf); Harding: hunted from ~30,000 to ~5,000 in Scandinavia in the early 1900s; all Swedish seals protected in the 1970s except some protective hunting at gear - [Harding 2022](https://goteborg.naturskyddsforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/94/2024/09/Presentation-Karin-Harding-knubbsalar.pdf)
- Epidemics: PDV killed "more than 50% of the population" in 1988 and "about 30%" in 2002; unexplained elevated mortality in 2007; 2014 avian influenza H10N7 caused "a total mortality of approximately 10%" - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf). Carroll et al. 2025 state PDV killed "up to 60%" each time - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933). The 1988 count fell 60% vs expected and 47% vs 1987 - [Harkonen & Heide-Jorgensen 1990](https://archive.org/download/biostor-183335/biostor-183335.pdf)
- Maximum growth: "Depleted, undisturbed harbour seal populations are expected to grow by approximately 12% per year"; the trend threshold for good status is "3% below the maximum rate of increase ... i.e. 9 % annual rate of increase for harbour seals" - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf)
- Carroll et al. 2025: maximum intrinsic growth for harbour seals "slightly under 13% per year"; after 1988 PDV 10.2-13.6 %/yr; after 2002 PDV 5% (Kattegat) and 6.5% (Skagerrak) - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- Skagerrak growth 11%/yr before 2002, 6.5% for 2003-2015 - [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- Silva et al. 2021 PVA (abstract): the metapopulation "appears close to carrying capacity"; it can sustain modest hunting and infrequent epizootics "if the population can resume its high intrinsic rate of increase at about 11% annually"; slight fecundity reductions make it much more vulnerable - [Silva et al. 2021, Ecosphere 12:e03343](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecs2.3343)
- Density dependence / K: logistic model fits Kattegat-Skagerrak, Kattegat and Skagerrak best; K = 13,965 +/- 772 counted seals for Kattegat-Skagerrak; leading hypothesis for the decline is food limitation, lower birth rates and possibly lower juvenile survival - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- HELCOM 2023: Kattegat growth 3.8 %/yr over 2003-2021 (80% support >= 3.3%); Kattegat had been assessed at TRL in HOLAS II; "levelling off ... as a consequence of density dependence cannot be ruled out" - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf)
- Early density-dependence signal: Skagerrak asymptotic length fell ~7 cm in both sexes between 1988 and 2002 (F 147.5 -> 138.9 cm; M 157.7 -> 148.7 cm) while Kattegat did not change - [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- Harding 2022: diet shifting from energy-rich herring/cod to lower-quality poor cod, Norway pout, snake blenny; prey size falling from ~10-30 cm to ~7-20 cm; "Knubbsalen far max en unge per ar nar de har fri tillgang pa foda, men vid lagre fodotillgang far de en unge bara vartannat ar" - [Harding 2022](https://goteborg.naturskyddsforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/94/2024/09/Presentation-Karin-Harding-knubbsalar.pdf)
- Pregnancy rate of Swedish west-coast females aged 6-25: 67% (8/12) in 2022/2023 - [NRM report 2023/2024](https://www.nrm.se/download/18.228a5aae196337e33b9543f/1744701997761/Rapport%20s%C3%A4lar%202023-2424.pdf)
- HaV 2025: no strong signs of increased disease, falling pregnancy rates (2016-2021) or abnormal mortality; the decline is probably a mix of fewer seals and fewer hauling out (foraging further offshore as coastal fish decline, or disturbance from boats and hunting) - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- Comparison: Dutch Wadden Sea harbour seals grew 12%/yr after the hunting ban (Brasseur et al. 2018) - [Aarts et al., bioRxiv 267567](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/267567v1.full)

### Inferences
- r_max: lambda_max ~1.11-1.13 -> instantaneous r = ln(lambda) ~ 0.10-0.12 /yr. The current 0.11 /yr is central and needs no change. If the model treats r_max as a discrete annual rate, 0.12 is the HELCOM value; the difference is within literature spread.
- The post-2002 (5-6.5%) and 2003-2021 (3.8%) rates are density-suppressed and must not be used as r_max.
- Since 2016 the population is at ~K; in the model, food limitation (not an extra mortality term) should hold the seal group at its starting level. Epidemics (1988, 2002, 2014) are rare pulses (roughly one per 12-14 years, 10-60% loss) and are outside a 1-year model horizon; they are not part of a steady M1.

### Gaps
- No separate post-2014 growth rate was found.
- Silva et al. 2021 full text (parameter tables, hunting scenarios) was not accessible (403).

## 3. Survival by age, natural mortality, and body mass

### Takeaway
Literature survival for this population: pups (0-1 yr) ~0.75 (mass-dependent 0.63-0.96 over the first winter), subadults ~0.89, adult females ~0.95, adult males ~0.91. My Leslie calculations give a biomass-weighted mortality of ~0.09-0.10 /yr for a growing population and ~0.10-0.14 /yr near K, and a population mean body mass of ~52-57 kg (not 80 kg). The mass-at-age vector is my assumption anchored on sourced adult masses.

### Cited Findings
- (Snippet, verify) Silva et al. 2021 model: survival 75% for pups 0-1, 89% for subadults 1-4, 95% for adults 4+, with age-specific fertility - [Silva et al. 2021 via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348743844_Risk_for_overexploiting_a_seemingly_stable_seal_population_influence_of_multiple_stressors_and_hunting)
- (Snippet, verify) Adult survival female 0.95 and male 0.91 derived from dead-seal data from the Kattegat-Skagerrak after the 1988 epizootic, assuming population growth rate 0.11 (Harkonen & Heide-Jorgensen 1990), as cited in a Scottish harbour seal survival study - [Arso Civil et al. 2025, Ecology and Evolution](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ece3.72349)
- First-winter pup survival is mass-dependent (Harding et al. 2005): 0.63 for lean pups (17 kg in early October), 0.96 for heavy pups (32 kg) - [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- 1988 epizootic mortality was selective: 52% of 1+ males died, and more mature (61%) than immature seals died than expected, altering the age structure - [Harkonen & Heide-Jorgensen 1990](https://archive.org/download/biostor-183335/biostor-183335.pdf)
- Females usually mature (ovulate) at ~4 years; pregnancy 67% in 2022/23 - [NRM report 2023/2024](https://www.nrm.se/download/18.228a5aae196337e33b9543f/1744701997761/Rapport%20s%C3%A4lar%202023-2424.pdf)
- Bycaught harbour seals examined 2022-2024 were 0-3 years old; shot ones 0-28 years - [NRM report 2023/2024](https://www.nrm.se/download/18.228a5aae196337e33b9543f/1744701997761/Rapport%20s%C3%A4lar%202023-2424.pdf)
- Adult mass: "Males rarely exceed 100 kg and females rarely exceed 90 kg (Harkonen and Heide-Jorgensen 1990)"; "Mature weight averages 90 kg for adult males, and 70 kg for females (Boulva and McLaren 1979)" (Canadian population) - [COSEWIC harbour seal report ch. 3](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/harbour-seal/chapter-3.html)
- Maximum sizes (Swedish museum teaching sheet): females up to ~125 kg / ~160 cm, males up to ~150 kg / ~180 cm (rounded maxima, not means) - [NRM Forskarjakten elevmaterial](https://www.nrm.se/download/18.7c635ae2187fd17ebf217b65/1684766661254/Forskarjakten%20-%20elevmaterial.pdf)
- Asymptotic length (Gompertz), Skagerrak 2002: females 138.9 cm, males 148.7 cm, shorter than other regions - [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- Pups weigh 17-32 kg in early October (Skagerrak) - [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00059/full)
- Energy requirement: Skagerrak harbour seals on average 4,680 kcal/day "when taking the local population structure into account" (vs 5,890 kcal/day for adult males), Harkonen & Heide-Jorgensen 1991 - [Aarts et al., bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/267567v1.full)

  | 0.60 / 0.85 / 0.95 / 0.91 / 0.67 / 5 | 1.045 | 56.3 | 0.153 | 0.111 |
  | 0.40 / 0.85 / 0.95 / 0.91 / 0.67 / 5 (towards K) | 1.017 | 57.1 | 0.198 | 0.126 |

  - The literature parameter set reproduces lambda ~1.12, consistent with the observed r_max; so M1 = 0.10 /yr (biomass-weighted) is the mortality that goes with r_max = 0.11 in the same demographic regime. Keep M1 = 0.10.
  - 0-3-year-olds are ~51-57% of numbers in these age structures, so a population "average seal" is much lighter than an adult.
  - Population mean mass ~52-57 kg (range ~50-60 kg allowing for the mass-vector uncertainty). This is driven by the assumed mass vector, not by survival (mean mass barely moves across the survival scenarios).
- Consequences for the library: 15 t corresponds to ~270 seals at 55 kg (not ~190 at 80 kg). For min_split_biomass (one indivisible seal), ~55 kg represents the population-mean animal; 70-90 kg represents an adult. 80 kg is defensible only as an "adult" unit; ~55-60 kg is the better match to "one average seal" in an age-structured population.
- Near K, real juvenile mortality is higher (lower s0), pushing biomass-weighted total mortality to ~0.11-0.14 /yr; in the model this extra loss should emerge from food limitation/starvation rather than a larger M1.

### Gaps
- A search snippet mentioned a mean overwinter pup survival of 0.89 (SD 0.08) from a ~2021 pup-weight study; the source could not be identified, so it is not used.
- No published mass-at-age table or Gompertz mass curve for Kattegat-Skagerrak harbour seals was found (Harkonen & Heide-Jorgensen 1990 Ophelia "Comparative life histories" was not accessed; the 1990 Z. Saugetierkunde paper has no mass data). The 0.75/0.89/0.95 and 0.95/0.91 survival values come from search snippets of papers that returned 403.

## 4. Anthropogenic losses and whether published growth rates are net of them

### Takeaway
Current Swedish hunting removes ~0-400 harbour seals/yr (285 in 2023/24, 31 in 2024/25, quotas 200 for 2025/26 and 2026/27), Norway ~35-50/yr in Skagerrak, Denmark ~10+/yr; bycatch is poorly known (150 [45-318]/yr on the Swedish west coast in 2001-2006; 410 in 2001). The claim that r_max is "net of bycatch and hunting" is partly true for bycatch (the high 1988-2002 growth occurred with 1980s-90s bycatch already present) but not for today's hunt, which started at scale only around 2020.

### Cited Findings
**Sweden - hunting**
- Hunting history on the west coast: 2005-2008 and 2012 individual permits only; county quotas for protective hunt 2009-2011 and from 2013; licence hunt from 2022 - [NRM report 2023/2024](https://www.nrm.se/download/18.228a5aae196337e33b9543f/1744701997761/Rapport%20s%C3%A4lar%202023-2424.pdf)
- "Protective hunting of harbour seals has occurred in Swedish waters since the early 2000s and licence hunting was introduced in 2022, with a current quota of 730 harbour seals" - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf)
- Protective hunting rose to 390 seals in 2020; 2022 licence quota ~700 with "less than 50%" filled; licence hunting not renewed in 2024 - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- 2021-early 2022: protective hunt permitted for 900 harbour seals (parliamentary answer, snippet) - [Riksdagen, skriftlig fraga licensjakt pa sal](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/skriftlig-fraga/licensjakt-pa-sal_h8112859/)
- 2023/2024 licence quota 630 (Vastra Gotaland 400, Halland 200, Skane 30) - [Naturvardsverket beslut licensjakt knubbsal 2023-2024](https://www.naturvardsverket.se/496636/contentassets/b2df51a2c90e49b8a0147abc33523424/beslut-licensjakt-knubbssal-2023-2024.pdf)
- (Snippet; page 404 on fetch) Reported shot: "2023 och borjan av 2024" Vastra Gotaland 178, Halland 107 (total 285); "2024 och borjan av 2025" Vastra Gotaland 24, Halland 7 (total 31; case-by-case protective hunt) - [Naturvardsverket, Jakt pa knubbsal 2023-2025](https://www.naturvardsverket.se/lagar-och-regler/beslut/sal/knubbsal/)
- Protective hunt 2025/2026: 200 harbour seals (VG, Halland, part of Skane) - [Naturvardsverket beslut skyddsjakt knubbsal 2025-2026](https://naturvardsverket.se/496464/contentassets/b2df51a2c90e49b8a0147abc33523424/2025-03-27-beslut-skyddsjakt-knubbsal-2025-2026.pdf); 2026/2027: 200 in total, first-period quota VG 140, Halland 50, Skane 10 - [Naturvardsverket beslut knubbsal 2026-2027](https://qr.jagareforbundet.se/contentassets/1464f50c739849c2b0d942216902f384/beslut-knubbsal-2026-2027.pdf)
- Hunters' association (2024) reports "vikande siffror for avskjutning av knubbsal" (falling bag) because there is no market for seal products and hunting is weather-dependent - [Jagareforbundet samrad 2024](https://jagareforbundet.se/contentassets/db5491c5b1b14a879a81e6047fc1957c/yttrande-over-samrad-infor-jakt-efter-sal-nv-06511-23.pdf)

**Norway and Denmark - hunting**
- Norway: 35-49 harbour seals taken annually in quota hunt (2015-2022) in the Skagerrak area; Denmark ~10/yr derogation shooting in 2018, since increased - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- Norwegian 2025 quota proposals: Ostfold 52, Vestfold 50, Telemark 17 harbour seals - [Fiskeridirektoratet horing 2025](https://fiskeridir.no/hoeringer/regulering-av-jakt-pa-kystsel-i-2025/horing-regulering-av-jakt-pa-kystsel-i-2025.pdf)

**Bycatch**
- Lunneryd et al. 2004 (fisher interviews): 461 (333-506) harbour seals drowned in Swedish commercial fisheries in 2001, considered an underestimate (part-time and recreational fishing excluded); most in fixed traps for salmon/eel and gill/trammel nets for flatfish and cod - [Fiskeriverket Finfo 2004:8](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.64f5b3211343cffddb2800019055/1348912831293/finfo2004_8.pdf)
- For the Swedish west coast alone, total bycatch 2001 estimated at 410 harbour seals, of which the trawl segment 18%; voluntary-logbook extrapolation for 2001-2006 gives 150 seals/yr (95% CI 45-318) in commercial fyke-net, gill-net and static-gear fisheries (gill nets 116, fyke nets 26, static gear 8); "the by-catch of harbour seals is not more than some hundred animals per year"; trawl and recreational fisheries not covered - [Lundstrom et al. 2010, NAMMCO Sci. Publ. 8](https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/NAMMCOSP/article/view/2697)
- "The current level of incidental catches of harbour seals in the HELCOM area is unknown" - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf); HaV 2025: number dying in bycatch "ar idag osakert" - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- Carroll et al. 2025: bycatch poorly quantified, "no reason to believe" it has increased; hunting "a possible contributing factor" to the reduced growth, but Danish colonies with negligible hunting also decline; the paper does not model hunting separately - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- 9 of 54 harbour seals examined by NRM in 2023/24 were bycaught (all 0-3 yr; 4 of them from Kalmarsund) - [NRM report 2023/2024](https://www.nrm.se/download/18.228a5aae196337e33b9543f/1744701997761/Rapport%20s%C3%A4lar%202023-2424.pdf)

### Inferences
- Verdict on double counting (own reasoning):
  - Bycatch: the r_max values (10.2-13.6 %/yr after 1988; Skagerrak 11% before 2002) were observed while gillnet/fyke-net bycatch of order 150-410 seals/yr was already occurring, and the survival rates behind M1 (0.95/0.91 adults, from 1988 carcass life tables) likewise embed that era's background bycatch. With a Swedish west-coast population of ~10,000-15,000 then (not directly sourced; from the ~18,000 metapopulation at 2002), that is ~1-3 %/yr of numbers, and less by biomass since victims are 0-3 yr old. So adding a full bycatch impact on top of r_max and M1 double counts roughly ~1 %/yr of biomass. The previous review's claim is therefore partly correct for bycatch.
  - Hunting: not double counted. Hunting was negligible until the 2000s and reached hundreds/yr only around 2020 (390 in 2020, ~285 in 2023/24). None of the growth rates usable as r_max include it. The only rates that include it (2003-2023 GAM rates ~-3 to +4 %/yr) are density-suppressed and not usable as r_max.
  - This fits the project's growth-budget split (AGENTS.md): r_max intrinsic, M1 non-FG natural losses, fishing/hunting as impacts outside the budget. Treat Swedish/Norwegian hunting as an explicit impact; treat bycatch either as already inside M1 (simplest: leave it out of the impact set) or, if added as an impact, reduce M1 by ~0.01-0.02 /yr to avoid the overlap.
- (Own calculation) Hunting rate scaled to Vastra Gotaland (~7,400 animals): 178/7,400 = 2.4 %/yr (2023/24), 24/7,400 = 0.3 %/yr (2024/25), quota ceiling 140/7,400 = 1.9 %/yr (2026/27). Hunting is concentrated near fixed gear and skerries close to the coast, so its intensity inside an offshore box is probably lower than the county mean. A box-level hunting impact of ~0.005-0.02 /yr (biomass) is a defensible range; 0.01 /yr is a central value.
- (Own calculation) Bycatch 150 (45-318)/yr on the Swedish west coast vs ~7,400 (VG) + Halland seals today is ~1-3 %/yr of numbers if the 2001-2006 rate still applied; gillnet effort has probably changed since but no recent estimate exists.

### Gaps
- No official full-year hunting-bag statistics for 2020-2025 were directly accessed (Naturvardsverket page 404); 2022 licence-hunt outcome is only "less than 50% of ~700" (Carroll).
- No post-2006 bycatch estimate for the Swedish west coast; no Norwegian Skagerrak or Danish Kattegat bycatch estimate was found.
- Silva et al. 2021's hunting scenarios (sustainable off-take levels) not accessed.

## 5. Grey seal vs harbour seal: should "seals" be harbour seals only?

### Takeaway
Yes. Grey seals are rare in Skagerrak-Kattegat (a few dozen historically, slowly increasing on the Danish Kattegat side) and nearly absent from Bohuslan; the model's seal FG should be parameterised as harbour seals only. Including grey seals would also break the one-seal unit size.

### Cited Findings
- HaV 2025: "I Vasterhavet (Nordostatlanten) finns en stor population av grasal, men endast ett fatal individer av den populationen forekommer i svenska vatten"; grey seal is Sweden's physically largest seal species - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- Grey seals "remain uncommon" in Kattegat-Skagerrak; displacement of harbour seals by grey seals considered an unlikely driver of the decline - [Carroll et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0326933)
- (Snippet) Kattegat-Skagerrak grey seals stable at about 25 individuals since the 1970s; successful pupping recorded only 3 times in 30 years - [Harkonen et al. 2007, NAMMCO Sci. Publ. 6](https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/NAMMCOSP/article/view/2721)
- (Snippet) Recent surveys show grey seals increasing in the Kattegat and present at haul-outs at Samso and Laeso with newborn pups, previously confined to Anholt - [Cambridge Core article (search snippet)](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/product/764FA3AF71B4FBED51EBE9D0028C8316/core-reader)
- Grey seals were extirpated in the Kattegat in the 1930s - [HELCOM 2023](https://indicators.helcom.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Population-trends-and-abundance-of-seals-Harbour-seals_Final_April_2023-1.pdf)
- Older Swedish guidance: only around ten grey seals on the west coast, not part of monitoring (search snippet) - [Naturvardsverket handledning grasal](https://www.naturvardsverket.se/globalassets/vagledning/miljoovervakning/handledning/undersokningstyper/grasal-v1-2.pdf)
- HaV 2025 lists growing grey seal numbers (from the Atlantic or the Baltic) as a possible future threat to harbour seals through competition and direct predation (Svensson 2012; Westphal et al. 2023) - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)
- For context (not Skagerrak): Baltic grey seals counted ~46,000 in 2023, growth 4.9 %/yr 2003-2023 - [HaV Rapport 2025:2](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.6ab16f9919457c08995bc8f9/1737630880438/Rappor%20t-RU-Sal-populationer-salskador-roll-i-ekosystemet.pdf)

### Inferences
- Use harbour seal parameters only (r_max 0.11, M1 0.10, mean mass ~55 kg). Grey seal biomass in the box is negligible (likely < 1 t equivalent, own judgement). A few grey seals mixed into the group would bias the unit size upwards, since grey seals are much larger.
- "No predator eats seals" is consistent with Carroll et al. 2025 (white sharks absent, killer whales sporadic). Grey seal predation on harbour seals is a documented risk elsewhere but not a present-day factor here.

### Gaps
- No current (2020-2025) grey seal counts for Swedish Skagerrak or the Danish Kattegat were retrieved (Danish DCE monitoring not accessed).
