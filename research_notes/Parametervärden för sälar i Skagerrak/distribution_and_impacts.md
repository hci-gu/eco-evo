## Q1. How seals should be distributed at the start of a simulation (spawn), and how dense they plausibly are in the 60x60 km offshore grid

### Takeaway
Harbour seal at-sea density is driven first by distance to haul-out and second by water depth (shallow preferred); hotspots extend to roughly 50 km from haul-outs, and half of trips stay within ~25 km. In this grid the haul-outs are all outside the east/north-east edge and the western two thirds is 180-450 m deep Norwegian Trench slope, so a coast-anchored spawn (density decaying westward from the east edge, strongest in the NE and SE corners) is far more realistic than 7 random colonies or a prey-driven spawn. Plausible abundance at sea inside the grid is of the order of 10^2 seals (roughly 100-700, Own calc), concentrated in the easternmost 10-20 km; the current `inference_initial_biomass: 15` t (about 190 seals at 80 kg) is inside that range.

### Cited Findings
Distribution drivers and ranges (literature):
- Carter et al. 2022 (239 GPS-tagged harbour seals, UK and Ireland): "distance to haulout site was also the primary driver of distribution for harbour seals in all regions"; harbour seal distribution was "predominantly driven by a negative association with increasing distance from haulout and water depth", so predicted density is "more tightly concentrated in coastal and inshore waters compared to grey seals". Harbour seal SAC-specific at-sea distributions had "hotspots of density extending outwards to ~50 km from the SAC boundaries". In one region the best model showed "a strong negative association with distance for areas >20 km from the haulout". The exception was SE England (The Wash/Humber, shallow sandbanks), where the distance and depth effects were weaker and density extended further offshore. — [Carter et al. 2022, Front. Mar. Sci. 9:875869 (PDF)](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/274391/1/274391.pdf)
- Carter et al. 2022: the maximum at-sea geodesic distance from a haul-out by any tracked seal was 273 km for harbour seals and 448 km for grey seals (used as the accessibility radius). Only 44% of harbour seal trips started and ended in the same 5x5 km haul-out cell (35% for grey seals), i.e. seals are not strict central-place foragers. — [Carter et al. 2022](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/274391/1/274391.pdf)
- Cunningham et al. 2009 (24 satellite-tagged harbour seals, W Scotland): 50% of trips were within 25 km of a haul-out, some seals went >100 km; seals returned to the haul-out they last used in ~40% of trips. ### Inferences
- (Own calc) Coast-anchored spawn, not prey-driven. Every telemetry and habitat model above puts distance to haul-out first and depth second; prey enters only through these. Porpoises spawn on herring because they are not tied to land; seals are. The Koster diet (about half gadoids by weight in both periods) means a coast-anchored spawn will in practice overlap gadoids better than random colonies do only if gadoids are also present in the shallower eastern part of the grid - worth checking, but it should not be the spawn rule.
- (Own calc) Suggested spawn shape: density proportional to exp(-d/L) with d the distance (km) to the nearest haul-out outside the grid (Hallo/Smogen just NE of the NE corner, Maseskar ~11 km E, Gullmarn ~17 km E, Pater Noster ~19 km E; Vaderoarna ~30 km N) and L about 10-15 km, so that cells 25-30 km from the haul-out hold ~10-20% of the edge density and the 180-450 m deep western half holds only a few percent of the total. A simpler proxy if per-site anchors are too much: decay with distance from the east edge (column 59) with the same L, multiplied by an optional depth factor once a depth field exists (the EIA's 40-50 m foraging depth suggests strong down-weighting beyond ~100 m). This replicates the Carter-type pattern (hotspots within tens of km, steep decline >20 km) and the EIA's statement that seals are unlikely in the farm area.
- (Own calc) Density chain, all assumptions explicit: Swedish Skagerrak-part counted 3,300 x 1.75 (moult correction) = ~5,800 seals. The grid's 56 km of latitude faces roughly 40-50% of the Swedish Skagerrak coast, but the large colonies at Koster and Vaderoarna lie north of it; assume 25-50% of the Swedish Skagerrak seals use haul-outs within ~20 km of the grid's east edge: ~1,500-2,900 seals. Outside the moult ~75-90% are at sea (Q3). Assume 10-30% of their at-sea usage falls inside the grid (it starts 5-20 km offshore of the haul-outs, and the steep distance and depth decline puts most usage inside the archipelago). Result: roughly 110-780, call it ~100-700 seals at sea in the grid at any one time, i.e. a grid mean of ~0.03-0.2 seals/km2, but maybe ~0.2-1 seal/km2 in the eastern 10 km strip and close to zero over the Mareld site. At 80 kg per seal (the model's `min_split_biomass`; I found no regional mass source) that is ~9-60 t. The current inference value of 15 t (~190 seals) and the 5-30 t training range are plausible; the uncertainty is a factor of ~3 either way.
- The model's seal FG is almost certainly harbour seal only; grey seals are occasional on the Swedish west coast and could be ignored or folded in without a separate group.
- Because there is no land in the grid, a seal "population" in the grid is really the offshore tail of a coastal population; seals that leave east are leaving to haul out, not dying. If the grid edge is closed, the model will hold seals at sea that in reality would be ashore 10-40% of the time (see Q3).

## Q2. Should the wind-farm impacts (`windfarm_noise`, `rotor`) and fishing impacts (`pelagic_trawling`, `bottom_trawling`) act on seals, and how?

### Takeaway
Operational wind farms show no measurable displacement of harbour seals in telemetry studies (Nysted, Rodsand II, Horns Rev, Egmond aan Zee), and operational turbine noise is audible to seals only within ~0.1 to a few km with no documented behavioural zone; pile-driving displacement (up to 25 km, recovery within 2 h) is a construction-only effect that the model does not represent, and Mareld is floating. So `windfarm_noise` should stay off for seals (or at most a tiny energy-cost term), and `rotor` should be neutral: the documented attraction (seals foraging along turbines and pipelines) comes from shallow bottom-fixed foundations and probably does not transfer to floating turbines in 180-450 m. Trawl bycatch of harbour seals in the Swedish Skagerrak is small (order of tens per year, ~1% of the population or less) and gillnets dominate; a trawl impact on seals is not warranted unless the porpoise-style gillnet bycatch shortcut is wanted.

### Cited Findings
Construction (pile driving):
- Russell et al. 2016 (The Wash, SE England): seal usage was significantly reduced up to 25 km from piling; within 25 km usage fell by 19-83% (95% CI), roughly 440 individuals displaced; displacement started at predicted received levels of 166-178 dB re 1 uPa (peak-to-peak); seals returned to the non-piling distribution within 2 h after piling stopped. Title: "Avoidance of wind farms by harbour seals is limited to pile driving activities". (Snippet) — [Russell et al. 2016, J. Appl. Ecol. 53:1642-1652](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/8856)
- Whyte et al. 2020 reanalysed the same data: predicted seal density fell within 25 km above a single-strike SEL of about 145 dB re 1 uPa2s, with considerable variation by depth and installation. (Snippet) — [Whyte et al. 2020 via St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/21156)
- Mareld EIA noise modelling (anchor pile driving, 2.5 h, 6,120 strikes, NMFS 2018 thresholds): for harbour and grey seal, TTScum Rmax 34.4 km (area 2,163 km2), with 15 dB damping 2.6 km (14.8 km2); PTScum Rmax 3.9 km (31.1 km2), with damping 0.2 km (0.13 km2). Behavioural threshold used for seals: 140 dB re 1 uPa2s single-strike SEL. Mitigation planned: Hydro Sound Damper (>=15 dB, not proven at these depths), 30 min soft start and ramp-up. Piling only "if it proves necessary" - the turbines are floating ("bottom-fixed foundations are not possible" at 180-450 m). — `/home/simon/eco-evo/MKB Mareld.pdf`, Sida 89-90 av 243 (Tabell 8-2, 8-3) and Sida 134; floating design at section 6.1
- Olsen et al. 2010: during Horns Rev and Nysted construction and operation (1999-2005) "No general change in behaviour at sea could be linked to the Horns Reef offshore wind farms"; on land the only effect was a short-term change in haul-out behaviour at Rodsand during pile driving about 10 km away; "The effect of wind farms on feeding behaviour of seals is still unknown." — [Olsen et al. 2010, NAMMCO Sci. Publ. 8](https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/NAMMCOSP/article/view/2674)

### Inferences
- `windfarm_noise` (operational): set `impact_affects` false for seals, as now, or at most a very small `energy_factor`. Evidence for operational displacement is null in all telemetry studies found; the audibility range (up to a few km) is an upper bound, not a response. A biomass loss term is not justified. If the model ever gets a construction phase, the Russell 2016 numbers (avoidance to 25 km, 19-83% reduction, recovery within 2 h) would apply - but Mareld is floating and may need no piling at all, and with 15 dB damping the EIA puts seal TTS inside 2.6 km.
- `rotor` (structure presence): neutral. The attraction in Russell et al. 2014 is seals foraging along bottom-fixed turbines and pipelines in shallow southern North Sea water, where the structures form reefs within diving range. Floating platforms in 180-450 m water are far beyond the 40-50 m foraging depth the EIA quotes, and seals are anyway unlikely to be that far offshore (Q1). If an attraction term is ever wanted, it should come from the prey (a reef effect on gadoids), not from a direct seal response. Mooring-line entanglement risk: low/unknown, no basis for a mortality term.
- (Own calc) Trawl impacts: 18% of 410 = ~74 seals/yr bycaught in trawls along the whole Swedish west coast in 2001, out of a population then of a few thousand (I did not find the 2001 west-coast count), i.e. of the order of 1-2% per year, spread along the whole coast and mostly in inshore fisheries. The grid's trawl effort (offshore shrimp and pelagic trawling) is a small subset, and seal density there is low. Recommendation: keep `pelagic_trawling` and `bottom_trawling` off for seals. If a fishing-related seal loss is wanted, the larger term is gillnet bycatch (150/yr excluding trawls, ~2-3% per year of ~5,800 Skagerrak-part seals by my arithmetic), which has no map; the porpoise precedent (section 165: gillnet bycatch riding on the bottom-trawl map) could be copied, but most west-coast gillnet and fyke-net effort is inshore, outside the grid, and it is cleaner to absorb it into M1 (`natural_mortality`, currently 0.10/yr).
- (Own calc) Proximity caveat: the EIA's "seals unlikely" statement concerns Mareld only. Poseidon Nord reaches 57.876 N, 11.105 E (`coordinates.txt`), about 22 km from Pater Noster and about 27 km from Maseskar - roughly the 25 km radius that holds half of harbour seal trips (Cunningham et al. 2009) and within which Riaz et al. 2026 found haul-outs typically occur. The operational recommendation (noise off, rotor neutral) still stands because the operational telemetry evidence is null regardless of proximity, but if the model ever gets a construction phase, Poseidon Nord - not Mareld - is where Russell 2016-type piling displacement would overlap real seal usage.
- Competition with fisheries belongs in the predation matrix (seals eat gadoids and herring, the same stocks the trawls take), not in an impact table.

## Q3. Should seals be able to hide/rest (haul out), and how much of the time?

### Takeaway
Outside the moult harbour seals spend roughly 10-30% of their time hauled out, in bouts of about 5-6 h (up to ~1 day); in the June pupping and August moult period the share rises to around 55-60% at survey time. In this grid there is no land, so real haul-out means leaving the grid toward the coast; the model's Rest/hide action in an offshore cell is better interpreted as resting at the surface or in the water column (which seals also do) than as hauling out, and the seasonal onshore absence is not represented.

### Cited Findings
- Cunningham et al. 2009 (24 satellite-tagged harbour seals, W Scotland): daily mean proportion of time hauled out 11-27%, varying by location, season and sex; mean haul-out duration 5 h, maximum >24 h. (Snippet) — [Cunningham et al. 2009](https://openpolar.no/Record/crwiley:10.1002%2Faqc.983)
- Riaz et al. 2026 (Norwegian Skagerrak, post-moult Aug-Nov): mean haul-out duration 5.8 h (95% CI 5.5-6.0; range 0.2-27.7 h); mean 74.3 haul-outs per seal over a mean deployment of 103.6 days. — [Riaz et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12796850/)
- Olsen et al. 2010 / Harkonen & Harding 2001: about 57% of harbour seals are hauled out during the late-August moult surveys in Southern Scandinavia (correction factor 1.75). — [Olsen et al. 2010](https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/NAMMCOSP/article/view/2674)
- HaV: the moult (late August) is "when the largest share of the seal population lies on land at the same time"; moult and breeding sites coincide. — [HaV 1.4b](https://www.havochvatten.se/download/18.1d23b59c190125a43f2e9577/1719557060445/1-4b-utbredning-av-knubbsal.pdf)
- Canadian (St Lawrence) tagging: 0.33 (95% CI 0.09-0.60) of the population hauled out on average under survey-like conditions in May-June; literature-weighted 0.61 (0.50-0.71) during the moult. (Snippet) — [DFO Res. Doc. 2024/048](https://dfo-mpo.gc.ca/csas-sccs/Publications/ResDocs-DocRech/2024/2024_048-eng.html)
- Diel/tidal pattern: seasonal, circadian and tidal rhythms in numbers ashore are typical but vary between areas; Svalbard harbour seals showed a diel haul-out rhythm when there was a day-night cycle. (Snippet) — [Hamilton et al. 2014, PLoS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3899210); Vance et al. 2021 found resting time rose by ~37 min/day during offshore trips, suggesting a resting deficit limits trip length (Snippet) — [Vance et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7985366/)
- Dietz et al. 2013: Kattegat seals ranged most in February-March and stayed close to the haul-out (Anholt) during the spring breeding season. (Snippet) — [Dietz et al. 2013](https://portal.findresearcher.sdu.dk/da/publications/movements-and-site-fidelity-of-harbour-seals-phoca-vitulina-in-ka/)

### Inferences
- (Own calc) Riaz et al. 2026 give a mean of 74.3 haul-outs per seal x mean 5.8 h over a mean deployment of 103.6 days: ~431 h of ~2,486 h, i.e. about 17% of time hauled out in Aug-Nov in the Norwegian Skagerrak (product of means, post-moult season only). This is the only regional figure and lies inside Cunningham's 11-27%.
- (Own calc) In 6-h ticks, a 5-6 h haul-out bout is about one tick, and 11-27% of time hauled out is about 0.4-1.1 ticks per day (4 ticks/day at 6 h). If the Rest/hide action is meant to stand in for hauling out, a learned rest share of roughly 15-30% outside summer and up to ~50-60% in June-August would be realistic. But the grid has no haul-out sites and the nearest are 5-30 km outside it, i.e. 5-30 ticks of travel at 1 cell/tick - so in reality a seal that hauls out spends many ticks travelling out of and back into the grid.
- Recommendation: allow Rest/hide for seals (surface and in-water resting at sea is real and the rest action doubles as reduced metabolic cost), but do not force a haul-out quota; the real-world hauled-out fraction should rather be accounted for in the spawn/biomass (the grid holds only the at-sea fraction - Q1 density chain already assumes 75-90% at sea) and, if seasonality is modelled, as a summer reduction of at-sea biomass. Being hidden at sea should not give the protection that land gives, but since nothing in the model preys on seals (`natural_mortality` only; no FG eats seals), the hide protection is moot for seals and only the energy side matters.
- The pupping (June) and moult (June-August) peaks mean that in summer seals concentrate at the coast; an east-edge-anchored spawn is even more appropriate if the inference run starts in summer.

### Gaps
- No monthly breakdown of percent time hauled out from Skagerrak/Kattegat tags; only a post-moult (Aug-Nov) season mean of ~17% can be derived from Riaz et al. 2026 bout counts and durations. Swedish 2025 tags not yet published.
- No Skagerrak-specific diel or tidal haul-out pattern found. (Unsourced expectation: Skagerrak tides are small, so the tidal haul-out rhythm typical of the Wadden Sea and UK is probably weak here.)
- Cunningham et al. 2009 seasonal breakdown not read (snippet only).
