# Evidence B: crabs, hermit crabs, squat lobsters, other benthic shrimps and hyperbenthos

Scope: mobile epifauna and hyperbenthos eaten by the benthivorous fish, other than Pandalus borealis and
Nephrops (evidence_A1, A2), and not already in the grab-based K (polychaetes, small bivalves, brittle stars,
amphipods). Standing stock in t WW/km2 = g WW/m2.

Tags: [F] full text read, [A] abstract only, [S] search snippet or secondary, [D] derivation. "(sub)" = read in
full by a research subagent of this task and not re-checked by me. Source PDFs: `data/epifauna/`.

## Bottom line

**No area-based biomass of crabs, hermit crabs, squat lobsters, small shrimps or mysids exists for the grid or for
Skagerrak mud at 100-300 m.** Everything below is a proxy (North Sea Ecopath, northern North Sea beam- and
Agassiz-trawl surveys, the Kattegat Ecopath diagram) combined into a judgement [D].

## 1. Area-based proxies

1. **Mackinson & Daskalov 2007**, North Sea Ecopath, Cefas Sci. Ser. Tech. Rep. 142 (sub; the large-crab row B 1.354, P/B 0.5541, Q/B 2.7704, EE 0.9322 and the "notably absent" hermit quote checked by me [F])
   https://www.cefas.co.uk/publications/techrep/tech142.pdf
   - Balanced values (t/km2, P/B): large crabs B 1.354, P/B 0.554; shrimp B 0.50, P/B 3.0; small mobile epifauna
     ("swarming crustaceans") B 30, P/B 1.9; epifaunal macrobenthos (mobile grazers) B 78, P/B 0.39; Nephrops B 1.1,
     P/B 0.37.
   - Survey-derived (Table 11.9): large crabs 0.79 (0.56-1.34), P/B 0.51; shrimp 0.44 (0.25-0.57), P/B 0.91.
   - Method: beam-trawl density / 0.35 efficiency, North Sea mean over 570,000 km2, "shell free", dominated by
     stations < 50 m. L. holsatus (a shallow-sand crab) is ~50 % of "large crabs".
   - Species means [D, sub]: Crangon allmanni 0.063 t/km2; P. montagui 0.043; Eualus 0.044; Spirontocaris
     lilljeborgi 0.034; Processa 0.025; Pandalina 0.018 -> non-borealis shrimps ~0.23. Munida rugosa 0.013 and
     M. sarsi 0.0017 as North Sea means; if confined to the deep north (~10 % of the area), ~0.13 locally.
   - Hermit crabs: "Pagurus bernhardus, Astropecten irregularis, Anapagurus laevis, Pagurus pubescens ... Pagurus
     prideaux are notably absent from the biomass ranking" (sub). So no hermit biomass from this source.
   - Transferability to 100-300 m Skagerrak mud: low-moderate. The North Sea mean is shallow-sand dominated;
     deep mud has fewer portunids and more Munida, Goneplax, Calocaris.
2. **Callaway et al. 2002**, ICES J. Mar. Sci. 59:1199, doi:10.1006/jmsc.2002.1288 (sub)
   - 2 m beam trawl, Q3 2000, "standardized to a tow length of 200 m (area = 400 m2)". Biomass classes in Fig. 2b
     read as g WW per 400 m2 give 0.05-21 g/m2 raw for all free-living epibenthos (starfish and urchins included)
     [D, sub; unit inferred].
   - "in the northern North Sea Echinus spp. and hermit crabs contributed to the high biomass recorded" (sub).
   - The 100-200 m cluster was characterised by Anapagurus laevis, Pagurus pubescens, Hyas coarctatus (sub).
   - The survey map stops at ~8 E: the Skagerrak was not sampled.
3. **Fladen Ground** (Hartwig et al. 1983, PANGAEA doi:10.1594/PANGAEA.609987) [S]: Agassiz trawl 1975-76, total
   epifauna 11-102 ind/1000 m2, 4.9-83.1 g DW/1000 m2 -> 0.02-1.04 g WW/m2 raw, ~0.06-5 g/m2 after an efficiency
   of 0.2-0.35 [D]. Basford et al. 1990 (NJSR 25:165) (sub): low richness on silt "may in part reflect the lower
   efficiency of the trawl on these sediments".
4. **Kattegat Ecopath diagram** (Belgrano & Tomczak, SIME report 2025:3) (sub): "Shrimps and mysids" 3.5 t/km2;
   "Crustacea" 20; Nephrops 3.0; no P/B or group definitions; a ~23 m-deep system with Crangon crangon. High
   bracket only.
5. **Hyperbenthos**: Portuguese shelf 21-299 m, suprabenthic sledge, "700.2 to 13591.7 ind. 100 m-2" in 0-100 cm
   (Cunha et al. 1997, MEPS 157:119) [A, sub] -> ~0.01-1.4 g WW/m2 at 2-10 mg per animal [D]. No Skagerrak or
   Norwegian Trench sledge biomass was found.
6. **Mareld EIA** (mkb.txt): scientific surveys record "havskräfta, krabbtaska och trollkrabba" in 45G0; the
   SLU species-project survey found Munida sarsi (VU) inside the project area; ROV transects 20 km east at
   109-144 m (Länsstyrelserna 2016) list Pandalus and Spatangus [F]. No densities.

Checks [D]: the North Sea Ecopath balanced crabs + shrimp give 1.85 t/km2 (including P. borealis and
C. crangon, no hermits or mysids); the Fladen range 0.06-5 g/m2 brackets the totals above.

## 2. P/B and r

- Brey-model P/B in Mackinson & Daskalov Table 11.8 (10 C, 90 m) (sub): L. holsatus 0.55, Hyas 0.64, Cancer 0.28,
  Pisidia 0.94, C. allmanni 1.00, P. montagui 0.86, Spirontocaris 0.87, Eualus 0.89, Processa 1.10.
- Brey (2001) runs at 6-7 C, 100-300 m, motile crustaceans (sub, [D]): mysids 10 mg 1.75-1.92, 50 mg 1.26-1.36;
  C. allmanni 1 g 0.68-0.72; hermits 1 g shell-free 0.66-0.70; Munida 4-10 g 0.42-0.54; Atelecyclus/Goneplax
  8 g 0.44-0.46; L. depurator 20 g 0.37-0.38.
- C. allmanni lives at most ~1.5 yr (Helgoland Mar. Res. 2002) (sub).
- r follows the same rule as the infauna and Nephrops (Hiddink et al. 2019, r = 5.31/longevity; 2.41 / 1.24 /
  0.34 for 1-3 / 3-10 / > 10 yr), checked against r ~0.7-2 x P/B (evidence_B of `Bentos och torskfiskar`):
  crabs and hermits 3-10 yr -> 0.8-1.24; Munida (P/B ~0.5) 0.6; small shrimps 1-3 yr -> 1.5; mysids (< 2 yr) 2.4.

## 3. Gear catchability (why raw survey numbers are minimums)

- **Reiss et al. 2006**, ICES J. Mar. Sci. 63:1453 (sub), 2 m beam trawl efficiency relative to the three-trawl
  total (abundance/biomass %): all taxa 44/32 (39 m muddy sand) and 36/45 (25 m coarse sand); L. holsatus 18/20
  and 9/9; Pagurus bernhardus 51/64; C. allmanni 56/58 and 26/27; Asterias 42/46; Astropecten 34/34. These are
  upper bounds on true efficiency.
- Otter trawl on shrimp: q 0.2 (Campelen/Norwegian survey, Mackinson & Daskalov) to 0.5 (SS3), evidence_A1.

## 4. Exposure to benthivorous fish

- Cod, Skagerrak, IYFS February 1991, 336 stomachs (Dorman et al. 1992, ICES CM 1992/G:70, Table 2) (sub):
  L. holsatus 14.9 % of stomach weight, L. depurator 4.8 %, other Brachyura 2.3 %, P. bernhardus 3.6 %,
  P. montagui 2.0 %, P. borealis 1.4 %, Nephrops 3.5 %, Pasiphaea ~7 %, C. allmanni 0.09 %, mysids 0.001 %;
  "crabs, mainly Portunidae, show a similar dominance".
- L. holsatus is a "night-active species, which buries in the sediment during daylight" (Reiss 2006) (sub).
- Suprabenthos: 76-97 % in the 0-50 cm layer by day, more dispersed at night (Cunha 1997) [A, sub].
- No data on Munida burrow/crevice use or hermit exposure. Hermit crabs live on the surface; Munida shelters in
  burrows and under stones by day (general knowledge, not read here) -> lower f.

## 5. Overlap with the grab-based K
Calocaris (a burrower), Goneplax, small Anapagurus, Processa and Philocheras are partly taken by 0.1 m2 grabs
(LYSE 8: Calocaris 0.4 ind/m2, crustaceans 0.6 % of SK3 biomass). The overlap is < 0.1 g/m2 and is ignored.

## Gaps
- No in-area crab, hermit, Munida or mysid biomass. The table is a judgement with a factor ~3 either way.
- Not opened: Zühlke et al. 2001 (EU report on epibenthos in the North Sea and Skagerrak), Basford et al. 1989
  (JMBA 69:387), invertebrate by-catch in the IMR shrimp survey and DATRAS. The IMR shrimp-survey by-catch tables
  (SCR 21/001 Table 10 lists fish predators only) would be the first place to look for in-area crab catches.
