# Diel and seasonal timing of gadoid feeding (cod, whiting, saithe, pollack, haddock) - evidence for a night/day attack-rate ratio (dark_ratio)

Scope note: about 25 tool calls. Many primary papers are paywalled (Springer, ScienceDirect, CJFAS, ResearchGate returned 403), so several findings below rest on abstracts or search-engine abstract snippets. Each entry says which. "Fetched" means the abstract or full page was read directly. "Snippet" means only the search-engine summary was seen; treat those with lower confidence.

## Q1. Stomach-content diel studies: day vs night fullness, freshness, time of last meal, by prey type

### Takeaway
No gadoid in the region feeds only by day. Cod eating herring in the North Sea show two fullness peaks, one in the morning and one in the evening. Whiting have one nocturnal feeding peak at 22-24 h and a minimum at 08-10 h. Whiting and Norway pout eating krill in the Oslofjord (59 N) show no day/night difference at all. In 0-group gadoids the pattern flips with size: small fish eat copepods by day, and fish-eating fish over about 3 cm feed at night.

### Cited Findings
- **Whiting, North Sea (Mergardt & Temming 1997, ICES J. Mar. Sci. 54:226).** Prey ingestion times were back-calculated from partly digested prey particles and lab particle-decay functions. They found "a single feeding peak with a maximum feeding between 2200 and 2400 h and minimal food intake between 0800 and 1000 h". Whiting therefore feed mainly at night. (Fetched abstract. Prey type and season were not given in the abstract; my fetch summary guessed sandeels, which is UNVERIFIED.) - [Mergardt & Temming 1997](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/54/2/226/620550)
- **Whiting, North Sea:** a fresh-food probability study found that the chance of a stomach holding fresh food fell when partly digested food was already present, while meal size did not change. Satiation therefore reduces search activity, not meal size. Lab whiting on demand feeding fed about every 21 h and started feeding again when the stomach was about 40% full. (Snippet.) - [Rindorf 2002, J. Fish Biol.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1095-8649.2002.tb00897.x)
- A separate DTU record titled "Diel feeding pattern of whiting in the North Sea" exists (Rindorf et al.). Its numbers were not retrieved. - [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/diel-feeding-pattern-of-whiting-in-the-north-sea/)
- **Whiting gastric evacuation:** linear evacuation of 0.31 g/h for 268 g whiting at 10 C. (Snippet.) - [Bromley 1988, J. Fish Biol.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1095-8649.1988.tb05475.x)
- **Cod, North Sea, 24-h survey (Adlerstein & Welleman 2000, Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci. 57:2363-2367).** GAM analysis with the trawl haul as the unit showed "two peaks in stomach fullness ... for herring, one in the morning and the other in the evening". The diel changes implied rapid gastric evacuation, especially of herring. The other prey analysed was probably Norway pout. That is likely but was not confirmed, because the full text returned 403. (Snippet; consistent across two searches.) - [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/40151128_Diel_variation_of_stomach_contents_of_North_Sea_cod_Gadus_morhua_during_a_24-h_fishing_survey_an_analysis_using_generalized_additive_models)
- **0-group gadoids, northern North Sea, late spring (Bromley, Watson & Hislop 1997, ICES J. Mar. Sci. 54:846).** Cod of 2-3 cm fed most during 12-20 h, mainly on invertebrates. Cod of 3-5 cm switched their peak to 00-08 h, as fish entered the diet and total stomach contents fell. Fish of 1-3 cm of all species ate mainly copepods in daylight, and larger, increasingly fish-eating fish reversed to night feeding. Haddock and saithe ate fish and crustaceans in roughly equal parts. Norway pout stayed almost entirely on copepods. Whiting showed "substantial levels of cannibalism and inter-specific predation". (Fetched abstract.) - [Bromley et al. 1997](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/54/5/846/751500)
- **Whiting and Norway pout on krill (Meganyctiphanes norvegica), Oslofjord 59 48 N, 120 m site, March, June and November (Onsrud, Kaartvedt, Rostad & Klevjer 2004, ICES J. Mar. Sci. 61:1278).**
  - Krill were up to 99% of whiting stomach weight, about 82% on average "during both day and night".
  - "No systematic trends were apparent comparing proportion of stomachs containing krill or state of digestion during the day and night."
  - Norway pout: "No significant differences in stomach fullness ... between day/night".
  - Whiting sat in the upper and middle part of the krill layer by day and in the upper 30-40 m at night.
  - Herring held the most krill per stomach by day, in March.
  - Night light at depth was 10^-4 to 10^-6 umol m^-2 s^-1. The 1% light depth was 4 m in November and 12-15 m in other months.
  - (Fetched.) - [Onsrud et al. 2004](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/61/8/1278/629825)
- **Juvenile cod, Newfoundland (Keats 1992, NAFO).** Stomach contents suggested feeding "mainly during the day, with little or no feeding at night". (Snippet; flagged as outside the region.) - [Keats, NAFO J.](https://journal.nafo.int/Portals/0/1992-2/keats.pdf)
- **Juvenile cod, Newfoundland nearshore (Grant & Brown 1998, CJFAS).** A size-related shift from age 0 to age 1: from zooplankton eaten by day to benthos eaten at night. (Snippet; outside the region.) - [CJFAS f97-291](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1139/f97-291)
- **Haddock larvae, East Shetland:** maximum ingestion early in the evening (19:00) and peak digestive enzyme activity at 01:00. This is larval data and is not usable for adults. (Snippet.) - [Marine Biology 1999](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s002270050635)

### Inferences
- Krill and other vertically migrating zooplankton as prey of whiting and Norway pout at 59 N: the evidence supports dark_ratio of about 1.0, with no detectable diel difference. Whiting simply follow the krill upward at night.
- Pelagic fish (herring) as prey of cod: the morning and evening peaks point to crepuscular feeding. In a model with only two states (light/dark) and a threshold sun elevation, twilight sits near the threshold. A dark_ratio of about 0.5-1.0 for cod -> herring is defensible. The evidence does not support a strongly day-only value (below 0.3).
- Fish and gadoid prey of whiting, including cannibalism: whiting feed mostly at night, so dark_ratio could be at or above 1.0 if the model allowed it. If it is capped at 1, use 1.0.
- These are my inferences. No source gives a numeric day:night consumption ratio for adult gadoids in this region (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Daan (1973, North Sea cod) and the ICES "Year of the Stomach" 1981/1991 diel analyses were not retrievable. I could not confirm whether they report day/night fullness splits.
- I found no explicit day:night consumption ratio in mass per hour or as a percentage of the daily ration for adult cod, whiting, saithe or haddock.
- Adlerstein & Welleman (2000): the actual sizes of the morning and evening peaks and the night trough were not retrieved.

## Q2. Do cod, whiting and saithe take herring or sprat at night, at dusk and dawn, or by day? (telemetry, acoustics, longline, video)

### Takeaway
Adult cod search actively by day and much less at night. Their smell-based search works equally well day and night, while visual capture of active prey depends on light. Juvenile cod at North Sea wind farms are clearly crepuscular, though they were eating epifauna, not fish. Cod predation on Baltic sprat is reported at dusk and dawn, when the sprat schools break up and re-form (source unconfirmed). Saithe activity peaks at sunrise and is low at night. Haddock feed by day, with the response to bait rising at dawn about 1 h before cod's.

### Cited Findings
- **Adult cod, northern Norwegian fjord, autumn, acoustic tags on 6 fish (Lokkeborg & Ferno 1999, Env. Biol. Fish.).**
  - Swimming speed, taken as food search, "increased at dawn, remained high during the day, then gradually decreased during the evening, and remained low throughout the night".
  - The response to bait odour was the same morning and night: response distances were 240 m and 227 m.
  - The authors' reading: olfactory search does not depend on light, but "visual detection distance and the ability to capture active prey are influenced by the light level, resulting in higher activity during the day".
  - (Snippet of abstract; consistent across two searches.) - [Lokkeborg & Ferno 1999](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1007504712163)
- **Captive cod (related Animal Behaviour paper):** a diurnal swimming rhythm with "slightly lower swimming speed during a 5-h period at night", and a larger share of active prey taken by day, as vision becomes more important. (Snippet.) - [Lokkeborg, Anim. Behav.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003347298907722)
- **Juvenile cod at offshore wind farms, Belgian North Sea (Reubens et al. 2014, J. Sea Res.).** Telemetry plus stomachs showed "crepuscular movements related to feeding activity". They found a 12 h cycle, and "the highest catch rates and stomach fullness were recorded close to sunset and sunrise". (Fetched abstract.) Directly relevant to an offshore wind setting.
  - CAVEAT on prey type: these juvenile cod ate mainly fouling epifauna on the turbine foundations. The amphipod Jassa herdmani made up 76.2 +/- 19.3% of stomach contents, and the porcelain crab Pisidia longicornis was also eaten. (Snippet from the Reubens group's wind-farm diet work.) The study therefore shows a crepuscular feeding rhythm in cod in general. It is NOT direct evidence on when cod catch herring or sprat.
  - [Reubens et al. 2014 (Tethys)](https://tethys.pnnl.gov/publications/diel-variation-feeding-movement-patterns-juvenile-atlantic-cod-offshore-wind-farms); diet: [Reubens et al., gadoids at offshore wind farms](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242016706_Offshore_wind_farms_as_productive_sites_or_ecological_traps_for_gadoid_fishes_-_Impact_on_growth_condition_index_and_diet_composition)
- **Cod, Norwegian Skagerrak coast (Somskilen), 181 fish of 30-80 cm, mean 46.3 cm (Freitas et al. 2015, Ecol. Evol.).**
  - Smaller cod rose to shallow water at night, interpreted as "nighttime rise to shallow feeding habitats" (eelgrass and macroalgae).
  - Larger cod kept roughly constant depth over 24 h.
  - Above about 15 C surface temperature, the migration was confined to deeper water.
  - This concerns benthic and littoral feeding, not pelagic fish. (Fetched.) - [Freitas et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4449760/)
- **Longline setting time, cod and haddock, Norway, February-March (Lokkeborg & Pina 1996, ICES CM 1996/B:34).**
  - Haddock: "Lines set at night produced higher catch rates than those set during the day". Lines set before 04:30 in February and before 03:30 in March caught significantly more. Elsewhere the result is summarised as pre-dawn lines catching about twice as much haddock.
  - Cod: "No differences were found in catch rates of cod between lines set at night and during the day".
  - Earlier behavioural work found that responses of cod, haddock and whiting to baited hooks "increase at dawn and decrease at dusk in correspondence with the seasonal photoperiod" (Ferno et al. 1986; Lokkeborg et al. 1989). Haddock responded about 1 h earlier in the morning than cod.
  - (Fetched full text.) - [Lokkeborg & Pina 1996](https://www.bmis-bycatch.org/system/files/zotero_attachments/library_1/TC6ZQN3N%20-%20CM_1996_B_34.pdf)
  - Caveat: longline catches measure response to bait smell, not capture of live prey.
- **Baltic cod on sprat and herring:** "Predation by cod took place primarily at dusk and dawn during ascent and descent of sprat associated with school dissolution and formation". Cod stayed near the bottom outside these windows. At night the clupeids were in the surface layer, out of reach of cod. (Snippet only. Attributed to Neuenfeldt and co-workers on Baltic cod-clupeid vertical overlap; the exact paper was not confirmed. A Baltic herring and sprat diel paper also appeared in the results.) - [Diel spatial and feeding activity of herring and sprat in the Baltic](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228416396_Diel_spatial_and_feeding_activity_of_herring_Clupea_harengus_and_sprat_Sprattus_sprattus_in_the_Baltic_Sea); see also [Ecology of overwintering sprat (Prog. Oceanogr.)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0079661115001755)
- **Sprat in Norwegian fjords:** a bottom-mounted echosounder recorded single fish fleeing from approaching predators. Sprat may use hypoxic deep water as a refuge from predators. (Snippet; the predator species and timing were not retrieved.) - [Ecology of overwintering sprat](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0079661115001755)
- **Saithe, telemetry at 15 salmon farms in Ryfylke, SW Norway, about 59 N (Skilbrei & Ottera 2016, ICES J. Mar. Sci. 73:1186).** (Fetched abstract.)
  - Activity "increased rapidly and reached its maximum around time of sunrise", and fish were "less active during night".
  - In December the mean SD of depth was 3.8 m at night against 9.1 m in the morning.
  - "Fish moved away from fish farms in daytime (16% of all observations)", "almost exclusively during autumn and early winter". The authors read this as leaving to feed elsewhere.
  - Depth: 38% of fish showed no diel vertical migration; 46% moved up or down by day. Median depth was 25-50 m in summer and 60-90 m in winter.
  - Caveat: these are farm-associated saithe, and the study measures activity, not stomach contents.
  - [Skilbrei & Ottera 2016](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/73/4/1186/2458753)
- **The general claim "saithe are visual feeders that forage in the daylight hours"** came only from a search-summary sentence whose source was mixed with non-scientific pages. Its source is UNCERTAIN; do not cite it as a finding. A possibly relevant related paper: [Vertical migrations of saithe in Icelandic waters (DST)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263031009_Vertical_migrations_of_saithe_Pollachius_virens_in_Icelandic_waters_as_observed_with_data_storage_tags) (not read).
  - Fast schooling predators such as saithe make herring dive steeply. Herring form loose demersal layers shortly after dark and dense pelagic schools by day. (Snippet.) - [Adaptive behaviour of herring schools, ICES JMS 53:449](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/53/2/449/792847?login=false)
- **Review-level statement:** "Atlantic cod has a flexible diel activity cycle linked to spatio-temporal variations in food availability and predation risk". (Snippet.) - [Behavioural diversity of Atlantic cod, Rev. Fish Biol. Fish. 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11160-017-9505-y)

### Inferences
- Cod -> pelagic fish: two sources point to capture at twilight. The North Sea cod stomach data show morning and evening peaks for herring. The Baltic dusk/dawn result points the same way, but its source is unconfirmed. The juvenile wind-farm cod (epifauna prey) add only a general crepuscular rhythm. Full night is weaker, because the clupeids rise out of reach and the light falls below visual capture levels. Full day is moderate. With a single sun-elevation threshold, a dark_ratio of about 0.5 is a reasonable central value, with a plausible range of 0.3-0.8. If the threshold sits a few degrees below the horizon, part of the twilight peak counts as "light", which would argue for the lower end.
- Saithe -> herring: activity peaks at sunrise and is low at night (Skilbrei & Ottera 2016, about 59 N). That suggests a dark_ratio of about 0.2-0.5. Confidence is LOW: the study measures activity, not stomachs, and covers farm-associated fish.
- Whiting -> fish: the nocturnal peak (Mergardt & Temming) suggests a dark_ratio of 1, or even above 1.
- Haddock: Lokkeborg & Pina explain the higher catch on pre-dawn lines by haddock's diurnal rhythm. Responses to bait rise at dawn and fall at dusk, and haddock start about 1 h earlier than cod. Night-set lines simply soak through the dawn peak. Haddock therefore lean toward daytime feeding and are NOT evidence of night feeding. Haddock prey are mostly benthic, so they matter little for the three prey types here.
- A combined "gadoids" functional group mixes cod, whiting and saithe. Weighted by biomass in the Skagerrak, the group ratio for fish prey plausibly lies around 0.5-0.8. For krill and zooplankton it is about 1.0.

### Gaps
- I found no direct acoustic or video study of cod or saithe predation on overwintering herring in Norwegian fjords (Vestfjorden/Ofotfjorden), and no study timing the attacks. The searches returned only killer-whale and Calanus work.
- Pollack (Pollachius pollachius): I found no diel feeding data at all.
- The Lofoten and Barents Sea cod-capelin and cod-herring diel studies were not reached. The Greenland capelin-cod DVM review (Rev. Fish Biol. Fish. 2025) was paywalled: [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11160-025-09989-w).
- Accelerometer-based feeding (as opposed to activity) data for wild adult cod were not found.

## Q3. Feeding on krill and vertically migrating zooplankton: at night when krill rise, or by day near the bottom?

### Takeaway
At 59 N in the Oslofjord, whiting and Norway pout take Meganyctiphanes equally by day and by night: both fish follow the krill vertically. In northern Norway, krill was probably the only prey cod took in midwater.

### Cited Findings
- **Whiting (Onsrud et al. 2004, Oslofjord).** Krill were about 82% of diet by weight both day and night, with no day/night trend in the share of stomachs with krill or in digestion state. Whiting sat in the upper and middle krill layer by day and in the upper 30-40 m at night.
- **Norway pout (same study).** They approached the krill from below by day and were deepest at night (midwater, none caught in the upper 25 m). Fullness did not differ between day and night. The authors note the pout may detect krill visually or by lateral line.
- **Herring (same study).** Herring took most krill by day, and their visual biting threshold is about 3.6x10^-2 to 7x10^-3 lux.
- All three - [Onsrud et al. 2004](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/61/8/1278/629825)
- **Cod, Balsfjord 69 N (Klemetsen 1982, ICES JMS 40:101).** The main prey were Thysanoessa krill, Pandalus borealis and capelin. "Krill was probably the only prey taken in midwater". (Fetched abstract.) - [Klemetsen 1982](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/40/2/101/603982)
- **Krill avoidance:** a recent paper reports "extensive avoidance behaviour of krill from predators and nets" (title only; details not retrieved). - [ICES JMS 81:1764](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/81/9/1764/7750087)

### Inferences
- For gadoids -> zooplankton or krill, a dark_ratio of about 1.0 is the best-supported value, from same-region, same-prey data (moderate confidence for whiting, low for cod).

### Gaps
- I found no diel stomach study for cod or saithe eating krill in the Skagerrak or North Sea.
- Calanus as gadoid prey (relevant only for juveniles and Norway pout): Bromley et al. (1997) show 0-group gadoids eat copepods by day. I found no adult data.

## Q4. Seasonal: does winter darkness at high latitude reduce feeding, compared with temperature effects?

### Takeaway
Polar-night studies at 78-80 N and a year-round study at 69 N found that cod and haddock keep feeding in darkness. At 69 N, fullness showed no seasonal trend and no effect of the polar night. Seasonal declines in consumption are therefore better attributed to temperature and prey availability than to light.

### Cited Findings
- **Svalbard fjords, 78-80 N, January sampling in 2012-2016 (Polar Biology 2023, "Diets of gadoid fish in Arctic waters of Svalbard fjords during the polar night"; Berge group).**
  - More than 70% of individuals of each species were feeding during the polar night. Few stomachs were well filled, which suggests moderate foraging success.
  - Empty stomachs: polar cod 40%, Atlantic cod 25%, haddock 21%.
  - Diets were opportunistic, with heavy overlap on Thysanoessa krill in the krill-rich years 2014-2015.
  - (Snippet; full text 403.) - [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371808267_Diets_of_gadoid_fish_in_Arctic_waters_of_Svalbard_fjords_during_the_polar_night); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00300-023-03167-z)
- **Same region, broader context:** "Unexpected levels of biological activity during the polar night" (Berge et al. 2015, Current Biology). Not fetched in detail. - [Current Biology](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(15)00995-1)
- **Balsfjord 69 N, cod, one year (Klemetsen 1982).** "The fullness of the stomachs showed no seasonal trend". Neither spawning nor the polar darkness appeared to affect feeding intensity. (Fetched abstract.) - [Klemetsen 1982](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/40/2/101/603982)
- **Longline timing across seasons:** the timing of activity peaks for cod and haddock shifts with photoperiod from September to December in the North Sea (Lokkeborg et al. 1989). Pre-dawn haddock catch timing moved between February and March. In other words, the rhythm follows the seasonal light cycle, but the total is not reported to drop. - [Lokkeborg & Pina 1996](https://www.bmis-bycatch.org/system/files/zotero_attachments/library_1/TC6ZQN3N%20-%20CM_1996_B_34.pdf)
- **Western Baltic adult cod:** a paper on seasonal and depth-specific intake exists (Funk et al. 2021, J. Fish Biol., "The forgotten feeding ground"). Numbers were not retrieved. - [Funk et al. 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfb.14615)

### Inferences
- At 58 N the night is never polar, and the gadoids feed in near-total darkness at 78 N. Seasonal light should therefore not cut gadoid attack rates beyond what the diel dark_ratio already does. Winter reductions should come from temperature, for example the Q10 metabolism term already in the model, and from prey availability.
- Because gadoids are partly non-visual (olfactory and lateral-line search), the light-sensitivity of their attack rate is weaker than for clupeids or seabirds.

### Gaps
- I found no study that separates light from temperature as the cause of seasonal change in gadoid consumption at North Sea or Skagerrak latitudes.

## Q5. Published day:night consumption ratios or evacuation-based diel consumption estimates

### Takeaway
I found no published numeric day:night consumption ratio for adult gadoids in the region. The closest material is the timing of feeding peaks: Mergardt & Temming (1997) for whiting, Adlerstein & Welleman (2000) for cod, and the size-dependent 0-group timing in Bromley et al. (1997).

### Cited Findings
- Whiting peak at 22-24 h and minimum at 08-10 h, back-calculated with evacuation models. - [Mergardt & Temming 1997](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/54/2/226/620550)
- Cod on herring: morning and evening peaks with rapid evacuation of herring. - [Adlerstein & Welleman 2000](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/40151128_Diel_variation_of_stomach_contents_of_North_Sea_cod_Gadus_morhua_during_a_24-h_fishing_survey_an_analysis_using_generalized_additive_models)
- Lab whiting: linear gastric evacuation of 0.31 g/h for 268 g fish at 10 C; demand-fed meals about every 21 h. - [Bromley 1988](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1095-8649.1988.tb05475.x); [Rindorf 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1095-8649.2002.tb00897.x)
- A CJFAS 2011 paper on estimating feeding patterns of piscivorous fish from individual prey data (probably whiting or cod, North Sea, Temming/Andersen group) exists, but its content returned 403 and was not retrieved. - [CJFAS f2011-020](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f2011-020)
- Baltic sprat, as a reference point for prey vertical migration: feeding in the deep by day is only 16-39% of the daily ration. (Snippet.) - [Baltic herring/sprat diel](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228416396_Diel_spatial_and_feeding_activity_of_herring_Clupea_harengus_and_sprat_Sprattus_sprattus_in_the_Baltic_Sea)

### Inferences (suggested dark_ratio per prey type for a combined gadoid FG at 58 N; my synthesis, not a published value)
| Prey | Suggested dark_ratio | Range | Confidence | Main basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zooplankton / krill | 1.0 | 0.8-1.0 | moderate | Onsrud et al. 2004: no day/night difference for whiting and Norway pout at 59 N |
| Pelagic fish (herring, sprat) | about 0.5-0.6 | 0.3-0.8 | low-moderate | Cod: morning and evening peaks on herring (Adlerstein & Welleman 2000); Baltic dusk/dawn predation on sprat (snippet, SOURCE UNCONFIRMED); general crepuscular rhythm in juvenile cod eating epifauna (Reubens et al. 2014, NOT fish prey); cod daytime search (Lokkeborg & Ferno 1999); saithe active by day and low at night (Skilbrei & Ottera 2016, activity only); offset by nocturnal whiting (Mergardt & Temming 1997) |
| Gadoids (cannibalism, Norway pout) | about 0.8-1.0 | 0.6-1.0 | low | Whiting nocturnal peak and cannibalism; pout prey is benthopelagic and active at night |

If the model's light threshold is set near civil twilight (sun at about -6 degrees), the dusk and dawn peaks fall mostly on the "light" side, which justifies the lower end for fish prey. If the threshold is at the horizon, twilight falls on the "dark" side, which pushes the value toward 0.7-0.8.

### Gaps
- No direct measurement of a day:night attack-rate or consumption ratio exists in the sources reached. All numeric dark_ratio values above are inferences.
- The size of the twilight peaks relative to midday was not retrieved for any study.
