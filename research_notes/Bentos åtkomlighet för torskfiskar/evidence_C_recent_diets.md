# Evidence C: gadoid diets, benthos share by mass (recent and in-area sources)

Scope: the share of benthos in gadoid stomach contents, by mass, for the Skagerrak, Kattegat, Norwegian
Deep and northern North Sea. The numbers are used only as an after-the-fact check of the model's emergent
diet.

This file adds to `../Bentos och torskfiskar/evidence_C_diets_and_consumption.md` (CRR 219, Mackinson &
Daskalov, Christensen 1995, Greenstreet via Heath, Cramer & Daan). It does not repeat that file.

**Tags.** [F] full text read; [A] abstract or search snippet only; [S] secondary citation.
- Values marked "computed" are my own arithmetic on tables I read in full.
- Values marked "fig." were read off a bar chart (accuracy about +/-3 percentage points).

**Classification used throughout.**
- Benthos: polychaetes, bivalves, gastropods, echinoderms, decapods other than Pasiphaea (Pandalus,
  Crangon, Nephrops, crabs, pagurids, galatheids), isopods, cumaceans, gammarids, ostracods, priapulids,
  echiurids.
- Zooplankton: euphausiids, hyperiids, calanoids, chaetognaths, siphonophores, the pelagic polychaete
  Tomopteris.
- Mysids sit near the bottom and are counted as benthos, as in the earlier file. Their share is always
  below 1 %.
- *Pasiphaea* (P. multidentata, P. tarda, P. sivado) is a pelagic or mesopelagic shrimp. The convention
  counts shrimps as benthos, but Pasiphaea is shown separately ("+Pas") so the reader can move it.
- Fish and cephalopods are not benthos.

## Short answers

1. **No post-2000 benthos share by mass was found for any gadoid in the Skagerrak, Kattegat or Norwegian
   Deep.** Every in-area by-mass dataset found comes from 1984-1991. The recent sources found are of three
   kinds:
   - They give frequency of occurrence or numbers only:
     - Werner 2015, hake, Norwegian North Sea and Skagerrak, 2014.
     - CINEA 2024, new North Sea stomachs from 2022-23. This includes ICES IIIa, but with only 12 cod and
       20 whiting. Its species-level summaries are explicitly by number.
   - They are qualitative abstracts: Schückel et al. 2010 (haddock, northern North Sea, 2007).
   - They lie outside the area: Haase et al. 2020 (Baltic); Funk et al. 2021 (western Baltic).
2. **Haddock, Norwegian Deep incl. Skagerrak, 1984-87** (Albert 1994, Table 4) [F, computed]. Benthos is
   **80-98 % of identified dry weight in winter and 54-96 % in summer**, by 10-cm class.
   - Mean over the 20-49 cm classes: 86 % in winter, 63 % in summer, 75 % across the year.
   - If the shell-heavy ophiuroid and bivalve terms are halved, the annual mean falls to 68 %. This allows
     for the dry-weight inflation that Albert himself notes.
   - All of these are far above the 0.38-0.45 of CRR 219 for the total North Sea in 1991, which is
     not dry weight and excludes the Skagerrak.
3. **Cod, Skagerrak, February 1991** (Dorman et al. 1992, ICES CM 1992/G:70, % by weight, ICES 1991 protocol) [F, computed from
   the primary weights].
   - Benthos is 88-96 % below 25 cm, 56-74 % at 25-50 cm, 49-52 % at 50-70 cm and 25-37 % at 70 cm and
     above.
   - All 294 stomachs with food pooled (4.54 kg of contents): **46 %** (54 % +Pas). Fish make up 46 % and
     krill 0.1 %.
   - Kattegat pooled: 23 %. Krill were 25 % of all food there, so the Kattegat differs strongly.
4. **Norway pout, Norwegian Deep, 1985-87** (Albert 1994b, Table 8, dry weight) [F, computed]. Benthos (mysids included) is
   **about 1 % in winter and 8-14 % in summer**. Euphausiids are 45-81 %.
   - On a coastal bank off SW Norway in July 1991 the same author found 60 % benthos, mostly Pandalus
     (Albert 1995) [F].
   - So Norway pout benthos ranges from almost nothing to dominant, depending on site.
5. **Whiting** (Hislop et al. 1991, total North Sea 1981, % by weight) [F, computed]. Benthos falls with
   length: 36-45 % at 10-15 cm, 15-18 % at 20 cm, 5-6 % at 25-40 cm and 0 % above 50 cm. No in-area
   by-mass data.
6. **Saithe and hake**: no new by-mass data. Saithe stays at about 1 % (CRR 219). In the only recent hake
   sample, crustaceans occurred in 13 % of stomachs (%F, not mass). In the Skagerrak shrimp survey the
   crustacean figure was 54 %F, n = 11 full stomachs.
7. **Community share, today's Skagerrak IBTS mix, built from in-area 1984-91 data:** central **0.47**,
   range **0.41-0.50**. With the total-North-Sea CRR 219 haddock value (0.42) instead of the in-area
   value, it is 0.27. **None of these inputs is post-2000.** Adding Norway pout dilutes it (see the last
   table).
8. **"Kappa et al."**: searching "Kappa" with gadoid, diet and Skagerrak found only unrelated
   **harbour seal** diet work in the Skagerrak.
   - No gadoid diet paper by a Kappa turned up.
   - The same search also turned up a 1978 GU thesis on the feeding ecology of six gadoids in the
     Skagerrak and Kattegat. Its record could not be opened (bot wall).

Albert 1994 quotes:
- "The diet of haddock consisted of five main groups: polychaetes, bivalves, eucaridean crustaceans,

  in previous accounts."

Method: benthos = 100 - zooplankton - fish - cephalopods - Pasiphaea - indeterminate, then divided by the
identified share. Unidentified "Crustacea" and "Decapoda" rows were counted as benthos (at most 5.6 %; they could include some euphausiids). Check against Fig. 9: Eucaridea in summer at 20-29 cm, computed 48.5 % of identified,
against about 49 % in the figure. Subareas and years are pooled. The Skagerrak subarea alone is not given
by mass.

- "In all three areas cod with a length <250 mm feed to more than 90% on invertebrates."

  weight of crustaceans compared to our 63%)". This cites Börje et al. 1987 [S] and shows large
  year-to-year swings.
- Skagerrak "other" crustaceans: "contains mostly Pasiphea sp."

  These are numbers, and nematodes in the counts are probably parasites.

## Community benthos share, Skagerrak IBTS biomass mix

Weights: haddock 0.54, whiting 0.27, saithe 0.12, cod 0.06, hake 0.01. The mix is biomass-weighted. The
earlier file showed that consumption weighting changes the result by 0.01 or less, because Q/B is similar
across these species. Norway pout is excluded, as in the brief.

Species values (post-2000? = no for all):

| Species | Central | Low | High | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haddock | 0.75 | 0.68 | 0.78 | Albert 1994, 20-49 cm classes, winter and summer averaged. Low = ophiuroid + bivalve halved (dry-weight sensitivity). High = Pasiphaea counted as benthos. |
| Whiting | 0.12 | 0.06 | 0.18 | CRR 219 1991 (earlier file) and Hislop 1991 at 20-30 cm. No in-area data. |
| Saithe | 0.01 | 0.00 | 0.014 | CRR 219 1991 |
| Cod | 0.50 | 0.46 | 0.54 | Dorman 1992 Skagerrak pooled (without / with Pas). Feb only. Pooled weight is dominated by cod over 40 cm. |
| Hake | 0.02 | 0.00 | 0.05 | assumption from Werner %F; negligible at 0.01 |

Worked central case: 0.54 x 0.75 + 0.27 x 0.12 + 0.12 x 0.01 + 0.06 x 0.50 + 0.01 x 0.02 = 0.405 + 0.032
+ 0.001 + 0.030 + 0.000 = **0.47**.

| Variant | Share |
|---|---|
| Central | **0.47** |
| Low: haddock 0.68, whiting 0.06, saithe 0, cod 0.46, hake 0 | **0.41** |
| High: haddock 0.78, whiting 0.18, saithe 0.014, cod 0.54, hake 0.05 | **0.50** |
| Haddock summer only (0.63), others central | 0.41 |
| Haddock = CRR 219 total North Sea 0.42, others low | 0.27 (cf. the earlier file's 0.28) |
| Central, plus Norway pout at 0.5 x the biomass of the five, NP d = 0.06 (Norwegian Deep, mean of 4 groups) | (0.47 + 0.5 x 0.06) / 1.5 = **0.33** |
| Same with NP d = 0.60 (coastal bank) | (0.47 + 0.30) / 1.5 = 0.51 |

Reading:
- The haddock term is about 85 % of the community share, so the answer turns on haddock.
- The in-area Norwegian Deep haddock diet is 0.6-0.8 benthos. That is roughly 1.5-2x the total-North-Sea
  1991 value, even after the dry-weight correction.
- Data years are 1984-87 (haddock, Norway pout) and 1991 (cod). Use them as a validation band, not a point.

## Not found / could not open

- **Post-2000 in-area by-mass diets:** none found for any of the six species. The ICES stomach database
  holds the 2013 and 2022-23 data, but no public summary tables were found. CINEA 2024 shows them only as
  per-prey charts by number. Its SMS case study maps non-modelled prey to "other prey", so the WGSAM
  key-run diets cannot give a benthos share.
- **Bergstad 1991** (Sarsia 75:269-313 and 315-325; doi 10.1080/00364827.1991.10413455 and .10413456):
  paywalled (T&F 403). Abstracts only, no numbers for saithe, cod, whiting or hake.
- **Mattson 1990** (cod, Sarsia 75:247-267) and **Mattson 1992** (haddock, Sarsia 77:33-45): abstracts only,
  with no site in the abstract.
  - The cod abstract gives 55-75 m, six occasions in one year, and food that "changed from medium-sized,
    hyperbenthic prey to large, epibenthic and endobenthic prey" with size.
  - The brief's "west Swedish coast" is not confirmed by the abstracts.
- **Pihl 1994** (CJFAS 51:321; doi 10.1139/f94-033): abstract only, SE Kattegat, May 1984-Apr 1988, cod and
  whiting among five species; no %. Quote: "Several infaunal species increased in dietary importance during
  recent hypoxia."
- **Schückel et al. 2010** (J Fish Biol 77:98-118): Wiley 403. Abstract via PubMed 20646141.
- **Huwer et al. 2014**, MARE/2012/02 final report (https://edepot.wur.nl/427448): "You have no access to
  this file."
- **Börje et al. 1987** (Meddelande Havsfiskelab. 320, Feb 1981 cod in the Skagerrak and Kattegat): not
  online. Known only via Dorman 1992 [S].
- **Fiskeriverket Informerar 2001:1** (cod review): the download returned HTML.
- **Haase et al. 2020** (MEPS 645): Baltic, not Kattegat. No Kattegat cod diet paper by Haase was found.
- **Ustups et al.**: Baltic only. Not pursued.
- **Funk et al. 2021**: western Baltic. Snippet only.
- **DAPSTOM**: described in CINEA 2024 (v6.3, joined to the ICES database through an alpha ShinyR app), but
  no download was attempted. Cefas whiting 2006-07 (FSP survey, 53.5-56.5 N): dataset record only, no
  results.
- **Jaworski & Ragnarsson 2006, Rindorf, Kempf, Bachiller, Gonzalez/Sell**: not retrieved in this pass.
  Tam et al. 2016 confirms that the Kempf, Rindorf and Sell North Sea haddock data are the 1981/1991 sets.
- **GU 1978 thesis** on six gadoids in the Skagerrak and Kattegat (gupea.ub.gu.se, item f21fa9c9...): blocked
  by Anubis.
