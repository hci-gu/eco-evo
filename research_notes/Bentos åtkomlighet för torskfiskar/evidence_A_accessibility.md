# Evidence A - Accessibility of prey-sized benthos to demersal fish (gadoids)

Scope: the fraction of the prey-sized macrofauna standing stock (`benthic_community`, ~90 t WW/km2,
Mareld 100-300 m mud) that demersal fish can reach at a given time. Compiled 2026-10-24.
Tags: [F] full text read, [A] abstract or search snippet only, [S] secondary citation.
"Measurement" is a number in a source; "judgement" is mine and is marked as such.

## Short answers

1. **Vertical distribution (biomass).** The best-matched datum is Dauwe et al. 1998, station SK, a
   280 m Skagerrak mud: digitized from their Fig. 2, **~27 % of macrofauna biomass (AFDW) in 0-2 cm,
   ~51 % in 0-5 cm, ~30 % in 5-10 cm, ~19 % in 10-20 cm** [F, +-5 pp]. Two other North Sea muds
   bracket it: German Bight 34 / 65 % (0-2 / 0-5 cm), Frisian Front (with burrowing shrimp) 13 / 33 %.
   No study sliced 0-1 cm for biomass in these muds (not found).
2. **Taxa.** Abra nitida and Thyasira flexuosa sit in 0-2 cm at SK, but Nucula peaks at 5-10 cm
   [F]. Thyasirids burrow 1.2-6.4 cm deep [F]. Polychaetes make up 70-80 % of the biomass at
   10-20 cm [F]. Amphiura filiformis/chiajei have the disc at ~4-6 (up to 8) cm [S]. Only the arm
   tips are exposed: about 2 arms per animal, 10-20 mm into the water, and few arms in still water
   [F]. Abra nitida holds its inhalant siphon >0.5 cm above the surface when filtering (A. ovata sweeps up to 6 cm). It is active
   25 % of the time at 10 C and 55 % at 15 C [F].
3. **Cropping evidence.** Regenerating tissue is **12-30 % (mean 22 %) of adult Amphiura
   biomass**, and 13.3 % of annual production goes to arm regeneration (Skagerrak, 40 m) [F]. Dab
   eat ~6 % of the arm population per year, and 20 % of all arms carry a recent distal regeneration
   (southern North Sea) [A]. No direct measurement of the arm share of Amphiura body mass was found.
4. **Direct "available fraction" for fish.** No subtidal study measures it. The closest analogue is
   for waders: **89 % of prey biomass not harvestable by knot** (too large, too small or too
   deep) [F]. Fish-trawling studies (Hiddink et al. 2016) treat availability as prey biomass per
   fish, not as a depth refuge [A].
5. **Range (judgement built on the measurements above):** accessible fraction of the prey-sized stock
   **100 m: 0.06 / 0.18 / 0.38; 300 m: 0.10 / 0.26 / 0.48 (min / central / max)**.
   **Mareld-wide suggestion: 0.2 (range 0.08-0.45).** The fraction is low at 100 m because the
   dominant Amphiura biomass (disc + buried arms) is out of reach. Only arm tips are exposed.

## Reasoned range (min / central / max)

**Definition used.** f = the share of prey-sized benthic biomass inside the reach of a gadoid,
averaged over a 6 h tick, before any profitability choice. Profitability and handling belong to the
functional response. Structure: f = sum over taxa of w_taxon x (share of biomass within the reach
depth + share of the remaining biomass that is an exposed body part x fraction of time exposed).

**Reach depth: judgement, not measured for gadoids.** Cod, whiting and saithe pick prey from the
surface or bite the top layer. Haddock eat infauna (inferior mouth) and may dig, but I found no
measured digging depth. I use 0-1 cm as min, 0-2 cm as central (the sanderling analogue: 60 % of
prey from the upper 1 cm, nothing below 2.7 cm) and 0-5 cm as max (haddock digging).

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Amphiura posture.** The central 0.06 assumes the vertical suspension-feeding posture (Loo 1996). In slack water few arms are raised and Amphiura deposit-feeds with its arms stretched across the surface (Buchanan 1964 via MarLIN). Duchene & Rosenberg attribute 40-80 % of the worked surface area to Amphiura arms. On a 100-300 m slope mud that posture is likely common. It exposes more arm length, though still only arms, and pushes the value toward the upper half of 0.02-0.15.

**Weights (from the model brief; judgement on the split).** At 100 m: Amphiura 40-60 % (central
50), polychaetes 22-32 % (30), bivalves 15-20 % (15), crustaceans 3-8 % (5). At 300 m:
Amphilepis 10-20 % (15), polychaetes 52-55 % (55), bivalves 20-30 % (25), crustaceans 5-8 % (5).
Min uses the high-Amphiura weights and max the low ones.

| Depth | min | central | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 m (Amphiura-dominated) | 0.06 | 0.18 | 0.38 |
| 300 m (polychaete / bivalve) | 0.10 | 0.26 | 0.48 |
| Mareld-wide (mostly 100-300 m; judgement, weighted slightly toward the 100-200 m Amphiura ground; the plain mean of the two rows is 0.08 / 0.22 / 0.43) | 0.08 | **0.20** | 0.45 |

Independent check at 300 m: Dauwe SK puts 27 % of biomass in 0-2 cm and 51 % in 0-5 cm, which
matches the central and max. The 0-1 cm share is not measured; half of the 0-2 cm share (~0.14) is
consistent with the min.

**What is measurement and what is judgement or fit.**
- Measured: vertical biomass profile at one 280 m Skagerrak mud (single campaign). Living depths of
  Abra, Thyasira and Nucula at that station. Thyasirid burrow depths in the lab. Amphiura arm
  extension height, number of arms and flow dependence. Abra siphon activity vs temperature.
  Regenerated-tissue share and dab cropping rate.
- Judgement: gadoid reach depth (no measurement found); the arm share of Amphiura mass; the
  Amphilepis numbers; the taxon weights; the conversion from arm length to arm mass.
- Only obtainable by fitting: the realized intake. With a Holling II response the accessible fraction
  and the attack rate a are confounded (a x f x B), so tuning either one to an observed gadoid diet
  silently fixes the other. Recommendation: fix f from the table above (0.2, test 0.08 and 0.45 as
  sensitivity runs) and derive a from fish search and handling biology, not from the diet.
- Time scale: at an instant, fewer arms and siphons are out than over a 6 h tick. Food pulses
  roughly double the worked surface area within hours (Duchene & Rosenberg), and 15 C doubles
  Abra activity compared with 10 C. Expect f to rise after the spring bloom sedimentation. The
  wader data show strong winter deepening on tidal flats. That analogue is weaker at a stable
  6-8 C, 100-300 m bottom.
- Caveat on cropped parts: arm tips and siphons regrow (~6 %/yr of Amphiura biomass). If the model
  removes "accessible" biomass as dead mass, the logistic regrowth must cover this. That is
  consistent only if r_max is not also counting regeneration separately.
