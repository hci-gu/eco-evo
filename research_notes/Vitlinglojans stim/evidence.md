## Norway pout: aggregation and acoustic appearance

- **E1** Albert 1994, ICES J Mar Sci 51:45-61, doi:10.1006/jmsc.1994.1005 [F, scanned full text read].
  Norwegian Deep incl. the Skagerrak (subarea 4), 16 bottom-trawl cruises 1984-87, winter/summer/autumn, with
  "continuous echo recordings". Results p. 48: "Comparisons of echo recordings from trawl stations with the
  corresponding catch composition revealed a consistent pattern of echo traces attributable to Norway pout. At
  night the recordings were typically more or less homogeneous in the lowest 5-10 m of the water column with
  distinct single-fish echoes of larger gadoids. At moderate depths the traces were often present continuously at
  relatively high density over long distances. In daytime, however, this type of recording was observed
  infrequently and then only over short distances." p. 50: "the acoustical recordings of Norway pout were most
  pronounced during the night, whereas the catch sizes did not vary according to time of day. On cruise 14
  recordings were made continuously while a series of seven trawl hauls at the same position were made during a
  22-h period. The disappearance and reappearance of the traces corresponded closely (within 10 min) with sunrise
  and sunset and the catches were highest in daytime when the traces were absent. In daytime, it seems probable
  that the fish were distributed below the detection height of the echo sounder of 1.2-1.5 m above the bottom".
  Discussion p. 55: "Norway pout were strongly associated with the seabed and recordings were seldom made higher
  than 20 m above the bottom. Within this range, however, a distinct diurnal vertical migration was observed, the
  fish rising at dusk and sinking at dawn to about the 1.5-m near-bottom zone. Only a few local recordings were
  made off the bottom in daytime". GLM on catches: "The factor for time of day was not significant in any of the
  age-groups". p. 57: catch variation "substantial, even locally ... possible explanations include affinities for
  specific bottom substrata, shoaling, and concentration in areas of high prey abundance" (speculative). p. 58:
  frequency of occurrence fell from 96 % (winter) to 82 % (summer): "this suggests that Norway pout aggregate more
  in summer" (km-scale). p. 59: feeding "more intense at night"; "by staying close to the bottom in daytime, the
  Norway pout reduces the predation pressure on itself from visual predators. Evidence for diurnal feeding
  behaviour of Norway pout is weak".
  Reading [D]: at night pout are a DISPERSED near-bed layer (homogeneous, continuous over long distances), not
  schools; by day the fish are in the bottom 1.5 m acoustic dead zone, so their day aggregation state in the box
  is unobserved. No school echoes of pout are described. The larger gadoids (predators) are in the night layer.
- **E2** Raitt 1968, FAO Fisheries Synopsis 33 Rev. 1 (Norway pout) [F synopsis; the cited observations S].
  Section 3.52 Schooling: "From information from the fishery ... it is clear that Norway pout tend either to be
  found in dense concentrations consisting almost entirely of the one species or in low concentrations mixed with
  a number of other species (Johannesen, 1964)." ... "There is no evidence to suggest that Norway pout form
  'organized' schools in the way in which herring do." Section 5.42/5.43 (page image read): "the Fladen ground
  in August 1961 duplicate hauls by day and night gave twice as big a catch by day"; "Dickson (1960) has shown
  that by doubling the headline height during day-hauls the catch could be doubled. The inference from these
  observations would appear to be that Norway pout are normally just clear of the bottom but are even further off
  the bottom by night than by day." Predators (3.34): "Jones (1954) shows that in the North Sea Norway pout is by
  far the most important food item in the diet of whiting over 21 cm long"; also cod (seasonally), saithe-like
  gadoids, hake, megrim, halibut [S].
- **E3** ICES FishMap factsheet "Norway pout" (2006) [S]: "Norway pout has a benthopelagic life style, living in
  extensive shoals in the open sea, frequently in midwater off the bottom" (cites Svetovidov 1986); "Because dense
  schools are usually found within a few metres of the seabed, the industrial Norway pout fishery is to a large
  extent carried out with bottom trawls"; 0-group "close to the seabed during the daytime and spending the night
  in midwater" (Bailey 1975); "Norway pout tend to feed more intensively at night" (Albert 1994); "cod, whiting
  and saithe are major predators of Norway pout of age 1 and older. Mackerel is the main predator of 0-group";
  Skagerrak population "mainly composed of 1-group fish, with a peak at a total length of 12 cm" (IBTS Q1).
- **E4** Pedersen, Korneliussen & Ona 2004, ICES CM 2004/R:16 [F, text read]: Lofoten cod survey 2004, EK60 at
  18-200 kHz; "the three selected species which could be found in clean concentrations, and also largely as
  resolved single targets" (cod, saithe, Norway pout); Fig. 2b "registration (0-250 m) of clean Norway pout (lower
  25 meters)". A near-bed layer of individually resolvable pout (other region, season not stated) [F].
- **E5** Fassler 2010, PhD thesis Univ. St Andrews (hdl:10023/1703), chapter 2 = Fassler, Santos, Garcia-Nunez &
  Fernandes 2007, Can J Fish Aquat Sci 64:362, doi:10.1139/f07-013 [F, thesis text read; numbers D].
  North Sea herring acoustic survey (FRV Scotia, NW North Sea, June-July 2000-2002), acoustic data "between sunrise
  and sunset from 03:00 to 23:00 hours". "distinguishing echotraces of herring schools from those due to Norway
  pout ... Both species occur close to the seabed in densely packed schools, which are similar in shape and size."
  Schools detected with the SHAPES school module inside trawled polygons; "Major fish schools were sampled with a
  pelagic trawl"; of 124 hauls only "2 trawls contained exclusively Norway pout"; hauls with > 85 % pout used; 62
  pout echotraces, fish 5-10 cm (31), 10-15 cm (25), 15-20 cm (6) (Table 2.2). Mean Sv at 38 kHz of pout and
  herring echotraces both ~ -45 dB (Fig. 2.3b, read from the figure), pout 5th-95th percentile ~ -50 to -37 dB;
  "The smaller Norway pout might just have had higher packing densities". No school dimensions given for pout.
  Reading [D]: pout DO form discrete, herring-sized school echotraces near the seabed by day, at least as small
  (0-1 group) fish in summer on the NW shelf. The selection (trawling on major schools) proves existence, not the
  share of the biomass in schools.
- **E6** Onsrud, Kaartvedt, Rostad & Klevjer 2004, ICES J Mar Sci 61:1278, doi:10.1016/j.icesjms.2004.09.005
  [F, publisher full text via web]: Oslofjord 120 m. The dusk-rising "benthopelagic fish ascending from near the
  bottom established a scattering layer in midwater, below ~40 m"; pout "had the deepest nocturnal distribution,
  with maximum abundance in midwater and no catches in the upper 25 m"; pout below the krill by day, "apparently
  associated with the bottom in March and living pelagically below about 90 m in November". Schooling fish there
  were herring and sprat; pout are part of the scattering layer. No day/night difference in stomach fullness;
  "Their deep night-time distribution renders visual predation unlikely".
- **E7** Torgersen, Kaartvedt, Melle & Knutsen 1997, Sarsia 82:87 [A, marineinfo record]: Norwegian shelf and
  eastern Norwegian Sea; Norway pout "lived near the bottom by day", degree of diel vertical migration differed
  among locations.
- **E8** Aglen 1996, ICES J Mar Sci 53:501 [A]: acoustic vs swept-area density; for Norway pout the best
  correlation was with the echo integral at about the headline height of the trawl (~4 m), other species 30-100 m.

## Norway pout: diel catchability and feeding

- **E9** Adlerstein & Ehrich, ICES CM 2000/K:01 [F, OCR text read]: 30 GOV hauls, northern North Sea, 22-27 Nov
  1997. "Rates of Norway pout, and of small and large haddock tend to be higher during the day while catch rates
  of dab, grey gurnard and small whiting are higher at night". Table 3 night coefficient (GLM) Norway pout -0.52;
  grey gurnard +0.70 = "night catches are more than double the day catches", so the link is log:
  [D] pout night/day catch = exp(-0.52) = 0.59. Interpretation in the paper: species "in close contact with the
  seabed" by day; pelagic species "higher in water column at night would escape the gear from above the headline".
- **E10** Albert 1995, ICES J Mar Sci 52:873 [F, text read]: coastal bank SW Norway, 48-h station at 140 m in
  July, 14 bottom-trawl hauls: changes in pout length composition "generally not related to time of day, although
  a slight tendency for relatively more large fish at night"; small pout (11-15 cm) stomach dry weight "by day was
  twice that at night (p<0.01)"; "The larger Norway pout showed no regular diel variation". Daily ration 1.1 % bw.
- **E11** Dickson 1960 and Bailey et al. 1983 (via E1, E2) [S]: horizontally divided trawl, pout "primarily 2-4 m
  off the bottom by day compared with 0-2 m by night" (Dickson) - Albert notes the direction is opposite to the
  acoustics; Bailey et al. found no significant time-of-day variation between the two net compartments.

## Predation on pout: timing and per-capita risk

- **E12** Rindorf & Gislason 2005, J Exp Mar Biol Ecol 324:1, doi:10.1016/j.jembe.2005.04.013 [F, postprint read]:
  whiting, five North Sea sites (44-137 m). Prey groups: "clupeids (herring and sprat) and gadoids (whiting,
  haddock and Norway pout) ... The first group represented schooling prey whereas the latter group consisted of
  non-schooling, more demersal prey (Bailey, 1975; Bromley and Kell, 1995)". "All prey species but Norway pout
  showed two diel peaks in ingestion, one in the morning and one in the afternoon ... around dawn and sunset ...
  Norway pout showed only one peak around 15.00 hours, and thus differed from the other prey species. Ingestion
  during darkness was low for all species but whiting at location 5." Pout eaten at one location only (small n).
  Gadoid prey half-saturation "much higher" (q >= 514) than clupeids (20-159), "Due to the unknown catchabilities
  it is not possible to conclude" whether real.
- **E13** Rindorf 2003, Mar Ecol Prog Ser 249:265 [F, text read]: whiting "feeding on bottom dwelling prey at
  night, whereas pelagic and free swimming prey were captured mainly during daylight"; "No diel pattern in the
  presence of larger fish prey could be found, presumably due to their longer digestion time". Pedersen 2000 (via
  Rindorf): demersal whiting at 90 m (85 % fish prey) "fed primarily during daylight hours".
- No source found on saithe or cod feeding on pout by time of day, on capture success per attack on pout, or on
  per-capita risk versus pout aggregation size.

## Inputs to the school-size derivation

- **E14** Stimskydd E21 (Misund et al. 1995, ICES JMS 52:11) [F via sibling]: Norwegian Trench herring schools
  ~25 000 fish, 4.25 fish/m3; Stimskydd E22 (Fernandes et al. 2001) [F via sibling]: day herring schools 21.2-27.2 m
  long, 3.5-5.2 m high.
- **E15** ICES standard 38 kHz TS-length relations [S, standard survey practice]: herring TS = 20 log L - 71.2,
  physoclist gadoids TS = 20 log L - 67.5 (L cm).
- **E16** Fassler 2010 abstract (repository page) [A]: herring TS falls with depth (~3 dB at 50 m, ~5 dB at 200 m
  relative to the surface; swimbladder compression of a physostome). Pout (physoclist) does not compress.
- [D] scripts/school_factor_pout.py section 1: pout 10 cm TS -47.5 dB, herring 27 cm -42.6 dB; at the shared
  echotrace Sv of -45 dB pout pack at 1.8 fish/m3 (0.6-11 over the 5-95 % Sv), herring at 0.57; pout/herring
  density ratio 3.1 (1.0-1.6 if herring TS at depth is 3-5 dB lower, E16). School N: (a) Fernandes-sized ellipsoid
  (1300 m3) x 1.8/m3 = 2300 (730-15 000); (b) Misund herring school x ratio = 78 000 (25 000-39 000 with the depth
  correction). Used: N 10 000 (2 500-50 000); R_s = half-length 10.5-13.5 m + 0-5 m detection = 13 m (10-18) [J].

## Blue whiting

- **E17** Johnsen & Godo 2007, ICES J Mar Sci 64:1202, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsm110 [A]: west of the British Isles;
  in shallow water (< 350 m) acoustic density generally highest at night, "when fish sat higher in the water column
  and were more dispersed" (summary wording); day-night differences smaller in deep water.
- **E18** Torgersen et al. 1997 (E7) [A]: blue whiting of the deep daytime layer "consistently swam into upper
  waters by night"; "dense concentrations at the shelf break ... remained near the bottom both day and night".
- No school size, packing or Skagerrak-specific aggregation data found. Kept: sigma day 0.2 (0-0.5) [J], night
  0 (0-0.1) [A] (note: 0.1).
