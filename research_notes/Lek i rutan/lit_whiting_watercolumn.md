# Spawning in the box: whiting and the water-column group (literature)

Literature agent, 2026-11-18. Scope: whiting (group `whiting`, 1.0) and the water-column group
(`predatory_fish`: saithe 0.797, spurdog 0.097, hake 0.048, anglerfish 0.027, ling 0.014,
velvet belly 0.009, pollack 0.008). Question: what a spawning term would need for every fish that
SPAWNS IN THE BOX (57.81-58.31 N, 10.07-11.15 E). That means gonad energy and timing, and also
feeding during maturation and spawning, because a pure reserve drain is simply eaten back.
Already known and not re-derived here: `research_notes/Svältgräns och startreserv/fish.md`,
sections 3-4 (whiting energy density by month, Bull 1928, Cooper 1983 liver remark, saithe NIFES
liver by quarter, hake HSI Galicia).

Tags:
- [F] = full text read (pdftotext, by me).
- [A] = abstract only (OpenAlex or publisher).
- [D] = my derivation, with the arithmetic shown.
- [J] = judgement.
- snip = search-engine snippet or summariser output only, not verified against the source.
  This includes pages read through WebFetch.

Units: MJ per tonne of MATURE fish of that sex (or of mature fish at a 1:1 sex ratio) per year.
The model reserve maximum is 2000 MJ/t for both whiting and the water-column group.

Bottom line [J]:
- **Whiting is the in-box spawner.** About 2/3 of whiting biomass is mature, and spawning-stage
  fish are caught in the box at the start of the season.
- **Saithe mostly matures in or near the box and leaves to spawn.** Recorded stages put ~half of
  its biomass as mature; a 60 cm length cut gives only a quarter to a third.
- **Hake:** a summer minority may spawn in or near the box.
- **Spurdog** likely pups in the region.
- **Gamete energy of a MATURE fish is large next to the 2000 MJ/t reserve:** whiting ~0.4,
  saithe ~0.2, sexes pooled.
- **Feeding evidence is a gadoid proxy only (captive cod):** females stop eating ~5 weeks before
  spawning and through most of it. That supports pairing a gonad drain with reduced intake, not a
  pure drain that is eaten back. No whiting or saithe intake measurement during spawning was
  found.

## 1. Spawning grounds and the in-box classification

### Whiting

- **ICES FishMap whiting factsheet [F]:**
  - "Part of the Skagerrak population is thought to migrate into the north-eastern North Sea to
    spawn" (citing Knudsen 1964).
  - "Spawning takes place from January in the southern North Sea to July in the northern part."
  - Catches in Skagerrak/Kattegat are "dominated by 1-year-old whiting".
  - URL: https://www.ices.dk/about-ICES/projects/EU-RFP/EU%20Repository/ICES%20FIshMap/ICES%20FishMap%20species%20factsheet-whiting.pdf
- **whg.27.3a stock annex 2026 [F, via repo note `Fiskgrupper i rutan/predators/evidence_B_population.md`]:**
  "Spawning occurs from January to July at 30-100 m depth." Much of the box is deeper than 100 m.
- **González-Irusta & Wright 2017** (Fish. Res. 195: 141-151, doi:10.1016/j.fishres.2017.07.005).
  - [A/snip] Species distribution model of whiting in spawning stage from NS-IBTS **Q1** data,
    2009-2015.
  - [D] I read the published GeoTIFF myself (Marine Scotland FishDAC 12350, OGL v3). It has no
    valid cell east of 10.04 E at 57.7 N, and none east of ~10 E at the box latitudes, so **it does
    not cover the box**.
  - [D] Index of persistence 0-1; EPSG:3857 pixel grid converted to lat/lon. For 57.6-58.5 N,
    9.0-10.07 E (264 cells), just west of the box:
    - mean 0.047, no cell >= 0.5;
    - the whole map has mean 0.22, with 15 % of cells >= 0.5;
    - for comparison, the NE North Sea at 57-60 N, 3-8 E has mean 0.05.
  - [J] The model is Q1 (Jan-Feb) based, so it under-represents northern spawning, which peaks
    Mar-Apr. A low index here therefore says little.
- **KINO-2 (IMR egg survey, Norwegian sector, 2017-2021).**
  - [F, slide pdftotext] Whiting is listed as "Central NS". This comes from Table 2's column of
    "Known spawning areas" (literature), not from the survey.
  - [snip, report HI 2021-17]:
    - Norwegian Trench station: whiting eggs Mar-Jun, peak April;
    - Sleipner: eggs Mar-Aug, peak March;
    - Tampen: eggs Mar-Jul, peak Mar-Apr.
  - [snip] None of these stations is in the Skagerrak.
- **Munk et al. 1999** (snip, via a Swedish paper's citation): major transport of whiting eggs and
  larvae from offshore NE North Sea spawning grounds towards the Skagerrak.
- **Cooper 1983** (PhD thesis, W Scotland; https://storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/21477/1/Cooper-thesis.pdf) [F]:
  - "Although ripe and spent whiting and Norway pout were caught at offshore sites no spawning fish
    were encountered."
  - Running-ripe females were almost absent, so a "spawning emigration" out of the sampled area was
    suspected.
  - [J] This is a cautionary analogue: survey trawls in the "home" area may miss the spawning act.
- **DATRAS in the box** [D].
  - Source: the sibling agent's extract in `data/datras/` (complete fetch, NS-IBTS Q1 and Q3,
    2000-2025), restricted to valid hauls in the 4 box rectangles.
  - Code meanings are from memory and were NOT verified against the ICES code list:
    - old scale 1 = immature, 2 = maturing, 3 = spawning, 4 = spent;
    - M6: 61 immature, 62 maturing, 63 spawning, 64 spent, 65 resting/skipped;
    - SMSF: A immature, B developing (Ba/Bb), C spawning (Ca capable / Cb actively spawning),
      D regressing/regenerating, E omitted.
  - **Q1 (Jan-Feb), females**, counts summed over records:
    - 61 = 124, A = 55 (immature);
    - 62 = 169, B/Ba/Bb = 279 (maturing or developing);
    - 63 = 9, C = 4, Cb = 17 (spawning);
    - 65 = 2;
    - so ~30 of ~480 mature females (6 %) are in spawning stage.
  - **Q1, males:** C/Ca/Cb = 9 of ~360 mature.
  - **Q3 (Jul-Aug), females:** mostly immature or resting (61 = 339, A = 94); 30 Db
    (regenerating) and 28 resting (65), i.e. post-spawners; 7 still spawning.
  - **Q1 share non-immature by length** (numbers): 10-14 cm 8/145; 15-19 cm 93/247;
    20-24 cm 208/238; 25-29 cm 207/210; 30-34 cm 205/207.
  - **Mature share of box whiting biomass** (HL CPUE x L^3 x the Q1 mature fraction by 5 cm bin):
    **0.65**. Q3 gives 0.18, but in Q3 resting adults are probably recorded as immature [J], so use
    Q1.
- **Classification [J]:** whiting mature in the box and at least start spawning there in Jan-Feb.
  The main season (Mar-Jun) falls between the surveys, so its location is not observed; part of the
  stock may leave for the NE North Sea. Confidence medium.

### Saithe

- **Where it spawns:**
  - "Spawning takes place from January ... to May ... along the edge of the continental shelf";
    a high-abundance zone lies along the Norwegian Deep edge (FishMap; repo
    `Sejens diet på unga torskfiskar/evidence_saithe_season.md` entry 5).
  - KINO-2 (snip): eggs dominate at Tampen (61 N) in Jan-Apr, peak February; at the Norwegian
    Trench station Mar-Apr, peak March. Saithe spawning area "Northern NS" [F slide].
  - Tagging: adults > 60 cm are farthest from their summer grounds in Feb-Mar and return (Homrum et
    al. 2013, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fst048; repo entry 7).
- **The box saithe are smaller and younger:**
  - "Smaller/younger fish are found in the Skagerrak compared to Subareas 4 and 6"; juveniles live
    "along the coast and in the fjords of western and southern Norway" and "migrate ... to the
    North Sea within the ages of 2-5" (pok stock annex; repo `evidence_B_population.md` section 4) [F].
  - Most are mature only at age 6, ~60 cm (FishMap).
- **DATRAS biomass share in the box** [D]: CPUE x L^3, pooled 2000-2025 hauls.
  - >= 60 cm: Q1 0.34, Q3 0.23. >= 50 cm: 0.63 / 0.48. >= 70 cm: 0.18 / 0.09.
- **Recorded maturity in the box** [D]:
  - Q1 share non-immature by length: 40-44 cm 9/36; 45-49 cm 20/54; 50-54 cm 18/40; 55-59 cm 24/28;
    >= 60 cm 35/38. This puts a box L50 at ~50-55 cm, below FishMap's ~60 cm.
  - Mature biomass share: **Q1 0.60, Q3 0.47**.
  - Caveat [J]: the old-scale "2" (maturing) is frequent in 40-59 cm fish in Q3 too, so on that
    scale it may include resting fish. The true mature share probably lies between the length-based
    ~0.3 and the stage-based ~0.5-0.6.
  - Q1 spawning stages: males 3 = 3, 63 = 1, Cb = 1; females spent 4 = 1, Db = 1. A few running
    males, no running females.
- **Classification [J]:** the box holds immatures plus adults that complete vitellogenesis in or
  near the box (Nov-Jan) and then leave to spawn at the shelf edge (Feb-Mar). Spawning in the box:
  only occasional running males. Confidence medium.

### Hake

- **Werner, Staby & Geffen 2016** (Fish. Res. 183: 200-209, doi:10.1016/j.fishres.2016.06.008)
  [snip, not OA]:
  - peak spawning in July in the central and northern North Sea;
  - "few hake in spawning condition" in Jan-Feb in the northern North Sea and Skagerrak;
  - post-spawners off Norway north of 62 N in Oct-Nov.
- **Werner 2015 MSc** (Univ. Bergen; precursor; `papers/whiting_watercolumn/werner2015_hake_MSc.pdf`) [F]:
  - 86 spawners among 2935 fish with maturity data;
  - "The shrimp survey in the Skagerrak showed exclusively immature individuals" (maturity recorded
    in 2014 only);
  - 0-group of 4.5-10.5 cm were "almost exclusively found in the Skagerrak during the first
    quarter", read as larvae transported from the Norwegian Trench edge spawning grounds;
  - Q1 spawners only at 60-61 N, 128-173 m.
- **DATRAS box** [D]:
  - Q3 (Jul-Aug), females: 61 = 103, A = 26, 62 = 35, B = 7, 63 = 6 (spawning), 65 = 4.
  - Q3, males: 61 = 109, A = 35, 62 = 47, B = 13, 63 = 33 (spawning), 64 = 4 (spent), C = 1.
  - Q1: almost all immature.
  - Mature biomass share: Q3 0.73 (fish >= 55 cm are all non-immature), Q1 0.10.
- **Classification [J]:** mostly nursery and immature by numbers. A summer adult minority, holding
  much of the Q3 biomass, includes spawning-stage fish (mostly males) in the box during the July
  peak. Spawning in or near the box by that minority is possible. This conflicts with the 2014
  shrimp-survey statement, which had one year and a different season.

### Spurdog

- **Jones & Ugland 2001** (Fish. Bull. 99: 685-690; NOAA SPO PDF) [F].
  - Setting: monthly samples off the Hvaler Islands, outer Oslofjord at ~59 N, 11 E, which is
    adjacent to the box; 50-460 m.
  - Reproductive cycle:
    - fertilisation Oct-early Feb;
    - parturition "from September to December";
    - pregnancy 18-24 months.
  - Size and litter:
    - L50 = 81 cm;
    - free-living embryos 2-14 per female, mean 6.6 (1987), and 3-15, mean 7.5 (1997);
    - ovarian eggs > 2 cm: mean 8.2 and 8.9;
    - embryos up to 24.9 cm (near term).
- **Ellis & Keable 2008** (ICES JMS 65: 979, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsn080) [A]: Irish Sea uterine
  fecundity 3-21, F = 0.428 L - 31.87.
- Seasonal shift (Börjesson et al. 2022, via repo note) [snip]: offshore Skagerrak and the
  Norwegian side in Q1, nearer the Swedish coast in Q3-Q4.
- **Classification [J]:** pupping in the region is documented, so pupping in the box is likely but
  not shown. DATRAS box: >= 81 cm is 0.66 of biomass in Q1 and 0.25 in Q3 [D], sexes pooled.

  - "L. piscatorius spawns in deep water" (Hislop et al. 2001 synthesis, ICES JMS) [snip].

  - KINO-2 Table 2 [F slide], literature column of known areas: "Northern edge of NS and Norw.

## 2. Gonad investment

### Whiting

- **Fecundity:**
  - Hislop & Hall 1974 (ICES JMS 36: 42-49, doi:10.1093/icesjms/36.1.42) [A]: Fecundity = a L^3.25
    (North Sea, Minch), with a highest in the southern North Sea.
  - FishMap [F]: "A female of 30 cm length may produce 400,000 ripe eggs during the spawning
    season ... equivalent to 1,700 eggs per gram body weight"; eggs 1.0-1.3 mm; "shed in numerous
    batches over a period that may last for up to fourteen weeks".
  - Check [D]: gutted weight at 30 cm = 0.0093 x 30^2.9456 = ~210 g (FishMap equation), i.e.
    ~235 g total; 400,000 / 235 g = ~1,700 per g.
- **Batch spawning and declining eggs.** Hislop 1975 (ICES JMS 36: 119-127,
  doi:10.1093/icesjms/36.2.119) [A]: in captivity, "numerous batches of eggs over a spawning period
  that lasted for many weeks"; batch size declines; early eggs are larger and heavier.
- **Egg energy.** Hislop & Bell 1987 (J. Fish Biol. 31: 1-20, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1987.tb05209.x)
  [A]: dry eggs of cod, haddock, whiting, Norway pout, pollack and saithe average **23.19 kJ/g dry
  (SD 1.50)**; ling 26.92. The per-species dry weight table was not accessible.
- **Egg dry mass** [D/J]:
  - Cod egg 0.0746 mg dry (Oosthuizen & Daan 1974, snip). Scaling by diameter cubed from a cod egg
    of ~1.4 mm:
    - whiting 1.15 mm -> 0.554 x 74.6 = **41 ug**;
    - 1.0 mm -> 27 ug; 1.3 mm -> 60 ug.
  - I use **30-50 ug** to allow for the later, lighter batches.
- **Potential reproductive energy of a female** [D]:
  - 1,700 eggs/g x 40e-6 g x 23.19 kJ/g = **1.58 kJ/g = 1,580 MJ/t female**;
  - range 1,700 x (30-50)e-6 x 23.19 = 1,180-1,970 MJ/t.
  - Realised fecundity is below potential through atresia. In cod, realised fecundity of females
    starved during spawning was 0.2-0.8 of potential (Kjesbu et al. 1991, below). [J] Realised
    0.75-0.9 of potential in fed fish gives **~900-1,800 MJ/t female/yr**.
- **Wet-mass cross-check** [D]: 1,700 eggs/g x 0.82 mg hydrated (1.15 mm sphere at 1.026 g/ml) =
  1.4 g of hydrated eggs per g of female. Captive cod shed 99-195 % (mean 150 %) of their
  post-spawning body volume as eggs (Fordham & Trippel 1999 [A]). The orders agree.
- **Total output vs one ovary.**
  - Supported [D]: in wet mass, the season's hydrated eggs (~1.4 g per g of female) exceed any
    ovary that fits in the body.
  - Not established: whether the season's egg ENERGY exceeds the energy of the peak ovary.
    Whiting peak GSI and ovary energy density were not found. A peak ovary of GSI ~26 % at
    ~6 kJ/g wet [J] would hold the whole 1,580 MJ/t, and that is not implausible.
  - Whether whiting keep building yolk during the spawning season, i.e. need food or reserve
    DURING the 14 weeks rather than before, is therefore open. The cod analogue (Kjesbu et al.
    1991, section 4) suggests they do.
- **Males: two routes, which disagree.**
  - (a) **Gamete energy** [J]: ~0.1-0.2 of the female's, i.e. ~100-300 MJ/t male. Basis: female
    GSI ~10x male in Adriatic whiting [snip]; Hislop 1975 [A] says spawning "imposes a greater
    physiological strain on females than on males".
  - (b) **Body loss** [D/J]: Cooper's gutted carcass falls ~16 % in BOTH sexes from stage III to
    spent (section 7), i.e. ~0.7 kJ/g at ~4.5 kJ/g of tissue. This bundles gametes, spawning
    activity and reduced feeding, and it is cross-sectional.
  - If (b) is right for males, their total spawning cost (~700 MJ/t from the carcass alone) is far
    above their gamete energy, and close to the female carcass loss.
- **Sex ratio:** 49.5 % males, n = 6,047 young fish (Cooper Table III.5) [F].
- **Per tonne of mature whiting, 1:1** [D]:
  - Gamete route: 0.5 x (900-1,800) + 0.5 x (100-300) = **~500-1,050, central ~800 MJ/t/yr**,
    ~0.4 of the 2000 MJ/t reserve.
  - Body-loss route: ~700 MJ/t from the carcass alone, both sexes, plus an unquantified liver
    drawdown.
  - The pooled 800 is GAMETE energy. Total energy spent is at least that, and higher for males if
    route (b) holds.
- **Mature share of box whiting biomass** [D]: 0.65 (Q1 maturity by length bin x HL biomass;
  section 1). Per tonne of the whole box group: **~330-680, central ~520 MJ/t/yr** of gamete
  energy.

### Saithe

- **Fecundity:**
  - Storozhuk, Golovanov & Golubyatnikova 1974 (ICES CM 1974/F:13) [F]: 90 females, N North Sea off
    Shetland, Jan-Feb, 56-99 cm, 1.8-5.0 kg; potential fecundity 0.677-8.985 million.
    Regression: **AIF (million) = 1.26 W(kg) - 1.92**, r = 0.84.
  - Skjæraasen et al. 2017 (J. Fish Biol., doi:10.1111/jfb.13161; accepted manuscript
    `papers/whiting_watercolumn/saithe_oocyte_2016_AM.pdf`) [F]:
    - realised fecundity in captivity ~0.84 million (0.77-0.93) per female, median 60 cm;
    - eggs 1.04-1.31 mm, volume-weighted mean 1.18 mm, decreasing through spawning;
    - spawning period "quite short" vs cod and haddock, "indicative of P. virens spawning fewer
      batches".
- **Eggs per g** [D]:
  - From Storozhuk (potential): 3 kg: 1.86 M -> 620/g; 5 kg: 4.38 M -> 876/g.
  - The regression is unreliable at the low end: it gives ~0.1 M at 1.6 kg and ~0.6 M at 2 kg.
  - Low end anchored instead on the captive realised 0.84 M at 60 cm. A 60 cm female weighs
    ~1.5-1.6 kg (maturity-manual example fish 59-61 cm: 1,482-1,616 g [F]) to ~2.2 kg [J].
    This gives **380-560 eggs/g**.
- **Egg dry mass and energy** [D/J]: (1.18/1.4)^3 x 74.6 = 45 ug (range 35-55); x 23.19 kJ/g =
  1.04 J per egg.
- **Per female** [D]: ~380-880 eggs/g x 1.04 J = **~0.40-0.92 kJ/g = ~400-920 MJ/t female**.
  Central ~550 MJ/t, at ~530/g.
- **GSI** [F], ICES saithe maturity manual (DTU Aqua 2007; North Sea Jan-Mar 2007): single example
  fish, total-weight basis, gonads probably partly hydrated, so **not means**.
  - Females: 11.4 % (61 cm, onset of spawning); 25.1 % (82 cm) and 20.1 % (59 cm) (main spawning).
  - Males: 4.1 % (69 cm, onset); 22.1 % (50 cm), 7.2 % (55 cm), 31.4 % (101 cm) (main to late
    spawning).
  - Testes are large in saithe. Their energy density was not found.
- **Males** [J]: ~0.3-0.5 of female energy, from the large testes and from captive cod weight loss
  (males 14 % vs females 29 %).
- **Per tonne mature, 1:1** [D]: 0.5 x (400-920) + 0.5 x (120-460) = **~260-690, central ~400
  MJ/t/yr**.
- **Mature share of box saithe biomass** [D/J]: ~0.3 (length) to ~0.5-0.6 (recorded stage).
  Per tonne of box saithe: ~80-410 MJ/t/yr. The spawning act happens outside the box (section 1);
  vitellogenesis (Nov-Jan) probably happens partly inside it.
- **Liver share** [D]: naive NIFES contrast in the Norwegian Sea (fish.md table).
  - Sep: 7.3 % liver x 71 % lipid = 5.2 % of body mass as liver lipid.
  - Jan-Mar: 5.6 % x 57 % = 3.2 %.
  - Difference 2.0 % x 39.5 kJ/g = **~0.8 kJ/g**.
  - Size-confounded (Sep: one station, n = 25, 64 cm), and it mixes winter and spawning. As an
    order of magnitude, the liver drop can cover the female gamete energy.

### Spurdog (viviparous) [D/J]

- Litter ~7 (6.6-7.5) per ~2-year cycle [F].
- Term pup <= 25 cm. Its mass was not found; [J] ~40-80 g from a generic squalid length-weight
  relation (e.g. 0.0026 L^3.1 = 56 g at 25 cm).
- Litter mass ~7 x 56 = ~390 g per 2 years, ~195 g/yr, for a mature female of ~3.5-4.5 kg
  (90-100 cm): ~4-6 % of body mass per year.
- At [J] ~5 kJ/g (embryo plus yolk; not found): **~150-500 MJ/t mature female/yr**.
- Males ~0. Investment is continuous through gestation, not a seasonal pulse.

### Hake, anglerfish, ling, velvet belly, pollack

- Not found for the area.
- Hake is an indeterminate batch spawner (Werner 2015) [F]. Its Galicia HSI and liver lipid are
  in fish.md.
- Ling eggs carry an oil globule: 26.92 kJ/g dry, the highest of the gadoids (Hislop & Bell 1987)
  [A].

## 3. Timing (North Sea / Skagerrak)

- **Whiting:**
  - In the box in Jan-Feb (DATRAS Q1) [D]: 87-99 % of 20-34 cm fish are non-immature, and 6 % of
    mature females are already in spawning stage.
  - W Scotland (Cooper 1983) [F]:
    - males' stage II peak in late January;
    - ripe fish from January to June, peak in May;
    - spent February-August, peak in April;
    - "recovery to the resting condition was obvious as a rise in the proportion of stage II fish
      from April onwards";
    - in mature fish, condition minimum Apr-Jun, maximum Aug-Sep.
  - Eggs at the Norwegian Trench station Mar-Jun, peak April [snip].
  - Annex: Jan-Jul [F, repo].
  - [J] Skagerrak: build-up Nov-Feb, spawning Feb-Jun (peak ~Apr), recovery Jun-Sep.
- **Saithe:**

  - Field spawning peak Jan-Feb (Coull et al. 1998, repo) to Mar (Norwegian Trench, snip).
  - [J] build-up Nov-Jan, spawning Feb-Mar, recovery Apr-Sep.
- **Hake:** peak July, central and northern North Sea [snip].
- **Spurdog:** parturition Sep-Dec, fertilisation Oct-Feb [F].
- **Ling:** Mar-Jul [snip]. **Pollack:** Feb-May, captive [A].

## 4. Source of gonad energy

- **Gadoid proxy, cod: Kjesbu et al. 1991** (CJFAS 48: 2333, doi:10.1139/f91-274) [A]:
  - captive cod at four rations;
  - "the depletion of the white muscle and liver was related to oocyte maturation and in
    particular to the proportion of eggs spawned";
  - actual fecundity of fish "deprived of food during the spawning period was between 20 and 80 %
    of the potential fecundity depending on the nutritional status".
  - So food during spawning feeds the later batches, and liver plus muscle pay for the rest.
- **Lambert & Dutil 2000** (CJFAS 57: 815, doi:10.1139/f00-022) [A]: poor-condition females had
  lower fecundity and egg dry weight, but lost relatively MORE somatic energy, which raises their
  mortality risk.
- **Whiting:**
  - Cooper 1983 [F]: gutted carcass (without liver and gonads) of a 30 cm fish falls through
    maturation (section 7). So muscle is drawn on, not only liver.
  - Bull 1928 (via fish.md): liver fat lowest in spent fish.
  - [J] Liver + muscle + concurrent food.
- **Saithe:** liver fat lowest Q1-Q2, highest Q3-Q4 (NIFES, fish.md) [F]. Muscle use not found.
- **Haddock liver series in the brief** (0.76 Jan-Feb -> 0.33 May-Jul): consistent with a drawdown
  from spawning (Feb-May) plus post-spawning recovery lag [J].

## 5. Feeding during maturation and spawning

- **Captive cod: Fordham & Trippel 1999** (J. Appl. Ichthyol. 15: 1-9,
  doi:10.1046/j.1439-0426.1999.00098.x) [A]. This is the most decisive quantitative source, but
  for cod. Summary:
  - 13 females and 13 males were monitored, with 6 immatures as controls.
  - Before spawning, feeding "ceased for females, on average, 36 days (15-54 days) before the
    onset of spawning".
  - Spawning lasted 42 d (10-61). Feeding "was suppressed by both sexes during the first
    three-quarters of each female's spawning period".
  - Mature females "went, on average, 70 days or 19 % of the year without eating".
  - Feeding resumed abruptly after ~91 % of eggs had been released.
  - Females ate more than males in winter and after spawning.
  - The 6 immatures "fed throughout" and gained 8 % of body weight.
  - Stomach fullness of Georges Bank cod by maturity state agreed.
  - [J] Feeding factor for mature female cod: ~0 for ~10 weeks around spawning; males suppressed
    for a shorter span.
- **Field cod: Michalsen et al. 2008** (ICES JMS 65: 571, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsn019) [snip]:
  - spawning cod in Lofoten DO feed, but more stomachs are empty and fullness is lower than in
    Barents Sea feeding cod;
  - females fuller than males.
- **Captive cod appetite:** Skjæraasen et al. 2004 (Fish Physiol. Biochem. 30: 163,
  doi:10.1007/s10695-005-4319-6) [snip]: appetite fell 2-3 months before spawning in both sexes,
  more strongly in males.
- **Saithe:**
  - Captive, fed pellets: "Pre-spawning fish increased in mass while growth ceased or even tended to
    decrease during the spawning season" (Skjæraasen et al. 2017) [F].
  - Intake was not measured.
  - Field Q1: 26-50 % empty stomachs at all ages, including immatures, so it is a winter ration
    depression and not only spawning (repo `evidence_saithe_season.md`).
- **Pollack, captive:** "decrease in feeding ... during the spawning period" (Suquet et al. 1996,
  ALR; DOI doi:10.1051/alr:1996013 matched by title search, not verified) [snip]. The full text
  was refused (403).
- **Whiting: not found.**
  - Hislop 1975's full text (the captive study, which might report feeding) was not accessible.
  - Whiting regurgitate when trawled, so stomach data are weak (Bull 1928 [snip]; Hislop et al.
    1991 [A]: ~50 % empty or regurgitated).
  - Baltic whiting consumption is lowest in Q1-Q2 [snip].
  - [J] Whether whiting fast during spawning is open.
    - Female gamete energy (~900-1,800 MJ/t) against the carcass loss (~700 MJ/t) plus a liver of
      up to 2000 MJ/t does not exclude a cod-like fast.
    - Hislop's 0.03-0.40 %/d female weight loss, at the low end, suggests that at least some
      captive females kept eating.
    - No intake reduction can be quantified for whiting. Use the cod proxy with low confidence:
      females ~0 intake for ~1-2 months around peak spawning, males a shorter or weaker
      suppression.

## 6. Post-spawning recovery

- **Whiting** (Cooper) [F]:
  - condition minimum Apr-Jun, maximum Aug-Sep;
  - "Recovery of males was faster than females";
  - the "spring minimum was lower in mature females".
  - [J] ~3-4 months.
- **Saithe:** NIFES liver ratio Jan-Mar ~5.6 % -> Sep ~7.3 % in the Norwegian Sea (fish.md) [F];
  [J] ~5-6 months.
- **Cod, captive** [A]: feeding resumes immediately after the last batch, and females eat more than
  males after spawning (Fordham & Trippel).

## 7. Weight loss at spawning

- **Whiting:**
  - Hislop 1975 [A]: females lost **0.03-0.40 % of body weight per day** while spawning.
  - [D] Over a 14-week (98 d) season: 3-39 %; at a mid rate of 0.2 %/d, ~20 %.
  - Cooper 1983 Table III.7 [F]: GUTTED weight (without liver and gonads), from cross-sectional
    length-weight regressions, of a 30 cm fish by maturity stage. Females 200.1 g at stage III ->
    174.3 at V -> 168.5 at VII (spent); males 202.8 -> 182.2 -> 171.3.
  - [D] Carcass loss stage III -> spent: **-15.8 % in females, -15.5 % in males**. This is carcass
    loss (relevant to questions 4 and 7) and excludes the liver.
- **Cod, captive** [A]: females -29 %, males -14 % of body weight during spawning (Fordham &
  Trippel). Proxy for saithe.
- **Saithe:** not found. Captive mass flat or falling during spawning [F].
