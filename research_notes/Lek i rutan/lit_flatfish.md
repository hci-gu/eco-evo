# Spawning of the benthivores' flatfish, starry ray and hagfish in the box: literature

Scope: long rough dab (0.126 of the benthivore biomass), dab (0.045), plaice (0.038), witch (0.017)
in full; starry ray (0.015) and hagfish (0.013) short. Box: eastern Skagerrak, 57.81-58.31 N,
10.07-11.15 E, rectangles 44G0, 44G1, 45G0, 45G1, 50-300 m. Read with
`research_notes/Svältgräns och startreserv/fish.md` section 2 (dab liver lipid / HSI, plaice
5.81 -> 4.10 kJ/g, long rough dab "stored 92 kcal"), which is not repeated here.

Tags: [F] full text read; [F via thesis] = the paper's reprint in Rijnsdorp's 1992 PhD thesis
(`research_notes/Fältmetabolism kolja och plattfisk/data/q3/vliz_385870/f.txt`); [F eye] = read
off a figure; [A] abstract only; snip = search-engine summary only; [D] own derivation (arithmetic

"MJ/t mature/yr" below = energy that leaves the body as gametes per tonne of MATURE fish of that sex

### 1.2 Gonad investment

- Female peak gonad 16 % of body weight Jan-Feb (4 % Oct, 13 % Dec) (Rijnsdorp, van Lent &
  Groeneveld 1983, ICES CM 1983/G:31 [F]); "circa 17 % of the gutted body weight between January
  and March" (Rijnsdorp 1989, ICES JMS 46:35 [F via thesis]); standard 35-cm female gonad 70 g = 16 %
  of gutted weight (Rijnsdorp & Ibelings 1989, J Fish Biol 35:401, doi
  10.1111/j.1095-8649.1989.tb02992.x [F via thesis]).
- Male testis < 2.5 % of body weight (Deniel 1981, cited by R&I 1989 [F via thesis]).
- Ripe egg: 6.60 kJ per 1000 eggs at stage 3 (24.10 kJ/g dry, 0.274 g dry per 1000 eggs); 5.0-5.5
  at stages 4-5 [F via thesis]; 1983: 6.136 J/egg, 24.4 kJ/g dry [F]. Formalin underestimates
  egg dry weight by ~25 % (Hislop & Bell 1987, cited [F via thesis]).
- Ovary energy density [D]: ripe ovary dry 33 %, protein 86 %, lipid 10 %, ash 4 % (Dawson & Grimm
  1980, as used by R&I [F via thesis]) -> 0.33 x (0.86 x 23.6 + 0.10 x 39.5) = 0.33 x 24.25 =
  8.0 kJ/g wet. Check: 70 g x 8.0 = 560 kJ against the fecundity route 90 000 eggs x 6.6 J = 594 kJ
  (6 % apart). This is the pre-hydration ovary; hydrated running eggs are mostly water.
- Female gametes per tonne [D]: 594-790 kJ (790 = with the 25 % dry-weight correction) / 498.2 g
  pre-spawning = 1 190-1 590 MJ/t (on the spent 378.6 g: 1 570-2 090). Age series (Rijnsdorp et al.
  1983 Table XIII [F]): egg energy / December weight = 322/344 = 0.94 (age 4), 548/487 = 1.13 (5),
  1 000/775 = 1.29 (7), 1 213/909 = 1.33 (8), 1 882/1 334 = 1.41 kJ/g (12); x 1.33 for the
  correction -> 1.25-1.88.
- Irish Sea comparison [F, citing Dawson & Grimm 1980]: 7-yr female 527 g, 3 506 -> 1 862 kJ Dec ->
  Mar (-53 %), 60 % of it in eggs -> 986 kJ / 527 g = 1.87 kJ/g [D]. Dawson & Grimm: ~42 % of the
  energy assimilated over the year goes to reproduction (snip of the abstract).
- Males [D/J]: testis <= 2.5 % x an assumed 4-6 kJ/g wet [J] = <= 100-150 MJ/t; R&I: "this loss
  will be very small in comparison with the total energy loss of 28 %" [F via thesis].
- Sex-averaged gametes [D]: (1 190-1 590 + 100) / 2 = ~650-850 MJ/t mature (per tonne, sexes
  weighted equally; mature biomass is female-biased by size, which would raise it). Fecundity is
  determinate in plaice in the sense that the season's eggs are yolked before spawning [J]; plaice
  females shed several batches over ~5 weeks (Simpson 1959, Rijnsdorp 1989, cited in Rijnsdorp et
  al. 1992 MEPS [F]).

| mature male | 436.9 | 387.2 | 643 | 1 470 MJ/t | ~75 |
| immature | 437.7 | 418.5 | 193 | 440 MJ/t | ~75 |

- Energy factor: females 65.2 -> 36.3 J/cm3 (-44 %), males 55.0 -> 40.0 (-27 %), immatures 48.7
  -> 44.2 (-9 %) [F via thesis]. Excess over immatures [D]: F 2.49 - 0.44 = 2.05 kJ/g, M 1.47 -
  0.44 = 1.03 kJ/g (immatures still feed at a low level in winter, so their loss understates their
  metabolism [F via thesis]).
- Spawning metabolism: F 6.4-9.1 kJf/day, M <= 7.4 (text 7.3) kJf/day at a 35-cm fish [F via
  thesis]; resting at 6.8 C ~3 kJ/day. Lipid supplied ~55 % of spawning metabolism (75 % in the
  Irish Sea) [F via thesis]. Lipid lost F 11.0 g, M 10.2 g; protein F 33.8 g, M 11.4 g [F via
  thesis].

### 1.4 Timing

- Female gonad development July-December, weight gain from October; max ~17 % Jan-Mar; female in
  spawning condition ~5 weeks, late Jan - early Mar; males >= 11 weeks, 2nd half Dec - 2nd half Mar;
  older fish spawn earlier and longer (Rijnsdorp 1989 [F via thesis]). North Sea spawning "end of
  December to the beginning of April" [F via thesis]. Rijnsdorp et al. 1992 MEPS 91:19 [F]: "The
  average duration of the spawning period was estimated at 11 wk and 5 wk in male and female plaice".
- Box: running fish already in January hauls (1.1) [D]; timing in the Skagerrak not found.

### 1.5 Source of gonad energy

- "Main gonad growth occurred between October and January. However, an estimated 50% of the gonad
  growth was subsidized from body reserves built up during the growing period" (Rijnsdorp 1990, Neth
  J Sea Res 25:279, doi 10.1016/0077-7579(90)90027-E [F via thesis]); "From November onwards gonad
  growth exceeded surplus production and was subsidized from body reserves" [F via thesis].
- Spawning products and spawning metabolism are "drawn from the energy reserves stored in the body
  tissues ... built up during the growing period from April to November" [F via thesis]. The carcass
  is the principal store of lipid and protein (Dawson & Grimm 1980, snip; fish.md).
- Body condition peaks Oct-Nov (1080e-5) and falls slightly to 1017e-5 by February before spawning
  (Rijnsdorp et al. 1983 [F]).

### 1.6 Feeding during maturation and spawning

- Rijnsdorp 1989 Table 2 (1985-86 maturity surveys; "visible food remains in the gut") [F via
  thesis]: feeding incidence immature F 35.9 % (n 1 799), ripe stage 2-5 F 2.1 % (n 419), spent F
  40.4 % (n 52); immature M 29.3 % (n 972), ripe M 8.6 % (n 1 666), spent M 28.4 % (n 134). "Ripe
  female plaice were hardly feeding at all wherever they were caught. The same applies to ripe males
  in the centre of the spawning areas. However, the smaller ripe male plaice at the edges of the
  spawning areas did show some feeding activity"; not caused by lack of food ("immature and spent
  plaice were observed feeding at stations where ripe plaice did not feed"); "spent fish resumed
  feeding as early as January".
- Ratio of incidences [D]: F 2.1 / 35.9 = 0.06, M 8.6 / 29.3 = 0.29. This is incidence relative to
  winter immatures, not a fraction of summer intake [J]. The stronger evidence is that the R&I
  energy budget closes as gametes + metabolism drawn from the body with no intake term [F via
  thesis].
- Rijnsdorp et al. 1983 [F]: the no-feeding claim "is widely cited but has only a small emperical
  base (TODD, 1911)"; in their samples "only in the last week of March some of the fish had
  remainings of food in their intestines". Mollet et al. 2023 (Ecol Evol, doi 10.1002/ece3.8070,
  CC BY) [F]: "During spawning in winter, male and female North Sea plaice cease feeding, but males
  remain twice as long in spawning condition than females" (citing R&I 1989); their model fixes the
  female spawning duration at 1/8 year.
- Duration [D/J]: females ~60 days of loss (R&I) to ~90-100 days (Dec - mid Mar, Rijnsdorp et al.
  1983); males ~75 days (R&I) to ~100 days (>= 11 weeks spawning plus ripening).
- Captive data: the 1984 tank experiment (Rijnsdorp 1990) ran May - January with lugworm ad lib
  and reports no winter intake measurement [F via thesis]; no captive winter-intake data found.
- Field FMR (Jones et al. 2025, Fish Res 289:107476, doi 10.1016/j.fishres.2025.107476 [A], via
  `Fältmetabolism kolja och plattfisk/evidence.md` E4): FMR peaked in early summer before spawning;
  males showed "higher apparent energy use outside of the summer feeding season".

### 1.7 Recovery and weight loss

- Weight loss: F -24 % wet, -44 % energy; M -11 %, -27 %; immature -4 %, -9 % (R&I [F via thesis]);
  F 487 -> 391 g (-20 %), 2 832 -> 1 602 kJ (-43 %), 5.81 -> 4.10 kJ/g (Rijnsdorp et al. 1983 [F]).
  Spent-female protein 10 % below spent males and 17 % below immatures [F via thesis].
- Recovery: condition lowest in March-April (spent females), increases "steadily towards a maximum
  in October and November" (Rijnsdorp et al. 1983 [F]); surplus production peaks April-September
  and ~25 % of it goes to somatic growth, the rest mainly to reserves until August and to reserves +
  gonad thereafter (Rijnsdorp 1990 [F via thesis]). Recovery ~6-8 months [D].

## 2. Dab (Limanda limanda)

- Spawning in the box: no maturity data (dab is not age/maturity-sampled in NS-IBTS CA; 0 records
  in `ca.csv`) [D]. Box-polygon biomass share >= 14 / 15 / 20 cm = 0.93 / 0.86 / 0.52 (Q1), 1.00 /
  0.99 / 0.82 (Q3) [D, GOV length frequencies 2000-2025, weight ~ L^3]; with L50 F 14 cm, M 11 cm
  (Bohl 1957, cited in Rijnsdorp et al. 1992 MEPS 91:19 [F]) 0.86-0.99 of box biomass is mature
  [D]. Bohuslän fjord egg
  survey 2017 identified dab eggs (snip). Classification [J]: probably spawns in the box (widely
  distributed batch spawner); not confirmed.
- Timing: "The spawning period runs from January to September with a broad peak from February to
  April"; in the southeastern North Sea it "starts in January and reaches a peak in March and
  April" (Rijnsdorp, Vethaak & van Leeuwen 1992, MEPS 91:19 [F], citing Harding & Nicholls 1987, van
  der Land 1991). Peaks shift north: Feb-Mar southern North Sea, Mar-Apr German Bight, May-June
  further north (snip). Females return to the coast after spawning earlier than males; males stay
  longer in spawning condition (Bohl 1957, cited [F]). Skagerrak timing not found; Feb-May [J].
- Gonad cycle: female gonad weight (18-22 cm; axis read as % of body weight [J]) German Bight ~1 %
  Jul-Oct, 2.5 % Nov, 8 % Jan, 14 % Mar, 12 % Apr, 5 % May; Dogger Bank 6 % Jan, 11.4 % Mar, 7 % May,
  1.4 % Aug (Knust 1987, Inf Fischwirtsch 34:161, Abb. 2 [F eye]). "the weight of ovaries can reach
  15-20% of the wet body weight" (Saborowski & Buchholz 1996, J Fish Biol 49:173, doi
  10.1111/j.1095-8649.1996.tb00015.x [F]). Gonad/body weight "seemed less influenced by food" than
  condition (Knust [F]). Male GSI: not found (Htun-Han 1978a-c, J Fish Biol 13, not obtained).
- Fecundity type: determinate - gap between previtellogenic and vitellogenic oocytes before
  spawning; annual fecundity close to potential annual fecundity, low atresia; unspawned
  vitellogenic oocytes degenerate at the end of spring (Le Duff et al. 1996, Aquat Living Resour
  9:249, doi 10.1051/alr:1996028, snip; full text refused). Hence the season's energy is set by the
  yolked oocyte stock, not by summing wet batch masses [J].
- MJ/t [D]: females 15-20 % GSI x an assumed 5-8 kJ/g wet (plaice pre-hydration 8.0; lower if the
  peak ovary holds hydrated batches) = 0.75-1.6 kJ/g = 750-1 600 MJ/t mature female; males not
  found (testis assumed ~1-2 % x 5 kJ/g ~ 50-100 MJ/t [J]); sex-averaged ~430-850 MJ/t. Low
  confidence: no dab ovary energy density or fecundity-energy data were obtained.
- Source of gonad energy and feeding (Saborowski & Buchholz 1996, German Bight 1993-94 [F]):
  "Feeding activity in males varied between summer and winter, while females fed more
  constantly"; female stomach contents showed "No distinct seasonal pattern"; "females use the
  comparatively higher food intake in winter entirely to meet the energy demand for gonad
  maturation"; the female HSI falls from its pre-spawning peak because "the maturing gonads need
  additional energy which cannot be supplied by feeding and must be obtained from the liver" and
  "at the beginning of the spawning season, females feed less due to sexual activity. This was
  indicated also by the elevated number of empty stomachs in females during the spawning period";
  males: "reduced but not suspended feeding activity" in winter. Muscle lipid flat (5-6.6 mg/g) -
  no muscle lipid store [F]. Knust 1987 [F]: Dogger Bank dab food intake through the year
  "konstant", German Bight a pronounced annual cycle.
- Reserve share [D]: the peak liver lipid of ~350 MJ/t (fish.md) against 750-1 600 MJ/t of ovary
  -> the liver can supply at most ~22-47 %; the rest from concurrent food (and unmeasured carcass
  protein). Fraction of normal intake during spawning: not quantified [J: females ~0.7-1.0,
  males ~0.5 in winter].
- Recovery: condition minimum Mar-May, maximum Jul-Nov (German Bight; Knust [F]); females after
  spawning return to coastal feeding grounds early (Bohl 1957 via Rijnsdorp et al. 1992 [F]).
  Weight / energy loss at spawning: not found.

## 3. Long rough dab (Hippoglossoides platessoides)

- Spawning in the box: no maturity data in NS-IBTS CA [D]. Box-polygon biomass share >= 15 / 20 /
  25 cm = 0.78-0.79 / 0.41-0.49 / 0.09-0.11 (Q1-Q3; the four rectangles 0.69 / 0.25-0.35 / 0.05) [D];
  L50 for the North Sea / Skagerrak not found, so the mature share is ~0.4-0.8 for an assumed L50
  of 15-20 cm [J]. Bohuslän inshore egg surveys (2017; winter
  spawners 2017-2022) found early-stage (Ia-Ib) eggs of long rough dab, treated as local spawning,
  and long rough dab among the most frequent large eggs (SLU Aqua reports 2019:12, Aqua notes
  2024:12, snip; the reports' links are now dead). Classification [J]: spawns in or next to the
  box - low-medium confidence.
- Timing: birth date set at 1 March "according to information on long rough dab spawning in the
  northeast Atlantic (Walsh 1996)" (Fryer et al. 1997, MEPS 158:303 [F]); Clyde: "All groups tend
  to be in best condition during November and December followed by a decline to the spawning time,
  in March and April, after which there is a slow recovery" (Bagenal 1957, JMBA 36:339, doi
  10.1017/S0025315400016854 [A]); Clyde spawning begins early April (snip).
- Gonad investment: peak GSI, gonad energy, fecundity per gram - NOT FOUND (Bagenal 1957 full text
  refused). MacKinnon 1972 (fish.md): a 35-cm female "stored 92 kcal" in summer, spent in winter.
  Proxy [J]: female 1 000-1 500 MJ/t from plaice/dab, sex-averaged 500-800; low confidence.
- Feeding: "The North Sea long rough dab feeds throughout the year with a definite maximum from
  April till August when over 85% of the fish sampled were feeding" (Ntiba & Harding 1993, Neth J
  Sea Res 31:189 [A]); "lowest feeding intensity occurred in December-January (31%), with the
  maximum in May (90%) to July" (as cited by Gonzalez et al. 2003, NAFO SCR 03/23 [F]). Incidence
  ratio winter / summer ~0.35 [D], sexes and maturity stages pooled. American plaice (NW Atlantic):
  "Condition and feeding intensity of spawning females and males showed an inverse relation"; low
  condition in spawning seasons (spring-summer); feeding increases after spawning (Gonzalez et al.
  2006, J Northw Atl Fish Sci 37:87 [F]); fasting in winter in Canadian waters (Roff 1982, cited by
  Burnett et al. 1992 [F]). Fraction of intake during spawning by sex: not found.
- Recovery: "slow" after Mar-Apr (Bagenal [A]). Weight / energy loss at spawning: not found.

  Local ogive [D]: L50 F 35 cm, M 32 cm from Q3 staging; F 42, M 27 cm from Q1 staging (a summer
  spawner's gonads are small in winter, so Q1 likely under-stages females [J]); Gulf of Maine F
  33.5, M 24-30 cm (Burnett et al. 1992 [F]). Box-polygon biomass share >= 25 / 30 / 35 cm =
  0.87-0.94 / 0.64-0.73 / 0.34-0.40; mature biomass share by the local ogives F 0.27-0.38, M
  0.53-0.76, sex-averaged ~0.45-0.55 [D]. Classification: no evidence either way for spawning in
  the box; mature fish are resident there [J].
- Timing: Gulf of Maine "spawn during May-August"; "ripe fish obseved from April to November";
  spawning at 24-360 m, 2.9-8.9 C (Burnett, Ross & Clark 1992, J Northw Atl Fish Sci 12:15 [F]);
  NE Atlantic May-September (snip). Skagerrak: not found.
- Gonad investment [D, crude]: fecundity = 210.47 W^1.054 (W g) (Burnett et al. 1992 [F]) -> 500 g:

- Source / feeding: the spawning season (May-Aug) falls in the feeding season [J: gonads largely

## 5. Starry ray (Amblyraja radiata) - short

- Oviparous; "10-45 egg cases each year" (FishBase; NatureScot 2023 citing Kulka et al. 2015,
  snip); NW Atlantic skates 41-56 egg cases per year (McPhie & Campana 2009, J Fish Biol, doi
  10.1111/j.1095-8649.2009.02282.x, snip). North Sea: hatching after ~4 months, pups 8-11 cm; egg
  cases mainly in waters > 50 m with juveniles and adults (snip). Gulf of Maine: reproductive
  parameters roughly constant through the year (Sulikowski et al. 2005, snip), i.e. continuous
  laying [J].
- Egg-case mass and energy: NOT FOUND, so MJ/t not derived. Spawns in the box: plausible (> 50 m
  egg cases, resident adults) [J]; no box evidence.

## 6. Hagfish (Myxine glutinosa) - short

- "only 19 to 30 having been counted in any one female" and eggs "up to 25 mm" (FishBase, snip);
  Newfoundland 11-38 eggs per female, fecundity rising with length (snip); Gulf of Maine (Martini et
  al. 1997, Fish Bull 95:311) fewer than 30 eggs per female and a large share of animals without
  macroscopically visible gonads (snip); no obvious seasonal peak, probably breeds year round in
  deep water (FishBase, snip). Only a few eggs complete maturation, the rest become atretic (NOAA
  report, snip).
- Gullmarsfjord / Skagerrak (Walvig, Patzner, Martini): NOT FOUND in accessible form. Spawning
  periodicity per female unknown, so a per-tonne annual energy is not derivable [J].

## 7. Cross-species notes for a spawning term [J unless tagged]

- The feeding evidence differs by species, not just the gonad size: plaice fast (incidence 0.06 F,
  0.29 M of immatures; budget closes with zero intake [F via thesis]); dab females keep feeding
  [F]; long rough dab feed at ~0.35 of summer incidence in Dec-Jan, maturity stages pooled
  [A/snip]; witch spawn inside the summer feeding season [F timing]. A pure reserve drain matches
  plaice only.
- Plaice is also the only species where the spawning-specific metabolism is measured: the female
  loses 2.05 kJ/g beyond an immature's loss, of which ~1.2-1.6 kJ/g are eggs [D from F via thesis].
- Rough flatfish contribution to the benthivore group [D, low confidence]: species gametes per
  tonne of species biomass = box mature share x sex-averaged gametes: plaice 0.6 x 750 = ~450, dab
  0.9 x 640 = ~580, long rough dab 0.4-0.8 x 650 = ~260-520, witch 0.5 x 250 = ~125 MJ/t; weighted
  by the group shares (0.038, 0.045, 0.126, 0.017): 17 + 26 + (33-66) + 2 = ~80-110 MJ per tonne of
  benthivore biomass per year from the flatfish (the gadoids are not covered here). Long rough dab
  dominates and is the least known term.
