# Spawning in the benthivores: cod and haddock (literature, 2026-11-18)

Scope: Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) and haddock (*Melanogrammus aeglefinus*). The focus is the North Sea,
Skagerrak and Kattegat; other stocks are used where needed and flagged. The aim is a spawning term for the
benthivores' liver reserve, which the NIFES haddock series shows falling from ~0.76 of max (Jan-Feb) to ~0.33
(May-Jul) and recovering to ~0.81 (Sep-Nov). A scratch test showed that a pure drain is eaten back, so this
note weighs feeding during maturation and spawning as heavily as the gonad energy itself.

Builds on: `research_notes/Svältgräns och startreserv/fish.md` section 2 (NIFES haddock liver lipid, Tronbøl
2022 HSI by maturity group, Yoneda & Wright 2004 HSI) and `research_notes/Ljusstyrd aptit/summary.md`
(Taranger / Hansen 2006: weight loss in spawning). These are not re-derived here.

Bottom line [J]:
- **Both species build their gonads in the box.** Cod spawn there at least in part. For haddock, spawning in
  the box is possible but unconfirmed.
- **Gonads cost ~250-450 MJ per tonne of mature fish per year (sex-averaged).** Females carry 2-4x the male cost.
- **The spawning period is a fast, or close to one, for each fish.** It lasts ~1-2 months per fish, and the
  stock's spawning is spread over ~3 months.
- **Vitellogenesis does not appear to suppress appetite.** The evidence conflicts (see Q5).
- **The spring liver low may not be purely a spawning effect** (suggestive only, low confidence). In NE Arctic
  haddock, skipping females (small n) also reach a spent-like HSI in April. Immature HSI peaks in February and
  is back at autumn levels in March (Q7). No Skagerrak data separate the two.

## Q1. Spawning grounds: eastern Skagerrak / the box

### Own analysis of NS-IBTS maturity stages in the box [D]

Data: `data/datras/ca.csv` and `hh.csv` (NS-IBTS 2000-2025, fetched by `scripts/datras_fetch.py`). Hauls are
restricted to the four box rectangles 44G0, 44G1, 45G0 and 45G1 (`StatRec` from HH). Almost all sampling is
Swedish (some Norwegian in 2014-21).

Stage mapping, using the ICES vocab definitions fetched from vocab.ices.dk:

| functionally immature first-time developer, shown as Ba(fi) | - | - | Ba |
| developing | 2 | 62 | B, Bb |
| spawning | 3 | 63 | C, Ca (actively spawning), Cb (spawning capable) |
| spent / regenerating | 4 | 64 | D, Da, Db |
| resting / skipping | - | 65 | E (omitted spawning) |

Old-scale code 5 is unknown and counted as "other".

Caveats:
- CA is length-stratified, so the shares are valid only within a length class, not for the population.
- Q1 covers January and February only. March-May are not sampled.
- Q3 September hauls (2001-11) use the old 4-stage scale, where code 2 evidently lumps resting adults with
  maturing ones: 85 % "developing" in September against 94 % "resting" in August, which uses the 65/E codes.

Spawning-stage fish were found in the box in these years:
- Cod: females in 12 years and males in 17 years (2000-2025).
- Haddock: females in 5 years (2018-25) and males in 5 years.

Splitting the "spawning" and "spent" classes by code [D]:
- Running is 3/63/C/Ca; spawning capable is Cb. Spent is 4/64/Da; regenerating, i.e. a resting adult, is Db.
- Cod >= 50 cm:
  - Running: 6 % F and 15 % M in January; 1 % F and 17 % M in February.
  - Spawning capable: ≤ 1 %.
  - February spent: 2 % F and 4 % M. February regenerating: 2 % F and 5 % M.
- Haddock >= 35 cm:
  - Running: 2 % F and 1 % M in January; 4 % F and 13 % M in February.
  - Spawning capable (females): 5 % in January, 7 % in February.
  - February females: 1 % spent, 9 % regenerating.

Reading [D/J]:
- **Cod.** Large males are already running in January. Large females are mostly in late development in
  January-February, with a few running. This fits local spawning starting in Jan-Feb and peaking after the
  survey window. Cod 35-50 cm are mostly immature (58-82 %), and this length class carries much of the box's
  cod biomass [J].
- **Haddock.**
  - Most fish >= 25 cm are developing in Jan-Feb, so the gonads are built in the box.
  - In February, 13 % of large males are running, but only 4 % of females are; another 7 % are spawning
    capable and 1 % spent.
  - So female spawning in the box before March is marginal. Whether the main Mar-May spawning happens there,
    or the fish move to deeper water or out of the area, cannot be told from Q1 data.
  - The 9 % regenerating (Db) females are resting adults that will probably skip, not post-spawners.
- **August.** Both species are mostly immature or resting. Developing fish are rare in cod (≤ 0.11 of the
  >= 50 cm classes) but 0.14-0.19 in haddock >= 35 cm, so most vitellogenesis starts after August.

### Literature

- **Svedäng H, Barth JMI, Svenson A, Jonsson P, Jentoft S, Knutsen H, André C (2019). Local cod (*Gadus
  morhua*) revealed by egg surveys and population genetic analysis after longstanding depletion on the
  Swedish Skagerrak coast. ICES J Mar Sci 76: 418-429. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsy166 [F-s]**
  - **Sampling.** Egg tows in Bottnafjord, Brofjord, Gullmarfjord, Havstensfjord and around Orust/Tjörn.
    Surveys ran 25 Feb-5 Apr 2013 and 2-6 Feb to 17-19 Mar 2014.
  - **Position relative to the box.** The paper gives coordinates only for adult samples (Gullmarfjord
    ~58.2 N 11.3 E, Havstensfjord ~58.2 N 11.4 E). From place names, the egg areas lie ~58.0-58.4 N,
    11.3-11.7 E: inshore and just east of the box edge at 11.15 E [J].
  - **Densities.** "Cod eggs in early developmental stages were found in all localities and both years", at
    0.18-5.3 stage I-II eggs m^-2. This is "considerably lower" than on the Norwegian Skagerrak coast.
  - **Origin.** "Early stage eggs were found inside fjords too far to have been transported by oceanic drift
    from offshore spawning areas." No offshore stations.
  - **Genetics disputed.** Cardinale, Mariani & Hjelm (2019, ICES JMS 76: 1209-1211, snip) re-analysed the
    genotypes and reject an independent coastal stock, but accept that spawning occurs.
- **Fox CJ et al. (2008). Mapping the spawning grounds of North Sea cod (*Gadus morhua*) by direct and
  indirect means. Proc R Soc B 275: 1543-1548. doi:10.1098/rspb.2008.0201 [F-s]**
  - The whole-North Sea egg survey ran late Jan-late Mar 2004.
  - "Rather low abundances of cod eggs were found ... north of latitude 58°".
  - Along the Norwegian Trench edge, trawl data predicted egg production that the egg samples did not confirm
    ("Some cod eggs were found in this area").
  - The Skagerrak is not addressed.
  - Of TaqMan-identified gadoid eggs, 17.6 % were cod and 48.3 % haddock.
- **González-Irusta JM, Wright PJ (2016). Spawning grounds of Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) in the North Sea.
  ICES J Mar Sci 73: 304-315. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsv180 [F-s]**
  - Based on NS-IBTS Q1 spawning-stage cod, 2009-14.
  - "Across the North Sea spawning has been reported to extend from January to April"; median survey date
    7 February.
  - "the shallower parts of the Norwegian trench" were classed as occasional spawning grounds.
  - Whether the Skagerrak is inside the model domain was not established; the supplement was not read.
- **González-Irusta JM, Wright PJ (2016). Spawning grounds of haddock in the North Sea and West of Scotland.
  Fish Res 183: 180-191. doi:10.1016/j.fishres.2016.05.028 — NOT obtained** (paywalled, no abstract in
  Crossref or Semantic Scholar). This is the key missing source for haddock in the Skagerrak.
- **ICES FishMap species factsheet, haddock (ICES, undated) [F]** (`papers/cod_haddock/ICES_FishMap_haddock_factsheet.pdf`):
  - "Spawning takes place from March to May, at depths of 100-150 m."
  - Juveniles "tend to be more abundant in the Skagerrak than adults".
  - The Norwegian Deep haddock "shows a seasonal migration between relatively shallow areas in summer and
    deeper parts in winter. Mature specimens are even migrating out of the area in winter" (citing Albert
    1991, ICES CM 1991/G:45).
- **Wright PJ, Gibb FM, Gibb IM, Millar CP (2011). Reproductive investment in the North Sea haddock: temporal
  and spatial variation. Mar Ecol Prog Ser 432: 149-160. doi:10.3354/meps09168 [F]**
  - Main spawning concentrations are in the northern North Sea and off the Scottish east coast.
  - Spawning runs "February to May" (citing Hislop 1984 and Wright & Gibb 2005).
- **Search snippet only.** An older ICES compilation argues that Skagerrak cod come from spawning elsewhere,
  citing "absence of spawning cod in the Skagerrak". The DATRAS table above contradicts this for recent
  decades.
- **Not pursued** within the budget: Vitale et al. 2008 (Kattegat), Höffle et al., Knutsen/Espeland
  (Norwegian Skagerrak coast egg surveys), André et al. 2016, Svedäng & Bardon 2003 (only the abstract was
  reachable; it holds no spawning statement).

Classification [J]:
- **Cod: builds gonads in the box, and some spawn in or right next to it.** The evidence is running males and
  some spawning females in Jan-Feb hauls, plus coastal eggs just east of the box. The offshore spawning
  density is unknown and probably minor compared with the northern North Sea.
- **Haddock: builds gonads in the box; whether it spawns there is unknown** (low confidence). In February, 13 %
  of large males are running, but only ~4 % of females are (plus 7 % spawning capable and 1 % spent). Adults
  of the Norwegian Deep are reported to move deeper or leave in winter.

## Q2. Gonad investment

### Cod

- **Relative potential fecundity, North Sea 2002-03 (Yoneda & Wright 2004, MEPS 276: 237-248,
  doi:10.3354/meps276237, Table 3) [F].**
  - In oocytes per g expected gutted weight, from the RF-age fits at ages 3 / 4 / 5: inshore 697 / 830 / 950;
    offshore 452 / 616 / 783.
  - Observed values range 301-2018 per g (inshore North Sea).
- **Realised against potential fecundity.** In captive cod starved during spawning, realised fecundity was
  "20 and 80% of the potential fecundity depending on the nutritional status". In moderate-ration fish,
  atresia rose to 33 % at 80 % of eggs spawned (Kjesbu et al. 1991, CJFAS 48: 2333-2343, doi:10.1139/f91-274)
  [A]. I use 0.7-0.9 for fed wild fish [J].
- **Batch spawning with determinate fecundity.**
  - Reared cod "spawn 17-19 batches" over "about 50-60 days"; egg dry weight fell 20-30 % from the first to
    the last batch (Kjesbu 1989, J Fish Biol 34: 195-206, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1989.tb03302.x) [A].
  - "The influx of protein into the ovary, the mean hydrated egg diameter, and the egg dry weight all reached
    maximum values at 10% PES and subsequently declined" (Kjesbu et al. 1991) [A].
  - So the ovary still takes up material after spawning begins. **Peak GSI is not the season's whole output**,
    and the ovary energy density at the peak is diluted by hydration [J]. I therefore use the egg route.
- **Egg dry mass and energy.**
  - Gadoid eggs without an oil globule average 23.19 kJ/g dry (SD 1.50) (Hislop & Bell 1987, J Fish Biol 31:
    1-20, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1987.tb05209.x) [A].
  - North Sea cod egg dry mass is ~0.075 mg (Oosthuizen & Daan 1974, Neth J Sea Res 8: 378; snip). Captive
    broodstock values run 0.11-0.16 mg (Røjbek et al. 2014; snip).
  - I use 0.050-0.075 mg, central 0.060 mg, which allows for the 20-30 % within-season decline [J].
- **Derivation (egg route) [D].** Realised eggs per g total weight = RF x (realised/potential) x
  (gutted/total), with gutted/total 0.80-0.88 for a pre-spawning female [J]. Energy per egg = dry mass x
  23.19 J/mg.

  | Case | Eggs per g total weight | Energy per egg | Energy per g female |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Central | 650 x 0.8 x 0.85 = 442 | 0.060 x 23.19 = 1.39 J | **0.615 kJ/g ≈ 615 MJ/t** |
  | Low | 450 x 0.7 x 0.80 = 252 | 0.050 x 23.19 = 1.16 J | 0.29 kJ/g |
  | High | 950 x 0.9 x 0.88 = 752 | 0.075 x 23.19 = 1.74 J | 1.31 kJ/g |

- **Cross-check by GSI [D].** GSI 0.15 x a pre-hydration ovary at ~4.5 kJ/g wet (~20 % dry at 23 kJ/g dry
  [J]) = 0.68 kJ/g. With the "gonadal tissue" value of 6.93 kJ/g wet (Holdway & Beamish 1984, JEMBE 81: 147,
  via Holt & Jørgensen 2014, Conserv Physiol 2: cou050, doi:10.1093/conphys/cou050 [S]; not sex-specific,
  low confidence) it is 1.04 kJ/g. Both fall within the egg-route range.
- **Peak female GSI.** No North Sea value was found in reachable full text. Snippets give "7 to 21 %, mean
  13.5" (stock unclear) and "~20 % of wet weight in females vs ~4 % in males, ~28 % vs ~4.5 % of energy"
  (attributed to Newfoundland or first-time spawners; unverified). I take ~10-20 % [J].
- **Males.**
  - North Sea-origin captive first-time spawners: male GSI 0.02-14.2 (Lowestoft) and 1.0-13.0 (St Andrews
    Bay) (Perutz 2007, PhD thesis, Univ. Glasgow, https://theses.gla.ac.uk/112/) [F]. Males were "sacrificed
    immediately prior to running" between January and March, so these are near-peak values; the low end is
    males that did not mature.
  - Food and temperature raised testis weight and GSI (Yoneda & Wright 2005, ICES JMS 62: 1387-1393,
    doi:10.1016/j.icesjms.2005.04.018) [F-s; GSI values only in a figure that was not read].
  - Testis energy density: not found. A testis is ~80-85 % water and protein-rich, so ~3.5-5 kJ/g wet [J].
  - Male gonad energy [D]: GSI 0.08 (0.02-0.14) x 4 (3.5-5) kJ/g = **0.32 kJ/g** (0.07-0.70). With the
    snippet's 4 % GSI it is 0.16. Central taken as ~0.25 kJ/g = 250 MJ/t.
  - Males release sperm over a longer period and pay a behavioural cost: courtship displays correlate with
    mass loss (Skjæraasen, Meager & Hutchings 2010, Can J Zool 88: 595-600, doi:10.1139/Z10-033) [A]. This is
    a spawning cost outside the gonad.
- **Sex ratio.** Not found for the box. The DATRAS CA counts of large fish are near 1:1 but length-stratified,
  so not a population ratio. I assume 1:1 by mass [J].
- **Sex-averaged [D].** (615 + 250)/2 ≈ **430 MJ per t of mature cod per year** (range (290 + 50)/2 = 170 to
  (1310 + 700)/2 ≈ 1000).
- **Skipped spawning lowers the annual mean.** 18 % of NE Arctic females under restricted food were skippers
  (Skjæraasen et al. 2009, CJFAS 66: 1582-1596, doi:10.1139/f09-102) [A]. Box August data show resting codes
  in most large fish, which cannot separate skippers from post-spawners [D].

### Haddock

- **Peak GSI (Filina, Khlivnoy & Vinnichenko 2009, The reproductive biology of haddock at the Rockall Bank,
  J Northw Atl Fish Sci 40: 59-73, doi:10.2960/J.v40.m639) [F].**
  - Females: "maximal values of GSI were 12.0-19.6%" in pre-spawning stage IVa in March-April; 0.9-2.4 % when
    spent in May; minimum 0.4-0.8 % at the end of Aug-Sep.
  - Males: March 2005 "GSI of males rose from 1.9 to 3.3%", mean 2.1 % (females 7.0 % mean in the same
    sample).
  - 11-19 batches; a batch is 5.3-9.5 % of potential fecundity.
  - Scotian Shelf peak "between 10 and 15% of body weight in mature fish" (snip).
- **Relative potential fecundity (W North Sea, per g eviscerated mass), Wright et al. 2011 [F].** 249 -> 341
  (age 2) and 516 -> 564 (age 3) between the start and end of the study period. ICES FishMap gives "500 eggs
  per gram body weight" for a four-year-old female [F].
- **Realised fecundity, captive NW Atlantic haddock (Trippel & Neil 2004, CJFAS 61: 2097-2110,
  doi:10.1139/f04-125) [A].** Mean total fecundity 535 000 eggs; 9 batches (3-16); spawning lasted 37 d with a
  5.4 d batch interval; egg diameter 1.37-1.53 mm, falling 10.4 % within a female.
- **Egg dry mass.** No haddock value was found in reachable text; Hislop & Bell 1987 tabulate it but only the
  abstract was read. Haddock eggs (1.19-1.62 mm, Fox et al. 2008) are the size of cod eggs (1.28-1.63 mm), so
  I use the cod 0.050-0.075 mg [J].
- **Derivation [D].**

  | Case | Eggs per g total weight | Energy per egg | Energy per g female |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Central | 450 x 0.8 x 0.85 = 306 | 1.39 J | **0.43 kJ/g ≈ 425 MJ/t** |
  | Low | 250 x 0.7 x 0.80 = 140 | 1.16 J | 0.16 kJ/g |
  | High | 564 x 0.9 x 0.88 = 447 | 1.74 J | 0.78 kJ/g |

  The GSI cross-check (12-19.6 % x 4.5 kJ/g = 0.54-0.88 kJ/g) is an upper bound: stage IVa ovaries are partly
  hydrated.
- **Males [D].** GSI 0.02-0.033 x 4 (3.5-5) kJ/g = **0.08-0.13 kJ/g**; I take ~110 MJ/t (range 50-200).
- **Sex-averaged [D].** (425 + 110)/2 ≈ **270 MJ per t of mature haddock per year** (range ~100-500).

Caveat [J]: these are the energies deposited in gametes. The synthesis cost of building them, the
spawning-season metabolism at near-zero intake, migration and male display are not included, and the fasting
cost is likely larger than the gonads (see Q4 and Q8).

## Q3. Timing (North Sea / Skagerrak unless stated)

| Phase | Cod | Haddock |
|---|---|---|
| Vitellogenesis onset | Mostly after August in the box: developing fish ≤ 0.11 of the >= 50 cm classes in August [D]. NE Arctic follicle proliferation ends "early November" (Skjæraasen et al. 2009) [A]. ## Q4. Source of gonad energy (liver against muscle against concurrent food)

**No study was found that gives the liver's share of gonad energy as a number.** The pieces:

- **Kjesbu et al. 1991 [A].** "The depletion of the white muscle and liver was related to oocyte maturation
  and in particular to the proportion of eggs spawned". Food-deprived females still realised 20-80 % of
  potential fecundity. So stores can fund much, but not all, of the eggs, and muscle protein contributes as
  well as liver lipid.
- **Yoneda & Wright 2005, female, J Fish Biol 67: 1225-1241, doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.2005.00819.x [A].**
  Captive, North Sea-origin fish. "the somatic growth of mature individuals was at least as high as those of
  immature fish over the period of vitellogenesis"; "they allocated a certain amount of energy relative to
  their size into egg production and any surplus was invested into somatic growth." So in fed fish, eggs can
  be funded from concurrent food, and the liver is not obliged to pay for them.
- **Lambert & Dutil 2000, CJFAS 57: 815-825, doi:10.1139/f00-022 [A].**
  - Lab: females in poor pre-spawning condition had lower fecundity and egg dry weight, yet lost relatively
    more somatic mass and energy.
  - Wild: "The condition of spent females suggested a greater impact of changes in environmental conditions on
    adult than on immature cod."
  - The full text likely holds the somatic-energy-loss numbers but was not obtained.
- **Schwalme & Chouinard 1999 [A].** Gulf of St Lawrence cod, a cold stock that fasts in winter. Over winter
  they lost 25-27 % of carcass weight and 76-84 % of liver dry weight; gonad growth accounted for only 4-19 %
  of the dry weight lost, and 81-96 % went to metabolism. **In a winter-fasting stock, most of the liver
  drawdown is maintenance, not gonads.** Whether this applies to the 6-8 °C Skagerrak bottom is doubtful [J].
- **Marshall CT, Yaragina NA, Lambert Y, Kjesbu OS (1999). Total lipid energy as a proxy for total egg
  production by fish stocks. Nature 402: 288-290. doi:10.1038/46272 [title only].** Liver lipid predicts egg
  production at stock level, which is correlation, not a share.
- **Haddock, NE Arctic HSI (W-ov) by group (Tronbøl 2022 Fig. 9a).** Values carried over from fish.md's
  eye-reads (±5 %), not re-read here; n per month is not given in the text (~75 immatures and 65 skippers over
  the whole series, so ~10 or fewer per cell).
  - Maturing females hold 5.1-6.8 % from December to April; the April value is 6.7.
  - Spent females in April: 0.88.
  - Liver drop from maturing to spent [D]: ~5.8 % of ovary-free body weight. At ~50 % liver lipid x 39.5 kJ/g
    that is ~1150 MJ/t, about 2.7x the central female egg energy (425 MJ/t).
  - This is cross-sectional (different fish, small n) and only suggestive [J]. In this stock the liver does
    not seem to be drawn down before spawning; it is spent across spawning, apparently on more than the eggs.

[D/J] For a model term: the female gonad energy (~430-615 MJ/t) is a lower bound on what the reserve loses
across spawning. The rest is probably maintenance during a near-fast. This is low confidence: no study
measures the split.

## Q5. Feeding during maturation and spawning

### Cod, captive

- **Fordham SE, Trippel EA (1999). Feeding behaviour of cod (*Gadus morhua*) in relation to spawning. J Appl
  Ichthyol 15: 1-9. doi:10.1046/j.1439-0426.1999.00098.x.** Full text and abstract not obtained (Wiley
  blocked; no abstract in Crossref, Semantic Scholar or Europe PMC). Content known only second-hand:
  - **[S], Dutil et al. 2006 [F]:** "Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) cease feeding for as much as 70 days during
    prespawning and much of the spawning period (Fordham and Trippel, 1999)". The citing paper is Dutil,
    Godbout, Blier & Groman 2006, J Appl Ichthyol 22: 138-144, doi:10.1111/j.1439-0426.2006.00716.x.
  - **[S], Jørgensen & Fiksen 2006 / IIASA IR-05-067 [F]:** "Atlantic cod eat little or nothing during the
    spawning season (Fordham and Trippel 1999)". Their model sets no net energy gain while spawning.
  - **[S], Michalsen et al. 2008 [F-s]:** Fordham & Trippel found that "females consumed greater quantities of
    food than males during both winter and post-spawning periods".
  - **snip (search summary of the abstract):** feeding rose sharply late in spawning, especially in females,
    near the end of spawning or after the final batch, at ~80-90 % of eggs released. Females lost ~29 % and
    males ~14 % of body weight.
- **Taranger et al. 2006 and Hansen et al. 2006 [A, in Ljusstyrd aptit/summary.md].** Maturation "results in
  loss of appetite and weight during spawning"; natural-light sea-cage cod "decreased in body weight during the
  spawning season (February-April)".
- **Conflicting: Yoneda & Wright 2005 [A].** In captivity, maturing females grew as well as immature fish
  through vitellogenesis (Nov-Feb), so appetite was not suppressed then.
- **Yoneda & Wright 2005, males [F-s].** Fed to satiation at 0.42-0.75 % BW/d (mean 0.49-0.53 %) from November
  to March, but "we were unable to control for individual food intake". No monthly intake.

### Cod, field (stomach data, weaker: emptiness is not intake)

- **Michalsen K, Johannesen E, Bogstad B (2008). Feeding of mature cod (*Gadus morhua*) on the spawning
  grounds in Lofoten. ICES J Mar Sci 65: 571-580. doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsn019 [F-s].**
  - "cod do feed, even when they are in a spawning state" (maturity stages 2 and 3 only).
  - Empty stomachs: 71 % on the spawning grounds against 25 % in the Barents Sea. Mean fullness index (TFI)
    0.34 against 1.43, a ratio of **0.24** [D]. The comparison season was not checked.
  - Females had fewer empty stomachs and fuller stomachs than males in some years.
  - The belief that cod stop feeding traces to Rollefsen (1933), who did not analyse stomachs.
- **Dutil J-D, Lambert Y, Chabot D (2000). Estimating natural mortality of wild cod from controlled feeding
  and starvation experiments conducted in the laboratory. ICES CM 2000/R:01 [F].** Gulf of St Lawrence, 1994.
  - From January to April the somatic condition decline "was less than predicted declines in unfed fish": cod
    "fed but did not meet their energy requirements in winter prior to spawning".
  - From April to May, "observed and predicted declines did not differ at 2°C or 6°C", so "wild cod probably
    fast at this time" (spawning). From May to June condition improved.
- **Schwalme & Chouinard 1999 [A].** "Cod fed very little during the five winter months (December to April)".
  This is a cold winter-fasting stock and confounds season with spawning.

### Haddock

- **Homans RES, Vladykov VD (1954). Relation between feeding and the sexual cycle of the haddock. J Fish Res
  Board Can 11: 535-542. doi:10.1139/f54-036 [A].** "As haddock ... approach the spawning period they
  practically cease to take food. This fast is continued throughout the spawning period. On the completion of
  spawning the fast is broken and the haddock feed voraciously for a few weeks regaining weight lost as a
  result of spawning." The stock is NW Atlantic and the method is presumably stomachs [J].
- **Hislop, Robb & Gauld 1978 [A].** Captive individual haddock were "maintained at different feeding levels
  ... from November until the completion of spawning". "Egg production and feeding level were correlated
  positively", and low-ration females (< 5 kcal/d) spawned less often with lighter eggs. Whether offered food
  was eaten during spawning is not in the abstract. This contrasts with Homans & Vladykov.
- **Adlerstein SA (2002). Comparison of stomach contents of haddock from the 1981 and 1991 North Sea
  International Stomach Sampling Projects. ICES J Mar Sci 59: 497-515. doi:10.1006/jmsc.2002.1197 [A via
  fetch].**
  - Content weights were lowest in winter in both years. Empty stomachs were most frequent in spring (1981)
    and in winter (1991).
  - Population-level, immatures included, and not linked to spawning by the authors.
- **FAO haddock synopsis (snip).** Adults feed less intensively in the spawning season but sometimes feed on
  capelin shoals at spawning.
- **Not found:** haddock broodstock appetite studies (Trippel & Neil; Martin-Robichaud) with measured intake.

### Synthesis [J]

- **Cod and haddock nearly fast during each fish's own spawning:** intake ~0-0.25 of normal (captive [S]; the
  Lofoten field ratio of 0.24 is an upper-side estimate because fish do feed).
- **Duration per fish is ~1-2 months.** Spawning lasts 50-60 d (cod) and 33-37 d (haddock); Fordham &
  Trippel's "up to 70 days" includes some late prespawning.
- **The stock's spawning spans ~3 months**, and larger fish spawn earlier, so the population-mean intake of
  mature fish falls by less than any one fish's does.
  - With ~45-60 d of near-fast inside a ~90 d window: mature-stock intake over Feb-Apr ≈ 1 - (45 to 60)/90 x
    (0.75 to 1.0) ≈ 0.33-0.62 of normal, i.e. a 38-67 % cut [D, crude].
  - Immatures are unaffected.
- **Appetite does not appear to drop during vitellogenesis (Oct/Nov-Jan)**: Yoneda & Wright 2005, and Dutil
  2000's January-April feeding. The cut begins late, around final maturation and hydration, and lasts until
  ~80-90 % of the eggs are released.
- **Females resume feeding earlier and eat more than males after spawning** (Fordham & Trippel [S]).
- **Post-spawning feeding is hyperphagic for "a few weeks"** (haddock [A]).

## Q7. Does the spring liver drawdown also happen in immature fish?

**Suggestive at most; low confidence.** NE Arctic haddock HSI (W-ov), Tronbøl 2022 Fig. 9a. The medians are
fish.md's eye-reads (±5 %), not re-read here; n per cell is not in the text (~10 or fewer).

| Group | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Immature | 2.9 | 3.65 | 3.5 | 3.3 | 3.3 | 4.0 | 5.0 | 2.7 | - |
| Maturing | 3.3 | 3.9 | 3.3 | 3.3 | 5.2 | 6.8 | 5.7 | 5.1 | 6.7 |
| Skipping | - | - | 2.4 | 2.1 | 4.1 | 3.9 | 4.0 | 3.2 | 0.85 |
| Spent | - | - | - | - | - | - | - | - | 0.88 |

Reading [J]:
- **Immature fish.** HSI rises to a February peak (5.0) and in March is back at the autumn level (2.7 against
  2.9-4.0 from August to January). That is the end of a winter rise, not a drawdown below the annual baseline.
- **Skippers.** The April 0.85 is a single small cell. Skippers are a low-condition group by definition.
- **Timing.** The series ends in April, before the NIFES low (May-Jul), so it cannot say whether immature fish
  share that low.
- **Weak hint only.** Non-spawning fish can lose liver in spring too, and part of the benthivore spring low
  could therefore be seasonal. This is not evidence that it is.

- **Cod, Gulf of St Lawrence.** The spring low affects the whole stock and is mostly winter-fasting metabolism
  (Schwalme & Chouinard 1999 [A]). Lambert & Dutil 2000 [A] found adults more affected than immatures.
- **Transferability [J].** Both stocks (NE Arctic and Gulf of St Lawrence) winter in colder water and with
  poorer winter food than the Skagerrak. Whether Skagerrak immatures share the spring liver low is **unknown**.
  NIFES haddock (mean 45 cm, mostly mature) shows the low but has no maturity split.
- **Implication [J].** Nothing found shows that the 0.76 -> 0.33 drop in the benthivore fill is entirely
  spawning, and nothing shows that it is not. Part may be seasonal, and the split cannot be settled from the
  literature found (low confidence either way).

## Q8. Weight loss at spawning

- **Cod, captive (Fordham & Trippel 1999, snip).** Females ~29 % and males ~14 % of body weight over spawning,
  gonad output included.
- **Cod, sea cages.** Weight decreased during Feb-Apr spawning (Hansen/Taranger et al. 2006 [A]); not
  quantified here.
- **Cod males.** Mass loss correlates with the number of courtship displays (Skjæraasen et al. 2010 [A]);
  values not in the abstract.
- **Haddock.** Not found. Implied by Homans & Vladykov's "regaining weight lost as a result of spawning" [A].
- **[D] Check on cod females.** If ~15-20 % of body weight leaves as gametes (wet, hydrated), the remaining
  ~10-14 % of the 29 % is somatic loss, consistent with ~1-2 months of fasting plus liver use (Q4).
