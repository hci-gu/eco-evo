# Schooling protection in the encounter a - evidence (2026-11-11)

Research only. Grades: [F] field (full text or data read), [L] lab (full text read), [A] abstract or
search-engine summary only, [S] secondary (cited in another source or in a sibling research note, not read
here), [D] derived here, [J] judgement. "Analog" = another species or system. PDFs in `papers/` were fetched
with the Unpaywall helper only (Handegard 2012, Andersen 2017); sibling-note PDFs are referenced in place.

## Theory and lab: encounter, dilution, confusion

- **E1** Turesson & Bronmark 2004, J Fish Biol 65:363, doi:10.1111/j.0022-1112.2004.00455.x [L]
  (PDF held: research_notes/"Attackrater ur motesteori"/data/schools_capture/turesson_bronmark2004_jfb.pdf;
  quotes via predators_encounter_evidence.md E2). Perch on roach: "34/168 = 0.20 for attacks towards single
  prey ... schools of two or more prey ... 1/31 = 0.03" per attack; per attack sequence 35/47. Pike 0.12
  pursuit / 0.36 sit-and-wait. The library's capture p 0.2 on fish prey is the SINGLE-prey figure.
- **E2** Herbert-Read et al. 2016, Proc R Soc B 283:20161671 [F, via sibling note]: sailfish on sardine
  schools, "Only 24% of these attacks resulted in a successful capture" (a school-hunting specialist).
- **E3** Neill & Cullen 1974, J Zool 172:549 [S]: cephalopod and fish predators; success per attack falls
  with prey shoal size (no numbers read; abstract not obtained).
- **E4** Landeau & Terborgh 1986, Anim Behav 34:1372, doi:10.1016/S0003-3472(86)80208-1 [A]: largemouth bass
  caught solitary minnows quickly; as the school grew they made more failed strikes and took longer to capture;
  one or two odd prey in a school of 8 restored success, not in a school of 15.
- **E5** Krause & Godin 1995, Anim Behav 50:465, doi:10.1006/anbe.1995.0260 [A]: cichlid hunting success
  decreased with shoal size (1-8); predators nevertheless preferred large, conspicuous shoals.
- **E6** Turner & Pitcher 1986, Am Nat 128:228, doi:10.1086/284556 [A]: "attack abatement" - neither avoidance
  of detection nor dilution alone protects; together they lower per-capita risk, without needing edge
  predation, confusion or active defence.
- **E7** Brock & Riffenburgh 1960, J Cons 25:307 [S, via citing works]: grouping lowers the chance that a
  member is attacked, provided the predator is satiated before the group is consumed (yield per encounter
  bounded) and detection of a group grows less than its number.
- **E8** Ioannou et al. 2011, Proc R Soc B 278:2985, doi:10.1098/rspb.2011.0003 [L, PMC full text summary]:
  sticklebacks searching for groups of 3-30 prey; at a fixed total, search time rose as prey were concentrated
  into fewer groups (group number effect LRT 5.04, p < 0.05); reaction distance = 69.5 ln(n + 1) (mm), i.e.
  logarithmic in group size; "prey are found with more difficulty when they aggregate"; grouping "decreases the
  absolute per capita risk".
- **E9** Krause & Ruxton 2002 (Living in Groups) via Pertzelan et al. 2022 [S]: "a mere twofold increase in the
  maximum detection distance for a 100-fold increase in the number of fish".
- **E10** Fryxell, Mosser, Sinclair & Packer 2007, Nature 449:1041, doi:10.1038/nature06177 [A]:
  group-dependent functional responses (encounters with groups, not individuals); grouping "substantially
  lowers" intake compared with random mixing and stabilises lion-wildebeest dynamics.
- **E11** Cosner, DeAngelis, Ault & Olson 1999, Theor Pop Biol 56:65, doi:10.1006/tpbi.1999.1414 [A]: mass-action
  derivation; homogeneous predators give a prey-dependent response; predators in a school or a limited
  encounter region give Beddington-DeAngelis, Hassell-Varley or ratio-dependent forms.
- **E12** Jeschke & Tollrian 2007, Anim Behav 74:387 [A]; Jeschke & Tollrian 2005, Oikos 111:547 [A]: confusion
  found in 16 of 25 predator-prey systems; visual predators confused mainly by agile prey; confusion need not
  make the functional response dome-shaped - it can change only its magnitude.
- **E13** Handegard et al. 2012, Curr Biol 22:1213, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2012.04.050 [F, PDF read]: spotted seatrout
  on juvenile Gulf menhaden schools (sonar, turbid estuary): "encounter rate saturates with prey group size,
  resulting in reduced per capita risk in larger groups"; risk proxy "increases steeply for groups below ~2 m2";
  predators in coordinated lines split schools; "for larger groups there is an approximately linear
  relationship between encounter rate and group size" (low visibility).
- **E14** Juanes, Buckel & Scharf 2002, chapter 12 in Handbook of Fish Biology and Fisheries [F, held in
  "Attackrater ur motesteori"/data/schools_capture]: "Usually about half of piscivore attacks on average are
  successful compared with 70-80% for planktivorous fishes"; success declines with prey:predator size ratio.

## Field: gadoids and clupeid schools

- **E15** Pitcher, Misund, Ferno, Totland & Melle 1996, ICES JMS 53:449 [A, via sibling notes]: Norwegian Sea,
  May: "Individual cod and haddock predators intimately accompanied the herring schools, their attacks causing
  frequent modifications to, but not dispersal of, school structure"; > 230 behavioural events at ~5 min
  intervals; saithe approaches made herring dive.
- **E16** Nottestad 1998, ICES CM 1998/J:20 [F, held]; Nottestad et al. 2002, ICES JMS 59:393 [F, held]:
  Vestfjord saithe, "85% of the individuals had recently been eating one or more herring"; only "relatively
  small sized (4.5 - 18 tonnes) and dense (2.6 - 5.2 ind*m-3) schools in shallow water (50 - 110 m) were
  pursued"; "Gadoids may accompany the schools and perform frequent shoal attacks but probably do not kill as
  many herring as the whales"; predatory events every ~27 min per tracked school.
- **E17** Andersen et al. 2017, MEPS 583:195, doi:10.3354/meps12319 [F, PDF read]: Bornholm Basin, late winter,
  cod on sprat. "Predation by cod took place primarily at dusk and dawn during ascent and descent of sprat
  associated with school dissolution and formation ... Sprat schools were located at the same depth as cod in
  the daylight hours". Of 194 sprat ingestion times, "87.1% ... at dusk (39.7%) or dawn (47.4%)", 12.4 % at
  night, one sprat by day. Cod feeding level f ~0.40. Twilight windows 17-18 h and 04:25-05:25 h.
  [D] (scripts/school_factor.py): ingestion per hour day 4.7e-4, twilight 0.435, night 0.011 (shares of 194);
  day / twilight 0.0011, day / night 0.042. Confounded by satiation after the dawn meal and by night overlap;
  read as an upper bound on the day-school per-capita risk relative to dissolving schools.
- **E18** Rindorf & Gislason 2005 [F, postprint; via "Attackrater ur motesteori"/addendum_validation_behaviour.md]:
  North Sea whiting, five sites: fish "ingested almost exclusively during dawn and dusk"; midday intake low,
  darkness low; clupeid plateau K 0.10-0.17 of Wmax (no fish prey above 0.20); Type II; no aggregative
  response. [D reading] K = 2.4-4.1 h per day at the digestive rate, the length of a twilight window.
  Field-equivalent a (three judgement conversions) 0.0063 (0.0005-0.10) vs the section 220 school variant
  0.0056 and dispersed 0.012: not discriminating.
- **E19** Engelhard et al. 2008 [F, via predators_encounter.md]: whiting intake vs local sandeel gives
  a 0.012-0.035, matching the dispersed variant (sandeel school and bury; different prey and site).
- **E20** Kaartvedt, Rostad & Klevjer 2009, MEPS 390:237 [A, sibling note]: Oslofjord, gadoids avoid < 15-20 % O2,
  "gadoids were foraging on the vertically migrating sprat at night". Solberg, Klevjer & Kaartvedt 2012,
  MEPS 464:245 [A]: under ice sprat shifted "from hiding at depth to hiding in schools".

## Field: school size, packing, diel dispersal

- **E21** Misund et al. 1995, ICES JMS 52:11 [F, held]: Norwegian Trench west slope, summer, 29 cm herring:
  "averaged 4.25 herring m-3" (0.3-22.2); mean area ~370 m2; "about 25 000 individuals, but varied from about
  100 ... to about 420 000".
- **E22** Fernandes et al. 2001, ICES CM 2001/Q:08 [F, held]: North Sea herring form "defined schools by day,
  either near the sea bed or in midwater. These schools disperse into surface waters at night"; day school
  length 21.2-27.2 m, height 3.5-5.2 m; break-up 18:16-20:56, re-formation 04:17-05:49.
- **E23** Beare et al. 2002, ICES JMS 59:469 [F, held]: herring "school size is regulated mainly by location and
  time-of-day and ... 'measured' school size is not influenced by the local 'school density'"; 0.40-0.84 schools
  per nmi. Supports a density-independent school size, i.e. a constant factor on a.
- **E24** Petitgas et al. 2001, ICES JMS 58:1150 [F, via sibling]: 0.28-0.59 schools/km2; 5.7-10 t per school
  [D, upper bound].
- **E25** Nilsson et al. 2003, Aquat Living Resour 16:317, doi:10.1016/S0990-7440(03)00039-1 [A]: Baltic, March;
  sprat and herring schools rise at dusk, break up once light falls below a threshold, then disperse as random
  walkers. Cardinale et al. 2003, Aquat Living Resour 16:283, doi:10.1016/S0990-7440(03)00007-X [A]: herring and
  sprat aggregate at dawn and disperse at dusk.
- **E26** North Sea acoustic survey cruise reports (BODC Solea reports, via search summary) [A]: "dense schools
  of herring/sprat were present largely during daytime. At night, the schools dispersed and often were not
  discernible from scattering layers".
- **E27** Fassler et al. (Heriot-Watt record, multifrequency backscatter of herring and Norway pout) [A]: the two
  "cohabit similar areas of the North Sea in summer and echotraces of their schools are difficult to
  distinguish" - Norway pout do form school echotraces. Albert 1994 (via sibling notes) [S]: pout 5-10 m above
  the bed by day. No pout school size or diel school cycle found.
- **E28** Misund 1993, ICES JMS 50:145, doi:10.1006/jmsc.1993.1016 [A, title and fragment only]: packing density,
  shape and size of herring, sprat and saithe schools; Misund 1990 (CM B:40, held, cite-with-consent): packing
  density inversely related to fish size. Not used for a number.
- **E29** Mackerel schools 1-7000 t, all < 100 m (research_notes/"Makrillens antipredatorbeteende" E5) [A]; mackerel
  school day and night (same note, E1, E4) [L/F].
- **E30** Blue whiting, whiting, juvenile cod and haddock: no school size, packing or diel school cycle found for
  the box's stages [J]. Search summaries: haddock juveniles stay nearer the bed than cod (Georges Bank) [A];
  no acoustic study of juvenile gadoid schools near the bed was found.

## Real refuges (what hide could stand for)

- **E31** Gotceitas & Brown 1993, Oecologia 93:31 [A]: juvenile cod hid in cobble interstices when a predator was
  present; fewer captured, longer latency to first capture; larger juveniles returned to finer substrate.
  Lindholm, Auster & Kaufman 1999, MEPS 180:247 [A]: emergent epifauna cut 0-group cod mortality vs flat sand;
  predator reaction distance fell with habitat complexity.
- **E32** Ellis & Gibson 1993, J Fish Biol (doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1993.tb01159.x) [A]: sand gave 0-group plaice
  only partial protection from cod, pollack, shrimps, crabs; predation higher in the dark. Anim Behav 1995
  (UHI/SAMS record "The effects of food, predation risk and endogenous rhythmicity on the behavioural activity
  of juvenile plaice"; authors not verified) [A]: predator presence suppressed migration and delayed foraging,
  "which greatly reduced feeding success" - a documented feeding-exclusive refuge state in a flatfish.
- **E33** Lynam & Brierley 2007, Mar Biol, doi:10.1007/s00227-006-0429-7 [A]: whiting residual survival
  correlated with Cyanea abundance; 0-group gadoids shelter among medusan tentacles in June-August (Hay et al.
  1990 [S]).
- **E34** Seals and porpoises: section 72 pair floors (porpoise 0.95 on planktivores, "biosonar not defeated by
  schooling"); research_notes/"Salars jakt i ljus och morker" prey_refuge.md [S]. Mammal pairs are not encounter
  derived and are out of scope here.

## Model-side facts used (repository, read-only)

  not a (dispersed predator variant)". The school variant (`pred_encounter_a.py` `aschool`: R_s 15 m, M_s 3 t,
  k 1, scan 1) gave 0.0056 vs dispersed 0.012; by prey type the day herring term fell ~6x.

  0.80-0.94 of the planktivore pairs of the water column and whiting; herring+sprat by day 0.29 (WC), 0.24
  (whiting), 0.51 (mackerel); Norway pout by day 0.54 / 0.52.

  (`_visible_share`); dark_ratio does not enter it.
