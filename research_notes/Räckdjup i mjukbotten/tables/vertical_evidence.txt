VERTICAL DISTRIBUTION OF INFAUNA BY SEDIMENT DEPTH, AND CROPPABLE EXPOSED MASS
Evidence log, literature round 2 (new sources only; round-1 sources listed in the
brief are not repeated: Dauwe et al. 1998, Loo et al. 1996, Rosenberg et al. 1997,
2002, Skold et al. 1994, Skold & Gunnarsson 1996, Zwarts et al. 1994, Gremare
et al. 2004, Zanzerl & Dufour 2017, etc.)

Tags: F = full text read; A = abstract only; S = secondary (named);
J = own judgement or arithmetic. Figure digitizing is stated where used.
Rows for the CSV are in tables/vertical_rows.csv.

Bottom line of this round
- No NEW sliced-core biomass profile was found for Skagerrak-like mud at
  70-300 m. Dauwe et al. 1998 station SK (280 m) stays the only direct anchor.
- New: one shelf-edge / slope sliced-core biomass profile (Flach & Heip 1996,
  Goban Spur, 185-208 m on fine sand, plus deeper stations); in situ burrow
  and disc-chamber depths and arm extension of Amphiura filiformis by
  time-lapse SPI (Solan & Kennedy 2002); the first measured arm vs disc mass
  split for an amphiurid (Dobson et al. 1991, Microphiopholis gracillima -
  NOT A. filiformis).
- The arm vs disc split of Amphiura filiformis itself is still not found.

1. Flach, E. & Heip, C. 1996. Vertical distribution of macrozoobenthos within
   the sediment on the continental slope of the Goban Spur area (NE Atlantic).
   Mar Ecol Prog Ser 141: 55-66. doi:10.3354/meps141055
   File: data/flach1996_meps141/m141p055.pdf (+ .txt). Tag F (text) and
   digitized figure (Fig. 5e/f).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Site: Goban Spur, Celtic margin, 12 stations 185-4460 m. Relevant here only
Stn 12 (185 m, 1 boxcore, 30 cm diameter) and Stn A (208 m, 3 boxcores),
10.5 C; Stn I (670 m) for context. Sediment: "Median grain size decreased
with increasing water depth from ~90 um at 208 m to ~8 um below 2000 m" -
i.e. Stn A (208 m) is very fine SAND on the shelf edge, not mud; Stn 12
(185 m) is assumed similar (J: no grain size given for it).
Slicing: "Boxcore samples were sliced in sediment layers of 0-1, 1-5, 5-10
and 10-15 cm and sieved on a 0.5 mm" sieve. Biomass as mg organic C m-2.

Quotes (abstract):
- "Most of the macrofauna was concentrated In the upper 1 cm of the sediment.
  The highest proportion (70 to 80%) was in the upper 1 cm found at
  intermediate depths (670 to 1425 m)" [DENSITY, not biomass].
- "At most stations the biomass was concentrated below the upper 1 cm, which
  was also reflected in the vertical distribution pattern of the mean
  individual weight, showing small individuals in the upper 1 cm and larger
  individuals in the deeper sediment layers."
Quotes (results):
- "Most of the macrofauna (40 to 80%) was found in the upper 1 cm of the
  sediment" [density].
- "Relatively high biomass values were found in the deepest sediment layer
  (10 to 15 cm) at shallow Stn A, whereas at the deeper stations the biomass
  in this deep sediment layer was relatively low."
- "At the shallowest stations deep burrowing Callianassa sp. were found,
  accounting for the high percentage below 5 cm sediment depth" [Crustacea
  density].
- Polychaetes: "at all stations small individuals were found in the upper
  1 cm and large individuals in the deeper sediment layers ... mainly
  filter-feeders and surface deposit-feeders ... in the upper 1 cm, whereas
  it was mainly subsurface deposit-feeders in the deeper layers."
- Echinoderms (mainly juvenile ophiuroids): "At Stns I and B ~90 % of the
  Echinodermata ... were found in the upper 1 cm" [density].
- Mollusca: "~75% of the Mollusca living in the upper 1 cm" [density].

Digitized: Fig. 5e/f, "Relative (%) importance in biomass (without large
individuals) of the different sediment layers". Page 6 rendered at 300 dpi,
pixel columns scanned (scripts/vert_column_scan.py; 0 % axis at y~372,

Caveats: biomass in organic C, "excluding the few extremely large

10 cm (~1 % vs ~48 %, the latter large deep burrowers), so they widen the
range rather than narrow it. Cores reach only 15 cm.

2. Solan, M. & Kennedy, R. 2002. Observation and quantification of in situ
   animal-sediment relations using time-lapse sediment profile imagery
   (t-SPI). Mar Ecol Prog Ser 228: 179-191. doi:10.3354/meps228179
   File: data/solan2002_meps228/m228p179.pdf (+ .txt). Tag F.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Site: Margaretta station, inner Galway Bay, Ireland, "approximately 22 m of
water", tidal ("mean bottom currents are usually < 25 cm s-1"), 5.8-16.8 C,
brown-green muddy fine sand ("fine sand (2 to 3 phi ...)"). Amphiura
filiformis > 700 ind m-2. Two 24 h time-lapse SPI sequences, 30 min frames.
NOT a Skagerrak analogue for depth or flow; the tidal feeding rhythm does
not transfer to 70-300 m.
Quotes:
- Excavation depth: "the maximum depth (Table 3) attained over the 24 h
  period ranging from 6.25 cm (Excavation B) to 11.73 cm (Excavation A).
  Apparent mean maximum excavation depth (+- 95% CI, n = 8) was
  8.95 +- 1.6 cm". Excavation = arm burrow + disc chamber, apparent against
  the faceplate.
- Disc chamber: "The disc chamber was located at 9.15 cm depth and was
  monitored for 7.5 h" (one individual). "Maximum apparent disc chamber
  volume was 0.27 cm3 after 7 h."
- Arm extension: "feeding 'arms' were positioned vertically and extended
  > 20 mm into the overlying water column".
- Rest: "During slack tidal periods, individuals remained below the sediment
  surface and either rested (i.e. arms largely retracted in the disc
  chamber) or maintained the integrity of the burrow structure (i.e. arms
  just short of the sediment-water interface)."
- Timing: "suspension feeding activity by A. filiformis commenced at
  approximately 1 to 2 h following high or low tide, peaked during the
  mid-tide, and ended at approximately 1 to 2 h before the next tide".
- Number of arms: "it is known that A. filiformis ordinarily extends at
  least 2 'arms' from the disc chamber" - cited prior knowledge, not
  measured here. Counts in Fig. 6 are per image (15 cm wide profile), not
  per individual ("approximately 25 to 35" at peak).
- Cropping: cites Duineveld & Van Noort 1986 and Baden et al. 1990.
J: time with arms out in a semidiurnal tide: each ~6.2 h half-cycle loses
1-2 h at each end -> 2.2-4.2 h of 6.2 h = 0.35-0.68 of the time (tidal
site; with weak deep currents the Skagerrak value is unknown).
J: disc depth here (9 cm, n=1; burrows 6-12 cm) is deeper than the ~4 cm
disc depth of Rosenberg et al. 1997 (round 1). Record both; do not average.

3. Dobson, W.E., Stancyk, S.E., Clements, L.A. & Showman, R.M. 1991.
   Nutrient translocation during early disc regeneration in the brittlestar
   Microphiopholis gracillima (Stimpson). Biol Bull 180: 167-184.
   File: data/biostor10379_microphiopholis/biostor-10379.pdf (BioStor scan,
   archive.org) + b.txt. Tag F (Table I read from the page image; OCR of the
   table was garbled).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Species: Microphiopholis gracillima - an AMPHIURID brittlestar (family
Amphiuridae; J: infaunal with buried disc and long arms like Amphiura; the
habitat and feeding mode were not in the parts read). It is an analogue for
Amphiura filiformis, not the species itself.
Method (biochemical composition, Table I): animals "dissected into the
following body fractions: proximal, medial, and distal thirds of the arms,
the oral" frame and the disc; dried 80 C; ashed 400 C 6 h. (The later
time-series experiment used arm halves; Table I uses thirds.) Units of
Table I are not visible in the header read (J: mg per individual).
Quotes (text): "About 60% of the total dry body weight is organic tissue (as
ash-free dry weight), and most of it is located in the arms. The central
disc has the highest organic content (74%) relative to inorganic material,
but this represents only 7% of the total dry body weight and 10% of the
total organic tissue weight. The proximal, medial, and distal arm parts and
the oral frame region contain 50 to 60% organic material, which accounts for
90% of the total organic material. The arms have a higher percentage of
organic material at their bases than at their tips."
Table I (mean +- SD, mg; % of whole):
  DW:   whole 91.32; disc 8.85 (9.7 %); proximal arms 29.27 (32 %); medial
        27.50 (30.0 %); distal 22.32 (24.5 %); oral frame 2.26 (2.4 %)
  AFDW: whole 52.47; disc 6.52 (12 %); proximal 15.96 (30.4 %); medial
        16.32 (31.1 %); distal 12.35 (23.5 %); oral frame 1.35 (2.5 %)
  Organic share of each part's DW: disc 74 %, proximal 54.5, medial 59.3,
        distal 55.3, oral frame 59.7 %.
  Protein (% of total protein): disc 24.35, proximal 31.24, medial 22.05,
        distal 17.91, oral frame 3.21.
Internal mismatch: text says disc = 7 % of DW and 10 % of organic; Table I
gives 9.7 % and 12 % (6.52/52.47). Use 0.10-0.12 for the disc's AFDW share.
J: arms (three thirds) = 85 % of AFDW (oral frame 2.5 % is inside the disc
body); distal third of all five arms = 23.5 % of AFDW, i.e. ~4.7 % per arm.
J transfer to Amphiura filiformis (flagged): A. filiformis also carries long
arms (~10x disc diameter, MarLIN, round 1), so arms ~0.85-0.90 of body
organic mass is plausible, but it is a cross-species transfer, not a
measurement.
J exposed arm mass of A. filiformis while feeding (arithmetic, crude):
  inputs: 2 arms out (Loo et al. 1996; Solan & Kennedy "at least 2"),
  10-20 mm out (Loo et al. 1996; Solan >20 mm), arm length L = 60-100 mm
  for adults (~10x a 6-10 mm disc, MarLIN), distal third of one arm =
  0.235/5 = 0.047 of body AFDW (Dobson analogue), medial third of one arm
  = 0.311/5 = 0.062.
  exposed share of arm length f = 10/100 ... 20/60 = 0.10-0.33 (within the
  distal third); with uniform mass per length inside that third, exposed
  mass per arm = 0.047 x f / (1/3) = 0.141 f = 0.014-0.047; two arms:
  0.03-0.09 of body organic mass (central ~0.05 at f 0.18).
  small animals (L 40 mm, 20 mm out, f 0.5) reach into the medial third:
  0.047 + 0.062 x (0.5 - 0.333)/0.333 = 0.078 per arm, 0.16 for two arms.
  Uniform mass per length overstates the tapering tip (Dobson: arm bases
  richer in organic matter than tips), so these are upper-leaning values.

   correlated benthic faunal quantity and infaunal burrow structures on the
   slopes of a marine depression. Estuar Coast Shelf Sci 50: 843-853.
   doi:10.1006/ecss.2000.0614. Tag S (search-engine snippet of the abstract;
   abstract page 403, Unpaywall: closed). NOT OBTAINED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Correction to the brief: the site is the Alkor Deep, northern Kattegat
(well oxygenated), not the Gullmarsfjord. Sampling: 0.1 m2 grabs and SPI
along four slope transects - i.e. probably no sliced cores. Snippet: "the
mean apparent RPD was found as deep as between 8.0 and 11.3 cm depth", RPD
correlated with water depth; abundance and biomass correlated with depth.

SYNTHESIS (J) - community biomass by layer, Skagerrak-like mud 70-300 m
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Inclusion rule: stations at 150-300 m water depth with sliced-core biomass:
Dauwe et al. 1998 SK (280 m, mud; central value, round 1) and Flach & Heip
1996 Stn 12 (185 m) and Stn A (208 m) (fine sand). Shallow North Sea
stations (German Bight, Frisian Front) are left out by depth, not by
sediment. Bounds are named station values; the width comes from the
non-mud shelf-edge stations and from cores of different length (15 cm
Flach vs 20 cm Dauwe).
  0-2 cm:   low 0.27 (SK) ... high 0.42 (Stn A 0-5 cm, the ceiling of its
            0-2 cm share; its 0-1 cm alone is 0.31-0.32, Stn 12 0.31)
  0-5 cm:   0.43 (Stn A) / 0.51 (SK) / 0.66 (Stn 12)
  5-10 cm:  0.09 (Stn A) / 0.30 (SK) / 0.33 (Stn 12)
  >10 cm:   0.01 (Stn 12, 10-15) / 0.19 (SK, 10-20) / 0.48 (Stn A, 10-15)
