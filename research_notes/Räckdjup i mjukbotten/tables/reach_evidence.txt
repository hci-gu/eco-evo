REACH DEPTH OF THE BENTHIVORE MEMBERS INTO SOFT SEDIMENT - evidence log (sub-question 1)
Compiled 2026-11-19 by a literature agent. Research only; no model files touched.
Tags: F = full text read, A = abstract only, S = secondary (cited in another source), J = judgement.
"reused" = the file already sits in another research_notes folder (path given); "new" = not used by the
three refuge/exposure notes before.

HEADLINE: no measured bite, dig or pit depth exists for haddock, cod, plaice, dab, long rough dab or
witch on mud (about 25 targeted searches: feeding pits, excavation, buried-prey experiments, sidescan,
bioturbation trait lists, flatfish feeding mechanics). Every reach below is a BOUND or an ordinal
statement, not a measurement. The tightest bound is the Amphiura disc: dab (F) and, per Skold et al. 1994,
haddock (S) crop the arms and leave the disc, so the routine reach is shallower than the disc depth.

=====================================================================================================
DAB (Limanda limanda; 0.045 of group biomass)
=====================================================================================================
- Kaiser & Ramsay 1997, MEPS 152:307 [F, reused: research_notes/"Exponering av bottenbyten"/data/
  A_infauna/kaiser_ramsay1997_meps152/]. 40 m, turbid, Amphiura ground. "In undisturbed sediments Amphiura
  spp. are positioned with their oral discs buried within the sediment while their arms protrude from the
  sediment surface. Under these circumstances, dab graze the exposed arms, while leaving the oral disc
  intact". Control-line stomachs held arms; after a beam trawl pass, "much larger numbers of oral discs";
  "as the tickler chains pass through surface layers of the sediment, they dig out or expose entire
  brittlestars". => dab reach < Amphiura disc depth (UPPER BOUND, F).
- Hinz, Kroncke & Ehrich 2005, J Fish Biol 67:125, doi 10.1111/j.0022-1112.2005.00918.x [A, new, via
  OpenAlex]: "only prey densities in the environment significantly influenced prey choice in dab, while
  other characteristics such as the position of the prey in the habitat, its palatability or mobility did
  not have significant effects ... although trends were apparent that suggested that buried living fauna
  was less likely to be ingested, which was also observed in previous studies." Main prey Pariambus
  typicus (epibenthic caprellid) and Ophiura albida (epifaunal ophiuroid).
- Johnson, Gorelli, Jenkins, Hiddink & Hinz 2015, Proc R Soc B 282:20142336 [F, reused file:
  research_notes/"Fiskgrupper i rutan"/benthivores/data/lit/johnson2015/; new to the refuge notes].
  Irish Sea mud: "dab are large-eyed visual, opportunistic" feeders; burrowing crustaceans (Goneplax,
  Callianassa, Jaxea) "Presence of these species in the stomachs of dab was generally as whole chelae
  only"; "feeding solely on their appendages may well remove the necessity to spend a lot of energy
  digging individual prey items out from their burrows". => dab crop exposed parts.
- de Groot 1969, J Cons 32:385 [F, reused: Refugium.../data/vliz_ocrd_268389/]: "Plaice, flounder and dab
  are day feeders"; group 3 = "day feeders, which although visual feeders, may use chemical clues ... They
  find their food in or near the bottom"; dab "more or less pure crustacea feeders".
=> Reach (J on F bounds): 0 / 0.5 / 1 cm (low / central / high) plus exposed parts.

=====================================================================================================
LONG ROUGH DAB (Hippoglossoides platessoides; 0.126)
=====================================================================================================
- de Groot 1969 [F, reused]: "Some species like the Greenland halibut, halibut and long rough dab link up
  with the Bothidae" (the visual hunters of "fish or ... other quick moving animals, such as shrimps").
- ICES stomach DB, NNS/CSNS pre-2000 [D, reused: research_notes/"Fiskgrupper i rutan"/benthivores/
  tables/ices_benth_diet_taxa.csv]: identified echinoderms are epifaunal Ophiothrix / Ophiuridae, a trace
  of Amphiura (0.14 g); bivalves unidentified. Diet mostly crustaceans and ophiuroids (group diet note:
  B 85 %, M&D 1991 via Ntiba & Harding 1993 [S]).
- Gonzalez-Iglesias et al. 2003 NAFO SCR 03/23 [A, new, via OpenAlex]: American plaice (same species,
  NW Atlantic and Svalbard) "is a daytime feeder"; main prey fish, Ophiuroidea, Crustacea.
- No digging, siphon or tail cropping reported.
=> Reach (J): 0 / 0.5 / 1 cm; a visual epibenthic feeder.

=====================================================================================================
WITCH (Glyptocephalus cynoglossus; 0.017)
=====================================================================================================
- Bergstad, Wik & Hildre 2003, J Northw Atl Fish Sci 31:165, Table 6 and text [F, reused file:
  research_notes/"Fiskgrupper i rutan"/benthivores/data/lit/bergstad2003/; new to the refuge notes].
  Skagerrak 156-490 m, 134 stomachs: polychaetes 59.3 %W, bivalves 20.4 %W of the identified part.
  "Twenty-three families of polychaetes were recorded, mainly Polynoidae, Sigalionidae, Capitellidae,
  Opheliidae, Cirratulidae, and Ampharetidae. The more prominent bivalve families were Scrobiculariidae and
  Nuculanidae." Living depth (J, tables/stomach_taxa_living_depth.csv): scale worms surface/shallow;
  cirratulids and ampharetids feed at the surface with palps/tentacles; Abra (Scrobiculariidae) 0-2 cm;
  Nuculanidae shallow protobranchs; capitellids the only deep-burrowing family (small ones also shallow).
- de Groot 1969 [F, reused]: witch has the "plaice type" brain (moderate olfactory, very large optic lobes;
  Evans 1937 [S]); intestine like lemon sole (a polychaete-mollusc feeder).
=> Reach (J): 0.5 / 1 / 2 cm; small mouth, surface and shallow polychaetes and small bivalves.

=====================================================================================================
PLAICE (Pleuronectes platessa; 0.038)
=====================================================================================================
- Johnson et al. 2015 [F, reused file]: "Plaice predominantly target infaunal prey of limited mobility
  (such as polychaetes and bivalves) through a well-developed suction capability, horizontal mouth/head
  down foraging position and large olfactory bulb"; Chesson alpha: preference for Abra alba, Glycera spp.
  and Nephtys spp.; "increased energetic costs of targeting the deep burrowing ... A. alba ... with more
  time and energy spent swimming, searching and foraging for buried food items". Abra alba: "a shallow
  burrower", inhalant siphon "held a few millimetres above the sediment surface" (MarLIN 1722 [S]).
  Glycera and Nephtys are mobile burrowers of the top ~5-10 cm (J; depth not in the paper).
- de Vlas 1979 (Wadden Sea) via de Vlas 1981 [S, earlier notes]: ~1/3 of plaice intake = siphon tips,
  tail ends, heads; Trevallion 1971 via Saward et al. 1975 [S, earlier notes]: juvenile plaice graze
  Tellina siphon tips.
- Hill, Burrows & Hughes 2000, J Fish Biol 56:1497 [A, new]: juvenile plaice "foraging for buried
  bivalves" by pause-travel search; burial depth not in the abstract.
- ICES stomach DB, Skagerrak 2024 [D, reused]: 185 fed plaice, ophiuroids 47 %, bivalves 26 %,
  polychaetes 15 %, echinoids 10 % (identified families: tables/stomach_taxa_living_depth.csv; deep-
  burrowing families < 2 % of the identified polychaete mass).
=> Reach (J on F mechanism): 1 / 2 / 4 cm (head-down suction into the top layer; whole shallow bivalves and
   mobile burrowers when near the surface; siphons and tails of deeper animals).

=====================================================================================================
HADDOCK (Melanogrammus aeglefinus; 0.386)
=====================================================================================================
- Skold, Loo & Rosenberg 1994, MEPS 103:81 [F, reused; Exponering.../data/A_infauna/skold1994_meps103]:
  dab, "haddock Melanogrammus aeglefinus (Mattson 1992) and Norwegian lobster ... These predators do not
  generally consume the entire brittle star but crop only the arms, which are later regenerated."
  => haddock routine reach < Amphiura disc depth (S: Skold's reading of Mattson 1992; "generally").
- Mattson 1992, Sarsia 77:33 [A, reused via the exposure note]: haddock feeding "aimed primarily at the
  entire fraction of small to medium-sized endobenthos"; "The prey size and the feeding depth in the
  sediment increased with fish size." Stomach contents "highly particulate". Ordinal only: no depth.
- Schuckel, Ehrich, Kroncke & Reiss 2010, J Fish Biol 77:98, doi 10.1111/j.1095-8649.2010.02657.x [A, the
  following sentence new]: "tubicolous polychaetes of the family spionids, especially Spiophanes kroyeri
  and Spiophanes bombyx as well as the tentaculate Phoronis spp., were not consumed and potentially avoided
  due to their ability to withdraw below the feeding depths or due to chemical deterrents"; "Other prey
  characteristics, such as the position in the sediment ... had no significant effect on the selection
  process". => a finite feeding depth; Spiophanes and Phoronis tubes reach ~10-20 cm (J, not measured
  there).
- Jensen c. 1960, "Haddock", US Fish & Wildlife Service Fishery Leaflet 489 [F text, new, data/
  jensen_noaa_leaflet489/; a popular account, anecdote]: "Adult haddock feed mostly on slow-moving small
  animals found on or burrowing in the upper layers of the ocean floor"; "Haddock gorge themselves on
  burrowing worms which they grub out of the bottom with their muscular lips ... 'carp of the sea' because
  of their habit of rooting on the bottom for food." No number; carries no value.
- Inferior mouth, the only gadoid of the mix built for infauna (morphology note, research_notes/"Bentos
  atkomlighet for torskfiskar"/evidence_B_gadoid_foraging.md [F there]).
=> Reach (J on S/A bounds): 1 / 2 / 4 cm. The high end is set at the Amphiura disc (~4 cm, Rosenberg et al.
   1997 [F]) because haddock "generally" leave the disc; the earlier note's 5 cm had no source.

COD (Gadus morhua; 0.314)
=====================================================================================================
- Brawn 1969, J Fish Res Board Can 26:583 [A, OpenAlex; reused]: "Food covered by sand was not found."
  "Food below gravel or stones was located by smell and uncovered by carrying away gravel in the mouth";
  "food deeply buried was uncovered by several fish digging in the same place". The two statements
  conflict for fine sediment; the gravel case does not transfer to mud (J).
- Mattson 1990, Sarsia 75:247 [A, reused]: cod adapted "for catching prey on and below the bottom
  surface. It captures prey mainly by suction".
- ICES stomach DB, Skagerrak and box 2000+ [D, reused]: identified infaunal prey almost only Aphrodita
  aculeata (sea mouse, a surface-dwelling scale worm); infauna is a trace of the box cod diet.
- Fischer, Lewis, Hawkins & Roberts 2025, Mar Environ Res, doi 10.1016/j.marenvres.2025.107158 [S, press
  release only, new]: cod scored a "vertical excavator" in a UK fish-bioturbation trait list; no depth.
=> Reach (J): 0 / 0.5 / 1 cm on mud plus exposed parts.
